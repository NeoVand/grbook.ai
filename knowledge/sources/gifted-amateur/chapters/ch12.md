# GA ch12 · The energy-momentum tensor

> Revisits the energy-momentum tensor as the matter side of Einstein's equation: reads off energy density, momentum density, energy flux and stress by feeding an observer's velocity into its slots, builds the perfect-fluid and point-particle tensors, rehearses local conservation with a scalar current, upgrades the flat-space law to the covariant constraint div T = 0, and warns that in curved spacetime this is not a global energy-conservation law because parallel observers (Killing symmetry) are generally missing.

**Pages:** printed 131–140 · pdf 148–157 · **Difficulty:** math 3/5, conceptual 3/5, novice-friendliness 3/5

Sections 12.1-12.2 are gentle once slot notation and the metric signature are comfortable. Section 12.3's delta-function manipulations and the curved-space sqrt(-g) factor are a step up for readers without a mathematical-methods background. Section 12.4 mixes a clear charge-current rehearsal with a terse closing argument (energy currents, Killing's equation) whose logic is compressed and has a few loose statements, and covariant derivatives of rank-2 tensors are used before they are developed (deferred to Part V).

## Role in the book

Second half of the set-up for the field equation in Part II. Chapter 11 supplied the geometric left-hand side (Riemann, Ricci); this chapter supplies the physical right-hand side and, crucially, the constraint div T = 0 that Chapter 13 immediately uses to reject the naive guess R^{mu nu} = kappa T^{mu nu} and force the Einstein tensor. It spirals back to the dust tensor T = p (x) J first met in Chapter 4, extends it to perfect fluids that are then used throughout cosmology (Part III) and stellar structure (Part IV), and plants seeds for Killing vectors (Ch. 33, Part IV), covariant derivatives of general tensors (Part V), fluid dynamics (Ch. 39) and field-theoretic energy-momentum tensors (Ch. 40).

## Learning objectives

- Reader can extract energy density, momentum density and energy flux measured by any observer by inserting that observer's 4-velocity (and a unit spatial vector) into the slots of T.
- Reader can state the physical meaning of each block of T^{mu nu} in an observer's orthonormal frame (energy density, momentum density = energy flux, momentum flux = stress) and explain why spatial components also act as force per unit area.
- Reader can track the sign changes when raising hatted indices with eta (time-space components flip, time-time and space-space do not).
- Reader can build the perfect-fluid tensor diag(rho, p, p, p) in the rest frame, decompose it into u (x) u and projector pieces, and write the frame-independent form in which the fluid velocity outer product carries rho + p and the metric carries p.
- Reader can compute the trace T = -rho + 3p and use it to show that radiation (rho = 3p) has a traceless energy-momentum tensor.
- Reader can write the energy-momentum tensor of a uniformly moving dust swarm with factors of c and gamma, and explain the gamma factors.
- Reader can represent a point particle's rest-mass density, mass current and energy-momentum tensor as proper-time integrals of a 4D delta function, evaluate the time integral to expose the 1/u^0 factor, and insert 1/sqrt(-g) for curved spacetime.
- Reader can distinguish local from global conservation, derive the continuity equation's integral form, and express total flux through a closed 3-surface as the integral of J^mu dSigma_mu.
- Reader can unpack d_mu T^{mu nu} = 0 into energy and momentum conservation equations and upgrade it to T^{mu nu}_{;mu} = 0 using a local inertial frame (comma goes to semicolon).
- Reader can take the covariant divergence of the perfect-fluid tensor using the Leibniz rule and metric compatibility, obtaining the fluid's equation of motion in tensor form.
- Reader can explain why div T = 0 does not give global energy conservation in curved spacetime: defining an energy current needs a velocity field whose symmetrized covariant derivative vanishes (Killing's equation), which generic spacetimes lack.

## Teaching approach

A short, component-heavy chapter that treats T as an old friend from Part I and systematically answers two questions: what are its components for matter we care about, and what constraint does it obey? Each construction is done first in the simplest frame (rest frame, flat space, LIF), recast as a manifestly covariant tensor statement, and then promoted to curved spacetime by eta -> g or comma -> semicolon. Conservation of T is rehearsed on the simpler scalar-charge current before being applied to the tensor, and the chapter ends with an unusually candid caveat that the resulting law is not energy conservation in the everyday sense, supported by a two-line calculation that lands on Killing's equation. Heavier proofs are pushed into margin notes, exercises and later chapters.

**Style:** spiral-revisit, components-first, example-driven, physical-motivation-first, analogy-driven, conversational-informal

**Narrative arc**

1. Motivating question: curvature is on the left of the field equation; what quantity sits on the right and what property must it have?
2. Recall T from Chapter 4 (momentum 1-form times number-current 1-form) and give the slot recipe: one velocity gives minus the momentum density, two give energy density.
3. Interpret orthonormal-frame components: energy density, momentum density/energy flux, momentum flux that doubles as stress; summarize in a block-matrix cartoon.
4. Model 1, perfect fluid: write diag(rho, p, p, p) at rest, split it into u (x) u and projector pieces, state the covariant form, check with the trace (radiation is traceless), and promote eta -> g.
5. Model 2, particles: moving dust swarm matrix with c restored; then delta functions along world lines for rest-mass density, mass current and T, with the 1/u^0 length-contraction factor and 1/sqrt(-g) in curved spacetime.
6. Conservation rehearsal with a scalar current: charge, continuity equation, local versus global conservation, flux through flat then curved 3-surfaces, closed-boundary integral over a 4-volume.
7. Transfer to T: d_mu T^{mu nu} = 0 in flat spacetime, unpacked into energy and momentum equations.
8. Upgrade: in a LIF partial and covariant derivatives agree, so the covariant law T^{mu nu}_{;mu} = 0 holds everywhere; apply it to the perfect fluid via the Leibniz rule to obtain the fluid equation of motion.
9. Critical check: define energy via E = -p(u) and parallel observers; show flat-space energy-current conservation needs u^nu_{,mu} = 0; in curved spacetime the analogous requirement is Killing's equation, which generally fails.
10. Resolution: gravity and matter exchange energy, so div T = 0 is a local (approximate) conservation statement and a vital constraint; hand T and its constraint to Chapter 13.

**Signature moves**

- Treat T as a slot machine: everything an observer measures comes from inserting that observer's velocity (and unit spatial vectors) into the slots.
- Build a tensor where it is diagonal and obvious, then reverse-engineer the matrix as a combination of tensor products (u (x) u and eta + u (x) u) so it becomes frame-independent.
- Use the trace as an instant sanity check that also delivers physics (radiation is traceless).
- Rehearse a conservation law on a scalar charge current before attempting the tensor version, and move from a flat box-shaped 3-surface to arbitrary closed hypersurfaces.
- Promote flat-space laws to curved spacetime by a named rule (eta -> g, comma goes to semicolon) justified by the local inertial frame argument already used in Example 8.8.
- Represent point particles with delta functions on world lines, explaining the resulting gamma factor through length contraction rather than algebra alone.
- Deliberately undercut an attractive interpretation (div T = 0 as energy conservation) with a short explicit calculation, then give the physical reason (gravity does work on matter).
- Temporarily restore c to make component meanings dimensionally transparent, then drop it again.
- Park proofs in margin notes with pointers (Part V for tensor covariant derivatives, Appendix C for diffeomorphism invariance, Ehlers-Geroch for the geodesic principle).

### §12.1 Another look at the energy-momentum tensor — p.131 (pdf 148)

Recalls that T can be viewed as a current of momentum and writes its (0,2) form, which is symmetric. A slot recipe follows: inserting an observer's 4-velocity gives minus the 4-momentum density 1-form; adding a unit spatial vector picks a component; inserting the velocity twice gives the energy density. Components in the observer's orthonormal frame are then interpreted: the time-time entry is energy density, time-space entries are momentum density (equivalently energy flux), space-space entries are the flux of i-momentum across a surface with normal e_j, which, since momentum transfer per unit time is force, is also a stress. Margin notes connect to the dust picture T = p (x) J and track sign changes when raising hatted indices. Fig. 12.1 and the block cartoon Fig. 12.2 summarize.

**Concepts:** energy-momentum tensor, symmetry of the energy-momentum tensor, slot recipe for observer-measured energy and momentum, orthonormal-frame components of the energy-momentum tensor, stress as momentum flux, raising and lowering orthonormal-frame indices, dust energy-momentum tensor as an outer product

**Key moves**
- T = T_{mu nu} omega^mu (x) omega^nu with T_{mu nu} = T_{nu mu} (12.2)
- T( , u) = -(momentum density), T(a, u) = -(its a-component), T(u, u) = energy density (12.4)-(12.6)
- With u = e_0 in the observer frame: T_00 energy density, T_i0 = -T^{i0} minus momentum density (12.8)-(12.9)
- Spatial components read two ways: i-momentum flux through surface with normal e_j, or i-force (per area) across that surface (12.10)-(12.11)
- Margin: raising both hatted indices flips sign only for mixed time-space components (12.7)

### §12.2 Example energy-momentum tensors — p.132 (pdf 149)

Argues that gravity acts on scales where matter is effectively continuous, so fluids are the natural model. Defines a perfect fluid (rest-frame energy density rho and isotropic pressure p, no viscosity or shear) and, in a margin note, dust (p = 0) and the equation of state. In the rest frame T is diag(rho, p, p, p); this matrix is decomposed using u (x) u = diag(1,0,0,0) and eta, giving T = (rho + p) u (x) u + p eta. The trace is -rho + 3p, so a photon gas is traceless (linked in a margin note to massless excitations and scale invariance). The equivalence principle then upgrades eta to g, giving the general-coordinate perfect-fluid tensor with indices up or down.

**Concepts:** perfect fluid, pressure, dust, equation of state, total energy density versus rest-mass density, perfect-fluid energy-momentum tensor, spatial projection tensor, trace of the energy-momentum tensor, radiation equation of state, traceless energy-momentum tensor and massless fields, valid tensor equation, principle of general covariance

**Key moves**
- Rest-frame matrix diag(rho, p, p, p) (12.12) from energy density plus pressure as normal force per area
- u_mu = (-1,0,0,0) so (u (x) u) = diag(1,0,0,0) (12.14)
- Split: rho(u (x) u) + p[eta + u (x) u] (12.15); both sides carry matching lower indices so it is a tensor equation
- T = (rho + p) u (x) u + p eta (12.16)-(12.17)
- Trace: T = -(rho + p) + 4p = -rho + 3p, zero for radiation (12.18)
- Swapping eta for g yields the curved-spacetime fluid tensor (12.19), usable with indices up or down

### §12.3 Classical particles — p.134 (pdf 151)

Treats point-like matter. First a uniform swarm of dust moving in the x-y plane: with c restored, T^{00} = n m gamma c^2, T^{0i} = n m gamma c v^i, T^{ij} = n m gamma v^i v^j, assembled into a symmetric matrix. Then a field-theory style description: a particle's rest-mass density is m times the proper-time integral of a 4D delta function along its world line. Changing the integration variable to coordinate time gives (m/u^0) times a 3D delta, the 1/u^0 = 1/gamma reflecting length contraction. The same construction gives the mass current (and, in a margin note, the charge current) and the point-particle energy-momentum tensor, a pressureless version of the fluid tensor. In curved spacetime each delta is divided by sqrt(-g) so it becomes a scalar.

**Concepts:** energy-momentum tensor of a moving dust swarm, world-line delta-function representation of a point particle, four-dimensional Dirac delta function, mass current, four-current, invariant volume element, length contraction of number density, dust

**Key moves**
- Component-by-component construction of moving-dust T^{mu nu} (12.20)
- rho_0(x) = m int dtau delta^(4)[x - z(tau)] (12.21)-(12.22)
- Variable change tau -> z^0 exposes 1/u^0 and leaves a 3D delta locating the particle (12.23)
- Mass current and T^{alpha beta} as integrals weighted by u^alpha and u^alpha u^beta (12.25)-(12.26); charge current in margin (12.24)
- Curved spacetime: divide delta by sqrt(-g) (12.27)

### §12.4 Conservation laws — p.136 (pdf 153)

Builds conservation laws in two stages. First a scalar charge with 4-current J = (rho, J-vector): total charge as a volume integral, the continuity equation d_mu J^mu = 0 as local conservation, contrasted with merely global conservation. Flux is then counted through a flat 3-surface of extent a x b x tau, generalized to J^alpha Sigma_alpha and to curved 3-surfaces, and finally to a closed boundary of a 4-volume where the net flux vanishes (Fig. 12.3). The same logic applies to baryon number and rest-mass currents. Second, for 4-momentum: d_mu T^{mu nu} = 0, whose nu = 0 and nu = i parts are energy and momentum conservation. In a local inertial frame connection coefficients vanish, so the law holds with semicolons, and general covariance gives T^{mu nu}_{;mu} = 0, i.e. div T = 0 (margin gives the two-connection-term formula). The divergence of the perfect-fluid tensor is expanded with the Leibniz rule and metric compatibility into the fluid's equation of motion.

**Concepts:** four-current, conserved charge, continuity equation, local versus global conservation, hypersurface element and flux through a 3-surface, spacetime divergence theorem for a conserved current, local conservation of energy-momentum in flat spacetime, local inertial frame, comma-goes-to-semicolon rule, covariant conservation law for the energy-momentum tensor, covariant derivative of a rank-2 tensor, Leibniz rule for the covariant derivative, metric compatibility, relativistic perfect-fluid equations of motion

**Key moves**
- Q = int rho d^3x (12.28); integral form -dQ/dt = flux out (12.29)
- d rho/dt + div J = 0, i.e. d_mu J^mu = 0 (12.30)-(12.31)
- Charge through box-shaped 3-surface: J^z Sigma_z -> J^alpha Sigma_alpha -> int J^mu dSigma_mu
- Closed boundary: oint J^mu dSigma_mu = 0 (12.32), charge in through the past face leaves through the future face
- d_mu T^{mu nu} = 0 (12.33); nu = 0 energy, nu = i momentum (Example 12.8)
- LIF: comma = semicolon (12.34)-(12.35), promote to T^{mu nu}_{;mu} = 0 (12.36), div T = 0 (12.38)
- Perfect fluid divergence expanded (12.39)-(12.41)

### Is div T = 0 energy conservation? (closing part of §12.4) — p.138 (pdf 155)

Questions the natural reading of div T = 0 as energy conservation. Energy is observer-dependent (E = -p(u) for a co-located observer), so assigning energy to a distant object needs a family of parallel observers, u^nu_{,mu} = 0 (Fig. 12.4). For dust, the energy current M^mu = -T^{mu nu} u_nu is conserved in flat spacetime exactly because u is constant. In curved spacetime the same step needs the symmetrized covariant derivative of u to vanish, which is Killing's equation and fails in general because parallelism depends on path; a margin note adds that summing T^{mu nu} dSigma_nu over a closed surface would require adding vectors from different tangent spaces. Physically, gravity and matter do work on each other, so only approximate local conservation survives. Margin notes call div T = 0 a conservation law by slight abuse of language, cite diffeomorphism invariance as its origin, and connect it to the geodesic principle. The two-bullet chapter summary closes the unit.

**Concepts:** observer-measured energy, family of parallel observers, energy current vector, path dependence of parallel transport, Killing's equation, non-existence of global energy conservation in curved spacetime, diffeomorphism invariance as origin of the conservation law, geodesic principle from energy-momentum conservation, energy conditions

**Key moves**
- E = -p(u) needs a local observer; distant energies need parallel observers u^nu_{,mu} = 0 (12.42)
- Dust: -T(u,u) = E J(u), energy current M^mu = -T^{mu nu} u_nu (12.43)-(12.44)
- d_mu M^mu = -T^{mu nu} u_{nu,mu} after using d_mu T^{mu nu} = 0 (12.45)
- Curved: need (T^{mu nu} u_nu)_{;mu} = 0, and symmetry of T reduces the condition to u_{nu;mu} + u_{mu;nu} = 0 (12.46)-(12.47)
- Physical reading: gravity-matter energy exchange; approximate local conservation over small regions

## Concepts

### energy-momentum tensor

*mathematical-object · core* · also: stress-energy tensor, T(x), energy-momentum tensor field

A symmetric rank-2 tensor field whose contraction with observer velocities and spatial unit vectors gives the energy density, momentum density, energy flux and stress of matter and fields at each event; it is the source term in Einstein's equation.

**How introduced:** Presented as the right-hand-side counterpart of curvature, recalled from Chapter 4 as a momentum current, and written in (0,2) component form with a symmetry statement.

**Prerequisites:** tensor as a linear map on vectors and 1-forms, 4-momentum, number-current 4-vector, metric tensor

$$
\mathbf{T}(x)=T_{\mu\nu}(x)\,\boldsymbol{\omega}^\mu\otimes\boldsymbol{\omega}^\nu
$$

$$
T_{\mu\nu}=T_{\nu\mu}
$$

**Where:** GA §12.1 p.131 (pdf 148); GA §12.1 p.132 (pdf 149)

### dust energy-momentum tensor as an outer product

*solution-or-model · revisited* · also: T = p (x) J, swarm-of-particles picture

For non-interacting particles all moving together, T equals the outer product of the momentum 1-form carried by each particle and the number-current 1-form, so T( , u) is minus the momentum density and T(u, ) minus the energy flux seen by observer u.

**How introduced:** Recalled in a margin note from Chapter 4 as the intuitive anchor for the general tensor.

**Prerequisites:** momentum 1-form, number-current 4-vector, tensor product

$$
\mathbf{T}(\;,\;)=\tilde{\mathbf{p}}(\;)\otimes\tilde{\mathbf{J}}(\;)
$$

**Notes:** Example 12.10 writes the product in the opposite order; symmetry makes this harmless.

**Where:** GA §12.1 p.131 (pdf 148); GA §12.4 p.138 (pdf 155)

### symmetry of the energy-momentum tensor

*identity · introduced* · also: T_{mu nu} = T_{nu mu}

The energy-momentum tensor is symmetric, so its two slots are interchangeable; physically momentum density equals energy flux (with c = 1) and the stress tensor is symmetric.

**How introduced:** Asserted when the (0,2) form is written; used to say it does not matter which slot receives the velocity, and later to simplify divergences and reduce u_{nu;mu} = 0 to Killing's equation.

**Prerequisites:** energy-momentum tensor

$$
T_{\mu\nu}=T_{\nu\mu}
$$

**Notes:** The book asserts rather than derives symmetry; the dust model makes it plausible.

**Where:** GA §12.1 p.131 (pdf 148); GA §12.4 p.137 (pdf 154); GA §12.4 p.139 (pdf 156)

### slot recipe for observer-measured energy and momentum

*technique · developed* · also: filling the slots of T, slot machine

To obtain what an observer with 4-velocity u measures, feed u into one slot of T to get minus the 4-momentum density 1-form, feed a unit spatial vector a into the other slot to pick out a component, or feed u into both slots to get the energy density.

**How introduced:** Given as a recipe right after the tensor is written, reusing the Part I idea that tensors are machines with slots.

**Prerequisites:** energy-momentum tensor, 4-velocity, 1-form

$$
\mathbf{T}(\;,\mathbf{u})=-(\text{momentum density})
$$

$$
\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u})=\text{energy density}
$$

**Where:** GA §12.1 p.131 (pdf 148); GA §12.1 p.132 (pdf 149)

### orthonormal-frame components of the energy-momentum tensor

*physical-quantity · core* · also: block structure of T^{mu nu}, Fig. 12.2 cartoon

In an observer's orthonormal frame (u = e_0), T^{00} is energy density, T^{0i} = T^{i0} is momentum density or equivalently energy flux along i, and T^{ij} is the flux of i-momentum across a surface with normal e_j.

**How introduced:** Derived by substituting basis vectors into the slot recipe and summarized in a block-matrix cartoon.

**Prerequisites:** slot recipe for observer-measured energy and momentum, orthonormal frame

$$
T_{\hat0\hat0}=T^{\hat0\hat0}=\text{energy density}
$$

$$
T_{\hat i\hat 0}=-T^{\hat i\hat0}
$$

$$
T_{\hat i\hat j}=T^{\hat i\hat j}
$$

**Where:** GA §12.1 p.132 (pdf 149)

### stress as momentum flux

*physical-quantity · developed* · also: stress tensor, force across a surface

The spatial components T^{ij} give the rate at which i-momentum crosses unit area with normal along j; since momentum transfer per unit time is force, they are equally the i-component of force per unit area on that surface, with pressure on the diagonal and shear stress off it.

**How introduced:** Offered as a second reading of the spatial components (Fig. 12.1) and then used to identify the diagonal entries of a fluid at rest with its pressure.

**Prerequisites:** orthonormal-frame components of the energy-momentum tensor, pressure

$$
T^{\hat i\hat j}=\text{flux of } p^{\hat i}\text{ across surface with normal } \mathbf{e}_{\hat j}
$$

**Notes:** The printed wording says 'force'; strictly it is force per unit area.

**Where:** GA §12.1 p.132 (pdf 149); GA §12.2 p.133 (pdf 150)

### raising and lowering orthonormal-frame indices

*technique · introduced* · also: sign flips with eta

In an orthonormal frame indices are raised with eta^{mu nu} = diag(-1,1,1,1), so each raised time index contributes a factor -1: T^{00} = T_{00}, T^{0i} = -T_{0i}, T^{ij} = T_{ij}.

**How introduced:** Explained in a margin note so the reader can move between T_{mu nu} and T^{mu nu} interpretations.

**Prerequisites:** Minkowski metric, orthonormal frame

$$
T^{\hat0\hat0}=T_{\hat0\hat0},\; T^{\hat0\hat i}=-T_{\hat0\hat i},\; T^{\hat i\hat j}=T_{\hat i\hat j}
$$

**Where:** GA §12.1 p.132 (pdf 149)

### perfect fluid

*solution-or-model · core* · also: ideal fluid

Continuous matter characterized in its local rest frame by an energy density rho and an isotropic pressure p only, with no viscosity, shear stress or heat conduction.

**How introduced:** Motivated by gravity acting on large scales where matter is effectively continuous; presented as the continuum generalization of the particle matter used so far.

**Prerequisites:** pressure, energy density, rest frame

$$
T_{\hat\mu\hat\nu}=\mathrm{diag}(\rho,p,p,p)
$$

**Where:** GA §12.2 p.133 (pdf 150)

### dust

*solution-or-model · developed* · also: pressureless matter, p = 0 fluid

A perfect fluid with zero pressure and no internal energy, so its energy density equals its rest-mass density and T^{mu nu} = rho_0 u^mu u^nu.

**How introduced:** Defined in a margin note as the no-internal-energy limit of a perfect fluid; used in the moving swarm example, point particles and the energy-current argument.

**Prerequisites:** perfect fluid, rest-mass density

$$
T^{\mu\nu}=\rho_0u^\mu u^\nu
$$

**Where:** GA §12.2 p.133 (pdf 150); GA §12.3 p.134 (pdf 151); GA §12.4 p.138 (pdf 155)

### pressure

*physical-quantity · introduced* · also: isotropic pressure

Normal force per unit area exerted across any surface in the fluid's rest frame, the same in every direction for a perfect fluid; it appears on the spatial diagonal of T.

**How introduced:** Identified with the diagonal spatial entries via the force-across-a-surface reading; a margin note warns against confusing p with momentum.

**Prerequisites:** stress as momentum flux

$$
T^{\hat i\hat i}=T_{\hat i\hat i}=p
$$

**Where:** GA §12.2 p.133 (pdf 150)

### equation of state

*definition · introduced*

The relation between a fluid's pressure and its energy density, p = p(rho) (possibly with other state variables), which closes the fluid equations.

**How introduced:** Named in a margin note and illustrated by the photon gas rho = 3p.

**Prerequisites:** perfect fluid, pressure

$$
p=p(\rho)
$$

$$
\rho=3p\;(\text{radiation})
$$

**Where:** GA §12.2 p.133 (pdf 150)

### total energy density versus rest-mass density

*physical-quantity · introduced* · also: rho vs rho_0

The rho in the fluid tensor is total rest-frame energy density, the rest-mass density rho_0 plus internal energy density from thermal motion and interactions; the two coincide only for dust.

**How introduced:** Clarified in a margin note next to the perfect-fluid definition.

**Prerequisites:** perfect fluid, dust

$$
\rho=\rho_0+\rho_{\text{internal}}
$$

**Where:** GA §12.2 p.133 (pdf 150)

### perfect-fluid energy-momentum tensor

*solution-or-model · core* · also: fluid stress-energy tensor

The symmetric tensor equal to (rho + p) times the outer product of the fluid 4-velocity with itself, plus p times the metric (signature -,+,+,+); it reduces to diag(rho, p, p, p) in the fluid rest frame and holds with all indices lowered or all raised.

**How introduced:** Built as diag(rho, p, p, p) in the rest frame, decomposed into u (x) u and a spatial projector, recognized as a tensor equation, then upgraded from eta to g via the equivalence principle.

**Prerequisites:** perfect fluid, tensor product, valid tensor equation, principle of general covariance, 4-velocity

$$
T_{\mu\nu}=(\rho+p)u_\mu u_\nu+p\,g_{\mu\nu}
$$

$$
T^{\mu\nu}=(\rho+p)u^\mu u^\nu+p\,g^{\mu\nu}
$$

**Where:** GA §12.2 p.133 (pdf 150); GA §12.2 p.134 (pdf 151)

### spatial projection tensor

### local versus global conservation

*definition · developed*

## Worked examples

### Example 12.1 · intro · GA §12.1 p.132 (pdf 149)

**Problem:** Identify the physical meaning of each component T_{mu nu} = T(e_mu, e_nu) in an observer's orthonormal frame.

**Method:** Set u = e_0 in the slot recipe, substitute basis vectors, and raise hatted indices with eta (margin note) to compare upper and lower components. The spatial reading is cross-checked with the dust form T_ij = p(e_i) J(e_j) = p_i J_j, then reinterpreted as force because momentum per unit time is force.

**Key insight:** Two time slots give energy density; one gives momentum density (equal to energy flux); none gives momentum flux, which is also stress because momentum per unit time is force.

**Result:** T_00 = energy density; T_i0 = -T^{i0} = minus momentum density; T_ij = T^ij = i-momentum flux across the surface with normal e_j.

**Concepts:** orthonormal-frame components of the energy-momentum tensor, stress as momentum flux, raising and lowering orthonormal-frame indices

### Example 12.2 · standard · GA §12.2 p.133 (pdf 150)

**Problem:** Construct the energy-momentum tensor of a perfect fluid, starting in its local rest frame.

**Method:** Fill the rest-frame matrix with rho and isotropic pressure p; compute the velocity outer product diag(1,0,0,0); split the matrix into a rho part and a p part expressed via u (x) u and eta; note both sides have matching lower indices.

**Key insight:** A diagonal matrix in one special frame becomes a frame-independent tensor once it is written using the fluid velocity and the metric.

**Result:** T = (rho + p) u (x) u + p eta, components T_{mu nu} = (rho + p) u_mu u_nu + p eta_{mu nu}.

**Concepts:** perfect fluid, perfect-fluid energy-momentum tensor, spatial projection tensor, valid tensor equation

### Example 12.3 · intro · GA §12.2 p.133 (pdf 150)

**Problem:** Find the trace of the perfect-fluid tensor and apply it to a photon gas.

**Method:** Contract with eta, using u_mu u^mu = -1 and eta^{mu nu} eta_{mu nu} = 4.

**Key insight:** With (-,+,+,+) signature the energy density enters the trace with a minus sign; radiation's rho = 3p makes the trace vanish, tied to masslessness.

**Result:** T = -rho + 3p; T = 0 for a photon gas.

**Concepts:** trace of the energy-momentum tensor, radiation equation of state, traceless energy-momentum tensor and massless fields

### Example 12.4 · intro · GA §12.3 p.134 (pdf 151)

**Problem:** Write the energy-momentum tensor for a swarm of identical non-interacting dust particles moving with common velocity in the x-y plane.

**Method:** Restore c; read off energy density, momentum density and momentum flux from the particle density, rest mass, gamma and velocity components; assemble the symmetric matrix.

**Key insight:** Every entry is n m gamma times a product of two 'velocity' components (c for time), which is the component version of rho_0 u^mu u^nu.

**Result:** T^{mu nu} = n m gamma [[c^2, v^x c, v^y c, 0], [v^x c, (v^x)^2, v^x v^y, 0], [v^y c, v^x v^y, (v^y)^2, 0], [0,0,0,0]].

**Concepts:** energy-momentum tensor of a moving dust swarm, dust, length contraction of number density

### Example 12.5 · standard · GA §12.3 p.135 (pdf 152)

**Problem:** Show how the proper-time delta-function integral for a particle's rest-mass density reduces to an ordinary density at a given time.

**Method:** Change integration variable from tau to z^0 using dtau = dz^0/u^0, split the 4D delta into a time delta and a 3D delta, and integrate out the time delta.

**Key insight:** The leftover 1/u^0 = 1/gamma is length contraction in disguise: the scalar rest density is smaller than the naive lab count.

**Result:** rho_0(x) = (m/u^0) delta^(3)[x - z(tau)] with tau fixed by z^0(tau) = t.

**Concepts:** world-line delta-function representation of a point particle, four-dimensional Dirac delta function, length contraction of number density

### Example 12.6 · standard · GA §12.3 p.135 (pdf 152)

**Problem:** Generalize the point-particle rest-mass density, mass current and energy-momentum tensor to curved spacetime.

**Method:** Use sqrt(-g) d^4x as the invariant volume element, so delta^(4)/sqrt(-g) is a scalar, and insert it into each integral.

**Key insight:** Delta functions are densities; a factor of 1/sqrt(-g) converts them into scalars fit for tensor equations.

**Result:** Each of rho_0, J_m^alpha and T^{alpha beta} acquires 1/sqrt(-g) inside the proper-time integral (12.27).

**Concepts:** invariant volume element, world-line delta-function representation of a point particle, mass current

### Example 12.7 · standard · GA §12.4 p.136 (pdf 153)

**Problem:** Formulate local and global conservation for a scalar charge described by a 4-current.

**Method:** Define Q as a volume integral; state the continuity equation and its 4-divergence form; count charge through a box-shaped 3-surface a x b x tau, generalize to J^alpha Sigma_alpha and to curved 3-surfaces; integrate over the closed boundary of a 4-volume.

**Key insight:** Conservation in spacetime is a statement about flux through closed hypersurfaces: what enters through the past boundary must exit through the future boundary.

**Result:** d_mu J^mu = 0 locally and oint J^mu dSigma_mu = 0 globally; same structure for baryon number and rest mass.

**Concepts:** four-current, continuity equation, local versus global conservation, hypersurface element and flux through a 3-surface, spacetime divergence theorem for a conserved current

### Example 12.8 · intro · GA §12.4 p.137 (pdf 154)

**Problem:** Justify reading d_mu T^{mu nu} = 0 as conservation of energy and of momentum.

**Method:** Set nu = 0 and nu = i separately and substitute dust-like components T^{00} = rho, T^{0i} = rho v^i, T^{ij} = rho v^i v^j.

**Key insight:** Each value of the free index is a separate continuity equation; the tensor law bundles four conservation laws.

**Result:** nu = 0: d rho/dt + div(rho v) = 0 (energy); nu = i: d(rho v^i)/dt + div(rho v^i v) = 0 (momentum).

**Concepts:** local conservation of energy-momentum in flat spacetime, continuity equation

### Example · standard · GA §12.4 p.137 (pdf 154)

**Problem:** Upgrade the flat-spacetime conservation law to curved spacetime (prose argument between Examples 12.8 and 12.9).

**Method:** Write the law in a local inertial frame; note connection coefficients vanish at the point so commas equal semicolons; recognize a tensor equation and remove the hats.

**Key insight:** Any tensor law involving first derivatives that holds in a LIF holds everywhere with covariant derivatives; this is the comma-goes-to-semicolon rule.

**Result:** T^{mu nu}_{;mu} = 0, i.e. div T = 0, with explicit connection terms in (12.37).

**Concepts:** comma-goes-to-semicolon rule, local inertial frame, covariant conservation law for the energy-momentum tensor

### Example 12.9 · standard · GA §12.4 p.137 (pdf 154)

**Problem:** Take the covariant divergence of the perfect-fluid energy-momentum tensor.

**Method:** Apply the Leibniz rule to each factor of (rho + p) u^mu u^nu + p g^{mu nu}; use f_{;mu} = f_{,mu} for scalars and g^{mu nu}_{;mu} = 0; regroup and translate into index-free directional derivatives.

**Key insight:** Conservation of T is not an extra law bolted onto fluids: it is the fluid's equation of motion (energy balance along u and pressure-driven acceleration across u).

**Result:** 0 = [nabla_u rho + nabla_u p + (rho + p) div u] u + (rho + p) nabla_u u + grad p (12.41).

**Concepts:** relativistic perfect-fluid equations of motion, Leibniz rule for the covariant derivative, metric compatibility

### Example 12.10 · standard · GA §12.4 p.138 (pdf 155)

**Problem:** Show that in flat spacetime a conserved energy current for dust needs a family of parallel observers.

**Method:** Form the energy current M^mu = -T^{mu nu} u_nu from the dust tensor; take its divergence with the Leibniz rule and use d_mu T^{mu nu} = 0; demand the leftover term vanish.

**Key insight:** Energy is observer-relative, so whether its current is conserved depends on the observer field chosen; a constant (parallel) velocity field is enough to guarantee it.

**Result:** d_mu M^mu = -T^{mu nu} u_{nu,mu}, which vanishes if u^nu_{,mu} = 0.

**Concepts:** energy current vector, family of parallel observers, observer-measured energy

### Example 12.11 · challenging · GA §12.4 p.139 (pdf 156)

**Problem:** Repeat the energy-current argument in curved spacetime.

**Method:** Replace commas by semicolons: require (T^{mu nu} u_nu)_{;mu} = 0; with div T = 0 the requirement falls on the velocity field; symmetry of T means only the symmetric part of u_{nu;mu} must vanish.

**Key insight:** The condition is Killing's equation, which generic curved spacetimes do not satisfy, so div T = 0 does not imply a globally conserved energy.

**Result:** u_{nu;mu} + u_{mu;nu} = 0 (12.47).

**Concepts:** Killing's equation, non-existence of global energy conservation in curved spacetime, path dependence of parallel transport


## Analogies and intuitions

### Tensor as a slot machine: insert the observer's velocity and out come the measured quantities → slot recipe for observer-measured energy and momentum · strong · GA §12.1 p.131 (pdf 148)

Every physical reading of T is produced by choosing what to feed into the two slots: u once for momentum density, u twice for energy density, u and a unit vector for a component.

**Where it breaks down:** Hides the sign conventions (minus signs from signature) and the requirement that u and a are normalized and orthogonal.

**App idea:** Drag-and-drop slot machine: learner drags u, e_x, e_y into two slots of a T machine and the output (number or 1-form arrow) appears with its physical label.

### Swarm of particles each carrying momentum through space → dust energy-momentum tensor as an outer product · strong · GA §12.1 p.131 (pdf 148)

T = p (x) J is read as 'momentum per particle times particle flow', making T( , u) a momentum density and T(u, ) an energy flux.

**Where it breaks down:** Only literally true for a single-velocity cold swarm; fluids with pressure and fields have T that is not a single outer product.

### Momentum flowing per unit time across a surface is a force on that surface → stress as momentum flux · useful · GA §12.1 p.132 (pdf 149)

Gives the spatial block a second, mechanical reading and lets pressure be placed on the diagonal of the fluid tensor.

**Where it breaks down:** The book's wording omits 'per unit area'; microscopic transport (kinetic pressure) versus contact forces is not discussed.

**App idea:** Gas-in-a-box animation where molecules bouncing off a wall deposit momentum; the running momentum-per-area-per-time equals the pressure entry T^{xx}.

### Electric charge conservation as a rehearsal for energy-momentum conservation → local conservation of energy-momentum in flat spacetime · strong · GA §12.4 p.136 (pdf 153)

The continuity equation for a scalar density is built first; conservation of the four components of momentum is then the same statement with one extra index.

**Where it breaks down:** Unlike charge, energy-momentum conservation does not survive as a global law in curved spacetime.

**App idea:** Split-screen: left a charge density map obeying continuity, right four synchronized density maps (energy and three momenta), each with its own flux arrows.

### What enters through the past part of a boundary exits through the future part → spacetime divergence theorem for a conserved current · strong · GA §12.4 p.136 (pdf 153)

Margin interpretation of the closed-hypersurface integral, turning a formal flux integral into a bookkeeping of world lines entering and leaving a region.

**Where it breaks down:** Orientation of dSigma on spacelike versus timelike boundary pieces is subtle and not worked out.

### Length contraction crowds particles together → world-line delta-function representation of a point particle · useful · GA §12.3 p.135 (pdf 152)

Explains the 1/u^0 factor that appears when the proper-time delta integral is reduced to a 3D density.

**Where it breaks down:** The factor's direction (divide by gamma for rest density, multiply for lab counts) is easy to get backwards.

### Adding up arrows that live in different places → path dependence of parallel transport · strong · GA §12.4 p.139 (pdf 156)

Margin note: totalling momentum over a closed surface means carrying many 4-vectors to one point, which is ambiguous when transport depends on path.

**Where it breaks down:** Requires a prior feel for tangent spaces and holonomy from Chapters 7 and 11.

**App idea:** On a sphere, the learner collects arrows from around a loop to the north pole along two routes and sees two different vector sums.

### Gravity and matter doing work on each other → non-existence of global energy conservation in curved spacetime · useful · GA §12.4 p.139 (pdf 156)

Physical reason offered for why matter energy alone need not be conserved: it can be exchanged with the gravitational field.

**Where it breaks down:** Suggests a gravitational field energy that could be added to restore conservation, which in GR has no local, covariant definition.


## Misconceptions addressed

### div T = 0 means total energy is conserved in curved spacetime, just as in flat spacetime. · GA §12.4 p.138 (pdf 155)

**Correction:** It is a local statement valid in each local inertial frame. A conserved total energy needs a Killing vector field, which a generic spacetime lacks; gravity and matter exchange energy.

**Why tempting:** In flat spacetime the identical-looking equation does imply global energy conservation, and the book itself calls it a conservation law.

### The p in the fluid tensor is momentum. · GA §12.2 p.133 (pdf 150)

**Correction:** It is pressure, an isotropic stress; momentum appears only in the off-diagonal time-space entries.

**Why tempting:** The same letter was used for 4-momentum throughout earlier chapters.

### rho in the perfect-fluid tensor is the rest-mass density. · GA §12.2 p.133 (pdf 150)

**Correction:** rho is total rest-frame energy density, including internal energy; it equals rest-mass density only for dust.

**Why tempting:** Newtonian fluid mechanics uses rho for mass density, and dust examples blur the distinction.

### Upper and lower orthonormal components of T are always equal. · GA §12.1 p.132 (pdf 149)

**Correction:** Raising with eta flips the sign of each time index, so mixed time-space components change sign while 00 and ij components do not.

**Why tempting:** In an orthonormal frame the metric looks almost like the identity.

### The spatial components of T only describe momentum being carried along by moving matter. · GA §12.1 p.132 (pdf 149)

**Correction:** They are also stresses: forces per area across surfaces, including pressure in a fluid at rest where nothing flows on average.

**Why tempting:** The dust picture has T^{ij} = rho v^i v^j, which vanishes for matter at rest.

### The trace of the fluid tensor is rho + 3p (sum of diagonal entries). · GA §12.2 p.133 (pdf 150)

**Correction:** The trace uses the metric: T = g^{mu nu} T_{mu nu} = -rho + 3p in signature (-,+,+,+).

**Why tempting:** Matrix trace habits from linear algebra ignore the metric.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### A traceless energy-momentum tensor means the matter carries no energy. · GA §12.2 p.133 (pdf 150)

**Correction:** Radiation has positive energy density; the trace vanishes because pressure contributes +3p against -rho.

**Why tempting:** Zero is read as 'nothing there'.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### A particle's density field is simply m times a 3D delta function in every frame. · GA §12.3 p.135 (pdf 152)

**Correction:** The scalar rest-mass density is (m/u^0) times the 3D delta; lab-frame mass counts and energy densities carry extra gamma factors.

**Why tempting:** Non-relativistically a point particle is just m delta^(3).

### Global conservation of a charge is as physically good as local conservation. · GA §12.4 p.136 (pdf 153)

**Correction:** Local conservation forbids disappearance here and reappearance elsewhere; in relativity a purely global law is not even frame-independent because distant simultaneity is relative.

**Why tempting:** Totals are what we usually quote, and the global integral seems to capture 'conservation'.

### Ordinary divergence d_mu T^{mu nu} = 0 holds in any coordinates. · GA §12.4 p.137 (pdf 154)

**Correction:** It holds only in inertial coordinates; in general coordinates (even curvilinear flat ones) connection terms are needed: T^{mu nu}_{;mu} = 0.

**Why tempting:** The flat law looks coordinate-free when written as div T = 0. The book itself only contrasts flat spacetime with curved spacetime and never mentions curvilinear coordinates on flat spacetime.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### Any family of freely falling observers can serve as the parallel reference needed for energy conservation. · GA §12.4 p.139 (pdf 156)

**Correction:** The velocity field must satisfy Killing's equation, which is not generally true in curved spacetime. (The book states only that velocity fields in curved spacetime generally fail Killing's equation; the free-fall framing is ours.)

**Why tempting:** Freely falling observers are locally inertial, so they seem as good as flat-space inertial observers.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*


## Thought experiments

### Counting charge through a box in spacetime · GA §12.4 p.136 (pdf 153)

**Setup:** A flat 3-surface of spatial size a by b, perpendicular to z, persisting for a time tau; count the charge that crosses it.

**Lesson:** The crossed charge is J^z times the 3-volume; generalizing the orientation gives J^alpha Sigma_alpha, and curved surfaces give an integral.

**App idea:** Extruded rectangle in 2+1 spacetime with charges passing through; learner tilts it from timelike to spacelike and sees flux switch from 'current through a wall' to 'charge present at an instant'.

### Charge that vanishes here and reappears there · GA §12.4 p.136 (pdf 153)

**Setup:** Imagine a charge disappearing at one location and instantly reappearing far away, keeping the total fixed.

**Lesson:** Such a process satisfies global but not local conservation; the continuity equation rules it out.

**App idea:** Two frames of a 1+1 diagram showing the jump; boosting reveals a moment where the charge exists twice or nowhere.

### Closed 4-volume ledger · GA §12.4 p.136 (pdf 153)

**Setup:** Enclose a region of spacetime with a closed 3-surface and tally current crossing each patch with its outward normal.

**Lesson:** Net flux is zero: charge entering the past face exits the future face.

### Measuring the energy of a distant object · GA §12.4 p.138 (pdf 155)

**Setup:** An observer wants the energy of a faraway object; energy is defined only for an observer present at the object, so a stand-in observer with 'the same' velocity is used.

**Lesson:** In flat spacetime parallel observers make this unambiguous; in curved spacetime 'same velocity' depends on the transport path, so no global energy is defined.

**App idea:** See the Fig. 12.4 redesign: compare transported velocities along two paths on a curved surface.

### Totalling momentum over a closed surface in curved spacetime · GA §12.4 p.139 (pdf 156)

**Setup:** Try to add the momentum flux 4-vectors from every patch of a closed boundary to test global momentum conservation.

**Lesson:** The vectors live in different tangent spaces; bringing them together is path-dependent, so the sum is not well defined.


## Applications and observations

- **Why fluids model gravitating matter** (astrophysical-system, GA §12.2 p.132 (pdf 149)): Gravity matters on scales so large that individual particles blur into a continuum, which is why the book models gravitating matter as fluids (and dust) for the cosmology and stellar chapters that follow.
- **Radiation: traceless energy-momentum tensor** (observation, GA §12.2 p.133 (pdf 150)): Photon gas thermodynamics gives rho = 3p, so T = 0; the electromagnetic field's massless photons fit the rule that traceless T accompanies massless excitations. Key numbers: rho = 3p; T = -rho + 3p = 0
- **Size of pressure relative to energy density (our estimate)** (numerical-estimate, GA §12.2 p.133 (pdf 150)): Our own estimate, not in the book, to justify treating ordinary matter as dust: compare p with rho c^2. Water at atmospheric pressure has p ~ 1e5 Pa against rho c^2 ~ 9e19 J/m^3. The solar core has p ~ 2.5e16 Pa against rho c^2 ~ 1.4e22 J/m^3. Neutron-star cores reach p/(rho c^2) of order 0.1 or more, where pressure matters gravitationally. Key numbers: p/(rho c^2): water ~1e-15; solar core ~2e-6; neutron star core ~0.1-0.3; radiation 1/3
- **Conserved currents beyond electric charge** (observation, GA §12.4 p.136 (pdf 153)): Baryon number flux and rest-mass flux obey the same closed-surface conservation law as charge, the basis for using rest-mass conservation alongside div T = 0 in fluid problems (Exercise 12.2).
- **Dust tensor with c restored (our numerical illustration)** (numerical-estimate, GA §12.3 p.134 (pdf 151)): Our own illustration: for dust moving at v = 0.6c along x (gamma = 1.25), T^{0x}/T^{00} = v/c = 0.6 and T^{xx}/T^{00} = (v/c)^2 = 0.36, showing how motion shifts weight from energy density into flux and stress entries. Key numbers: gamma = 1.25; ratios 1 : 0.6 : 0.36

## Historical notes

- **Jürgen Ehlers, Robert Geroch (2004):** Published a proof that small bodies obeying the conservation law and an energy condition follow geodesics (Ann. Phys. 309, 232). — Shows the geodesic principle need not be a separate postulate once T is covariantly conserved. (GA §12.4 p.139 (pdf 156))

## Notation and conventions

- **Metric signature:** (-,+,+,+); in a rest frame u_mu = (-1,0,0,0) and u_mu u^mu = -1. — Responsible for the minus signs in the slot recipe and for T = -rho + 3p. (GA §12.2 p.133 (pdf 150))
- **Hatted indices:** Components in an observer's orthonormal frame (local measurement frame), raised and lowered with eta. (GA §12.1 p.132 (pdf 149))
- **Comma and semicolon:** T^{mu nu}_{,mu} is a partial derivative, T^{mu nu}_{;mu} a covariant derivative; div T = 0 is shorthand for either, depending on context. — The contracted derivative index is written on the first index in (12.36) and on the second in (12.39); symmetry makes them equal. (GA §12.4 p.137 (pdf 154))
- **Empty-slot notation:** T( , ), u( ) show how many vector arguments a tensor or 1-form takes; tilde marks 1-forms. (GA §12.1 p.131 (pdf 148))
- **Units:** c = 1 by default; c restored temporarily in Example 12.4 and a margin note on J^mu = (rho c, J). (GA §12.3 p.134 (pdf 151))
- **Density symbols:** rho total energy density, rho_0 rest-mass density, p pressure (not momentum), n number density; the charge density in a 4-current is also written rho. — rho is overloaded between charge density (§12.4) and energy density (§12.2). (GA §12.2 p.133 (pdf 150))
- **Currents:** J number or charge current, J_m mass current, J_q charge current, M energy current, N baryon-number current. (GA §12.3 p.135 (pdf 152))
- **Hypersurface elements:** dSigma_mu is a normal 4-vector surface element; partial V is the closed boundary of a 4-volume V. (GA §12.4 p.136 (pdf 153))
- **World lines and delta functions:** z^mu(tau) is a particle world line parametrized by proper time; delta^(4) is the Cartesian product of four 1D deltas, divided by sqrt(-g) in curved spacetime. (GA §12.3 p.135 (pdf 152))
- **Margin equation numbering:** Equations in margin notes get numbers in the main sequence (12.1, 12.3, 12.7, 12.24, 12.29, 12.37), so numbers can appear out of order on a page. (GA §12.1 p.131 (pdf 148))

About 5 exercises (pdf pages 157).

**Skills practiced**

- **12.1** (intro): Take the rest-frame fluid tensor with rho and p on the Cartesian diagonal and rewrite its components in spherical polar coordinates. — *Shows that coordinate components differ from physical ones (T_rr = p, T_theta theta = p r^2, T_phi phi = p r^2 sin^2 theta) while the physics is unchanged, a useful warm-up before spherically symmetric problems.* Skills: tensor transformation law, Jacobians

## Teaching gems

### Read every physical quantity out of T by choosing what to feed its slots: the observer's velocity once, twice, or with a unit direction. · GA §12.1 p.131 (pdf 148)

**Why it works:** Turns an abstract symmetric tensor into an operational measuring device tied to a specific observer, and makes frame dependence obvious.

**App idea:** Slot-machine widget with draggable vectors (u, e_x, e_y, e_z) and a matter-model selector; outputs appear with units and physical labels, and changing u shows the same T giving different readings.

### Write the fluid tensor as a diagonal matrix in the rest frame, then peel it into a u (x) u piece and a projector piece to get a frame-independent formula. · GA §12.2 p.133 (pdf 150)

**Why it works:** The learner sees where each term of (rho + p) u u + p g comes from rather than memorizing it, and the rest-frame check is built in.

**App idea:** Animated matrix split: diag(rho,p,p,p) separates into rho diag(1,0,0,0) + p diag(0,1,1,1), the pieces morph into tensor symbols, then a boost slider shows all three matrices transforming together.

### Use the trace as a one-line diagnostic: -rho + 3p, which vanishes exactly for radiation. · GA §12.2 p.133 (pdf 150)

**Why it works:** Cheap calculation, memorable physics, and a sign check on the metric convention.

**App idea:** Equation-of-state slider w = p/rho from 0 (dust) to 1/3 (radiation) to 1 (stiff) with a trace meter and a gauge showing which matter types sit where.

### Rehearse conservation with a scalar current (charge) before the tensor, then add one index. · GA §12.4 p.136 (pdf 153)

**Why it works:** The continuity equation and flux picture are familiar from electromagnetism, so the new content is only the extra index.

**App idea:** Toggle between a single density-and-flux panel for charge and a four-panel view for energy and momentum components, each obeying its own continuity equation.

### Build flux through spacetime surfaces from a box of size a x b x tau up to arbitrary closed hypersurfaces. · GA §12.4 p.136 (pdf 153)

**Why it works:** Grounds dSigma_mu in a concrete counting exercise and makes the closed-boundary law intuitive via past-in, future-out.

**App idea:** See Fig. 12.3 redesign: region-drawing flux ledger in a 1+1 diagram.

### Put a point particle into field language with a delta function along its world line and let length contraction explain the leftover 1/gamma. · GA §12.3 p.135 (pdf 152)

**Why it works:** Links particle and continuum descriptions and gives a physical reason for an algebraic factor.

**App idea:** Moving particle smeared into a narrow Gaussian; learner boosts it and watches the lab-frame blob contract and brighten while the integrated rest mass and the energy (gamma m) readouts behave differently.

### Undercut 'div T = 0 means energy conservation' with a two-line calculation: the energy current is conserved only if the observer field has vanishing symmetrized gradient (Killing). · GA §12.4 p.139 (pdf 156)

**Why it works:** Honest about a common oversimplification and connects it to symmetry, preparing Killing vectors and conserved quantities for orbits.

**App idea:** Observer-field sandbox: learner chooses a velocity field on a 2D spacetime (flat, expanding universe, static star); the app computes the symmetrized gradient and shows where the energy current has sources.

### Comma goes to semicolon: justify a curved-spacetime law by noting that connection coefficients vanish at a point in a local inertial frame. · GA §12.4 p.137 (pdf 154)

**App idea:** Zoom animation on a curved surface: as the learner zooms into a point the grid becomes Cartesian and Gamma readouts drop to zero, so the partial-derivative law and covariant law read identically there.


## Gaps and pitfalls

- **Eqn 12.43 calls -T(u,u) the energy density, but by (12.6) T(u,u) is the energy density; since J(u) = -n, E J(u) is actually -En.** — Sign confusion when checking the dust energy current against earlier results. *Suggestion:* Present T(u,u) = En as energy density and M^mu = -T^{mu nu} u_nu = E J^mu as the energy current, noting M^0 = En in the observer frame.
- **Example 12.11 says u_{nu;mu} = 0 can be 'rewritten' as Killing's equation; in fact symmetry of T means only the symmetrized derivative must vanish, a weaker condition. Also the standard result uses a Killing vector that need not be a normalized observer velocity.** — May think Killing's equation is equivalent to covariantly constant velocity, or that Killing vectors must be 4-velocities. *Suggestion:* Show that contracting symmetric T with the gradient of u keeps only the symmetrized gradient, conclude the Killing condition is sufficient, and state the general theorem with a Killing vector xi (e.g. the static time translation of Schwarzschild).
- **Example 12.10 says u^nu_{,mu} = 0 is required. It is only sufficient: because T is symmetric, only the symmetrized gradient u_{(nu,mu)} must vanish, which in flat spacetime also allows boost and rotation generators (not unit 4-velocity fields).** — Learners may think constant velocity is the unique route to a conserved energy current, and then find the Killing condition in Example 12.11 an unexplained weakening. *Suggestion:* State the flat-space condition as u_{nu,mu} + u_{mu,nu} = 0 from the start, show constant u satisfies it, so Example 12.11 becomes a literal comma-to-semicolon replay.
- **Example 12.8's unpacked equations use T^{0i} = rho v^i and T^{ij} = rho v^i v^j without saying they are the dust (pressureless) components, with rho the lab-frame energy density rather than the rest-frame one; a fluid with pressure gives a pressure-gradient term (relativistic Euler equation).** — Believes momentum conservation for fluids has no pressure force term. *Suggestion:* Redo the nu = i equation with the perfect-fluid tensor to show d_t(rho v^i) + d_j(rho v^i v^j + p delta^{ij}) = 0.
- **Example 12.4 does not say whether n is measured in the lab or rest frame; the given components require the lab-frame density n = gamma n_0.** — Using rest-frame density gives the wrong power of gamma. *Suggestion:* State n = gamma n_0 and show the equivalence with rho_0 u^mu u^nu = n_0 m gamma^2 (c, v)(c, v).
- **The rest-mass flux is written rho_0(1, u) with rho_0 the rest-mass density; if rho_0 is the rest-frame value the 4-current is rho_0 gamma(1, u).** — Minor inconsistency with J_m = rho_0 u. *Suggestion:* Write the mass current as rho_0 u^mu and note its low-speed limit.
- **Equations 12.11 and the pressure identification in Example 12.2 speak of 'force' across a surface when force per unit area is meant.** — Dimensional confusion between force and pressure. *Suggestion:* Always say stress = force per unit area and check units with c restored.
- **The local-versus-global discussion does not mention that a purely global conservation law is incompatible with relativity of simultaneity.** — Misses the strongest physical reason relativistic conservation laws must be local. *Suggestion:* Add a boosted-frame picture: a charge vanishing at A and appearing at B simultaneously in one frame is absent or duplicated in another.
- **The covariant divergence of a rank-2 tensor is used (and the Leibniz rule applied to it) before the general covariant derivative is developed; the formula is only quoted in a margin note.** — Students may apply one connection term instead of one per index. *Suggestion:* Give a short derivation from T^{mu nu} = sum of V^mu W^nu products, or verify with polar coordinates in flat space.
- **Symmetry of T is asserted, not motivated beyond the dust example, yet it is used repeatedly (slot interchange, choice of divergence index, Killing reduction).** — Unclear whether symmetry is physics or convention. *Suggestion:* Link symmetry to equality of momentum density and energy flux (E = mc^2 flow) and to angular momentum conservation.

## Tutor notes

- Open with: 'Einstein's equation says curvature equals something. What should that something describe, and what property must it have if the left side automatically satisfies an identity?' Let the learner propose energy density, then push to momentum and stress.
- Before any formulas, check that the learner can compute an observer's measured energy E = -p(u) for a particle; the slot recipe for T is the density version of the same move.
- Suggested order: slot recipe -> block anatomy of T (energy, momentum, stress) -> fluid at rest -> covariant fluid tensor -> trace and radiation -> moving dust with c -> conservation of charge -> conservation of T -> covariant upgrade -> fluid equation -> energy conservation caveat.
- Check understanding: ask what T^{0x} means physically and why it equals T^{x0}; then ask what T^{xx} is for a gas at rest (pressure) even though nothing flows on average.
- Check signs: have the learner compute the trace in (-,+,+,+) and explain why radiation is traceless. If they get rho + 3p, revisit index raising with the metric.
- For the delta-function section, first show a non-relativistic particle as m delta^(3), then ask why a moving particle's scalar rest density should have 1/gamma. Connect to length contraction before doing the tau -> t change of variables.
- Use the box-shaped 3-surface a x b x tau to ground dSigma_mu. Ask: if the box is tilted to lie entirely at one instant, what does the flux through it count? (The charge present then.)
- Common learner question: 'If div T = 0, why isn't energy conserved in an expanding universe?' Answer: energy needs a time-translation symmetry (a timelike Killing vector); FRW has none, so e.g. photon energy redshifts away without a global bookkeeping.
- Common learner question: 'Why does the conservation law need semicolons if physics is local?' Answer: in a LIF it is just partial derivatives; the semicolon form is the same statement in arbitrary coordinates.
- Flag to advanced learners that Killing's equation is a condition on a vector field, not necessarily a normalized 4-velocity, and that Chapter 13 uses div T = 0 to require a divergence-free curvature tensor (the Einstein tensor).
- For learners heading to astrophysics, give the ratio p/(rho c^2) for ordinary matter versus neutron stars to explain when dust suffices and when pressure matters gravitationally.
