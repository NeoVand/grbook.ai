# GA ch22 · Motion in the Schwarzschild geometry

> Shows that free motion outside a spherical mass can be analysed without ever writing the geodesic equation: read two conserved quantities (energy and angular momentum per unit rest mass) off the metric's missing coordinates, combine them with the normalization of the four-velocity, and derive the gravitational redshift, a relativistic effective potential with an extra 1/r^3 term, and the radial plunge from rest at infinity, whose finite proper time but divergent Schwarzschild coordinate time at r = 2M sets up the later black-hole chapters.

**Pages:** printed 237–245 · pdf 254–262 · **Difficulty:** math 3/5, conceptual 3/5, novice-friendliness 4/5

Algebra is light: substitutions into u.u = -1, one separable ODE and one partial-fraction integral. Conceptual demands come from the recipe being unproved, from keeping track of which quantities are coordinate components and which are measured, and from the vielbein and 1-form steps in Example 22.5, which are terse. The margin footnotes carry essential arguments (energy at infinity, the falling frame) that a hurried reader may skip. Minor printed slips: footnote 10 says E-tilde > 1 where E-tilde >= 1 is meant, footnote 14's angular basis vectors are not normalized, and eqn 22.26 is only a leading-order equality.

## Role in the book

Third chapter of Part IV. Chapter 20 built Newtonian orbits around an effective potential and Chapter 21 produced the Schwarzschild line element; this chapter is the bridge that turns that metric into trajectories. It supplies the working toolkit (Killing-vector constants of motion, u.u = -1 or 0, the dx/dt = u^i/u^t trick, static-observer vielbeins) that Chapter 23 uses for bound orbits and perihelion precession, Chapter 24 for photon paths, and Chapter 25 for the horizon. Consistent with the book's spiral design, the Killing-vector rule is used as an unproved recipe here and only justified geometrically in Chapter 33, and the puzzle of the infinite coordinate time to reach r = 2M is flagged and deliberately left for Chapter 25.

## Learning objectives

- Reader can scan a metric for coordinates that appear in none of its components and write down the corresponding Killing vectors in component form.
- Reader can form the conserved quantities E-tilde = -xi.u and L-tilde = eta.u for Schwarzschild and interpret them as energy and angular momentum per unit rest mass as judged from far away.
- Reader can state the normalization u.u = -1 for massive particles (proper time as parameter) and u.u = 0 for light (affine parameter) and explain why it carries information about the kind of particle.
- Reader can derive the gravitational redshift formula omega_inf = omega_R (1 - 2M/R)^{1/2} by writing a static observer's four-velocity as a multiple of the time Killing vector and using conservation of xi.p.
- Reader can reduce equatorial geodesic motion to the one-dimensional equation E_eff = (1/2)(dr/dtau)^2 + V_eff(r) and identify the extra -M L^2/r^3 term relative to the Newtonian effective potential.
- Reader can sketch the relativistic effective potential, explain the inner maximum and the fall to minus infinity at small r, and restore G and c to show the correction is order 1/c^2.
- Reader can solve the radial plunge from rest at infinity: E-tilde = 1, dr/dtau = -(2M/r)^{1/2}, the four-velocity components, and r(tau) proportional to (tau_* - tau)^{2/3}.
- Reader can integrate dt/dr for the plunge and show that Schwarzschild coordinate time diverges logarithmically as r -> 2M while proper time stays finite.
- Reader can contrast the coordinate speed dr/dt, which goes to zero at r = 2M, with the speed measured by a local static observer, which tends to the speed of light there for any infall energy.
- Reader can convert coordinate quantities into what a static observer measures using the vielbein factors (1 - 2M/r)^{+1/2} for time and (1 - 2M/r)^{-1/2} for radial distance.
- Reader can apply the same toolkit to exercises such as the gravitational clock effect for airborne clocks and the relative speed of two radially separated observers.

## Teaching approach

A deliberately economical chapter whose message is that symmetry plus normalization beats brute force. The authors open by admitting that solving the geodesic equation looks daunting, then promise that conserved quantities, exactly as in Newtonian orbit problems, will do the work. A boxed three-step recipe (look for absent coordinates, name the Killing vector, dot it with the velocity) is given without proof and immediately applied to Schwarzschild. Every later result uses only these ingredients, most of them packaged as shaded Example blocks: the redshift (argued in running text rather than an Example) via a static observer whose velocity is proportional to the time Killing vector, the effective potential via u.u = -1, a Newtonian-limit check by restoring G and c, and the radial plunge with its three clocks and two speeds. The recurring pedagogical contrast is coordinate quantities versus what a local observer actually measures, handled with vielbein factors. Surprising results (frozen coordinate time at 2M) are stated, graphed, and explicitly postponed to Chapter 25.

**Style:** computational-recipe, physical-motivation-first, example-driven, derivation-heavy, conversational-informal, spiral-revisit, diagram-driven

**Narrative arc**

1. Motivating worry: finding orbits seems to require the full geodesic equation and all the Christoffel symbols of the Schwarzschild metric
2. Reassurance by analogy: Newtonian orbit problems collapse once energy and angular momentum conservation are used, so look for relativistic counterparts
3. Recipe (stated, not proved): coordinates absent from the metric give Killing vectors, and their dot product with a geodesic's tangent is constant
4. Application: t and phi are absent, giving xi = e_t and eta = e_phi and the constants E-tilde and L-tilde, interpreted per unit rest mass and as seen from infinity
5. Second ingredient: u.u = -1 or 0 encodes whether the free-falling object is massive or light
6. First payoff (photons): static observer's velocity written as (1 - 2M/r)^{-1/2} xi, so conserved xi.p yields the gravitational redshift in one line
7. Toolbox recap as a margin checklist beside the start of §22.3: normalization, Killing rule, and dx/dt = (dx/dtau)/u^t
8. Second payoff (massive particles): substitute E-tilde and L-tilde into u.u = -1 for equatorial motion to get an energy equation with a relativistic effective potential
9. Check: compare with the Newtonian effective potential of Chapter 20; restore G and c to show the new term is a 1/c^2 correction scaling as 1/r^3
10. Measurement caveat: coordinate components must be converted with the static-observer vielbein, and moving observers need their own frame
11. Worked application: radial plunge from rest at infinity; derive u^mu, proper-time law r(tau) and coordinate-time law t(r)
12. Surprise: infinite coordinate time but finite proper time to reach r = 2M; explicitly deferred to Chapter 25
13. Second surprise: coordinate speed vanishes at 2M while a local static observer measures the infall speed approaching c
14. Hand-off to Chapter 23 on orbits; exercises extend to proper acceleration, escape speed, Hafele-Keating clocks and relative velocities

**Signature moves**

- Replace the geodesic equation by 'conserved quantities from absent coordinates plus u.u = -1', presented as a boxed, numbered recipe.
- Express a static observer's four-velocity as a scalar multiple of the time-translation Killing vector so that measured energies become ratios of normalization factors.
- Force the relativistic result into the exact algebraic shape of the Newtonian effective-potential equation of Chapter 20, then point to the single extra term.
- Define the Newtonian energy through E-tilde = (mc^2 + E_N)/mc^2 and restore G and c to read the relativistic correction as an order-1/c^2 addition.
- Put the same plunging particle on three graphs (r against tau, r against t, speed against r) so the coordinate-versus-proper contrast is visual.
- Use margin notes to carry supporting derivations (the observer-at-infinity energy rule, the falling-frame vielbein, the coordinate escape velocity) so the main text stays short.
- State an alarming result plainly (the infaller never reaches 2M in coordinate time) and promise its resolution later rather than resolving it immediately.

## Section by section

### Chapter opening: from metric to motion — p.237 (pdf 254)

Two literary epigraphs about being tossed by the stars and setting off on pilgrimage frame the topic. The Schwarzschild line element and its four non-zero components are recalled, and the programme is announced: study free fall as geodesic motion, find richer behaviour than in Newtonian gravity, and avoid solving the geodesic equation directly by leaning on conserved quantities and the normalization of the four-velocity.

**Concepts:** Schwarzschild metric, geodesic equation, four-velocity normalization, constants of the motion

**Key moves**
- Quote the Schwarzschild metric (22.2) and list g_tt, g_rr, g_theta theta, g_phi phi in the margin (22.1).
- Observe that the geodesic equation with all connection coefficients looks heavy.
- Promise that, as in Newtonian orbit theory, conserved quantities plus u.u = -1 or 0 will suffice.

### §22.1 Constants of the motion — p.238 (pdf 255)

Gives an operational rule for conserved quantities: if no metric component depends on a coordinate x^alpha then e_alpha is a Killing vector and its dot product with the tangent of any geodesic is constant. For Schwarzschild this yields the time Killing vector xi = e_t and the rotational one eta = e_phi, and hence E-tilde = (1 - 2M/r) dt/dtau and L-tilde = r^2 sin^2(theta) dphi/dtau, read as energy and angular momentum per unit rest mass. A margin note explains why the energy is the one measured by a static observer far away, whose four-velocity is xi. The section closes by fixing the tangent's norm, -1 for massive particles parametrized by proper time and 0 for light with an affine parameter.

**Concepts:** Killing vector field, conservation law from a Killing vector, time-translation Killing vector, rotational Killing vector, conserved energy per unit rest mass, conserved angular momentum per unit rest mass, energy measured by an observer, four-velocity normalization, null tangent condition, affine parameter

**Key moves**
- Shaded recipe: a coordinate missing from every metric component gives a Killing vector e_alpha, and its dot product with the geodesic tangent is constant (22.3).
- Spot that Schwarzschild components depend only on r and theta, so t and phi give xi and eta (22.4, 22.5).
- Evaluate xi.u = u_t = g_tt u^t and define E-tilde as its negative (22.6-22.8).
- Margin argument: a static observer at infinity has u_obs = xi in flat asymptotic space, so -p.xi is the energy measured there.
- Evaluate eta.u = u_phi = r^2 sin^2 theta u^phi (22.9) and note it reduces to r^2 phi-dot in the equatorial plane.
- Fix the particle type through u.u = -1 (22.10) or u.u = 0 (22.11).

### §22.2 Gravitational redshift — p.239 (pdf 256)

A photon travels radially outwards from a static observer at radius R to one at infinity. Measured energy is minus the dot product of the photon momentum with the observer's velocity. A static observer's velocity has only a time component fixed by normalization, (1 - 2M/r)^{-1/2}, so it equals that factor times the time Killing vector. Because xi.p is constant along the photon's geodesic, the measured frequencies at R and at infinity differ only by the normalization factor, giving omega_inf = omega_R (1 - 2M/R)^{1/2}. Light loses energy climbing out, and the frequency needed at R to deliver a fixed frequency far away grows without bound as R approaches 2M.

**Concepts:** gravitational redshift, static observer, energy measured by an observer, photon four-momentum, time-translation Killing vector

**Key moves**
- Note that what is conserved is g_tt p^t, not the flat-space p^t, and that a local observer measures the hatted component, not p^t.
- Write hbar omega = -p.u_obs (22.12).
- Static observer: spatial components zero, g_tt (u^t)^2 = -1 gives u^t = (1 - 2M/r)^{-1/2} (22.13, 22.14).
- Key identity: u_obs(r) = (1 - 2M/r)^{-1/2} xi.
- Hence hbar omega_R = (1 - 2M/R)^{-1/2}(-xi.p)_R (22.15), with the factor tending to 1 at infinity.
- Conservation of xi.p along the geodesic equates the two brackets, giving (22.16).

### §22.3 Motion in Schwarzschild spacetime — p.240 (pdf 257)

With a margin checklist of the tools alongside, Example 22.1 restricts to the equatorial plane and substitutes the constants of motion into the normalization condition. Rearranging gives an energy-like equation with effective energy (E-tilde^2 - 1)/2 and an effective potential -M/r + L^2/2r^2 - M L^2/r^3, identical in shape to the Newtonian problem of Chapter 20 apart from the last term. The potential has an inner maximum and plunges to minus infinity near the origin, which is where the new relativistic trajectories come from. Example 22.2 restores G and c and defines a Newtonian energy to show the extra term is a 1/c^2 correction. A margin note lists the static observer's vielbein, needed to turn coordinate components into measurements, and cautions that moving observers require a different frame.

**Concepts:** equatorial-plane reduction, effective energy, relativistic effective potential, Newtonian effective potential, Newtonian limit of the relativistic energy equation, coordinate velocity, static-observer orthonormal frame (vielbein), geometrized units

**Key moves**
- Checklist: u.u = -1 or 0; Killing rule gives conserved u_alpha; dx^i/dt = (1/u^0) dx^i/dtau (22.17).
- Set theta = pi/2 and write u.u = -1 in components (22.18).
- Replace u^t by E-tilde/(1 - 2M/r) and u^phi by L-tilde/r^2 (22.19).
- Rearrange so the left side is the constant (E-tilde^2 - 1)/2 (22.20).
- Expand the bracket into -M/r + L^2/2r^2 - M L^2/r^3 (22.21-22.23).
- Compare with the Newtonian effective potential per unit mass (margin note) and with Fig. 22.3.
- Restore G and c (22.24), introduce E_N (22.25), and obtain the Newtonian energy equation plus a 1/r^3 correction (22.26).
- Quote static-observer vielbein components; they become unity at infinity so far-away measurements equal coordinate values.

### §22.4 Example: the radial plunge — p.242 (pdf 259)

A massive particle released from rest at infinity falls straight in. Zero angular momentum and E-tilde = 1 give dt/dtau = (1 - 2M/r)^{-1} and, from the effective-potential equation, dr/dtau = -(2M/r)^{1/2}, so the four-velocity is fully determined. Margin notes construct the orthonormal frame carried by the falling particle and record the coordinate speed dr/dt, whose magnitude is the coordinate escape speed.

**Concepts:** radial plunge from rest at infinity, conserved energy per unit rest mass, freely falling observer frame, coordinate escape velocity

**Key moves**
- Radial motion means L-tilde = 0; rest at infinity means u^t = 1 there, hence E-tilde = 1 and zero effective energy for the whole fall.
- E-tilde = 1 gives u^t = (1 - 2M/r)^{-1} (22.27).
- Effective-potential equation reduces to (1/2)(dr/dtau)^2 = M/r (22.28); choose the negative root for infall.
- Assemble u^mu = ((1 - 2M/r)^{-1}, -(2M/r)^{1/2}, 0, 0) (22.29).
- Margin: build the falling frame with e_t-hat = u and a radial unit vector orthogonal to it (22.30).

### Radial plunge: proper time versus coordinate time — p.242 (pdf 259)

Example 22.4 integrates the plunge twice. In proper time r falls as (tau_* - tau)^{2/3}, reaching r = 0 after a finite interval with nothing special at 2M. In Schwarzschild time, which is the time kept by a distant static observer, dt/dr picks up a factor (1 - 2M/r)^{-1}; integrating gives an expression with a logarithm that diverges at r = 2M. The distant bookkeeping therefore never sees the infaller cross 2M, a curiosity flagged for Chapter 25.

**Concepts:** proper time of radial infall, coordinate-time divergence at the Schwarzschild radius, Schwarzschild radius

**Key moves**
- Separate variables in (22.28): r^{1/2} dr = -(2M)^{1/2} dtau, integrated up to r = 0 at tau_* (22.31).
- Obtain r(tau) (22.32) and invert to tau(r) (22.33).
- Chain rule dt/dr = u^t/u^r (22.35), with u^t blowing up at 2M.
- Quote the integrated result (22.36); the book omits the steps (one route: substitute s = (r/2M)^{1/2} and use partial fractions).
- Conclude: finite tau, infinite t to reach 2M; defer interpretation.

### Coordinate speed versus locally measured speed — p.243 (pdf 260)

Example 22.5 drops the assumption of starting from rest. Using u.u = -1 and the conserved u_t, the coordinate speed satisfies (dr/dt)^2 = (1 - 2M/r)^2 [1 - (1 - 2M/r)/u_t^2], which falls to zero as r approaches 2M. A static observer at radius r converts dt and dr with the vielbein factors, so the speed they measure is (1 - 2M/r)^{-1} dr/dt, and this tends to 1 (the speed of light) at 2M whatever the infall energy. The chapter concludes that normalization and two conservation laws already expose several strange relativistic effects.

**Concepts:** coordinate velocity of radial infall, locally measured speed of a static observer, transformation of 1-forms to an orthonormal frame, conserved energy per unit rest mass

**Key moves**
- Factor (u^t)^2 out of u.u = -1 and convert dr/dtau into dr/dt (22.37).
- Replace u^t by u_t using u_t = g_tt u^t and solve for (dr/dt)^2 (22.38).
- Transform basis 1-forms: dt-hat = (1 - 2M/r)^{1/2} dt, dr-hat = (1 - 2M/r)^{-1/2} dr (22.39).
- Divide to get the static observer's measured speed (22.40) and take r -> 2M.

### Chapter summary and exercises — p.244 (pdf 261)

## Concepts

### Schwarzschild metric

*solution-or-model · revisited* · also: Schwarzschild line element, Schwarzschild geometry

The static, spherically symmetric vacuum solution of Einstein's equations outside a mass M; in Schwarzschild coordinates its only non-zero components are g_tt = -(1 - 2M/r), g_rr = (1 - 2M/r)^{-1}, g_theta theta = r^2 and g_phi phi = r^2 sin^2 theta (geometrized units).

**How introduced:** Recalled from Chapter 21 at the very start and listed component by component in the margin so that the reader can inspect which coordinates appear.

**Prerequisites:** metric tensor, geometrized units

$$
ds^2=-\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)dt^2+\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)^{-1}dr^2+r^2(d\theta^2+\sin^2\theta\,d\phi^2)
$$

**Where:** GA p.237 (pdf 254)

### geodesic equation

*law · mention* · also: equation of free fall

Second-order equation d^2x^mu/dtau^2 + Gamma^mu_{alpha beta} (dx^alpha/dtau)(dx^beta/dtau) = 0 whose solutions are the world lines of freely falling particles; the connection coefficients come from derivatives of the metric.

**How introduced:** Recalled in a footnote as the apparently heavy route that the chapter intends to avoid; Exercise 22.2 asks the reader to confirm the shortcut against it.

**Prerequisites:** connection coefficients, four-velocity

$$
\frac{d^2x^\mu}{d\tau^2}+\Gamma^\mu_{\alpha\beta}\frac{dx^\alpha}{d\tau}\frac{dx^\beta}{d\tau}=0
$$

**Where:** GA p.237 (pdf 254); GA p.244 (pdf 261)

### four-velocity normalization

*identity · core* · also: velocity identity, u.u = -1

For a massive particle whose world line is parametrized by proper time, the tangent four-velocity has fixed squared length g_{mu nu} u^mu u^nu = -1 (signature -+++); it acts as a first integral that fixes the particle type and replaces one component of the geodesic equation.

**How introduced:** Named in the opening as a 'simple fact' and then used as the engine of every example: combined with the conserved quantities it yields the redshift normalization, the effective potential and the plunge velocities.

**Prerequisites:** four-velocity, proper time, metric tensor

$$
\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{u}=g_{\mu\nu}\frac{dx^\mu}{d\tau}\frac{dx^\nu}{d\tau}=-1
$$

**Where:** GA p.237 (pdf 254); GA §22.1 p.239 (pdf 256); GA §22.3 p.240 (pdf 257); GA §22.4 p.243 (pdf 260)

### null tangent condition

*identity · developed* · also: u.u = 0 for light, null geodesic condition

The tangent vector of a light ray's world line, parametrized by an affine parameter lambda, has zero squared length; equivalently ds^2 = 0 along the ray.

**How introduced:** Stated alongside the massive-particle condition as the second option for 'what kind of particle is falling', and listed in the margin toolbox as a constraint for photon problems.

**Prerequisites:** null vector, affine parameter

$$
g_{\mu\nu}\frac{dx^\mu}{d\lambda}\frac{dx^\nu}{d\lambda}=0
$$

**Where:** GA §22.1 p.239 (pdf 256); GA §22.3 p.240 (pdf 257)

### affine parameter

*definition · mention*

A parameter along a geodesic in terms of which the geodesic equation takes its standard form; it is unique up to linear rescaling. Proper time serves for timelike geodesics; for null geodesics some other affine parameter lambda must be used because proper time does not advance.

**How introduced:** Mentioned in passing when stating the two normalization conditions.

**Prerequisites:** geodesic equation

**Where:** GA §22.1 p.239 (pdf 256)

### constants of the motion

*principle · core* · also: conserved quantities

Quantities that keep the same value along a trajectory; for geodesics in a metric with symmetries they reduce the number of equations that must be integrated.

**How introduced:** Motivated by the Newtonian orbit problem, where energy and angular momentum conservation make the problem solvable; the chapter promises the same simplification in relativity.

**Prerequisites:** symmetry, geodesic equation

**Where:** GA p.237 (pdf 254); GA §22.1 p.238 (pdf 255); GA p.244 (pdf 261)

### Killing vector field

*mathematical-object · introduced* · also: Killing vector, symmetry vector of the metric

A vector field along which the metric does not change (its Lie derivative of the metric vanishes); it generates an isometry. In coordinates where no metric component depends on x^alpha, the basis vector e_alpha is a Killing vector.

**How introduced:** Introduced operationally through a boxed three-step recipe, explicitly without proof, with a margin pointer to the geometric treatment in Chapter 33.

**Prerequisites:** metric tensor, coordinate basis vectors

$$
\boldsymbol{\xi}=\mathbf{e}_\alpha\ \text{if}\ \partial_\alpha g_{\mu\nu}=0
$$

**Notes:** The recipe is sufficient but not necessary: a metric can have Killing vectors that are not coordinate basis vectors in the chosen chart. Proof deferred to GA ch33 §33.5.

**Where:** GA §22.1 p.238 (pdf 255)

### conservation law from a Killing vector

*theorem · core* · also: Killing rule, cyclic-coordinate rule

If xi is a Killing vector and u is the tangent of a geodesic, then xi.u is constant along the geodesic; in adapted coordinates this says the covariant component u_alpha is conserved whenever the metric does not depend on x^alpha.

**How introduced:** Step 3 of the recipe, restated in the margin toolbox as the rule that independence from x^alpha conserves u_alpha; used for both massive particles (u) and photons (p).

**Prerequisites:** Killing vector field, geodesic equation, index lowering

$$
\boldsymbol{\xi}\cdot\mathbf{u}=g_{\mu\nu}\xi^\mu u^\nu=\text{const}
$$

**Notes:** Not proved in this chapter; it is the relativistic analogue of a cyclic coordinate giving a conserved canonical momentum in Lagrangian mechanics.

**Where:** GA §22.1 p.238 (pdf 255); GA §22.2 p.239 (pdf 256); GA §22.3 p.240 (pdf 257)

### time-translation Killing vector

*mathematical-object · developed* · also: xi, energy Killing vector

For a static metric, the vector xi = e_t with components (1, 0, 0, 0), reflecting the independence of the metric from t; at infinity it coincides with the four-velocity of a static observer.

**How introduced:** Read off the Schwarzschild metric by step 2 of the recipe and later used to express any static observer's four-velocity as a rescaled xi.

**Prerequisites:** Killing vector field, Schwarzschild metric

$$
\xi^\mu=(1,0,0,0)
$$

**Where:** GA §22.1 p.238 (pdf 255); GA §22.2 p.240 (pdf 257)

### rotational Killing vector

*mathematical-object · developed* · also: eta, axial Killing vector

The vector eta = e_phi with components (0, 0, 0, 1), expressing the independence of the Schwarzschild metric from the azimuthal angle phi.

**How introduced:** Found by step 2 of the recipe alongside xi.

**Prerequisites:** Killing vector field, Schwarzschild metric

$$
\eta^\mu=(0,0,0,1)
$$

**Where:** GA §22.1 p.238 (pdf 255)

### conserved energy per unit rest mass

*physical-quantity · core* · also: E-tilde, specific energy, energy at infinity per unit mass

E-tilde = -xi.u = (1 - 2M/r) dt/dtau, constant along a Schwarzschild geodesic; for timelike geodesics it is the particle's total energy per unit rest mass as measured by a static observer at infinity (E-tilde = 1 for a particle at rest at infinity).

**How introduced:** Defined as minus the conserved dot product with xi (sign chosen so it is positive), then interpreted via a margin argument that a static observer at infinity has four-velocity xi and measures energy -p.xi.

**Prerequisites:** time-translation Killing vector, conservation law from a Killing vector, energy measured by an observer

$$
\tilde E=-\boldsymbol{\xi}\cdot\mathbf{u}=\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)u^t
$$

$$
u_t=-\tilde E
$$

**Notes:** Example 22.5 works with u_t, which equals minus E-tilde; only its square appears.

**Where:** GA §22.1 p.238 (pdf 255); GA §22.4 p.242 (pdf 259); GA §22.4 p.243 (pdf 260)

### conserved angular momentum per unit rest mass

*physical-quantity · core* · also: L-tilde, specific angular momentum

L-tilde = eta.u = r^2 sin^2 theta dphi/dtau, constant along a Schwarzschild geodesic; in the equatorial plane it is r^2 dphi/dtau, formally the Newtonian angular momentum per unit mass with proper time replacing time.

**How introduced:** Picked out as eta.u = u_phi and compared with the cylindrical-coordinate expression from mechanics.

**Prerequisites:** rotational Killing vector, conservation law from a Killing vector

$$
\tilde L=\boldsymbol{\eta}\cdot\mathbf{u}=r^2\sin^2\theta\,u^\phi
$$

**Where:** GA §22.1 p.239 (pdf 256); GA §22.3 p.240 (pdf 257)

### energy measured by an observer

*principle · core* · also: E = -p.u_obs

A particle with four-momentum p has energy E = -p.u_obs in the rest frame of an observer with four-velocity u_obs; this is the time component of p in that observer's orthonormal frame.

**How introduced:** Stated in a footnote on constants of motion to interpret E-tilde, then used as equation 22.12 for photon frequencies measured by static observers.

**Prerequisites:** four-momentum, four-velocity, orthonormal frame

$$
\hbar\omega=-\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{u}_{\rm obs}
$$

**Where:** GA §22.1 p.238 (pdf 255); GA §22.2 p.239 (pdf 256)

### static observer

*definition · developed* · also: stationary observer, observer at rest with respect to the star

An observer held at fixed r, theta, phi in the Schwarzschild geometry (necessarily accelerating unless at infinity); their four-velocity has only a time component u^t = (1 - 2M/r)^{-1/2}, so u_obs = (1 - 2M/r)^{-1/2} xi.

**How introduced:** Introduced as the two measuring stations of the redshift argument; normalization fixes the time component and yields the key identity with xi.

**Prerequisites:** four-velocity normalization, time-translation Killing vector

$$
\mathbf{u}_{\rm obs}(r)=\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)^{-1/2}\boldsymbol{\xi}
$$

**Notes:** Exercise 22.1 asks for the proper acceleration needed to stay static.

**Where:** GA §22.2 p.239 (pdf 256); GA §22.2 p.240 (pdf 257); GA §22.3 p.241 (pdf 258); GA §22.4 p.243 (pdf 260)

### gravitational redshift

*phenomenon · core* · also: gravitational frequency shift, Einstein shift

Light sent from radius R to a distant static observer arrives with lower frequency, omega_inf = omega_R (1 - 2M/R)^{1/2}, because the conserved quantity is -xi.p while each static observer's measured energy carries a different normalization factor.

**How introduced:** First application of the toolkit: two static observers and a radial photon (Fig. 22.1); presented as revisiting the equivalence-principle shift of Chapter 6 with the exact Schwarzschild factor.

**Prerequisites:** energy measured by an observer, static observer, conservation law from a Killing vector, photon four-momentum

$$
\omega_\infty=\omega_R\left(1-\frac{2M}{R}\right)^{1/2}
$$

**Notes:** Exercise 22.3 reproduces it with vielbein components instead of the Killing-vector trick.

**Where:** GA §22.2 p.239 (pdf 256); GA §22.2 p.240 (pdf 257)

### photon four-momentum

*physical-quantity · introduced* · also: null momentum

The four-momentum p of light is a null vector tangent to the light ray's null geodesic, so the Killing conservation law applies to it just as to u for massive particles.

**How introduced:** Assumed in a footnote (p parallel to u for light) so that xi.p is conserved; a careful treatment is promised for Section 24.7.

**Prerequisites:** null tangent condition, four-momentum

$$
\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{p}=0
$$

**Where:** GA §22.2 p.239 (pdf 256)

### coordinate velocity

*technique · developed* · also: dx/dt trick

The rate of change of spatial coordinates with coordinate time, obtained from the four-velocity as dx^i/dt = u^i/u^t; it is a bookkeeping quantity, not in general what any local observer measures.

**How introduced:** Listed in the margin toolbox and used for the plunge (dt/dr and dr/dt) and for Example 22.5.

**Prerequisites:** four-velocity, chain rule

$$
\frac{dx^i}{dt}=\frac{1}{u^0}\frac{dx^i}{d\tau}
$$

**Where:** GA §22.3 p.240 (pdf 257); GA §22.4 p.242 (pdf 259); GA §22.4 p.243 (pdf 260)

### equatorial-plane reduction

*technique · introduced* · also: theta = pi/2 restriction

Because of spherical symmetry any geodesic can be rotated into the plane theta = pi/2, where u^theta = 0 and the problem involves only t, r and phi.

**How introduced:** Adopted 'for simplicity' at the start of Example 22.1 without a detailed symmetry argument.

**Prerequisites:** spherical symmetry

**Where:** GA §22.3 p.240 (pdf 257)

### effective energy

*physical-quantity · core* · also: script E, E_eff

The constant (E-tilde^2 - 1)/2, playing the role of the total energy per unit mass in the one-dimensional radial equation; it is zero for a particle released from rest at infinity and positive for unbound particles.

**How introduced:** Defined after rearranging the normalization condition so that the left-hand side is constant; the notation follows Hartle.

**Prerequisites:** conserved energy per unit rest mass

$$
\mathcal{E}=\frac{\tilde E^2-1}{2}
$$

**Where:** GA §22.3 p.241 (pdf 258); GA §22.4 p.242 (pdf 259)

### relativistic effective potential

*mathematical-object · core* · also: Schwarzschild effective potential, V_eff

The function V_eff(r) = -M/r + L^2/(2r^2) - M L^2/r^3 such that equatorial geodesics obey E_eff = (1/2)(dr/dtau)^2 + V_eff(r); compared with Newtonian gravity it contains an extra attractive term that dominates at small r, producing an inner maximum and a fall to minus infinity.

**How introduced:** Derived in Example 22.1 from u.u = -1 plus the two conserved quantities, recognized as the same structure as Chapter 20's Newtonian problem, and plotted against the Newtonian curve in Fig. 22.3.

**Prerequisites:** four-velocity normalization, conserved energy per unit rest mass, conserved angular momentum per unit rest mass, Newtonian effective potential, effective energy

$$
V_{\rm eff}(r)=-\frac{M}{r}+\frac{\tilde L^2}{2r^2}-\frac{M\tilde L^2}{r^3}
$$

$$
\mathcal{E}=\frac12\left(\frac{dr}{d\tau}\right)^2+V_{\rm eff}(r)
$$

**Notes:** Radial variable is proper-time based: the equation is exact in tau, not in t.

**Where:** GA §22.3 p.240 (pdf 257); GA §22.3 p.241 (pdf 258)

### relativistic 1/r^3 correction

*phenomenon · developed* · also: extra attractive term

The term -M L^2/r^3 (GM L^2/(c^2 r^3) in ordinary units) that distinguishes the Schwarzschild effective potential from the Newtonian one; it is suppressed by 1/c^2 but overwhelms the centrifugal barrier at small r.

**How introduced:** Singled out when comparing with the Newtonian effective potential and made explicit by restoring G and c in Example 22.2.

**Prerequisites:** relativistic effective potential

$$
-\frac{GML^2}{mc^2r^3}
$$

**Where:** GA §22.3 p.241 (pdf 258)

### Newtonian effective potential

*mathematical-object · revisited* · also: U_eff/m

For Newtonian motion under a point mass, V_eff = -M/r + L^2/(2r^2) per unit mass (with G = 1), combining gravitational and centrifugal terms so radial motion is one-dimensional.

**How introduced:** Recalled in a margin note from Chapter 20 with the factor of m explained, and drawn dotted in Fig. 22.3.

**Prerequisites:** conservation of angular momentum

$$
V_{\rm eff}^{\rm N}=\frac{U_{\rm eff}}{m}=-\frac{M}{r}+\frac{\tilde L^2}{2r^2}
$$

**Where:** GA §22.3 p.241 (pdf 258)

### Newtonian limit of the relativistic energy equation

*technique · developed* · also: E_N

Writing E-tilde = (mc^2 + E_N)/(mc^2) and restoring G and c turns the effective-potential equation into the Newtonian radial energy equation plus a correction of order 1/c^2 proportional to 1/r^3.

**How introduced:** Example 22.2 carries out the substitution; Exercise 22.4 asks the reader to check it.

**Prerequisites:** relativistic effective potential, geometrized units

$$
\tilde E=\frac{mc^2+E_N}{mc^2}
$$

**Notes:** The printed result silently drops a term E_N^2/(2mc^2) and identifies dtau with dt at leading order.

**Where:** GA §22.3 p.241 (pdf 258); GA p.244 (pdf 261)

### geometrized units

*convention · introduced* · also: G = c = 1

Units in which G and c are set to one so that mass is measured as a length, M standing for GM/c^2; physical formulas are recovered by reinserting G and c by dimensional analysis.

**How introduced:** Used throughout; Example 22.2 shows how to put the constants back into V_eff.

$$
M\to\frac{GM}{c^2}
$$

**Where:** GA p.237 (pdf 254); GA §22.3 p.241 (pdf 258)

### static-observer orthonormal frame (vielbein)

*mathematical-object · revisited* · also: vielbein for a stationary observer, hatted frame

The orthonormal basis carried by a static observer, related to Schwarzschild coordinate components by (e_t)^t-hat = (1 - 2M/r)^{1/2}, (e_r)^r-hat = (1 - 2M/r)^{-1/2}, (e_theta)^theta-hat = r, (e_phi)^phi-hat = r sin theta; hatted components are what that observer measures.

**How introduced:** Listed in a margin note as the tool for converting coordinate results into measurements, with the remark that at infinity the time and radial factors are one; later used in Example 22.5.

**Prerequisites:** orthonormal frame, static observer

$$
(\mathbf{e}_t)^{\hat t}=\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)^{1/2},\ (\mathbf{e}_r)^{\hat r}=\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)^{-1/2}
$$

**Where:** GA §22.3 p.241 (pdf 258); GA §22.4 p.243 (pdf 260)

### transformation of 1-forms to an orthonormal frame

*operation · introduced*

Basis 1-forms transform with the vielbein as omega^mu-hat = (e_mu)^mu-hat omega^mu, so the static observer's time and radial displacement forms are rescaled coordinate forms.

**How introduced:** Recalled in Example 22.5 to get the local speed from the coordinate speed.

**Prerequisites:** static-observer orthonormal frame (vielbein), one-forms

$$
\mathbf{d}\hat t=\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)^{1/2}\mathbf{d}t,\quad \mathbf{d}\hat r=\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)^{-1/2}\mathbf{d}r
$$

**Where:** GA §22.4 p.243 (pdf 260)

### freely falling observer frame

*mathematical-object · introduced* · also: infaller's orthonormal frame, frame of the plunging particle

The orthonormal frame carried by a freely falling particle: its time vector is the particle's four-velocity and its radial vector is the unit vector in the t-r plane orthogonal to u; for the plunge from rest at infinity e_r-hat has coordinate components (-(2M/r)^{1/2}/(1 - 2M/r), 1, 0, 0).

**How introduced:** Built in a margin note to Example 22.3, emphasizing that it is locally flat but moving relative to the static frame.

**Prerequisites:** orthonormal frame, radial plunge from rest at infinity

$$
(\mathbf{e}_{\hat r})^\mu=\left(\frac{-\sqrt{2M/r}}{1-2M/r},1,0,0\right)
$$

**Notes:** The angular vectors quoted with components (0,0,1,0) and (0,0,0,1) are not unit length; normalized versions carry 1/r and 1/(r sin theta).

**Where:** GA §22.4 p.242 (pdf 259)

### radial plunge from rest at infinity

*solution-or-model · core* · also: radial infall, marginally bound radial geodesic

The timelike geodesic with L-tilde = 0 and E-tilde = 1: u^mu = ((1 - 2M/r)^{-1}, -(2M/r)^{1/2}, 0, 0), so dr/dtau has the same form as the Newtonian infall speed from rest at infinity.

**How introduced:** Chosen as the showcase example: all constants follow from the initial conditions, the equation of motion is a one-liner, and it exposes the proper-versus-coordinate time contrast.

**Prerequisites:** conserved energy per unit rest mass, relativistic effective potential, four-velocity normalization

$$
u^\mu=\left(\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)^{-1},-\left(\frac{2M}{r}\right)^{1/2},0,0\right)
$$

**Where:** GA §22.4 p.242 (pdf 259); GA p.244 (pdf 261)

### proper time of radial infall

*physical-quantity · core* · also: infaller's clock

Along the plunge, tau(r) = tau_* - (2/3)(2M)^{-1/2} r^{3/2}, so reaching any smaller radius, including 2M and r = 0, takes a finite proper time; from r = 2M to r = 0 it is 4M/3.

**How introduced:** Obtained in Example 22.4 by separating variables in dr/dtau and plotted in Fig. 22.5.

**Prerequisites:** radial plunge from rest at infinity, proper time

$$
r(\tau)=\left(\frac32\right)^{2/3}(2M)^{1/3}(\tau_*-\tau)^{2/3}
$$

**Where:** GA §22.4 p.242 (pdf 259); GA §22.4 p.243 (pdf 260)

### coordinate-time divergence at the Schwarzschild radius

*phenomenon · core* · also: infinite coordinate time to reach 2M, frozen infall as seen from far away

Schwarzschild time t, which matches the clocks of distant static observers, grows without bound as a radially falling particle approaches r = 2M, because dt/dtau = E-tilde/(1 - 2M/r) diverges; the particle's own clock records only a finite interval.

**How introduced:** Read off the logarithm in the integrated t(r) (22.36) and Fig. 22.6, stated as the most notable curiosity, and deferred to Chapter 25.

**Prerequisites:** proper time of radial infall, coordinate velocity, Schwarzschild radius

$$
t=t_*+2M\left[-\frac23\left(\frac{r}{2M}\right)^{3/2}-2\left(\frac{r}{2M}\right)^{1/2}+\ln\left|\frac{(r/2M)^{1/2}+1}{(r/2M)^{1/2}-1}\right|\right]
$$

**Notes:** A statement about the Schwarzschild coordinate chart, not about any physical barrier; resolution in GA ch25 and ch27.

**Where:** GA §22.4 p.243 (pdf 260)

### Schwarzschild radius

*definition · introduced* · also: r_S = 2M, gravitational radius

The radius r = 2M (2GM/c^2) where g_tt vanishes and g_rr diverges in Schwarzschild coordinates; static observers cannot exist there.

**How introduced:** Appears as the place where the redshift factor, the coordinate time and the local infall speed all misbehave; marked as a dotted line in Figs. 22.2, 22.6 and 22.7.

**Prerequisites:** Schwarzschild metric

$$
r_S=2M
$$

**Where:** GA §22.2 p.240 (pdf 257); GA §22.4 p.243 (pdf 260)

### coordinate velocity of radial infall

*physical-quantity · developed* · also: dr/dt of a plunging particle

For radial geodesics with conserved u_t, (dr/dt)^2 = (1 - 2M/r)^2 [1 - (1 - 2M/r)/u_t^2]; it tends to zero as r -> 2M whatever the energy, and for release from rest at infinity it equals -(1 - 2M/r)(2M/r)^{1/2}.

**How introduced:** Derived in Example 22.5 from the normalization condition written in terms of dr/dt, and plotted as the solid line in Fig. 22.7.

**Prerequisites:** coordinate velocity, four-velocity normalization, conserved energy per unit rest mass

$$
\left(\frac{dr}{dt}\right)^2=\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)^2\left[1-\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)\frac{1}{u_t^2}\right]
$$

**Where:** GA §22.4 p.242 (pdf 259); GA §22.4 p.243 (pdf 260)

### locally measured speed of a static observer

*physical-quantity · developed* · also: physical infall speed, v-hat

The radial speed of a passing particle measured by a static observer at the same r, dr-hat/dt-hat = (1 - 2M/r)^{-1} dr/dt; it tends to the speed of light as r -> 2M for any infall energy (for release from rest at infinity it equals (2M/r)^{1/2}).

**How introduced:** Obtained in Example 22.5 by converting dt and dr with the static-observer vielbein, and drawn dashed in Fig. 22.7 next to the vanishing coordinate speed.

**Prerequisites:** static-observer orthonormal frame (vielbein), coordinate velocity of radial infall

$$
\frac{d\hat r}{d\hat t}=\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)^{-1}\frac{dr}{dt}
$$

**Where:** GA §22.4 p.243 (pdf 260)

### coordinate escape velocity

*physical-quantity · mention*

The magnitude of dr/dt for a particle that just reaches infinity with zero speed, (1 - 2M/r)(2M/r)^{1/2}; by time reversal it equals the coordinate speed of the plunge from rest at infinity.

**How introduced:** Mentioned in a footnote beside eqn 22.34 and pursued in Exercise 22.5, which asks for the escape speed and energy in the launcher's rest frame.

**Prerequisites:** coordinate velocity of radial infall

$$
\left|\frac{dr}{dt}\right|=\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)\left(\frac{2M}{r}\right)^{1/2}

**Where:** GA §22.4 p.242 (pdf 259); GA p.244 (pdf 261)

### proper acceleration of a static observer

*physical-quantity · mention*

The magnitude of the four-acceleration a = nabla_u u of an observer held at fixed r; it is non-zero because such an observer is not following a geodesic.

**How introduced:** Only posed in Exercise 22.1, which asks for the acceleration components of a particle at rest and then its frame-measured value.

**Prerequisites:** geodesic equation, static observer, covariant derivative

$$
a^\mu=u^\alpha u^\mu{}_{;\alpha}
$$

**Where:** GA p.244 (pdf 261)

### gravitational clock effect

*experiment-or-observation · mention* · also: Hafele-Keating effect

Clocks at different heights and speeds around the rotating Earth accumulate different proper times because of both the gravitational potential and their motion; eastward and westward flights differ because of the Earth's rotation.

**How introduced:** Posed as Exercise 22.6 with approximate proper-time rate formulas; the authors note the Hafele-Keating flights of the early 1970s agreed with such predictions.

**Prerequisites:** Schwarzschild metric, proper time, time dilation

$$
d\tau_1\approx\left(1-\frac{M}{r}-\frac{r^2\omega^2}{2}\right)dt
$$

**Where:** GA p.244 (pdf 261); GA p.245 (pdf 262)

### relative velocity from the dot product of four-velocities

*identity · mention* · also: gamma = -u.v

Two observers with four-velocities u and v passing at the same event have Lorentz factor of relative motion gamma = -u.v.

## Worked examples

### Killing vectors and constants of motion for Schwarzschild (§22.1, in prose) · intro · GA §22.1 p.238 (pdf 255)

**Problem:** Identify the symmetries of the Schwarzschild metric and the quantities conserved along its geodesics.

**Method:** Apply the three-step rule: none of the components involve t or phi, so e_t and e_phi are Killing vectors; dot each into u using g_{mu nu} = e_mu.e_nu and the diagonal metric.

**Key insight:** Conserved quantities are simply the lowered components u_t and u_phi; no Christoffel symbols are needed.

**Result:** E-tilde = (1 - 2M/r) dt/dtau and L-tilde = r^2 sin^2 theta dphi/dtau are constant along geodesics.

**Concepts:** Killing vector field, conservation law from a Killing vector, conserved energy per unit rest mass, conserved angular momentum per unit rest mass

### Gravitational redshift derivation (§22.2, in prose) · standard · GA §22.2 p.239 (pdf 256)

**Problem:** Relate the frequency of a radially outgoing photon measured by a static observer at R to that measured by a static observer at infinity.

**Method:** Use hbar omega = -p.u_obs; normalize the static observer's velocity to get u_obs = (1 - 2M/r)^{-1/2} xi; use conservation of xi.p along the null geodesic to equate the two evaluations.

**Key insight:** The static observer's four-velocity is parallel to the time Killing vector, so each measured frequency is a conserved number times a local normalization factor.

**Result:** omega_inf = omega_R (1 - 2M/R)^{1/2}.

**Concepts:** gravitational redshift, static observer, energy measured by an observer, time-translation Killing vector

### Example 22.1 · standard · GA §22.3 p.240 (pdf 257)

**Problem:** Reduce equatorial motion of a massive particle in Schwarzschild spacetime to a one-dimensional radial equation.

**Method:** Set theta = pi/2, write u.u = -1 in components, replace u^t and u^phi by E-tilde and L-tilde, and rearrange so that a constant equals a kinetic-like term plus a function of r.

**Key insight:** The normalization condition is itself the first integral of the radial geodesic equation once the two conserved quantities are inserted.

**Result:** (E-tilde^2 - 1)/2 = (1/2)(dr/dtau)^2 + V_eff(r) with V_eff = -M/r + L^2/(2r^2) - M L^2/r^3.

**Concepts:** relativistic effective potential, effective energy, four-velocity normalization, equatorial-plane reduction

### Example 22.2 · intro · GA §22.3 p.241 (pdf 258)

**Problem:** Show how the relativistic radial energy equation relates to the Newtonian one.

**Method:** Reinsert G and c in V_eff, define a Newtonian energy by E-tilde = (mc^2 + E_N)/mc^2, multiply through by mc^2 and write L = m L-tilde.

**Key insight:** Every Newtonian term reappears, and relativity contributes one additional attractive term suppressed by 1/c^2 and growing as 1/r^3.

**Result:** E_N = (m/2)(dr/dtau)^2 + L^2/(2mr^2) - GMm/r - GML^2/(mc^2 r^3), valid to leading order in E_N/mc^2.

**Concepts:** Newtonian limit of the relativistic energy equation, relativistic 1/r^3 correction, geometrized units

### Example 22.3 · intro · GA §22.4 p.242 (pdf 259)

**Problem:** Find the four-velocity of a massive particle that falls radially from rest at infinity.

**Method:** Radial motion gives L-tilde = 0; rest at infinity gives u^t = 1 there, so E-tilde = 1 and zero effective energy for the whole fall; E-tilde fixes u^t and the effective-potential equation fixes u^r with the inward sign.

**Key insight:** Initial conditions at infinity pin both constants, so the entire four-velocity follows algebraically without integrating anything.

**Result:** u^mu = ((1 - 2M/r)^{-1}, -(2M/r)^{1/2}, 0, 0).

**Concepts:** radial plunge from rest at infinity, conserved energy per unit rest mass, effective energy

### Example 22.4 · standard · GA §22.4 p.242 (pdf 259)

**Problem:** Compute how much proper time and how much Schwarzschild coordinate time elapse during the radial plunge.

**Method:** Separate variables in dr/dtau = -(2M/r)^{1/2} and integrate to the arrival time tau_* at r = 0; for t, form dt/dr = u^t/u^r and integrate. The book states the logarithmic result without steps; a tutor can supply them with s = (r/2M)^{1/2} and partial fractions.

**Key insight:** The factor (1 - 2M/r)^{-1} in u^t makes the coordinate-time integral diverge logarithmically at 2M, while the proper-time integrand is perfectly regular there.

**Result:** r(tau) = (3/2)^{2/3}(2M)^{1/3}(tau_* - tau)^{2/3}; t(r) given by (22.36), which tends to infinity as r -> 2M.

**Concepts:** proper time of radial infall, coordinate-time divergence at the Schwarzschild radius, coordinate velocity

### Example 22.5 · standard · GA §22.4 p.243 (pdf 260)

**Problem:** For radial infall with arbitrary energy, find the coordinate speed dr/dt and the speed measured by a static observer at the same radius.

**Method:** Pull (u^t)^2 out of the normalization condition to convert dr/dtau into dr/dt, express u^t via the conserved u_t, solve for (dr/dt)^2; then rescale dt and dr with the static-observer vielbein 1-forms and divide.

**Key insight:** Coordinate speed is a chart artefact that vanishes at 2M; the physically measured speed is larger by (1 - 2M/r)^{-1} and approaches c there for every initial speed.

**Result:** (dr/dt)^2 = (1 - 2M/r)^2[1 - (1 - 2M/r)/u_t^2]; dr-hat/dt-hat = (1 - 2M/r)^{-1} dr/dt -> 1 as r -> 2M.

**Concepts:** coordinate velocity of radial infall, locally measured speed of a static observer, transformation of 1-forms to an orthonormal frame


## Analogies and intuitions

### Newtonian orbit problems become easy once energy and angular momentum conservation are used → constants of the motion · strong · GA p.237 (pdf 254)

Sets expectations that the relativistic problem, which looks like a system of coupled geodesic equations, can be cut down in the same way, and that close cousins of energy and angular momentum will be conserved.

**Where it breaks down:** Relativistic 'energy' E-tilde includes rest energy and is defined relative to a static observer at infinity; conservation follows from metric symmetry, not from a force law.

**App idea:** Split-screen: a Newtonian orbit and a Schwarzschild orbit with identical initial data, each with a live bar showing its conserved quantities staying fixed while r and phi change.

### L-tilde = r^2 dphi/dtau mirrors angular momentum per unit mass in cylindrical coordinates → conserved angular momentum per unit rest mass · useful · GA §22.1 p.239 (pdf 256)

Reassures the reader that the Killing-vector construction reproduces a familiar expression in the equatorial plane.

**Where it breaks down:** The derivative is with respect to proper time and r is the areal radius, not a Euclidean distance.

### A photon loses energy as it climbs out of the gravitational potential → gravitational redshift · useful · GA §22.2 p.240 (pdf 257)

Offered in one sentence after the derivation as a heuristic for why the frequency drops; the book gives no mechanical picture beyond 'climbing the potential' (a thrown-ball comparison is a natural tutor extension, not the book's).

**Where it breaks down:** The photon's speed does not change and nothing is 'lost' locally; the effect is really a comparison of clocks at different radii linked by the conserved xi.p.

**App idea:** Toggle between a 'climbing ball' cartoon and the two-clock picture, prompting learners to say which quantity is actually conserved.

### The relativistic radial equation is a particle rolling in a one-dimensional potential, exactly as in Chapter 20 → relativistic effective potential · strong · GA §22.3 p.241 (pdf 258)

The derived equation is cast in the familiar 'energy = kinetic + effective potential' shape so the reader can reuse turning-point and well reasoning, and the new behaviour is read off the shape of V_eff.

**Where it breaks down:** The 'time' is proper time, the 'energy' is (E-tilde^2 - 1)/2 rather than a physical energy, and the equation gives r(tau) but says nothing directly about phi(t) or what distant observers see.

**App idea:** Bead-on-a-wire animation: a bead slides on a wire shaped like V_eff while the orbit is traced in a side panel; reshape the wire with the L-tilde slider.

### Plunge from rest at infinity has dr/dtau = -(2M/r)^{1/2}, the same form as Newtonian free fall from infinity (escape speed) → radial plunge from rest at infinity · useful · GA §22.4 p.242 (pdf 259)

Implicit in eqn 22.28, which is the Newtonian energy statement for zero total energy; lets learners recognize the result before seeing the relativistic twists.

**Where it breaks down:** The match is with proper time and Schwarzschild r; in coordinate time the motion is very different near 2M.

**App idea:** Overlay Newtonian r(t) and relativistic r(tau) and r(t) for the same release; the first two coincide exactly, the third peels away near 2M.


## Misconceptions addressed

### To find orbits in general relativity you must first compute all connection coefficients and solve the four coupled geodesic equations. · GA §22.3 p.240 (pdf 257)

**Correction:** Two conserved quantities from the metric's symmetries plus the normalization of the four-velocity supply three first integrals, enough for equatorial motion; the geodesic equation is only a cross-check.

**Why tempting:** Earlier chapters present the geodesic equation as the law of free fall, and it is the general route.

### The conserved photon energy along a geodesic is the coordinate component p^t, as in flat space. · GA §22.2 p.239 (pdf 256)

**Correction:** What stays constant is g_tt p^t = -xi.p; p^t itself changes with r.

**Why tempting:** In special relativity p^t is the energy and is conserved for free particles.

### An observer at radius r measures the coordinate component p^t of a photon's momentum as its energy. · GA §22.2 p.239 (pdf 256)

**Correction:** Measurement gives the component in the observer's orthonormal frame, -p.u_obs, which differs from p^t by the factor (1 - 2M/r)^{1/2} for a static observer.

**Why tempting:** Coordinate components look like the physical ones, and far from the mass they coincide.

### Because a distant observer's time coordinate runs to infinity, an infalling astronaut never actually reaches r = 2M. · GA §22.4 p.243 (pdf 260)

**Correction:** The astronaut's proper time to reach 2M and even r = 0 is finite; the divergence belongs to the Schwarzschild time coordinate, whose meaning is examined in Chapter 25.

**Why tempting:** Schwarzschild t matches the clocks of observers far away, so it feels like 'real' time.

### An infalling particle slows down and stops as it approaches r = 2M. · GA §22.4 p.243 (pdf 260)

**Correction:** Only dr/dt vanishes; a static observer at the same radius measures the particle rushing past at a speed approaching c.

**Why tempting:** dr/dt looks like an ordinary velocity and its graph drops to zero.

### The static-observer vielbein can be used to convert components for any observer at that radius. · GA §22.3 p.241 (pdf 258)

**Correction:** Each observer's measurements require their own orthonormal frame; a moving (for example freely falling) observer needs a different vielbein.

**Why tempting:** At a given r the metric is the same, so one may think the frame is too.

### E-tilde is the particle's energy as measured by whoever is nearby. · GA §22.1 p.238 (pdf 255)

**Correction:** E-tilde is energy per unit rest mass relative to a static observer at infinity; a local static observer measures E-tilde divided by (1 - 2M/r)^{1/2}.

**Why tempting:** It is called 'energy' and is conserved, like the everyday notion.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### The relativistic effective potential is just the Newtonian one with a small correction everywhere, so orbits change only slightly. · GA §22.3 p.241 (pdf 258)

**Correction:** At large r the correction is tiny, but at small r the -ML^2/r^3 term beats the centrifugal term, so the potential has a finite maximum and then falls without limit: a particle with energy above the barrier is captured whatever its angular momentum, whereas in Newtonian gravity any nonzero angular momentum prevents reaching r = 0.

**Why tempting:** Example 22.2 stresses that the extra term is order 1/c^2.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### A metric has a symmetry only if some coordinate is missing from its components. · GA §22.1 p.238 (pdf 255)

**Correction:** The recipe finds symmetries adapted to the chosen coordinates; Schwarzschild has two further rotational Killing vectors not visible as missing coordinates, and a clumsy chart can hide even t-independence.

**Why tempting:** The chapter's recipe is simple and presented as the way to find conserved quantities.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### The r in V_eff is the distance from the centre, as the axis label |r| in Fig. 22.3 suggests. · GA §22.3 p.241 (pdf 258)

**Correction:** r is the Schwarzschild areal coordinate (circumference over 2 pi); radial proper distance differs from differences in r near the mass.

**Why tempting:** Newtonian intuition and the vector notation on the plot.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

**App idea:** Globe with two planes and three clocks; sliders for altitude and speed show accumulated nanoseconds for each direction.

### A dropped observer passing a static one · GA p.245 (pdf 262)

**Lesson:** Relative speed at the meeting event follows from the dot product of their four-velocities, combining the conserved energy of the faller with the static observer's normalization.

## Applications and observations

- **Size of gravitational redshift for real objects** (numerical-estimate, GA §22.2 p.240 (pdf 257)): Our own estimates from eqn 22.16 (not in the book): fractional shift is roughly M/R when M/R is small. Key numbers: Earth surface GM/(c^2 R) ~ 7e-10; Sun ~ 2.1e-6; white dwarf such as Sirius B ~ 3e-4 (about 80 km/s equivalent); 1.4 solar-mass neutron star with R = 10 km: 2M/R ~ 0.41, redshift z ~ 0.3.
- **Hafele-Keating airborne clock experiment** (experiment, GA p.244 (pdf 261)): Exercise 22.6 derives approximate proper-time rate differences for an aircraft at height h and speed v versus a clock on the rotating Earth, and notes the early-1970s flights agreed with such predictions. Key numbers: Our estimate with h = 10 km, v = 230 m/s, equatorial rotation speed 465 m/s: rate difference about +0.4e-12 eastward (flying clock loses) and about -2e-12 westward (flying clock gains), i.e. tens to hundreds of nanoseconds over a roughly 45-hour circumnavigation; the published measurements were about -59 ns east and +273 ns west.
- **Infall time from the horizon to r = 0** (numerical-estimate, GA §22.4 p.242 (pdf 259)): Our own evaluation of eqn 22.33 for release from rest at infinity: the proper time from r = 2M to r = 0 is 4M/3, i.e. (4/3) GM/c^3. Key numbers: About 6.6 microseconds per solar mass: ~66 microseconds for a 10 solar-mass black hole, ~28 s for Sagittarius A* (4.3 million solar masses), ~12 hours for M87* (6.5 billion solar masses).
- **Features of the effective potential in Fig. 22.3** (numerical-estimate, GA §22.3 p.241 (pdf 258)): Our computation for L-tilde/M = 4.3: extrema of V_eff occur where r^2 - L^2 r + 3L^2 M^2 = 0 (units of M). Key numbers: Barrier maximum near r = 3.8M with V_eff about +0.04; well minimum near r = 14.7M with V_eff about -0.031; Newtonian minimum at r = L^2/M = 18.5M with V = -0.027. The printed barrier looks much taller relative to the well than these numbers, so the sketch is qualitative. Barrier and well merge when L-tilde/M = sqrt(12).
- **Maximum coordinate infall speed** (numerical-estimate, GA §22.4 p.242 (pdf 259)): Our own evaluation of eqn 22.34: for release from rest at infinity |dr/dt| = (1 - 2M/r)(2M/r)^{1/2} peaks and then falls to zero at 2M, while the locally measured speed (2M/r)^{1/2} rises monotonically to 1. Key numbers: Peak coordinate speed 2/(3 sqrt 3) ~ 0.385 c at r = 6M; locally measured speed at r = 6M is ~0.577 c.
- **Smallness of the relativistic correction in the Solar System** (numerical-estimate, GA §22.3 p.241 (pdf 258)): Our estimate: the ratio of the 1/r^3 term to the centrifugal term in eqn 22.26 is 2GM/(c^2 r). Key numbers: About 5e-8 at Mercury's orbit, which is why its effect shows up only as a slow cumulative perihelion precession (Chapter 23).

## Notation and conventions

- **Units:** Geometrized units G = c = 1 throughout; M means GM/c^2 and has dimensions of length. Example 22.2 shows how to restore G and c. (GA p.237 (pdf 254))
- **Metric signature and coordinates:** Signature (-,+,+,+); coordinates ordered (t, r, theta, phi) with u^0 = u^t. (GA p.237 (pdf 254))
- **Tilde quantities:** E-tilde and L-tilde denote energy and angular momentum per unit rest mass; E-tilde = -xi.u carries a minus sign so it is positive. Physical angular momentum L = m L-tilde. — Follows Hartle's notation; the authors warn other books define these terms differently. (GA §22.1 p.238 (pdf 255))
- **Effective energy symbol:** Script E = (E-tilde^2 - 1)/2 is the constant in the radial equation, distinct from E-tilde and from the Newtonian E_N. (GA §22.3 p.241 (pdf 258))
- **Killing vector symbols:** xi for the time-translation Killing vector e_t and eta for the rotational one e_phi. (GA §22.1 p.238 (pdf 255))
- **Parameters along world lines:** tau (proper time) for massive particles; lambda for an affine parameter on null geodesics; overdot denotes d/dtau (as in L-tilde = r^2 phi-dot). (GA §22.1 p.239 (pdf 256))
- **Hatted components and vielbeins:** Hats label components in an observer's orthonormal frame; vielbein components are written (e_mu)^mu-hat and basis 1-forms transform as omega^mu-hat = (e_mu)^mu-hat omega^mu. — Bold d denotes a basis 1-form (dt, dr), plain d an ordinary differential. (GA §22.3 p.241 (pdf 258))
- **Schwarzschild radius symbol:** r_S = 2M, drawn as a dotted line on the plunge graphs. (GA §22.4 p.243 (pdf 260))
- **Equation numbering in margins:** Some numbered equations sit in the margin rather than the main text: 22.1 (metric components), 22.17 (coordinate-velocity trick), 22.30 and 22.34 (plunge footnotes); 22.1 is printed beside 22.2. — The reading copy lists 22.2 before 22.1 for this reason. (GA p.237 (pdf 254))
- **Measured photon energy:** hbar omega = -p.u_obs, with p the null four-momentum and omega angular frequency. (GA §22.2 p.239 (pdf 256))

## Teaching gems

### A three-step, inspection-only recipe for conserved quantities: find coordinates missing from the metric, promote each to a Killing vector, dot it into the velocity. · GA §22.1 p.238 (pdf 255)

**Why it works:** Converts an abstract symmetry concept into an action a beginner can perform in seconds, and transfers to any metric they meet later (FRW, Kerr).

**App idea:** Metric inspector: learner pastes or selects a metric; each component is shown as a card, coordinates appearing in any card light up, the absent ones are offered as Killing vectors, and the corresponding conserved u_alpha expression is generated.

### Writing a static observer's four-velocity as (1 - 2M/r)^{-1/2} times the time Killing vector, so the redshift becomes a ratio of normalization factors. · GA §22.2 p.240 (pdf 257)

**Why it works:** The whole derivation shrinks to one line and makes clear that the effect is a comparison of two local clocks linked by one conserved number.

**App idea:** Arrow diagram in the t-r plane showing xi as the same arrow everywhere and the static observer's unit u_obs as xi stretched by a radius-dependent factor that grows toward 2M.

### Substituting E-tilde and L-tilde into u.u = -1 turns geodesic motion into the Chapter 20 effective-potential problem with a single extra term. · GA §22.3 p.241 (pdf 258)

**Why it works:** Reuses a mental model the learner already owns and localizes all the new physics in one visible term.

**App idea:** Term-by-term potential builder: sliders fade each of the three V_eff terms in and out so the learner watches the Newtonian curve morph into the relativistic one.

### Restoring G and c and defining E_N through E-tilde = (mc^2 + E_N)/mc^2 to exhibit the relativistic term as an order-1/c^2 correction. · GA §22.3 p.241 (pdf 258)

**Why it works:** Shows exactly how general relativity contains Newtonian orbits as a limit, and gives a quantitative sense of when relativity matters.

**App idea:** A 'speed of light' slider that increases c from its true value toward infinity, collapsing the relativistic curve onto the Newtonian one, with a readout of 2GM/(c^2 r) at a chosen radius.

### The radial plunge told through three graphs of one trajectory: r against proper time (finite), r against Schwarzschild time (diverges at 2M), and speed against r (coordinate speed to zero, local speed to c). · GA §22.4 p.243 (pdf 260)

**Why it works:** The contrast between chart quantities and measured quantities becomes visual and unforgettable, preparing the ground for the horizon chapter.

**App idea:** Plunge dashboard with one scrubber controlling all three plots plus an animated radial track; mass slider converts to seconds and km.

### Initial conditions at infinity fix all constants (L-tilde = 0, E-tilde = 1), so the plunge four-velocity follows algebraically with no integration. · GA §22.4 p.242 (pdf 259)

**Why it works:** Demonstrates the power of conserved quantities: a relativistic trajectory problem reduces to reading off components.

**App idea:** Step-through derivation card where each click applies one fact (radial, at rest at infinity, normalization) and fills in one slot of u^mu.

### Consistently distinguishing coordinate components from what an observer measures, using vielbein factors that become one at infinity. · GA §22.3 p.241 (pdf 258)

**Why it works:** Addresses the single most common confusion in Schwarzschild physics and gives a concrete conversion rule.

**App idea:** Toggle on every plot between 'coordinate' and 'measured by static observer here', with the conversion factor displayed and animated as r changes.


## Gaps and pitfalls

- **The Killing-vector conservation rule is used without any justification, and the recipe is only a sufficient condition in the chosen coordinates.** — Learners may treat it as magic, misapply it to non-geodesic motion, or conclude a metric has no symmetry when a coordinate change would reveal one. *Suggestion:* Give a two-line reason: lower the geodesic equation, note du_alpha/dtau = (1/2) partial_alpha g_{mu nu} u^mu u^nu, which vanishes when g is independent of x^alpha. Point to Chapter 33 for the covariant version.
- **Footnote 10 says particles coming in from infinity have E-tilde > 1, yet Section 22.4 treats a particle from rest at infinity with E-tilde = 1 and zero effective energy.** — Apparent contradiction about the marginally bound case. *Suggestion:* State E-tilde >= 1 for particles that reach infinity, with equality for release from rest there; E-tilde < 1 means bound.
- **Eqn 22.26 is written as an equality, but multiplying (E-tilde^2 - 1)/2 by mc^2 gives E_N + E_N^2/(2mc^2); the quadratic term is dropped, and proper time is kept where a Newtonian reader expects coordinate time.** — Readers attempting Exercise 22.4 may fail to reproduce the exact equation. *Suggestion:* Label it a leading-order result in E_N/mc^2 and note dtau ~ dt at that order.
- **Fig. 22.3 labels its axes with a vector r and |r|, and the barrier appears much taller than the well; for L-tilde/M = 4.3 the actual barrier height is only about +0.04 versus a well depth of about -0.03.** — Encourages Euclidean reading of the Schwarzschild r and misjudges which energies are captured. *Suggestion:* Plot to scale in units of M, label the axis r (areal coordinate), and mark the energy of a particle at rest at infinity (E_eff = 0).
- **Footnote 14 chooses the falling frame's angular basis vectors with components (0,0,1,0) and (0,0,0,1), which are not unit vectors; orthonormality requires 1/r and 1/(r sin theta). The reading-copy OCR additionally labels both as phi-hat.** — Learners who check orthonormality get inconsistent results and doubt the method. *Suggestion:* Use (e_theta-hat)^mu = (0,0,1/r,0) and (e_phi-hat)^mu = (0,0,0,1/(r sin theta)); the radial vector (22.30) is correctly normalized.
- **The constant t_* in eqn 22.36 is described as the coordinate time at r = 0, but the formula has separate branches outside and inside 2M separated by a logarithmic divergence, so one constant cannot physically link them.** — Suggests Schwarzschild time is a continuous, meaningful clock through the horizon. *Suggestion:* Treat the exterior branch alone, with t_* an arbitrary offset, and state that Schwarzschild t is not a good coordinate across 2M (Chapters 25 and 27).
- **The step 'dr/dt equals the ratio of bold 1-forms dr/dt' in Example 22.5 is notationally loose; 1-forms cannot be divided.** — Confusion about what operation turns the vielbein 1-form relations into a speed. *Suggestion:* Say: apply both 1-forms to the particle's tangent vector (or displacement); the ratio of the resulting numbers gives dr-hat/dt-hat.
- **Fig. 22.7 calls the dashed curve a 'coordinate velocity' although it is the physically measured speed, and neither caption nor text states the energy used; the solid curve tends to about half the speed of light far away, so the example is an unbound plunge (E-tilde of roughly 1.1 by our reading).** — Blurs the very distinction the example is meant to teach, and learners may try to match it to the E-tilde = 1 plunge. *Suggestion:* Relabel as local speed measured by static observers and state the energy; also show the E-tilde = 1 case, where local speed is sqrt(2M/r) and coordinate speed peaks at r = 6M.
- **Example 22.5 switches from E-tilde to u_t without reminding the reader that u_t = -E-tilde.** — Momentary confusion about a new constant; sign worries. *Suggestion:* Write (u_t)^2 = E-tilde^2 explicitly and present the neat form (dr-hat/dt-hat)^2 = 1 - (1 - 2M/r)/E-tilde^2.
- **The plunge integrals run to r = 0 while the static-observer interpretation (and the Schwarzschild coordinates' timelike t) fails inside 2M; the chapter postpones all discussion.** — Learners may apply static-observer ideas or the redshift formula inside the horizon. *Suggestion:* Add an explicit flag: formulas involving static observers or 1 - 2M/r under a square root are valid only for r > 2M.
- **The photon's momentum is simply assumed tangent to the null geodesic, and the affine parameter normalization for light is not discussed.** — Unclear why xi.p rather than some rescaled quantity is conserved. *Suggestion:* Note that choosing lambda so that p = dx/dlambda makes p the tangent, and any affine rescaling only multiplies the conserved number by a constant.

## Tutor notes

- Open with: 'In Newtonian gravity, what two numbers make planetary orbits easy to solve, and why are they conserved?' Then ask what in the Schwarzschild metric could play the role of time-translation and rotation symmetry.
- Order of explanation: (1) scan the metric for missing coordinates, (2) define E-tilde and L-tilde, (3) normalization u.u = -1 or 0, (4) redshift as a quick win, (5) effective potential, (6) radial plunge and its three clocks. Resist starting from the geodesic equation.
- Before the redshift, check the learner can compute a measured energy as -p.u_obs in special relativity; if not, review that first, since the whole argument hinges on it.
- Check for understanding after 22.1: 'Which component of u is conserved if the metric did not depend on theta?' and 'Why is E-tilde defined with a minus sign?'
- When presenting V_eff, have the learner predict what happens to a particle with small angular momentum before showing the plot; then contrast the Newtonian infinite centrifugal wall with the finite relativistic barrier.
- Keep a running two-column table 'coordinate quantity' versus 'measured by someone', filling in dt/dtau, p^t, dr/dt against proper time, hbar omega, dr-hat/dt-hat.
- Common learner question: 'Does the astronaut ever cross the horizon?' Answer: yes, in finite proper time; the infinite t is a feature of the distant observer's coordinate labels. Promise the full story (tortoise and Kruskal coordinates) rather than overloading now.
- Common learner question: 'If dr/dt goes to zero, does the particle hover at 2M?' Walk through 22.40: the local static observer sees it pass at nearly c, and static observers themselves become impossible at 2M.
- Use concrete numbers to anchor scales: solar redshift about 2 parts per million, neutron-star surface z about 0.3, horizon-to-centre fall of about 6.6 microseconds per solar mass (tutor-computed estimates, not from the book).
- For advanced learners, show the two-line proof that u_alpha is conserved when the metric is independent of x^alpha, and mention that Schwarzschild has two more rotational Killing vectors not visible as missing coordinates.
- For novices, skip the falling-frame vielbein (22.30) and the 1-form language in Example 22.5; state the conversion factors for static observers as 'clock rate factor' and 'ruler factor' instead.
- Point out the minor errata when they come up: E-tilde >= 1 (not > 1) for particles from infinity, eqn 22.26 holds to leading order, and footnote 14's angular basis vectors need 1/r factors.
