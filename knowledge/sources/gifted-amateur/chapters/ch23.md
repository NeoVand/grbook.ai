# GA ch23 · Orbits in the Schwarzschild geometry

> Extends the Schwarzschild motion analysis from radial plunges to orbits with angular momentum: proves motion stays in a plane, dresses the radial equation as a Newtonian-looking effective potential with one extra -M L^2/r^3 term, reads off circular, scattering, capture and precessing orbits (including the 6M innermost stable orbit and the L/M = 4 capture threshold), shows Kepler's third law survives in coordinate time, derives the orbit equation dr/dphi, perturbs it about a Newtonian ellipse to get a perihelion advance of 6 pi M/r0 per orbit, and applies it to Mercury's 43 arcsecond per century anomaly.

**Pages:** printed 246–253 · pdf 263–270 · **Difficulty:** math 3/5, conceptual 3/5, novice-friendliness 3/5

The effective-potential half (§23.1-23.2) is gentle and graphical for anyone who has done Chapter 20. The perihelion derivation in Example 23.5 compresses a completing-the-square and coefficient-matching step into 'massaged', and several printed slips (2M^2 for 12M^2 in 23.6, r_+^2 for r_+ in 23.9, epsilon for epsilon^2 before 23.21, '=' for '+' in 23.22, E-tilde for E-tilde^2 in 23.32) will stall careful readers. The physical interpretation (space versus time curvature) is only hinted at in the text and needs the two harder exercises.

## Role in the book

Third of the Part IV chapters on the Schwarzschild exterior. Chapter 20 built Newtonian orbits (effective potential, u = 1/r, eccentricity), Chapter 21 produced the Schwarzschild metric, and Chapter 22 supplied the conserved quantities E-tilde and L-tilde, the effective-potential form of the radial equation, and the L = 0 radial plunge. This chapter switches angular momentum on and delivers the first classical solar-system test of the theory, perihelion precession. It sets up Chapter 24, which repeats the effective-potential method for photons (light bending, photon sphere, the unstable circular orbit flagged here), and Chapter 25 on black holes, where the capture orbits found here no longer end on a stellar surface. The exercises also seed the idea, returned to repeatedly in the book, that the rubber-sheet picture misses the time part of curvature.

## Learning objectives

- Reader can show from the theta geodesic equation that an orbit starting with theta = pi/2 and zero polar velocity stays in the equatorial plane, and link this to angular momentum conservation.
- Reader can write the Schwarzschild radial equation as effective energy (E-tilde^2 - 1)/2 = (dr/dtau)^2/2 + V_eff and identify which term in V_eff has no Newtonian counterpart.
- Reader can locate the extrema r_+ (stable, outer) and r_- (unstable, inner) of V_eff and explain how their existence depends only on L-tilde/M.
- Reader can explain why stable circular orbits require L-tilde/M >= sqrt(12) and do not exist inside r = 6M.
- Reader can show that at L-tilde/M = 4 the potential peak sits at r = 4M with V_eff = 0, and use this to sort incoming unbound particles into scattered and captured.
- Reader can classify orbits (circular, bound precessing, scattering, capture/spiral-in) by drawing an energy line across a sketch of V_eff.
- Reader can combine the circular-orbit conditions to obtain L-tilde^2/r^2 = M/(r - 3M), E-tilde^2 and L-tilde/E-tilde, and deduce that (dphi/dt)^2 = M/r^3 exactly as in Kepler's third law.
- Reader can derive the orbit equation dr/dphi from u.u = -1 and the two conserved quantities without solving the full geodesic equations.
- Reader can rewrite the orbit equation in u = M/r as a Newtonian equation plus relativistic correction terms and justify keeping only the leading one.
- Reader can obtain the perihelion advance delta phi = 6 pi M/r0 per orbit by rescaling the angle so the perturbed equation becomes a harmonic oscillator.
- Reader can restore G and c and estimate Mercury's relativistic perihelion shift (about 41-43 arcseconds per century) and place it in the budget of the total observed precession.
- Reader can explain that the precession comes partly from spatial curvature (about one third) and mostly from the g_tt part of the metric (about two thirds), and why this undercuts the rubber-sheet picture.

## Teaching approach

A compact, Newtonian-parallel chapter. Every relativistic formula is deliberately written to look like its Chapter 20 counterpart so that the learner only has to track the differences: one extra attractive r^-3 term in the effective potential, and extra correction terms in the u-equation for the orbit. Orbit types are taught graphically with side-by-side panels pairing a potential curve plus energy line with the resulting trajectory. Formal Example boxes carry the algebra (plane confinement, circular-orbit relations, the massaged orbit equation, the Mercury numbers). The perihelion result is obtained by an order-of-magnitude argument that discards the smaller correction and then a variable rescaling that turns precession into a mismatch between the radial period and 2 pi. The physical interpretation (space curvature versus time curvature) is only sketched in the text and farmed out to two guided exercises, one using Flamm's paraboloid and a cone-wedge picture, the other a toy metric with only g_tt modified.

**Style:** physical-motivation-first, derivation-heavy, example-driven, diagram-driven, conversational-informal, observational-astrophysics, computational-recipe

**Narrative arc**

1. Motivation: Newtonian ellipses were a triumph, but GR's first success was improving on them; relax the L-tilde = 0 restriction of Chapter 22 to allow angular motion.
2. Simplify: prove from the theta equation that orbits can be taken in the equatorial plane without loss of generality (Example 23.1).
3. Model: reuse the effective-energy form of the radial equation and the Schwarzschild V_eff; point to the -M L^2/r^3 term as the single new ingredient.
4. Analyse the potential: find the two extrema r_+ and r_- from V_eff' = 0 and show everything depends on L-tilde/M (Fig. 23.1).
5. Classify motion graphically: circular, scattered and captured trajectories paired with their energy lines (Example 23.2, Fig. 23.2); contrast with the Newtonian infinite centrifugal wall.
6. Read off thresholds: L-tilde/M = sqrt(12) gives an inflection and the smallest stable circular orbit at 6M; L-tilde/M = 4 puts the peak at V_eff = 0 so marginally unbound particles cannot bounce.
7. Circular orbits quantitatively: derive L-tilde^2/r^2, E-tilde^2 and L-tilde/E-tilde (Example 23.3), then show Kepler's third law holds unchanged in coordinate time.
8. Formalism for general orbits: from u.u = -1 and the conserved quantities get dr/dtau, then divide by dphi/dtau to get the orbit equation dr/dphi (23.15), with the Newtonian version in a margin note.
9. Check against Newton: in u = M/r, massage the orbit equation into Newtonian form plus two correction terms and compare their sizes (Example 23.5).
10. Solve: keep the leading correction, rescale the angle, and read off that one radial period takes more than 2 pi of azimuth, giving delta phi/2 pi = 3M/r0 (Fig. 23.4).
11. Application: restore constants for Mercury, compare with the 43 arcsecond anomaly and the full 575 arcsecond budget; history of failed Newtonian fixes and Einstein's reaction (Example 23.6).
12. Interpretation: precession arises partly from spatial curvature shortening the orbit and mainly from curvature in the time part, so it is a spacetime effect; exercises quantify the one-third/two-thirds split.

**Signature moves**

- Newtonian mirror: equations are arranged so that each relativistic formula sits beside its Newtonian analogue (margin notes 2 and 8), and the learner hunts for the extra terms.
- Paired visual panels: every orbit type is shown as a V_eff curve with a horizontal energy line on the left and the corresponding orbit shape on the right.
- Threshold numbers read directly off one dimensionless ratio L-tilde/M (sqrt(12) and 4), rather than a zoo of cases.
- Avoids the second-order geodesic equations entirely: normalization of u plus two conserved quantities give dr/dtau and dphi/dtau, whose ratio is the orbit shape.
- Order-counting in powers of u0 and epsilon to justify discarding a correction term before solving.
- Rescaled angle psi = (1 - 6u0)^{1/2} phi, which turns the perturbed orbit into an exact oscillator and makes the precession a period mismatch.
- Full observational bookkeeping for Mercury (total precession, planetary part, solar oblateness, residual) plus the human story of the anomaly.
- Decomposes a relativistic effect into space-curvature and time-curvature parts using deliberately artificial metrics in the exercises, reinforcing the book's warning about rubber-sheet pictures.
- Ends with a programming exercise that asks learners to integrate the orbit equation numerically, including handling the sign flip at turning points.

## Section by section

### Chapter opening: from plunges to orbits — p.246 (pdf 263)

Opens with an epigraph from Hobbes about a leap into the dark and recalls that Newtonian orbit theory was a crowning success, while one of GR's earliest wins was predicting orbits close to, but measurably different from, Kepler ellipses. Orbits are described as periodic geodesics. The chapter will reuse the Schwarzschild line element and the conserved quantities E-tilde and L-tilde from Chapter 22, dropping the earlier restriction to zero angular momentum, and will end with perihelion precession. A margin panel restates the line element and the definitions of E-tilde and L-tilde.

**Concepts:** Schwarzschild metric, conserved energy per unit mass, conserved angular momentum per unit mass, perihelion precession

**Key moves**
- Frame orbits as a special class of geodesics executing periodic motion
- Announce the relaxation of L-tilde = 0 from the radial plunge
- Recall the conserved quantities as the main tools

### §23.1 Orbits for massive particles — p.246 (pdf 263)

Massive-particle orbits are timelike geodesics parametrized by proper time. Example 23.1 uses the theta geodesic equation to show that equatorial motion with no initial polar velocity stays equatorial, the relativistic version of planar Kepler motion. The radial equation is written in effective-energy form with E = (E-tilde^2 - 1)/2, and the Schwarzschild effective potential is quoted from Example 22.1: Newtonian gravity and centrifugal terms plus an extra attractive -M L^2/r^3 term that matters most for large M, large L and small r. Its extrema r_+ (a Newton-like minimum) and r_- (a maximum with no Newtonian analogue) follow from V_eff' = 0 and depend only on L-tilde/M (Fig. 23.1). Example 23.2 and Fig. 23.2 show a circular orbit at the minimum, an incoming particle bouncing off the peak, and a higher-energy particle clearing the peak and spiralling inward, which cannot happen in Newtonian gravity where the centrifugal wall is infinitely high. For L-tilde/M below sqrt(12) there is only an inflection and no stable orbit; for L-tilde/M = 4 the peak is at r = 4M with V_eff = 0, so particles arriving from infinity cannot be scattered unless L-tilde exceeds 4M.

**Concepts:** plane confinement of Schwarzschild orbits, effective energy variable, Schwarzschild effective potential, relativistic r^-3 term in the effective potential, extrema of the effective potential, scattering orbit, capture orbit, critical angular momentum L = 4M

**Key moves**
- Theta equation d/dtau(r^2 dtheta/dtau) = r^2 sin theta cos theta (dphi/dtau)^2 is solved by theta = pi/2 for all tau
- Radial equation in Newtonian form: E = (1/2)(dr/dtau)^2 + V_eff(r)
- Identify -M L^2/r^3 as the sole new term and when it is large
- Solve V_eff'(r) = 0 as a quadratic to get r_+/- = (L^2/2M)[1 +/- (1 - 12M^2/L^2)^{1/2}]
- Classify motion with an energy line: where E = V_eff the radial velocity vanishes but the particle still moves in phi
- Contrast: Newtonian barrier deflects every particle with L != 0; Schwarzschild barrier has finite height so energetic particles are captured
- Threshold cases L/M = sqrt(12) (inflection) and L/M = 4 (peak at 4M touches V_eff = 0)

### §23.2 Stable circular orbits — p.248 (pdf 265)

Stable circular orbits sit at the potential minimum r_+, which exists only for L-tilde/M >= sqrt(12); at that limiting value r_+ = 6M, the smallest radius for a stable circular orbit. A margin note adds that the potential maximum is an unstable circular orbit, previewing the photon case in Chapter 24. Example 23.3 sets dr/dtau = 0 and E = V_eff to get E-tilde^2 = (1 - 2M/r)(1 + L^2/r^2), combines it with the extremum condition to find L-tilde^2/r^2 = M/(r - 3M), E-tilde^2 = (1 - 2M/r)(r - 2M)/(r - 3M), and a formula for (L/E)^2. Dividing dphi/dtau by dt/dtau then gives dphi/dt in terms of L/E, and substituting yields (dphi/dt)^2 = M/r^3, so Kepler's third law for circular orbits is unchanged when time is Schwarzschild coordinate time.

**Concepts:** stable circular orbit, innermost stable circular orbit, unstable circular orbit, energy and angular momentum of circular Schwarzschild orbits, Kepler's third law in Schwarzschild spacetime

**Key moves**
- Circular orbit = minimum of V_eff with E equal to V_eff(r_+)
- Smallest stable radius 6M at L-tilde/M = sqrt(12)
- Eliminate L between E = V_eff and V_eff' = 0 to get L^2/r^2 = M/(r - 3M)
- Compute E-tilde^2 and (L/E)^2 (printed 23.6 and 23.9 contain slips; see gaps)
- Angular velocity as ratio dphi/dt = (dphi/dtau)/(dt/dtau) = (1/r^2)(1 - 2M/r)(L/E)
- Result (dphi/dt)^2 = M/r^3 identical to Newton-Kepler

### §23.3 Precession of the perihelion — p.249 (pdf 266)

Introduces perihelion precession as one of the three classical solar-system tests (with light bending and gravitational redshift; Shapiro delay as a fourth). Example 23.4 and Fig. 23.3 show a bound particle oscillating between two turning points in V_eff while its near-elliptical orbit rotates, forming a rosette. The orbit equation is built without the full geodesic equations: u.u = -1 with the inverse metric and conserved quantities gives (dr/dtau)^2, eqn 23.14; dphi/dtau = L/r^2; their ratio gives dr/dphi, eqn 23.15, the master equation for Schwarzschild paths, printed beside the Newtonian version. Example 23.5 moves to u = M/r, rewrites the equation as the Newtonian circle-offset form ((u')^2 + (u - u0)^2 - epsilon^2 u0^2) plus two relativistic terms 6u0(u - u0)^2 and 2(u - u0)^3, identifies u0 with the circular-orbit radius of eqn 23.4, and argues the cubic term is smaller by a factor epsilon. Keeping the quadratic correction, the substitution psi = (1 - 6u0)^{1/2} phi makes the radial motion periodic with period 2 pi in psi, so one radial cycle spans Delta phi = 2 pi (1 - 6M/r0)^{-1/2}, about 2 pi (1 + 3M/r0), i.e. delta phi = 6 pi M/r0 per orbit (Fig. 23.4). Example 23.6 restores G and c for Mercury: about 4.8 x 10^-7 rad per orbit, about 41 arcsec per century, versus a more careful GR value of 43.0 and an observed residual of 43.1 +/- 0.5 out of about 575 total, 532 from other planets and 0.03 from solar oblateness. The closing paragraph attributes precession partly to spatial curvature shortening the orbital path and mainly to curvature of the time part, a genuine spacetime effect; a margin note warns that rubber-sheet models show only the spatial half.

**Concepts:** classical solar-system tests of general relativity, bound precessing orbit, Schwarzschild orbit equation, Binet substitution u = 1/r, near-Newtonian expansion of the orbit equation, angle rescaling for the perturbed orbit, perihelion precession, anomalous perihelion precession of Mercury, space and time contributions to perihelion precession, rubber-sheet model

**Key moves**
- Use u.u = -1 plus conserved E-tilde and L-tilde instead of the full geodesic equations
- Lower/raise indices: g^{rr}(u_r)^2 = g_{rr}(u^r)^2 for a diagonal metric
- Solve for (dr/dtau)^2 (23.13-23.14) and divide by dphi/dtau = L/r^2 to get dr/dphi (23.15)
- Substitute u = M/r to get (du/dphi)^2 as a cubic in u (23.17)
- Complete the square about u0 to expose Newtonian form plus corrections (23.18-23.21), matching 6u0 to eqn 23.4
- Order counting: 6u0(u-u0)^2 ~ u0^3 epsilon^2 kept, 2(u-u0)^3 ~ u0^3 epsilon^3 dropped
- Rescale psi = (1 - 6u0)^{1/2} phi so the equation is an oscillator in psi with period 2 pi
- Delta phi = 2 pi/(1 - 6M/r0)^{1/2} ~ 2 pi(1 + 3M/r0), so delta phi = 6 pi M/r0
- Restore constants: delta phi = 6 pi G M_sun/(c^2 r0) for Mercury and convert to arcseconds per century
- Physical reading: spatial curvature plus (dominant) temporal curvature

### Chapter summary and exercises — p.252 (pdf 269)

The two-bullet summary stresses that orbits follow from E-tilde, L-tilde conservation and u^2 = -1, and that perihelion precession is a classical test passed with excellent agreement. Seven exercises follow: proper orbital period of a circular orbit two ways; a circular orbit in an exponentially expanding de Sitter-type metric; t versus tau for a particle held at fixed r; the acceleration of a prescribed circular world line and when it vanishes; isolating the spatial-curvature share of precession via Flamm's paraboloid and a cone-wedge argument (delta about 2 pi M/r); isolating the g_tt share with a flat-space, curved-time metric (two thirds of the total); and a numerical trajectory integrator for L-tilde/M = 4.3.

**Key moves**
- Summary reduces the chapter to conserved quantities plus normalization
- Exercises 23.5 and 23.6 split the precession into a one-third spatial and a two-thirds temporal share
- Exercise 23.7 turns eqn 23.15 into a computational lab

## Concepts

### Schwarzschild metric

*solution-or-model · revisited* · also: Schwarzschild line element

The unique static, spherically symmetric vacuum solution of Einstein's equations outside a mass M; in Schwarzschild coordinates and geometric units ds^2 = -(1 - 2M/r)dt^2 + (1 - 2M/r)^{-1}dr^2 + r^2 dOmega^2.

**How introduced:** Not rederived; restated in a margin panel at the chapter start as the geometry in which all orbits here are computed. Its inverse components are listed in a margin note for the u.u = -1 calculation.

**Prerequisites:** metric, geometrized units

$$
ds^2=-\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)dt^2+\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)^{-1}dr^2+r^2(d\theta^2+\sin^2\theta\,d\phi^2)
$$

**Where:** GA p.246 (pdf 263); GA §23.3 p.249 (pdf 266)

### conserved energy per unit mass

*physical-quantity · revisited* · also: E-tilde, specific energy, Killing energy

E-tilde = (1 - 2M/r) dt/dtau, the constant of geodesic motion associated with time-translation symmetry of Schwarzschild spacetime; it equals the particle's energy per unit rest mass measured by a static observer at infinity, so E-tilde > 1 for particles that can reach infinity with nonzero speed.

**How introduced:** Imported from Chapter 22 via the opening margin panel; used to set the energy line in effective-potential plots and to eliminate dt/dtau from the normalization condition.

**Prerequisites:** Schwarzschild metric, four-velocity

$$
\tilde E=\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)u^t
$$

**Where:** GA p.246 (pdf 263); GA §23.1 p.247 (pdf 264); GA §23.3 p.249 (pdf 266)

### conserved angular momentum per unit mass

*physical-quantity · developed* · also: L-tilde, specific angular momentum

L-tilde = r^2 sin^2(theta) dphi/dtau, the constant of geodesic motion associated with rotational symmetry about the polar axis; in the equatorial plane L-tilde = r^2 dphi/dtau.

**How introduced:** Imported from Chapter 22; the chapter's novelty is allowing L-tilde to be nonzero. Its ratio to M controls the whole shape of the effective potential.

**Prerequisites:** Schwarzschild metric, four-velocity

$$
\tilde L=r^2\sin^2\theta\,u^\phi
$$

$$
\frac{d\phi}{d\tau}=\frac{\tilde L}{r^2}
$$

**Where:** GA p.246 (pdf 263); GA §23.1 p.247 (pdf 264); GA §23.3 p.250 (pdf 267)

### four-velocity normalization

*identity · core* · also: velocity identity, u.u = -1

For a massive particle parametrized by proper time, g_{mu nu} u^mu u^nu = -1; this first integral of the geodesic equation replaces the radial second-order geodesic equation when conserved quantities are available.

**How introduced:** Used as the first line of the orbit-equation derivation, written with the inverse metric and lowered components u_t = -E-tilde, u_phi = L-tilde, then solved for (u^r)^2.

**Prerequisites:** four-velocity, metric, index raising and lowering

$$
g^{tt}(u_t)^2+g^{rr}(u_r)^2+g^{\phi\phi}(u_\phi)^2=-1
$$

**Where:** GA §23.3 p.249 (pdf 266); GA p.252 (pdf 269)

### plane confinement of Schwarzschild orbits

*theorem · developed* · also: equatorial-plane reduction, planar orbits

A free particle in Schwarzschild spacetime moves in a plane through the centre; choosing coordinates so the initial position and velocity lie in theta = pi/2, the theta geodesic equation keeps theta = pi/2 for all proper time.

**How introduced:** Proved in Example 23.1 from the theta Euler-Lagrange equation: at theta = pi/2 the source term sin theta cos theta vanishes, so r^2 dtheta/dtau stays at its initial value zero. Linked to angular momentum conservation, as in the Newtonian case.

**Prerequisites:** geodesic equation, spherical symmetry

$$
\frac{d}{d\tau}\left(r^2\frac{d\theta}{d\tau}\right)=r^2\sin\theta\cos\theta\left(\frac{d\phi}{d\tau}\right)^2
$$

**Where:** GA §23.1 p.246 (pdf 263)

### effective energy variable

*definition · developed* · also: script E, (E-tilde^2 - 1)/2

The combination (E-tilde^2 - 1)/2, chosen so that the exact Schwarzschild radial equation takes the Newtonian form of kinetic plus potential energy per unit mass; it is negative for bound motion and positive for particles that reach infinity.

**How introduced:** Carried over from Chapter 22 so that energy lines can be drawn on V_eff plots exactly as in Chapter 20; margin note 3 explains the sign for particles from infinity.

**Prerequisites:** conserved energy per unit mass

$$
\mathcal{E}=\frac{\tilde E^2-1}{2}
$$

$$
\mathcal{E}=\frac12\left(\frac{dr}{d\tau}\right)^2+V_{\rm eff}(r)
$$

**Notes:** It is not the kinetic plus potential energy of the particle; the Newtonian appearance is a bookkeeping device valid in proper time.

**Where:** GA §23.1 p.247 (pdf 264)

### Schwarzschild effective potential

*mathematical-object · core* · also: relativistic effective potential, V_eff

The function V_eff(r) = -M/r + L^2/(2r^2) - M L^2/r^3 (per unit mass, geometric units) that governs radial motion in proper time for a massive particle with specific angular momentum L-tilde around a Schwarzschild mass.

**How introduced:** Quoted from Example 22.1 and deliberately compared with the Newtonian effective potential energy in margin note 2; plotted for several L-tilde/M in Fig. 23.1 and used as the stage for all orbit classification.

**Prerequisites:** effective potential energy, effective energy variable, conserved angular momentum per unit mass

$$
V_{\rm eff}(r)=-\frac{M}{r}+\frac{\tilde L^2}{2r^2}-\frac{M\tilde L^2}{r^3}
$$

**Where:** GA §23.1 p.247 (pdf 264); GA §23.1 p.248 (pdf 265); GA §23.3 p.249 (pdf 266)

### relativistic r^-3 term in the effective potential

*physical-quantity · core* · also: -M L^2/r^3 term, relativistic correction to the centrifugal barrier

The extra attractive term -M L-tilde^2/r^3 in the Schwarzschild effective potential. It dominates the centrifugal term at small r, so the angular momentum barrier has finite height and eventually turns over, creating a maximum, an innermost stable orbit, capture orbits and precession.

**How introduced:** Singled out right after the potential is written as the source of everything new; the authors note it grows with M and L-tilde and at small r.

**Prerequisites:** Schwarzschild effective potential, angular momentum barrier

$$
-\frac{M\tilde L^2}{r^3}
$$

**Notes:** In ordinary units the term is -GM L^2/(c^2 r^3); it is a correction of relative size roughly L^2/(c^2 r^2), i.e. (v/c)^2 for orbital speed.

**Where:** GA §23.1 p.247 (pdf 264)

### extrema of the effective potential

*mathematical-object · core* · also: r_+ and r_-, turning points of V_eff (book usage)

The radii where V_eff'(r) = 0: an outer minimum r_+ (stable circular orbit) and an inner maximum r_- (unstable circular orbit), given by r_+/- = (L^2/2M)[1 +/- (1 - 12M^2/L^2)^{1/2}]; they are real only if L-tilde/M >= sqrt(12) and merge at 6M when equality holds.

**How introduced:** Found by setting the derivative of V_eff to zero, which gives a quadratic in r; the dependence on L-tilde/M alone is emphasized as the relativistic echo of the Newtonian case.

**Prerequisites:** Schwarzschild effective potential, stability of equilibria

$$
r_\pm=\frac{\tilde L^2}{2M}\left[1\pm\left(1-\frac{12M^2}{\tilde L^2}\right)^{1/2}\right]
$$

**Notes:** The book calls these turning points of the potential; in orbit language 'turning points' normally means radii where the energy line meets V_eff.

**Where:** GA §23.1 p.247 (pdf 264); GA §23.1 p.248 (pdf 265)

### stable circular orbit

*solution-or-model · core* · also: circular orbit at the potential minimum

An orbit of constant r at the minimum r_+ of V_eff with effective energy equal to V_eff(r_+) (negative, hence bound); small radial perturbations oscillate about it.

**How introduced:** Shown first in Fig. 23.2(a) as a dot at the bottom of the potential well paired with a circle; quantified in Example 23.3.

**Prerequisites:** extrema of the effective potential, circular orbit

$$
\mathcal{E}=V_{\rm eff}(r_+)
$$

$$
\frac{\tilde L^2}{r^2}=\frac{M}{r-3M}
$$

**Where:** GA §23.1 p.247 (pdf 264); GA §23.2 p.248 (pdf 265)

### innermost stable circular orbit

*phenomenon · core* · also: ISCO, minimum radius for stable circular orbits, r = 6M

In Schwarzschild spacetime no stable circular orbit exists inside r = 6M; at r = 6M the potential minimum and maximum merge into an inflection, which happens for L-tilde/M = sqrt(12).

**How introduced:** Read off from eqn 23.4 and Fig. 23.1: for L-tilde/M below sqrt(12) = 3.464 there is only an inflection, not enough angular momentum to hold an orbit; the limiting minimum lies at 6M.

**Prerequisites:** extrema of the effective potential, stable circular orbit

$$
\tilde L/M=\sqrt{12}\;\Rightarrow\;r_+=r_-=6M
$$

**Notes:** The acronym ISCO is not used in the book. At 6M, E-tilde = sqrt(8/9), so a particle spiralling in through circular orbits radiates about 5.7 percent of its rest energy (our computation from eqn 23.8, not in the text).

**Where:** GA §23.1 p.248 (pdf 265); GA §23.2 p.248 (pdf 265)

### unstable circular orbit

*solution-or-model · introduced* · also: circular orbit at the potential maximum

A circular orbit at the potential maximum r_-; any small perturbation sends the particle either inward to capture or outward, so it is dynamically unstable. For massive particles it lies between 3M and 6M.

**How introduced:** Mentioned in margin note 5 as the counterpart of the minimum, with a pointer to the photon analogue (the photon sphere) in Chapter 24.

**Prerequisites:** extrema of the effective potential

**Notes:** The 3M lower bound is our addition: from L^2/r^2 = M/(r - 3M), circular orbits need r > 3M.

**Where:** GA §23.2 p.248 (pdf 265)

### scattering orbit

*solution-or-model · introduced* · also: hyperbolic-like unbound orbit, encounter with turning point

An unbound trajectory (effective energy > 0) whose energy line meets the inner potential barrier, so the particle comes in from infinity, reaches a minimum radius, and returns to infinity.

**How introduced:** Fig. 23.2(b) and Example 23.2: incoming particle hits the peak of V_eff and is sent back out; its orbit sketch loops around the centre.

**Prerequisites:** Schwarzschild effective potential, effective energy variable

**Where:** GA §23.1 p.247 (pdf 264)

### capture orbit

*phenomenon · developed* · also: spiral into the star, plunge orbit with angular momentum

A trajectory whose effective energy exceeds the maximum of V_eff (or for which no maximum exists), so the radius decreases through the barrier and the particle spirals into the central object; absent in Newtonian gravity for any nonzero angular momentum.

**How introduced:** Fig. 23.2(c) and Example 23.2, contrasted explicitly with the Newtonian infinite centrifugal barrier at small r.

**Prerequisites:** Schwarzschild effective potential, relativistic r^-3 term in the effective potential

**Notes:** The book assumes a star is present to crash into; for a black hole the particle crosses r = 2M (Chapter 25).

**Where:** GA §23.1 p.247 (pdf 264); GA §23.1 p.248 (pdf 265)

### critical angular momentum L = 4M

*phenomenon · developed* · also: marginal capture threshold, marginally bound orbit at r = 4M

For L-tilde = 4M the maximum of V_eff lies at r = 4M with height exactly zero, so particles arriving from infinity (effective energy > 0) cannot be turned back; only for L-tilde > 4M can some incoming particles be scattered.

**How introduced:** Worked from eqn 23.4 in a short paragraph of §23.1 and backed by margin note 4 (such particles spiral in).

**Prerequisites:** extrema of the effective potential, capture orbit, scattering orbit

$$
\tilde L=4M\;\Rightarrow\;r_-=4M,\;V_{\rm eff}(r_-)=0
$$

**Notes:** The threshold is energy dependent: more energetic particles need larger L-tilde to be scattered. The circular orbit at 4M with E-tilde = 1 is the marginally bound orbit (term not used in the book).

**Where:** GA §23.1 p.248 (pdf 265)

### energy and angular momentum of circular Schwarzschild orbits

*law · developed* · also: circular-orbit relations

For a circular geodesic at radius r: L-tilde^2 = M r^2/(r - 3M), E-tilde^2 = (1 - 2M/r)^2 r/(r - 3M), and (L-tilde/E-tilde)^2 = M r (1 - 2M/r)^{-2}.

**How introduced:** Example 23.3: impose dr/dtau = 0 and V_eff' = 0, eliminate between them.

**Prerequisites:** stable circular orbit, conserved energy per unit mass, conserved angular momentum per unit mass

$$
\tilde E^2=\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)\frac{r-2M}{r-3M}
$$

$$
\left(\frac{\tilde L}{\tilde E}\right)^2=Mr\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)^{-2}
$$

**Notes:** The printed eqn 23.9 has r_+^2 where r_+ is correct; see gaps.

**Where:** GA §23.2 p.248 (pdf 265)

### Kepler's third law in Schwarzschild spacetime

*law · developed* · also: relativistic Kepler's law

For circular geodesics in Schwarzschild spacetime the angular velocity with respect to Schwarzschild coordinate time obeys (dphi/dt)^2 = M/r^3, identical in form to the Newtonian result.

**How introduced:** Derived straight after Example 23.3 by dividing dphi/dtau by dt/dtau and inserting L/E; the authors state the outcome matter-of-factly as identical to Kepler's own law and conclude relativity leaves the third law for circular orbits untouched.

**Prerequisites:** energy and angular momentum of circular Schwarzschild orbits, Kepler's third law

$$
\left(\frac{d\phi}{dt}\right)^2=\frac{M}{r^3}
$$

**Notes:** The simplicity depends on using coordinate time t (clock at infinity) and the areal radius r; proper period and proper circumference differ (Exercise 23.1).

**Where:** GA §23.2 p.249 (pdf 266)

### bound precessing orbit

*solution-or-model · core* · also: rosette orbit, precessing near-ellipse

A bound orbit with effective energy between the potential minimum and the smaller of zero and the maximum, oscillating between periapsis and apoapsis radii while the azimuth advances by more than 2 pi per radial cycle, so the near-ellipse rotates.

**How introduced:** Example 23.4 and Fig. 23.3 (exaggerated precession) pair the two turning points on V_eff with the dotted boundary circles of a rosette.

**Prerequisites:** Schwarzschild effective potential, turning points

**Where:** GA §23.1 p.248 (pdf 265); GA §23.3 p.249 (pdf 266)

### Schwarzschild orbit equation

*law · core* · also: dr/dphi equation, trajectory equation 23.15

The first-order equation for the spatial shape of an equatorial geodesic, dr/dphi = +/-(r^2/L){[-1 + E^2(1 - 2M/r)^{-1} - L^2/r^2](1 - 2M/r)}^{1/2}, obtained from normalization and the conserved quantities.

**How introduced:** Derived in §23.3 by solving u.u = -1 for (dr/dtau)^2 and dividing by dphi/dtau = L/r^2; printed beside its Newtonian counterpart (23.16) and used to draw Figs. 23.2-23.3.

**Prerequisites:** four-velocity normalization, conserved energy per unit mass, conserved angular momentum per unit mass

$$
\frac{dr}{d\phi}=\pm\frac{r^2}{\tilde L}\left\{\left[-1+\tilde E^2\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)^{-1}-\frac{\tilde L^2}{r^2}\right]\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)\right\}^{1/2}
$$

**Notes:** The sign must be switched at each turning point when integrating numerically (Exercise 23.7).

**Where:** GA §23.3 p.249 (pdf 266); GA §23.3 p.250 (pdf 267)

### Binet substitution u = 1/r

*technique · revisited* · also: u = M/r scaled inverse radius

Replacing r by u = 1/r (or dimensionless u = M/r) turns orbit equations into polynomial equations in u, where Newtonian conic orbits become simple harmonic motion in phi.

**How introduced:** Recalled from Chapter 20 in margin note 8 and applied as u = M/r in Example 23.5; margin note 9 warns not to confuse it with the four-velocity u.

**Prerequisites:** Binet orbit equation

$$
\left(\frac{du}{d\phi}\right)^2=\frac{\tilde E^2-(1-2u)\left[1+(\tilde L/M)^2u^2\right]}{(\tilde L/M)^2}
$$

**Where:** GA §23.3 p.250 (pdf 267)

### near-Newtonian expansion of the orbit equation

*technique · developed* · also: relativistic corrections to the Newtonian orbit equation

Rewriting the relativistic (du/dphi)^2 equation around the circular-orbit value u0 as the Newtonian ellipse equation plus correction terms 6u0(u - u0)^2 and 2(u - u0)^3, then keeping only the dominant correction by comparing orders in u0 and eccentricity epsilon.

**How introduced:** Example 23.5: expand, match coefficients to fix u0 (which reproduces eqn 23.4) and E0, introduce epsilon by analogy with the Newtonian definition, and estimate term sizes.

**Prerequisites:** Binet substitution u = 1/r, eccentricity, conic-section solutions of the Kepler problem

$$
\left(\frac{du}{d\phi}\right)^2+(u-u_0)^2-\epsilon^2u_0^2=6u_0(u-u_0)^2+2(u-u_0)^3
$$

**Notes:** u0 is the relativistic circular-orbit value M/r_+, not the Newtonian GMm^2/L^2; epsilon plays the role of eccentricity only approximately.

**Where:** GA §23.3 p.250 (pdf 267); GA §23.3 p.251 (pdf 268)

### angle rescaling for the perturbed orbit

*technique · developed* · also: psi = (1 - 6u0)^{1/2} phi

Absorbing the correction factor into a new angle psi = (1 - 6u0)^{1/2} phi so that the linearized orbit equation becomes an exact oscillator with period 2 pi in psi; the physical azimuth per radial period is then 2 pi/(1 - 6u0)^{1/2}.

**How introduced:** Used immediately after Example 23.5 to solve eqn 23.22 and read off the precession.

**Prerequisites:** near-Newtonian expansion of the orbit equation, simple harmonic oscillator

$$
\psi=(1-6u_0)^{1/2}\phi
$$

$$
\left(\frac{d\mu}{d\psi}\right)^2+\mu^2=\frac{u_0^2\epsilon^2}{1-6u_0}
$$

**Where:** GA §23.3 p.251 (pdf 268)

### perihelion precession

*phenomenon · core* · also: periapsis advance, apsidal precession, precession of the perihelion

The rotation of a bound orbit's closest-approach point in the orbital plane; for a Schwarzschild orbit the perihelion advances by delta phi = 6 pi M/r0 per orbit to first order in M/r0, where r0 = 1/u0 in the chapter's notation is effectively the semi-latus rectum a(1 - e^2).

**How introduced:** Motivated as a classical test, visualized in Figs. 23.3-23.4, derived via the rescaled angle, then applied to Mercury.

**Prerequisites:** bound precessing orbit, angle rescaling for the perturbed orbit, orbital precession

$$
\Delta\phi=\frac{2\pi}{(1-6M/r_0)^{1/2}}\approx2\pi\left(1+\frac{3M}{r_0}\right)
$$

$$
\frac{\delta\phi}{2\pi}=\frac{3M}{r_0}
$$

$$
\delta\phi=\frac{6\pi GM}{c^2r_0}
$$

**Notes:** The identification of r0 with a(1 - e^2) is ours; the book plugs in Mercury's semi-major axis.

**Where:** GA §23.1 p.248 (pdf 265); GA §23.3 p.249 (pdf 266); GA §23.3 p.251 (pdf 268)

### anomalous perihelion precession of Mercury

*experiment-or-observation · core* · also: Mercury perihelion problem, 43 arcseconds per century

Mercury's perihelion advances about 575 arcseconds per century; Newtonian perturbations from the other planets account for about 532, solar oblateness for about 0.03, and the residual of about 43 arcseconds per century is explained by general relativity.

**How introduced:** Example 23.6 converts the Schwarzschild formula into numbers and lays out the observational budget; margin note 10 recounts the failed Newtonian fixes and Einstein's reaction.

**Prerequisites:** perihelion precession, geometrized units

$$
\delta\phi=6\pi\frac{GM_\odot}{c^2r_0}\approx4.8\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm{rad\ per\ orbit}
$$

**Where:** GA §23.3 p.251 (pdf 268)

### space and time contributions to perihelion precession

*phenomenon · introduced* · also: spatial versus temporal curvature in precession, one-third/two-thirds split

To first order, the g_rr (spatial curvature) part of the Schwarzschild metric supplies one third of the perihelion advance and the g_tt (time) part supplies two thirds, so the effect cannot be captured by spatial geometry alone.

**How introduced:** Stated qualitatively at the end of §23.3 (curved space leaves too little path length to close the orbit; the larger share comes from the time part), then quantified in Exercises 23.5 (spatial slice, delta about 2 pi M/r) and 23.6 (flat-space metric with curved g_tt, two thirds of the total).

**Prerequisites:** perihelion precession, Flamm's paraboloid, gravitational time dilation

$$
\delta_{\rm space}\approx\frac{2\pi M}{r}

$$
\delta_{\rm time}\approx\frac23\cdot\frac{6\pi M}{r}
$$

**Notes:** The split depends on the coordinates used; the invariant statement is the total 6 pi M/r0.

**Where:** GA §23.3 p.251 (pdf 268); GA p.252 (pdf 269); GA p.253 (pdf 270)

### eccentricity

*definition · revisited* · also: epsilon

The dimensionless parameter of a conic orbit u = u0(1 + epsilon cos phi); in the relativistic treatment epsilon is defined through epsilon^2 u0^2 = (M^2/L^2)(E^2 - E0^2), which reduces to the Newtonian eccentricity in the weak-field limit.

**How introduced:** Recalled from Chapter 20 in margin note 8 and redefined in Example 23.5 to make the relativistic equation mirror the Newtonian one.

**Prerequisites:** conic-section solutions of the Kepler problem

$$
u=u_0(1+\epsilon\cos\phi)

\epsilon^2u_0^2=\frac{M^2}{\tilde L^2}(\tilde E^2-\tilde E_0^2)

**Where:** GA §23.3 p.250 (pdf 267)

### effective potential energy

*mathematical-object · revisited* · also: Newtonian effective potential, U_eff

In Newtonian central-force motion, U_eff(r) = L^2/(2 m r^2) - GMm/r, the gravitational potential energy plus the centrifugal energy of the angular motion, which governs one-dimensional radial motion.

**How introduced:** Recalled in margin note 2 as the template the relativistic potential imitates.

**Prerequisites:** conservation of angular momentum

$$
U_{\rm eff}(r)=\frac{L^2}{2mr^2}-\frac{GMm}{r}

**Where:** GA §23.1 p.247 (pdf 264)

### angular momentum barrier

*phenomenon · developed* · also: centrifugal barrier

The repulsive L^2/(2r^2) contribution to an effective potential that prevents particles with angular momentum from reaching small r; in Newtonian gravity it grows without bound as r goes to zero, but in Schwarzschild spacetime it is overwhelmed by the -M L^2/r^3 term.

**How introduced:** Used implicitly in the contrast of Example 23.2: Newtonian particles with any angular momentum are always deflected, relativistic ones may not be.

**Prerequisites:** effective potential energy

**Where:** GA §23.1 p.247 (pdf 264)

### geometrized units

*convention · mention* · also: G = c = 1

Units in which G and c are set to 1, so mass M stands for the length GM/c^2 (about 1.5 km for the Sun) and time is measured in lengths.

**How introduced:** Used throughout; constants are restored only in Example 23.6 to compute Mercury's shift.

M\to\frac{GM}{c^2}

**Where:** GA §23.3 p.251 (pdf 268)

### de Sitter spacetime

*solution-or-model · mention* · also: exponentially expanding flat metric

Spacetime of a positive cosmological constant with no matter; in flat slicing ds^2 = -dt^2 + a(t)^2(dr^2 + r^2 dphi^2 + ...) with a(t) = e^{Ht}.

**How introduced:** Only appears in Exercise 23.2 as a setting for computing the proper period of a prescribed circular motion.

### four-acceleration of a non-geodesic world line

*physical-quantity · mention* · also: acceleration of a circular world line

The covariant derivative of four-velocity along a world line, a^mu = du^mu/dtau + Gamma^mu_{alpha beta} u^alpha u^beta; it vanishes exactly when the world line is a geodesic.

**How introduced:** Exercise 23.4 asks for it on a world line of fixed r and constant angular speed, and for the condition that makes it zero (the Keplerian angular speed).

## Key equations

### Schwarzschild line element (margin recap) · supporting · GA p.246 (pdf 263)

$$
ds^2=-\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)dt^2+\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)^{-1}dr^2+r^2\left(d\theta^2+\sin^2\theta\,d\phi^2\right)
$$

The exterior geometry of a spherical mass in which all orbits of the chapter move.

**Symbols:** M mass in length units; t, r, theta, phi Schwarzschild coordinates

### Conserved quantities (margin recap) · central · GA p.246 (pdf 263)

$$
\tilde E=\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)u^t,\qquad \tilde L=r^2\sin^2\theta\,u^\phi
$$

Energy and angular momentum per unit rest mass, constant along geodesics because the metric is independent of t and phi.

**Symbols:** u^t = dt/dtau, u^phi = dphi/dtau

### (23.1) Theta geodesic equation · supporting · GA §23.1 p.246 (pdf 263)

$$
\frac{d}{d\tau}\left(r^2\frac{d\theta}{d\tau}\right)=r^2\sin\theta\cos\theta\left(\frac{d\phi}{d\tau}\right)^2
$$

Euler-Lagrange equation for theta; at theta = pi/2 the right side vanishes, so an orbit that starts in the equatorial plane with no polar velocity stays there.

**Symbols:** tau proper time

### (23.2) Radial equation in effective-energy form · central · GA §23.1 p.247 (pdf 264)

$$
\mathcal{E}=\frac12\left(\frac{dr}{d\tau}\right)^2+V_{\rm eff}(r),\qquad \mathcal{E}=\frac{\tilde E^2-1}{2}
$$

Exact Schwarzschild radial motion written as one-dimensional Newtonian energy conservation in proper time.

**Symbols:** script E effective energy; V_eff effective potential

### (23.3) Schwarzschild effective potential · central · GA §23.1 p.247 (pdf 264)

$$
V_{\rm eff}(r)=-\frac{M}{r}+\frac{\tilde L^2}{2r^2}-\frac{M\tilde L^2}{r^3}
$$

Newtonian gravity plus centrifugal barrier plus a new attractive r^-3 term responsible for all novel orbital behaviour.

**Symbols:** L-tilde specific angular momentum

### Newtonian effective potential energy (margin note 2) · supporting · GA §23.1 p.247 (pdf 264)

$$
U_{\rm eff}(r)=\frac{L^2}{2mr^2}-\frac{GMm}{r}
$$

The Chapter 20 template for comparison; divide by m and set G = 1 to match the first two terms of 23.3.

**Symbols:** L angular momentum; m particle mass

### (23.4) Extrema of the effective potential · central · GA §23.1 p.247 (pdf 264)

$$
r_\pm=\frac{\tilde L^2}{2M}\left[1\pm\left(1-\frac{12M^2}{\tilde L^2}\right)^{1/2}\right]
$$

Radii of the stable (r_+, minimum) and unstable (r_-, maximum) circular orbits; real only for L-tilde/M >= sqrt(12).

**Symbols:** r_+ outer minimum; r_- inner maximum

### Threshold values of L-tilde/M · central · GA §23.1 p.248 (pdf 265)

$$
\frac{\tilde L}{M}=\sqrt{12}\Rightarrow r_+=r_-=6M;\qquad \frac{\tilde L}{M}=4\Rightarrow r_-=4M,\;V_{\rm eff}(r_-)=0
$$

Innermost stable circular orbit at 6M; for L = 4M the barrier top is exactly at zero effective energy, the capture threshold for particles from infinity.

### (23.5) Energy of a circular orbit · supporting · GA §23.2 p.248 (pdf 265)

$$
\tilde E^2=\left(1-\frac{2M}{r_+}\right)\left(1+\frac{\tilde L^2}{r_+^2}\right)
$$

Setting dr/dtau = 0 in 23.2 at the minimum. The printed version writes the first factor with plain r and the second with r_+; both mean the circular-orbit radius. With general r it holds at any radial turning point.

### (23.6) Squared extremum condition (corrected) · derivation-step · GA §23.2 p.248 (pdf 265)

$$
\left(2Mr_+-\tilde L^2\right)^2=\tilde L^4\left(1-\frac{12M^2}{\tilde L^2}\right)
$$

Eqn 23.4 rearranged and squared to eliminate the square root. The printed equation has 2M^2 in place of 12M^2, a typographical slip.

### (23.7) Angular momentum of a circular orbit · central · GA §23.2 p.248 (pdf 265)

$$
\frac{\tilde L^2}{r_+^2}=\frac{M}{r_+-3M}
$$

Specific angular momentum needed for a circular orbit at radius r_+; it diverges as r approaches 3M.

### (23.8) Energy of a circular orbit in terms of radius · central · GA §23.2 p.248 (pdf 265)

$$
\tilde E^2=\left(1-\frac{2M}{r_+}\right)\left(\frac{r_+-2M}{r_+-3M}\right)
$$

Energy per unit mass of a circular geodesic; at r = 6M it gives E-tilde^2 = 8/9.

### (23.9) Ratio L/E for a circular orbit (corrected) · derivation-step · GA §23.2 p.248 (pdf 265)

$$
\left(\frac{\tilde L}{\tilde E}\right)^2=Mr_+\left(1-\frac{2M}{r_+}\right)^{-2}
$$

Divide 23.7 by 23.8. The printed version has r_+^2, which is dimensionally inconsistent and would spoil 23.11; r_+ is correct.

### (23.10) Coordinate angular velocity · derivation-step · GA §23.2 p.249 (pdf 266)

$$
\frac{d\phi}{dt}=\frac{d\phi/d\tau}{dt/d\tau}=\frac{1}{r^2}\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)\frac{\tilde L}{\tilde E}

Angular speed as seen with Schwarzschild coordinate time, built from the conserved quantities.

### (23.11) Kepler's third law in Schwarzschild coordinates · central · GA §23.2 p.249 (pdf 266)

\left(\frac{d\phi}{dt}\right)^2=\frac{M}{r^3}
$$

Circular geodesics obey exactly the Newtonian period-radius relation when t and the areal radius r are used.

### Inverse Schwarzschild metric (margin) · supporting · GA §23.3 p.249 (pdf 266)

$$
g^{tt}=-\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)^{-1},\ g^{rr}=1-\frac{2M}{r},\ g^{\theta\theta}=r^{-2},\ g^{\phi\phi}=(r\sin\theta)^{-2}

Needed to write u.u = -1 in terms of the lowered components u_t and u_phi.

g^{tt}(u_t)^2+g^{\phi\phi}(u_\phi)^2+g^{rr}(u_r)^2=-1

Four-velocity normalization in the equatorial plane; u_t and u_phi are fixed by E-tilde and L-tilde.

\frac{dr}{d\tau}=\pm\left\{\left[-1+\tilde E^2\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)^{-1}-\frac{\tilde L^2}{r^2}\right]\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)\right\}^{1/2}

Radial proper-time velocity as a function of r for given conserved quantities; equivalent to 23.2-23.3.

### (23.15) Schwarzschild orbit equation · central · GA §23.3 p.250 (pdf 267)

\frac{dr}{d\phi}=\pm\frac{r^2}{\tilde L}\left\{\left[-1+\tilde E^2\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)^{-1}-\frac{\tilde L^2}{r^2}\right]\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)\right\}^{1/2}

### (23.16) Newtonian orbit equation (margin note 8) · supporting · GA §23.3 p.250 (pdf 267)

\frac{dr}{d\theta}=\pm\frac{r^2}{L}\left\{2m\left[E+\frac{GMm}{r}\right]-\frac{L^2}{r^2}\right\}^{1/2}

### Newtonian u-equation (margin note 8) · supporting · GA §23.3 p.250 (pdf 267)

$$
(u')^2+u^2-\frac{2GMm^2}{L^2}u-\frac{2Em}{L^2}=0\;\Longleftrightarrow\;(u')^2+(u-u_0)^2-u_0^2\epsilon^2=0,\quad u_0=\frac{GMm^2}{L^2},\ u_0(1-\epsilon^2)=-\frac{2Em}{L^2}
$$

Newtonian orbits in u = 1/r: a circle in the (u, u') plane centred at u0, giving the conic u = u0(1 + epsilon cos theta).

**Symbols:** u' = du/dtheta

### (23.17) Orbit equation in u = M/r · supporting · GA §23.3 p.250 (pdf 267)

$$
\left(\frac{du}{d\phi}\right)^2=\frac{\tilde E^2-(1-2u)\left[1+(\tilde L/M)^2u^2\right]}{(\tilde L/M)^2}
$$

Dimensionless form of 23.15; the right side is a cubic polynomial in u.

**Symbols:** u = M/r (dimensionless)

### (23.18) Orbit equation around u0 · derivation-step · GA §23.3 p.250 (pdf 267)

$$
\left(\frac{du}{d\phi}\right)^2+(u-u_0)^2=6u_0(u-u_0)^2+2(u-u_0)^3+\frac{M^2}{\tilde L^2}\left(\tilde E^2-\tilde E_0^2\right)
$$

The cubic rewritten as a Newtonian-looking left side plus terms that vanish in the Newtonian limit.

**Symbols:** u0 circular-orbit value of u; E0 its energy

### (23.19) Matching condition for u0 · derivation-step · GA §23.3 p.250 (pdf 267)

$$
6u_0=1\pm\left(1-\frac{12M^2}{\tilde L^2}\right)^{1/2}
$$

Coefficient matching reproduces the circular-orbit radii of 23.4, so u0 = M/r_+ (or M/r_-).

### (23.20) Energy of the reference circular orbit · derivation-step · GA §23.3 p.250 (pdf 267)

$$
\tilde E_0^2=(1-2u_0)\left(1+\frac{\tilde L^2}{M^2}u_0^2\right)
$$

Constant-term matching; identical to 23.5 at u0 = M/r_+. The printed left side drops the tilde.

### (23.21) Newtonian form plus relativistic corrections · central · GA §23.3 p.250 (pdf 267)

$$
\left(\frac{du}{d\phi}\right)^2+(u-u_0)^2-\epsilon^2u_0^2=6u_0(u-u_0)^2+2(u-u_0)^3,\qquad \epsilon^2u_0^2\equiv\frac{M^2}{\tilde L^2}\left(\tilde E^2-\tilde E_0^2\right)
$$

Left side is the Newtonian ellipse equation; the right side holds a leading correction of order u0^3 epsilon^2 and a smaller one of order u0^3 epsilon^3. The printed definition just above 23.21 reads epsilon u0^2, missing the square on epsilon.

### Newtonian solution used as zeroth order · supporting · GA §23.3 p.250 (pdf 267)

$$
u=u_0(1+\epsilon\cos\phi)
$$

Ellipse with the focus at the origin that the relativistic orbit only slightly perturbs.

### (23.22) Orbit equation with leading correction (corrected) · derivation-step · GA §23.3 p.251 (pdf 268)

$$
(u')^2+(1-6u_0)(u-u_0)^2=u_0^2\epsilon^2
$$

Keeping only the 6u0(u - u0)^2 correction turns the equation into an oscillator with a slightly reduced frequency. The printed version has '=' where '+' belongs.

### (23.23) Oscillator in the rescaled angle · derivation-step · GA §23.3 p.251 (pdf 268)

$$
\left(\frac{d\mu}{d\psi}\right)^2+\mu^2=\frac{u_0^2\epsilon^2}{1-6u_0},\qquad \psi=(1-6u_0)^{1/2}\phi,\ \mu=u-u_0
$$

Radial motion is exactly periodic with period 2 pi in psi. The book writes du/dpsi, which equals dmu/dpsi.

### (23.24) Azimuth per radial period · central · GA §23.3 p.251 (pdf 268)

$$
\Delta\phi=\frac{2\pi}{\left(1-\frac{6M}{r_0}\right)^{1/2}}\approx2\pi\left(1+\frac{3M}{r_0}\right)
$$

One perihelion-to-perihelion cycle sweeps slightly more than a full turn.

**Symbols:** r0 = M/u0

### (23.25) Perihelion advance per orbit · central · GA §23.3 p.251 (pdf 268)

$$
\frac{\delta\phi}{2\pi}=\frac{3M}{r_0}\quad(\delta\phi=6\pi M/r_0)
$$

Fractional excess angle per orbit; the central prediction of the chapter.

### (23.26) Mercury's perihelion shift · central · GA §23.3 p.251 (pdf 268)

$$
\delta\phi=6\pi\frac{GM_\odot}{c^2r_0}\approx4.8\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm{rad\ per\ orbit}
$$

With r0 = 5.8e10 m and M = 2e30 kg, about 2e-4 rad or 41 arcsec per century (43 with r0 replaced by the semi-latus rectum).

**Symbols:** M_sun solar mass; r0 orbital radius

### Proper period of a circular orbit (Exercise 23.1) · supporting · GA p.252 (pdf 269)

$$
\Delta\tau=2\pi r\left(\frac{r}{M}-3\right)^{1/2}
$$

Orbiting clock's time per revolution, from dphi/dtau = L/r^2 and 23.7.

### (23.27) Exponentially expanding metric (Exercise 23.2) · supporting · GA p.252 (pdf 269)

$$
ds^2=-dt^2+a(t)^2\left(dr^2+r^2d\phi^2\right),\quad a(t)=e^{t}
$$

Setting for a proper-period calculation in a de Sitter-type geometry.

### (23.28) Equatorial spatial slice (Exercise 23.5) · supporting · GA p.252 (pdf 269)

$$
ds^2=\frac{dr^2}{1-\frac{2M}{r}}+r^2d\phi^2
$$

Geometry of space alone at fixed t, isolating the g_rr contribution.

### (23.29) Flamm's paraboloid (Exercise 23.5) · supporting · GA p.252 (pdf 269)

$$
z(r)^2=8M(r-2M)
$$

Embedding surface in flat 3-space with the same intrinsic geometry as 23.28 (printed with a +/- sign on the right).

**Symbols:** z height in cylindrical coordinates

### (23.30) Spatial-curvature wedge angle (Exercise 23.5) · supporting · GA p.253 (pdf 270)

$$
\delta\approx\frac{2\pi M}{r}
$$

Angle removed from a flat disc to match the true circumference; one third of the full 6 pi M/r advance.

### (23.31) Flat-space curved-time metric (Exercise 23.6) · supporting · GA p.253 (pdf 270)

$$
ds^2=-\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)dt^2+dr^2+r^2\left(d\theta^2+\sin^2\theta\,d\phi^2\right)
$$

Toy spacetime keeping only the g_tt deformation, used to isolate the time contribution to precession.

### (23.32) Orbit equation in the toy metric (corrected) · supporting · GA p.253 (pdf 270)

$$
\frac{d^2u}{d\phi^2}+u=\frac{M\tilde E^2}{\tilde L^2(1-2Mu)^2}
$$

Second-order orbit equation for 23.31 with u = 1/r. The printed right side has E-tilde to the first power; differentiating the normalization gives E-tilde squared, consistent with 23.33.

### (23.33) Circular orbit in the toy metric (Exercise 23.6) · supporting · GA p.253 (pdf 270)

$$
\left[1-4\left(\frac{M\tilde E}{\tilde L}\right)^2\right]u_c\approx\frac{M\tilde E^2}{\tilde L^2}
$$

Weak-field expansion of 23.32 for a circular orbit; perturbing about it yields two thirds of the Schwarzschild precession.

**Symbols:** u_c = 1/r_c

## Worked examples

### Example 23.1 · intro · GA §23.1 p.246 (pdf 263)

**Problem:** Justify restricting Schwarzschild orbits to the equatorial plane.

**Method:** Vary the line element with respect to theta to get the theta geodesic equation, note that theta = pi/2 makes its source term vanish, so r^2 dtheta/dtau keeps its initial zero value; rotate coordinates so any orbit starts this way and use conservation of L-tilde along the whole geodesic.

**Key insight:** Spherical symmetry lets us choose the orbital plane as the equator; planarity is the relativistic shadow of angular momentum conservation.

**Result:** theta = pi/2 and dL-tilde/dtau = 0 for all proper time.

**Concepts:** plane confinement of Schwarzschild orbits, conserved angular momentum per unit mass

### Example 23.2 · intro · GA §23.1 p.247 (pdf 264)

**Problem:** Interpret three sample trajectories computed for one choice of M and nonzero L-tilde in terms of the effective potential.

**Method:** Overlay the particle's effective energy on V_eff: at the well minimum the orbit is circular; an energy line above zero that hits the barrier gives a scattering orbit; an energy line above the barrier top gives a capture spiral. Compare with the Newtonian potential whose centrifugal wall is infinite.

**Key insight:** Where the energy line meets V_eff only the radial velocity vanishes; the finite barrier height is what allows relativistic capture of particles with angular momentum.

**Result:** Three orbit classes: circular (negative energy), scattered (positive energy below peak), captured (energy above peak).

**Concepts:** stable circular orbit, scattering orbit, capture orbit, Schwarzschild effective potential

### Example 23.3 · standard · GA §23.2 p.248 (pdf 265)

**Problem:** Find the energy, angular momentum and their ratio for a circular geodesic of radius r_+.

**Method:** Set dr/dtau = 0 so E = V_eff(r_+), giving E-tilde^2 = (1 - 2M/r)(1 + L^2/r^2); square the extremum condition 23.4 and rearrange to L^2/r^2 = M/(r - 3M); substitute to get E-tilde^2, then divide to get (L/E)^2.

**Key insight:** Circular orbits need infinite angular momentum as r approaches 3M, and the L/E ratio is exactly what is needed for the coordinate angular velocity.

**Result:** L^2/r^2 = M/(r - 3M); E^2 = (1 - 2M/r)(r - 2M)/(r - 3M); (L/E)^2 = M r (1 - 2M/r)^{-2} (printed with r^2 and 2M^2 slips in 23.9 and 23.6).

**Concepts:** energy and angular momentum of circular Schwarzschild orbits, stable circular orbit

### Example · intro · GA §23.2 p.249 (pdf 266)

**Problem:** Show that Kepler's third law for circular orbits survives in Schwarzschild spacetime.

**Method:** Write dphi/dt as (dphi/dtau)/(dt/dtau) using L-tilde = r^2 dphi/dtau and E-tilde = (1 - 2M/r) dt/dtau, then insert (L/E)^2 from Example 23.3.

**Key insight:** All relativistic factors cancel when time is Schwarzschild coordinate time.

**Result:** (dphi/dt)^2 = M/r^3.

**Concepts:** Kepler's third law in Schwarzschild spacetime

### Example 23.4 · intro · GA §23.3 p.249 (pdf 266)

**Problem:** Describe qualitatively a bound orbit whose energy lies inside the potential well.

**Method:** Mark the two intersections of the energy line with V_eff as the minimum and maximum radii and follow the particle oscillating between them while phi advances (Fig. 23.3).

**Key insight:** Bound Schwarzschild orbits are rosettes: near-ellipses whose axes rotate.

**Concepts:** bound precessing orbit, perihelion precession

### Example · standard · GA §23.3 p.249 (pdf 266)

**Problem:** Derive the equation for the shape r(phi) of an equatorial orbit without solving the second-order geodesic equations.

**Method:** Write u.u = -1 with inverse metric components and lowered u_t, u_phi fixed by E-tilde and L-tilde; use g^{rr}(u_r)^2 = g_{rr}(u^r)^2 to isolate (dr/dtau)^2; take the root and divide by dphi/dtau = L/r^2.

**Key insight:** Two conserved quantities plus normalization give a complete first-order description; the orbit shape is just the ratio of two proper-time rates.

**Result:** Eqn 23.15 for dr/dphi.

**Concepts:** four-velocity normalization, Schwarzschild orbit equation

### Example 23.5 · challenging · GA §23.3 p.250 (pdf 267)

**Problem:** Recast the Schwarzschild orbit equation so that it looks like the Newtonian ellipse equation plus small corrections.

**Method:** Substitute u = M/r to get a cubic for (du/dphi)^2; posit the form Newtonian circle-offset + 6u0(u - u0)^2 + 2(u - u0)^3 + constant; match powers of u to fix u0 (reproducing the circular-orbit radii) and E0; define epsilon via the leftover constant; estimate the correction sizes as u0^3 epsilon^2 and u0^3 epsilon^3.

**Key insight:** Relativity adds a quadratic term that changes the oscillation frequency and a cubic term that is smaller by a factor of epsilon; the quadratic one is what drives precession.

**Result:** Eqn 23.21 with 6u0 = 1 +/- (1 - 12M^2/L^2)^{1/2}.

**Concepts:** near-Newtonian expansion of the orbit equation, Binet substitution u = 1/r, eccentricity

### Example · standard · GA §23.3 p.251 (pdf 268)

**Problem:** Solve the leading-order perturbed orbit equation and extract the perihelion advance.

**Method:** Keep (u')^2 + (1 - 6u0)(u - u0)^2 = u0^2 epsilon^2; define psi = (1 - 6u0)^{1/2} phi and mu = u - u0 to obtain a harmonic oscillator of period 2 pi in psi; convert back to phi and expand the square root to first order in M/r0.

**Key insight:** Precession is a frequency mismatch: the radial oscillation is slightly slower than the azimuthal rotation, so perihelion returns after more than 2 pi.

**Result:** Delta phi = 2 pi (1 - 6M/r0)^{-1/2} ~ 2 pi (1 + 3M/r0); delta phi = 6 pi M/r0 per orbit.

**Concepts:** angle rescaling for the perturbed orbit, perihelion precession

### Example 23.6 · intro · GA §23.3 p.251 (pdf 268)

**Problem:** Estimate Mercury's relativistic perihelion shift and compare with observation.

**Method:** Restore G and c in 6 pi M/r0, insert M = 2e30 kg and r0 = 5.8e10 m, multiply by the number of 88-day orbits per century and convert radians to arcseconds; then set against the measured total precession and its Newtonian contributions.

**Key insight:** A tiny fractional effect (GM/c^2 r of order 1e-8) accumulates to a measurable 43 arcseconds per century that Newtonian models could not explain.

**Result:** About 4.8e-7 rad per orbit, 2e-4 rad or 41 arcsec per century; refined GR value 43.0; observed residual 43.1 +/- 0.5 out of about 575 total.

**Concepts:** anomalous perihelion precession of Mercury, geometrized units

### Example · intro · GA §23.1 p.248 (pdf 265)

**Problem:** Find the special angular momenta at which the potential changes character.

**Method:** Use eqn 23.4: the square root vanishes at L/M = sqrt(12), giving a single inflection at 6M; for L/M = 4 evaluate r_- = 4M and check V_eff(4M) = -1/4 + 1/2 - 1/4 = 0.

**Key insight:** One dimensionless number decides whether stable orbits exist and whether particles from infinity can be turned back.

**Result:** ISCO at 6M for L/M = sqrt(12); zero-height barrier at 4M for L/M = 4.

**Concepts:** innermost stable circular orbit, critical angular momentum L = 4M


## Analogies and intuitions

### A bead rolling along a one-dimensional hill-and-valley track (the effective potential) with an energy line as its water level → Schwarzschild effective potential · strong · GA §23.1 p.247 (pdf 264)

The radial motion is literally one-dimensional energy conservation, so circular orbits are the valley floor, turning points are where the level meets the track, and capture happens when the level tops the hill.

**Where it breaks down:** The bead picture hides the phi motion (the particle moves even at a turning point, which the book stresses) and the fact that script E is not a real kinetic plus potential energy; it also only describes proper-time dynamics.

**App idea:** Split view with a bead on the V_eff track and the actual orbit, synchronized in proper time.

### The Newtonian centrifugal wall versus a finite hill with a pit behind it → relativistic r^-3 term in the effective potential · useful · GA §23.1 p.247 (pdf 264)

In Newtonian gravity any angular momentum builds an infinitely tall wall near the centre; the extra relativistic term caps the wall at a finite height, so a fast enough particle rolls over it into the pit.

**Where it breaks down:** Height and position of the hill depend on L-tilde, so the picture must be redrawn for each particle.

**App idea:** Morph animation from the Newtonian to the Schwarzschild potential as a 'relativity dial' turns on the r^-3 term.

### A Spirograph rosette: an ellipse that is slowly rotated each time it is drawn → bound precessing orbit · strong · GA §23.3 p.251 (pdf 268)

Figs. 23.3 and 23.4 show near-ellipses whose axes turn, letting learners see precession as rotation of a familiar shape.

**Where it breaks down:** The real orbit is a single continuous curve, not a sequence of closed ellipses; the ellipse is only an osculating approximation.

**App idea:** Draw the true orbit and, on demand, the osculating ellipse at each perihelion passage.

### Two clocks that tick at slightly different rates: the radial oscillation and the angular sweep → angle rescaling for the perturbed orbit · useful · GA §23.3 p.251 (pdf 268)

The substitution psi = (1 - 6u0)^{1/2} phi says the radial motion completes a cycle at 2 pi in psi, i.e. a little after 2 pi in phi; the mismatch accumulates as precession.

**Where it breaks down:** Our framing of the book's rescaling; it works cleanly only at linear order in the eccentricity-sized perturbation.

**App idea:** Two dials, one for phi and one for radial phase, rotating side by side; their lag angle after each radial cycle is the precession.

### Making a cone from a paper disc by cutting out a wedge → space and time contributions to perihelion precession · strong · GA p.252 (pdf 269)

Exercise 23.5 approximates the curved spatial slice by a tangent cone; a flat-looking circle of radius R has too much circumference, so a wedge delta must be removed, and an orbit laid on the cone advances by that wedge each turn.

**Where it breaks down:** Captures only the spatial third of the effect and only approximately (the paraboloid is not a cone).

**App idea:** Paper-cone cutter: unroll the tangent cone to a sector, draw an ellipse, roll it back up and watch the perihelion move.

### Not enough path length to close the orbit → perihelion precession · useful · GA §23.3 p.251 (pdf 268)

Spatial curvature makes the path around the centre shorter than flat intuition predicts, so the particle 'runs out of room' and overshoots the Newtonian closing point.

**Where it breaks down:** The authors immediately add that the larger part comes from the time part of the metric, so this intuition alone gives the wrong magnitude (one third).

### The rubber-sheet model of gravity → rubber-sheet model · weak · GA §23.3 p.251 (pdf 268)

Invoked negatively: a stretched-sheet picture depicts only spatial curvature and misses the half of the story that controls slow particles.

**Where it breaks down:** Actively misleading for orbital precession and for why things fall at all.

**App idea:** Toggle between a 'space only' and 'space plus time' simulation of Mercury to show the sheet gives one third of the precession.


## Misconceptions addressed

### At a point where the energy line meets V_eff the particle is momentarily at rest. · GA §23.1 p.247 (pdf 264)

**Correction:** Only the radial velocity is zero there; with nonzero L-tilde the particle keeps moving in phi.

**Why tempting:** In the one-dimensional bead picture a turning point means zero speed.

### A particle with any angular momentum can never fall straight into the central mass. · GA §23.1 p.247 (pdf 264)

**Correction:** In Schwarzschild spacetime the centrifugal barrier has finite height; particles with energy above the peak spiral in, and for L-tilde < 4M no particle from infinity is turned back.

**Why tempting:** Newtonian intuition: the L^2/r^2 barrier grows without limit at small r.

### Stable circular orbits exist at every radius outside the central object, just as in Newtonian gravity. · GA §23.2 p.248 (pdf 265)

**Correction:** No stable circular orbit exists inside r = 6M; between 3M and 6M circular orbits are unstable and inside 3M none exist.

**Why tempting:** Newtonian circular orbits exist at every radius, and the potential looks Newtonian far away.

### General relativity must modify Kepler's third law for circular orbits. · GA §23.2 p.249 (pdf 266)

**Correction:** Using Schwarzschild coordinate time and the areal radius, (dphi/dt)^2 = M/r^3 exactly. The orbiting clock's proper period does differ.

**Why tempting:** Relativity changes most orbital formulas, so the learner expects correction factors here too.

### Assuming the orbit lies in the equatorial plane is a special case that loses generality. · GA §23.1 p.246 (pdf 263)

**Correction:** Spherical symmetry lets coordinates be rotated so any orbit starts equatorially, and the theta equation keeps it there.

**Why tempting:** Setting theta = pi/2 looks like an arbitrary simplifying choice.

### All of Mercury's roughly 575 arcsec per century perihelion advance is a relativistic effect. · GA §23.3 p.251 (pdf 268)

**Correction:** About 532 arcsec per century comes from other planets and a tiny amount from solar oblateness; only the residual 43 arcsec per century is relativistic.

**Why tempting:** Popular accounts mention Mercury's precession as proof of GR without the budget.

### Perihelion precession is caused by the curvature of space, as a rubber-sheet picture suggests. · GA §23.3 p.251 (pdf 268)

**Correction:** Spatial curvature gives only about a third of the advance; the larger part comes from the time-time part of the metric.

**Why tempting:** Visualizations of curved space are ubiquitous and time curvature is hard to picture.

### The variable u = 1/r (or M/r) is a component of the four-velocity u. · GA §23.3 p.250 (pdf 267)

**Correction:** It is an unrelated inverse-radius variable borrowed from Chapter 20 that happens to share the letter.

**Why tempting:** Both appear in the same derivation within a few lines.

### The maximum of the effective potential is irrelevant because nothing can sit there. · GA §23.2 p.248 (pdf 265)

**Correction:** It is a genuine but unstable circular orbit; its photon analogue (at 3M) matters in the next chapter.

**Why tempting:** Only minima are usually associated with orbits.

### The gap between the chapter's 41 arcsec and the accepted 43 arcsec per century reflects subtle higher-order GR physics. · GA §23.3 p.251 (pdf 268)

**Correction:** Almost all of it comes from inserting the semi-major axis for r0. In the derivation r0 = 1/u0 plays the role of the semi-latus rectum a(1 - e^2); for Mercury (e about 0.21) that length, with the accurate solar mass, raises the leading-order estimate to about 43.0 (about 43.2 if the book's rounded 2e30 kg is kept).

**Why tempting:** The book attributes the refined value to less severe approximations without saying which.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### Schwarzschild r is the physical distance from the centre, so the orbit radius is just r. · GA p.252 (pdf 269)

**Correction:** r is defined by circumference 2 pi r; proper radial distance is larger, which is exactly the geometric fact exploited in Exercise 23.5.

**Why tempting:** The letter r and the Newtonian-looking formulas invite a flat-space reading.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### Precession only works for nearly circular orbits because the derivation assumed small eccentricity. · GA §23.3 p.250 (pdf 267)

**Correction:** To first order in M/r0 the advance 6 pi M/[a(1 - e^2)] holds for any eccentricity; the dropped cubic term affects orbit shape and only shifts the frequency at second order in M/r0.

**Why tempting:** The chapter justifies dropping the cubic term by appealing to small epsilon.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*


## Thought experiments

### Throwing test particles in from infinity · GA §23.1 p.247 (pdf 264)

**Setup:** Send particles toward a Schwarzschild mass with the same angular momentum but increasing energy, or the same energy but decreasing angular momentum.

**Lesson:** Below the barrier top they scatter; above it they are captured; for L-tilde below 4M nothing arriving from infinity comes back.

**App idea:** Capture cross-section map: a 2D grid over (energy, L-tilde) coloured by fate, with a click launching the corresponding trajectory.

### Lowering a circular orbit toward the mass · GA §23.2 p.248 (pdf 265)

**Setup:** Imagine a satellite on ever smaller circular orbits, reading off the required L-tilde and E-tilde at each radius from Example 23.3.

**Lesson:** The required angular momentum has a minimum at 6M and blows up at 3M; below 6M the orbits become unstable, so gradual inspiral ends in a plunge.

**App idea:** Plot L-tilde(r) and E-tilde(r) for circular orbits with a draggable radius marker and a stability colour band.

### Space-only and time-only Schwarzschild worlds · GA p.252 (pdf 269)

**Setup:** Build two artificial geometries: one keeping only the curved spatial slice (Exercise 23.5) and one keeping only the modified g_tt with flat space (Exercise 23.6), and compute orbital precession in each.

**Lesson:** Space curvature alone gives about one third of the advance and time curvature about two thirds, so gravity is not just bent space.

**App idea:** Three-column orbit simulator (space-only, time-only, full Schwarzschild) sharing initial conditions, with precession readouts adding up.

### Unrolling the tangent cone · GA p.253 (pdf 270)

**Setup:** Lay a circular orbit on the cone tangent to Flamm's paraboloid and unroll the cone onto a flat table.

**Lesson:** The unrolled circle is missing a wedge of angle about 2 pi M/r, so a closed flat ellipse drawn across the gap reappears rotated.

**App idea:** Physical-feel 3D cone with a cut seam that the learner drags open and closed.


## Applications and observations

- **Mercury's relativistic perihelion advance** (numerical-estimate, GA §23.3 p.251 (pdf 268)): Restoring constants, 6 pi GM/(c^2 r0) with the book's inputs gives about 4.8e-7 rad per orbit; with about 415 orbits per century that is about 2e-4 rad or 41 arcsec per century. A careful GR treatment gives 43.0. Our check: replacing r0 by Mercury's semi-latus rectum a(1 - e^2) = 5.55e10 m gives 43.0 arcsec per century with GM_sun = 1.327e20 m^3 s^-2 (about 43.2 with the book's rounded M = 2e30 kg). Key numbers: Period 88 d (7.6e6 s); r0 = 5.8e10 m; M_sun = 2e30 kg; 4.8e-7 rad/orbit; ~41 arcsec/century (book), 43.0 (refined); with a(1-e^2) = 5.55e10 m, 43.0-43.2 depending on the solar mass used (our estimate).
- **Mercury precession budget** (observation, GA §23.3 p.251 (pdf 268)): Observed total advance compared with Newtonian perturbations from the other planets, the Sun's oblateness and the relativistic residual. Key numbers: Total ~575 arcsec/century; planets ~532; solar oblateness ~0.03; residual observed 43.1 +/- 0.5; GR 43.0.
- **Classical solar-system tests** (observation, GA §23.3 p.249 (pdf 266)): Perihelion precession of Mercury, light bending by the Sun and gravitational redshift, plus Shapiro time delay as a fourth test.
- **Innermost stable circular orbit and accretion efficiency (our extension)** (astrophysical-system, GA §23.2 p.248 (pdf 265)): Not discussed in the chapter, but follows directly from eqns 23.4 and 23.8: gas in an accretion disc around a non-rotating black hole spirals through circular orbits down to 6M and then plunges, radiating the binding energy 1 - E-tilde(6M). Key numbers: r_ISCO = 6GM/c^2 (about 89 km for 10 solar masses); E-tilde(6M) = sqrt(8/9) = 0.943, so about 5.7 percent of rest-mass energy released (our computation).
- **Size of the relativistic correction in the solar system** (numerical-estimate, GA §23.3 p.251 (pdf 268)): The dimensionless parameter controlling precession is GM/(c^2 r0), which for Mercury is about 2.6e-8, explaining why the effect is tiny per orbit but accumulates over centuries (our estimate from the book's numbers). Key numbers: GM_sun/c^2 ~ 1.5 km; GM_sun/(c^2 r0) ~ 2.6e-8 for Mercury.
- **Numerical orbit integration** (data-analysis, GA p.253 (pdf 270)): Figures 23.2-23.3 were generated by integrating eqn 23.15, and Exercise 23.7 asks readers to write such a program for L-tilde/M = 4.3, handling the change of sign at turning points. Key numbers: For L-tilde/M = 4.3: r_+ ~ 14.7M, r_- ~ 3.77M, V_eff(r_+) ~ -0.031, V_eff(r_-) ~ +0.040 (our computation).

## Historical notes

- **Johannes Kepler, Isaac Newton:** Elliptical planetary orbits and their Newtonian explanation, recalled as the benchmark GR had to improve on. — Frames the chapter: GR's early success was matching observations more closely than Kepler-Newton ellipses. (GA p.246 (pdf 263))
- **History (1850s):** An unexplained residual in Mercury's perihelion advance was known from the 1850s; proposed Newtonian fixes included raising Venus's mass by about ten percent or postulating an unseen swarm of asteroids near Mercury. — Shows the anomaly was robust against ad hoc Newtonian repairs, which made GR's parameter-free explanation compelling. (Year supplied by us; the book gives none.) (GA §23.3 p.251 (pdf 268))
- **Ludwig Flamm:** Flamm's paraboloid, the embedding of the Schwarzschild spatial slice, is named after him in Exercise 23.5 (life dates 1885-1964 given). — Earliest visualization of Schwarzschild spatial geometry, used here to isolate spatial curvature's share of precession. ## Notation and conventions

- **Units:** Geometric units G = c = 1 throughout; M denotes GM/c^2. Constants are restored only in Example 23.6. (GA §23.3 p.251 (pdf 268))
- **Metric signature:** (-, +, +, +), so u.u = -1 for massive particles. (GA §23.3 p.249 (pdf 266))
- **Tilded conserved quantities:** E-tilde and L-tilde are energy and angular momentum per unit rest mass; E-tilde = (1 - 2M/r)u^t, L-tilde = r^2 sin^2 theta u^phi (r^2 u^phi in the equatorial plane). — Lowered components: u_t = -E-tilde, u_phi = L-tilde. (GA p.246 (pdf 263))
- **Effective energy:** Script E = (E-tilde^2 - 1)/2, with V_eff per unit mass; contrasted with the Newtonian U_eff that carries the particle mass m. (GA §23.1 p.247 (pdf 264))
- **r_+ and r_-:** r_+ is the outer extremum (minimum, stable circular orbit) and r_- the inner one (maximum, unstable), from the +/- sign in eqn 23.4. (GA §23.1 p.247 (pdf 264))
- **Axis labels in potential plots:** Figures label the vertical axis V_eff(r-vector) and the horizontal axis |r-vector|, a Newtonian-style notation for the Schwarzschild radial coordinate. — In GR there is no position vector; read it as the areal coordinate r. (GA §23.1 p.247 (pdf 264))
- **Overloaded symbol u:** Bold u is the four-velocity; plain u = 1/r in the Newtonian margin note, and u = M/r (dimensionless) from Example 23.5 on; primes denote d/dphi. — Margin note 9 flags the clash. (GA §23.3 p.250 (pdf 267))
- **u0, r0, epsilon, E0:** u0 = M/r0 is the circular-orbit value of u about which the orbit is expanded, E-tilde_0 its energy, epsilon defined by epsilon^2 u0^2 = (M/L)^2 (E^2 - E0^2). — Printed text sometimes drops the tilde (E0) and the square on epsilon. (GA §23.3 p.250 (pdf 267))
- **Precession angles:** Delta phi is the azimuth swept between successive perihelia; delta phi = Delta phi - 2 pi is the advance per orbit. (GA §23.3 p.251 (pdf 268))
- **Rescaled variables:** psi = (1 - 6u0)^{1/2} phi and mu = u - u0. (GA §23.3 p.251 (pdf 268))

- **23.4** (standard): A world line at fixed radius with uniform angular rate is prescribed; fix its time-dilation constant from normalization, compute the four-acceleration, and find when it vanishes. — *Recovers circular geodesics (and Kepler's law) from the acceleration side instead of the potential side.* Skills: four-acceleration with Christoffel symbols, geodesic condition
- **23.5** (challenging): Embed the equatorial spatial slice as Flamm's paraboloid, approximate it by a tangent cone, show the circumference mismatch requires removing a wedge of about 2 pi M/r, argue this advances an elliptical orbit's perihelion by that amount, and compare with the full shift. — *Isolates the spatial-curvature third of the precession with a tactile geometric picture.* Skills: embedding diagrams, geometric approximation, interpreting precession

Note the printed eqn 23.32 needs E-tilde squared.* Skills: modified metrics, second-order orbit equation, perturbation about circular orbit
- **23.7** (standard): Write a program that integrates eqn 23.15 for L-tilde/M = 4.3, choosing the root sign according to whether the particle is approaching or receding from periapsis. — *Turns the chapter into a hands-on lab and reproduces the book's figures.* Skills: numerical ODE integration, turning-point handling, visualizing orbits
- **23.2** (standard): Compute the proper time for one circuit of uniform circular motion in a flat, exponentially expanding metric with a specified velocity definition. — *Transfers the proper-period calculation to a cosmological geometry from Part III.* Skills: time-dependent metrics, proper time integration
- **23.3** (intro): Find how Schwarzschild time t relates to proper time for a particle held at constant radius. — *Quick reminder that the g_tt factor alone controls static clocks, setting up the time-curvature argument.* Skills: gravitational time dilation, normalization condition

## Teaching gems

### Write the relativistic radial equation so it is visually identical to the Newtonian one and point to a single extra term (-M L^2/r^3) as the source of every new orbital phenomenon. · GA §23.1 p.247 (pdf 264)

**Why it works:** Learners reuse all their Chapter 20 intuition and focus attention on one small, nameable difference.

**App idea:** A 'relativity dial' that scales the r^-3 term from 0 to 1, morphing the potential and the orbit simultaneously.

### Pair each potential plot with its energy line and the orbit it produces (circular, scattered, captured, precessing). · GA §23.1 p.247 (pdf 264)

**Why it works:** Connects an abstract 1D graph to a 2D trajectory and makes orbit classification a visual reading task.

**App idea:** Draggable energy line on V_eff with a synchronized orbit integrator and a bead moving on the potential curve.

### Reduce the zoo of orbit types to two magic numbers of L-tilde/M: sqrt(12) (no stable orbits below, ISCO at 6M) and 4 (barrier top at zero, capture threshold). · GA §23.1 p.248 (pdf 265)

**Why it works:** Memorable anchors that organize the phase space and can be checked with one line of algebra.

**App idea:** Slider with snap points at sqrt(12) and 4 that trigger short narrated explanations and highlight the potential's shape change.

### Show that Kepler's third law is unchanged in Schwarzschild coordinate time. · GA §23.2 p.249 (pdf 266)

**Why it works:** A surprising null result that doubles as a strong algebra check and prompts a discussion of which time is being used.

**App idea:** Circular-orbit panel showing coordinate period, proper period and Newtonian period side by side as radius shrinks toward 6M.

### Get the orbit shape from normalization plus conserved quantities, dividing dr/dtau by dphi/dtau, avoiding second-order geodesic equations. · GA §23.3 p.249 (pdf 266)

**Why it works:** Short, reusable recipe that works for any metric with two Killing symmetries (and is reused for photons).

**App idea:** Step-through derivation card deck where each step highlights which symmetry or identity is used.

### Rescale the angle so the perturbed orbit is an exact oscillator; precession becomes the mismatch between 2 pi in psi and 2 pi in phi. · GA §23.3 p.251 (pdf 268)

**Why it works:** Turns a messy perturbation problem into a one-line frequency argument that generalizes to any near-circular precession.

**App idea:** Two rotating phase dials (radial phase and azimuth) whose growing offset traces the rotating apsidal line.

### Give the full Mercury bookkeeping (575 total, 532 planets, 0.03 oblateness, 43 residual) with the story of failed Newtonian patches. · GA §23.3 p.251 (pdf 268)

**Why it works:** Shows how a small but stubborn residual becomes decisive evidence, and teaches scientific skepticism about the headline number.

**App idea:** Stacked bar or waterfall chart of precession contributions with hover cards for each historical hypothesis.

### Split precession into spatial (cone wedge, one third) and temporal (g_tt only toy metric, two thirds) contributions through deliberately artificial geometries. · GA p.252 (pdf 269)

**Why it works:** Directly refutes the rubber-sheet picture with a calculation the learner can do, making time curvature tangible.

**App idea:** Three synchronized simulators (space-only, time-only, full) whose precession readouts sum to the Schwarzschild value.


## Gaps and pitfalls

- **Printed eqn 23.6 has (1 - 2M^2/L^2) where squaring eqn 23.4 gives (1 - 12M^2/L^2).** — A reader checking the algebra cannot reproduce 23.7 and may doubt the result. *Suggestion:* Present the corrected relation and derive L^2/r^2 = M/(r - 3M) directly by eliminating between dV/dr = 0 and E = V_eff.
- **Printed eqn 23.9 gives (L/E)^2 = M r_+^2 (1 - 2M/r_+)^{-2}; the correct power is r_+ to the first.** — Substituting the printed form into 23.10 gives (dphi/dt)^2 = M/r^2, not the Kepler result, and is dimensionally wrong. *Suggestion:* Use the corrected formula and include a dimensional check as a habit.
- **Just before eqn 23.21 the definition is printed as epsilon u0^2 rather than epsilon^2 u0^2; eqn 23.20 writes E0 without the tilde.** — Confusion about whether epsilon is an eccentricity. *Suggestion:* Define epsilon^2 u0^2 = (M/L)^2 (E^2 - E0^2) explicitly and show it reduces to Newtonian eccentricity.
- **Printed eqn 23.22 reads (u')^2 = (1 - 6u0)(u - u0)^2 = u0^2 epsilon^2; the first '=' should be '+'.** — The equation as printed is inconsistent and the oscillator structure is obscured. *Suggestion:* Write (u')^2 + (1 - 6u0)(u - u0)^2 = u0^2 epsilon^2 and point out it is an ellipse in the (u - u0, u') plane.
- **Exercise eqn 23.32 has E-tilde on the right; differentiating the normalization condition gives E-tilde squared, as the following eqn 23.33 assumes.** — Students attempting Exercise 23.6 will not reach the printed target. *Suggestion:* Flag the correction in any assignment based on this exercise.
- **The step from 23.17 to 23.18 is described only as massaging; the completing-the-square and coefficient matching are left to the reader, and it is not stated that u0 is the relativistic circular-orbit value rather than the Newtonian u0.** — Readers may think the Newtonian u0 = M/L^2 is intended and get lost in the matching. *Suggestion:* Show the expansion explicitly or use the second-order form u'' + u = M/L^2 + 3M u^2 (in u = 1/r) and linearize about the circular orbit.
- **r0 is plugged in as Mercury's orbital radius (5.8e10 m, the semi-major axis) although in the derivation it is effectively the semi-latus rectum a(1 - e^2); the 41 versus 43 arcsec discrepancy is attributed vaguely to cruder approximations.** — Learners do not see that the leading-order formula already gives 43 arcsec when the correct length is used. *Suggestion:* Teach delta phi = 6 pi GM/[c^2 a(1 - e^2)] and have learners compute 43.0 arcsec per century.
- **Dropping 2(u - u0)^3 is justified by small eccentricity, but the first-order precession is independent of eccentricity; the cubic term only affects the frequency at second order in M/r0.** — Creates the false impression that the prediction fails for eccentric orbits such as binary pulsars. *Suggestion:* Mention that 6 pi M/[a(1 - e^2)] holds for any e to first order in M/a, and optionally show the Lindstedt argument.
- **The book calls the extrema of V_eff 'turning points', a term usually reserved for radii where the energy line meets V_eff.** — Mixes up circular-orbit radii with periapsis/apoapsis radii. *Suggestion:* Use 'extrema' or 'circular-orbit radii' for V_eff' = 0 and 'turning points' for E = V_eff.
- **The L-tilde = 4M capture statement is really about marginally unbound particles (effective energy just above zero); more energetic incoming particles need larger L-tilde to avoid capture.** — May conclude that any particle with L-tilde > 4M is safe. *Suggestion:* Show the capture region in the (E, L) plane rather than a single threshold.
- **Kepler's third law is said to be unaltered without stressing that this holds in Schwarzschild coordinate time and areal radius, not in proper time or proper distance.** — Learners may overgeneralize to 'GR does not change orbital periods'. *Suggestion:* Pair the result with Exercise 23.1 (proper period) and note what a distant observer versus the orbiter measures.
- **The space-versus-time interpretation of precession is asserted in one paragraph and demonstrated only in exercises, whose one-third/two-thirds split is coordinate dependent.** — The key conceptual payoff may be skipped, or taken as an invariant fact. *Suggestion:* Do the two toy-metric calculations in the main teaching flow and add a caveat that the split refers to Schwarzschild coordinates.
- **Example 23.2 imagines captured particles crashing into the star, without discussing that neutron stars or black holes can be compact enough for capture to matter, while ordinary stars are far larger than 6M.** — Unclear when ISCO and capture are physically relevant. *Suggestion:* Compare the Sun's radius (about 470000 M_sun-lengths) with 6M and point forward to black holes and accretion discs.

## Tutor notes
