# GA ch47 · The properties of gravitons

> Asks what a field theorist with no geometric theory of gravity would conclude, uses force ranges and force signs to argue that the mediator of gravity must be massless with spin 2, computes the tree-level photon exchange amplitude and splits it into a Coulomb piece plus two transverse helicity states, replays the calculation with the energy-momentum tensor, removes a spurious trace (spin-0) piece by adding half the product of traces, identifies helicity +-2 graviton polarizations, reads off the graviton propagator and recovers the momentum-space weak-field Einstein equation, and closes by explaining why this perturbative route to quantum gravity breaks down.

**Pages:** printed 512–519 · pdf 529–536 · **Difficulty:** math 3/5, conceptual 4/5, novice-friendliness 2/5

The algebra is mostly careful component expansion with the Minkowski metric, well within reach after Chapters 45-46. The conceptual load is high because propagators, amplitudes, off-shell states, helicity and renormalization are used with only one-line explanations, and key claims (spin-sign rule, why the pole term is the physical part, why spin 0 fails) are asserted rather than derived. Several small sign and index-placement slips and overloaded symbols (T for tensor and trace, alpha twice) make independent checking harder. The chapter is explicitly optional.

## Role in the book

An optional (skip-sign) chapter in Part VI that turns the classical weak-field and gravitational-wave material of Chapters 45-46 into the language of particle exchange. It reuses the Lagrangian field theory of Chapter 40, the electromagnetic gauge-field template of Chapters 42-44 and the virtual-particle idea from the Hawking radiation chapter (28), and it offers a second, non-geometric derivation of the trace-reversed source term that first appeared in the Einstein equation (Chapter 13). Its sobering coda about non-renormalizability sets up Chapter 48 (extra dimensions) and Chapter 49 (string theory, loop quantum gravity and other routes to quantum gravity).

## Learning objectives

- Reader can state the exchange-particle picture of forces and explain what it means for a virtual particle to be off the mass shell.
- Reader can show that the Yukawa potential is the Green's function of the static Klein-Gordon operator and explain why a 1/r potential forces the mediator mass to vanish.
- Reader can argue, from the sign of the force between like sources, that gravity cannot be carried by an odd-spin particle, and explain why spin 2 (a symmetric tensor field) is the natural choice.
- Reader can write the tree-level photon exchange amplitude -J_a eta J_b / k^2 and use current conservation k_mu J^mu = 0 to split it into an instantaneous Coulomb term and a propagating transverse term.
- Reader can rewrite the transverse part as a sum over circular polarization states and identify their helicities +-1 from the phase winding e^{+-i phi}.
- Reader can expand the graviton exchange amplitude -T_a eta eta T_b / k^2 in components, identify the Newtonian T^00 T^00 piece, and use k_mu T^{mu nu} = 0 to eliminate components along the propagation direction.
- Reader can explain why the trace of T produces a spurious spin-0 contribution and show that adding (1/2) T_a T_b / k^2 leaves exactly two retarded terms.
- Reader can identify the graviton polarizations (T^11 - T^22) and (T^12 + T^21), combine them into helicity +-2 states and verify the e^{+-2i theta} behaviour under rotations about the propagation axis.
- Reader can write the graviton propagator D_{sigma tau mu nu} and recognise h_{mu nu}(k) proportional to (T_{mu nu} - (1/2) eta_{mu nu} T)/k^2 as the momentum-space weak-field Einstein equation.
- Reader can explain qualitatively why perturbative quantum gravity is non-renormalizable, why the expansion parameter is G E^2, and where the breakdown scale lies.
- Reader can estimate gravitational-wave power and graviton emission rates for the Earth-Sun system and for everyday motion, and explain why single gravitons are hopelessly hard to detect.

## Teaching approach

A counterfactual, field-theorist's tour of gravity in the spirit of Feynman's gravitation lectures. No QFT machinery is assumed: Feynman diagrams, propagators and amplitudes are introduced as bookkeeping devices, and all the physics is read off by expanding contractions into components, applying conservation laws in momentum space, and inspecting signs and counting terms. The photon case is done first in full as a template, then the identical sequence of steps is replayed for the graviton with one extra index, where a new wrinkle (the trace of T) appears and is fixed by a free coefficient chosen to give the right number of polarizations. The payoff is recognising the trace-reversed Einstein source term from a completely different route; the chapter then candidly notes that the programme fails at higher orders.

**Style:** physical-motivation-first, thought-experiment, analogy-driven, components-first, derivation-heavy, spiral-revisit, conversational-informal

**Narrative arc**

1. Counterfactual hook: a civilization that knows QFT but not curved spacetime tries to describe gravity and is led to a quantized gravitational excitation, the graviton.
2. Principle: interactions happen through the exchange of virtual, off-shell force carriers (Yukawa); classical field theory already has the source-field-source pattern.
3. Model check (Example 47.1): a massive carrier gives a Yukawa potential (Green's function of the static Klein-Gordon operator); a 1/r potential needs a massless carrier, so both photon and graviton are massless.
4. Selection by force sign: vector exchange makes like charges repel, even-spin exchange gives single-sign forces; spin 0 is too simple, so assume spin 2 and a symmetric tensor field.
5. Template calculation for light: Feynman diagram, momentum-space field A = J/k^2, amplitude -J_a eta J_b/k^2 (Example 47.2).
6. Decomposition: current conservation removes the longitudinal component, leaving an instantaneous Coulomb term plus a retarded term with two transverse currents; recombine into circular states of helicity +-1.
7. Replay for gravity: h proportional to T/k^2, coupling h_{mu nu} T^{mu nu}, amplitude -T_a eta eta T_b/k^2, Newtonian term with the attractive sign, full component expansion (47.15).
8. Complication and fix: the trace of T sneaks in a spin-0 piece and an apparent third polarization; add alpha T_a T_b/k^2, use energy-momentum conservation, and choose alpha = 1/2 to leave two polarizations (Example 47.3).
9. Check: the two retarded structures recombine into helicity +-2 states whose phase winds as e^{+-2i theta} (47.26).
10. Payoff (Example 47.4): graviton propagator and the field of a source proportional to (T - (1/2) eta T)/k^2, recognised as the momentum-space weak-field Einstein equation.
11. Coda: perturbative quantization fails (non-renormalizable, divergence at E ~ G^{-1/2}); pointers to extra dimensions (Ch. 48) and quantum-gravity candidates (Ch. 49).
12. Exercises: helicity transformation laws for spin-1 and spin-2 polarizations, the binary luminosity formula and graviton-counting estimates.

**Signature moves**

- Feynman's 'parallel world' counterfactual: pretend geometry is unknown and see how far particle-physics reasoning gets.
- Photon-first template, then 'the same steps with an extra index' for the graviton, so every new feature is exactly the difference between rank 1 and rank 2.
- Reading physical content (attraction versus repulsion, instantaneous versus propagating, number of polarizations) directly from signs and term counts in a component expansion, instead of using representation theory.
- Using a conservation law in momentum space (k_mu J^mu = 0, k_mu T^{mu nu} = 0) to eliminate longitudinal components.
- Introducing a free coefficient (alpha) and fixing it by a physical requirement (exactly two polarizations), which then turns out to reproduce the Einstein trace reversal.
- Identifying spin/helicity by the phase acquired under rotation: (x +- iy) gives one unit, its square gives two.
- Honest scaffolding: skip-sign for a hypothetical particle, a margin admission that the source/probe split is not really symmetric but gives the right answer, and a closing reminder that nobody has quantized gravity.

## Section by section

A margin box marks the chapter as skippable because the graviton is hypothetical.

**Concepts:** graviton, field-theoretic route to gravity, weak-field limit

**Key moves**
- Pose the counterfactual question (no geometry, only QFT).
- Restrict to weak fields with indices moved by eta.
- Announce the photon-to-graviton comparison as the method.
- Flag the graviton as hypothetical and the chapter as optional.

### §47.1 Force-carrying particles — p.512 (pdf 529)

Introduces Yukawa's idea that forces arise from exchanging virtual, off-shell particles, and recalls the classical pattern in which sources shape a field that then pushes the sources. In QED the source is the current four-vector and the carrier is the massless photon. Example 47.1 shows that a carrier of mass m yields an exponentially screened Yukawa potential obeying a static Klein-Gordon Green's-function equation, whereas the Coulomb potential obeys Poisson's equation, the m = 0 case. Since Newton's potential also falls as 1/r, the graviton is massless. Spin is fixed by the sign of the force: vector exchange makes like charges repel, which gravity never does, so the spin must be even; spin 0 is dismissed as too simple, leaving spin 2 and a symmetric tensor field.

**Concepts:** force-carrying particle, virtual particle, Yukawa potential, range of a force and mediator mass, Green's function, Poisson's equation as the massless limit, masslessness of the graviton, spin of the mediator and sign of the force, rejection of scalar gravity, field rank and particle spin

**Key moves**
- State the exchange principle in one sentence.
- Match potentials to Green's functions: Yukawa for (nabla^2 - alpha^2 m^2), Coulomb for nabla^2.
- Infer m = 0 for photon and graviton from the 1/r fall-off.
- Use like-charge repulsion to rule out spin 1 for gravity; even spin gives single-sign forces.
- Discard spin 0 (pointer to Exercise 42.13) and adopt spin 2 with a symmetric tensor field.
- Plan: work out the photon case and repeat each step for gravity.

### §47.2 Photon propagation and polarization — p.514 (pdf 531)

Scattering of two electrons is described by a quantum amplitude, organised by Feynman diagrams (Fig. 47.1) or, equivalently, by treating one current as the source of a field that the other current feels (Fig. 47.2). With the coupling J^mu A_mu and a gauge choice, the field of current b in momentum space is J_b/k^2, and the amplitude has the structure current-propagator-current, with photon propagator proportional to eta/k^2. In Example 47.2 the photon travels along z; current conservation eliminates J^3, and the amplitude separates into a J^0 J^0/(k^3)^2 term whose Fourier transform is an instantaneous, like-charge-repelling Coulomb interaction (an artefact of a non-covariant split), and a retarded term coupling only J^1 and J^2. That transverse piece is rewritten as a sum of two circular combinations J^1 +- i J^2, which behave as j e^{+-i phi} and so carry helicity +-1.

**Concepts:** Feynman diagram, scattering amplitude, interaction Lagrangian, momentum-space field equation, photon propagator, iε prescription, current conservation in momentum space, Coulomb (instantaneous) versus retarded decomposition, transverse photon polarizations, circular polarization and helicity

**Key moves**
- Picture exchange as source-plus-probe (Fig. 47.2).
- Solve the gauge-fixed wave equation in momentum space: A = J_b/k^2.
- Form the amplitude -J_a^mu eta_{mu nu} J_b^nu / k^2 (dropping i epsilon).
- Choose k along z and expand the contraction.
- Use k_mu J^mu = 0 to trade J^3 for J^0.
- Identify the Coulomb term (instantaneous, repulsive for like charges) and the retarded transverse term.
- Rewrite J^1 J^1 + J^2 J^2 as two circular products and read helicity from e^{+-i phi}.

### §47.3 Graviton propagation and polarization — p.516 (pdf 533)

The photon calculation is repeated with the energy-momentum tensor as source: h proportional to T/k^2, coupling h_{mu nu} T^{mu nu}, and an amplitude with two metric factors in the propagator. The T^00 T^00 term, with an overall minus sign, is the attractive Newtonian piece, and the full contraction is expanded into ten kinds of terms. Because a rank-2 tensor carries a scalar trace, a naive split seems to give three polarizations; the fix is to add alpha times the product of traces over k^2. Example 47.3 uses k_mu T^{mu nu} = 0 to eliminate the 3-components, isolates the retarded part, and shows that alpha = 1/2 leaves exactly two structures, (T^11 - T^22) and (T^12 + T^21), which correspond to the plus and cross polarizations of Chapter 46 (a link the book makes explicit only in Exercise 47.3). These recombine into states with phase e^{+-2i theta}, confirming helicity +-2. Example 47.4 then extracts the graviton propagator and the field of a source proportional to (T - (1/2) eta T)/k^2, the momentum-space weak-field Einstein equation. The section ends with a caution that this perturbative programme is non-renormalizable (margin note on the G E^2 series and the Fermi-theory analogy) and points to Chapters 48-49.

**Concepts:** gravitational coupling h_{mu nu} T^{mu nu}, graviton exchange amplitude, Newtonian limit of graviton exchange, energy-momentum conservation in momentum space, trace contamination and trace subtraction, graviton polarizations, helicity, graviton propagator, momentum-space weak-field Einstein equation, non-renormalizability of perturbative quantum gravity, Planck-scale breakdown of the G E^2 expansion

**Key moves**
- Replace J by T and eta by eta eta in the amplitude.
- Pick out -T^00 T^00/(k^3)^2 as Newton's attraction.
- Expand the double contraction, noting factors of 2 from symmetric off-diagonal pairs and minus signs for mixed time-space components.
- Diagnose the trace as a hidden spin-0 part producing an apparent third polarization.
- Add alpha T_a T_b / k^2 and eliminate T^{3 nu} using k_mu T^{mu nu} = 0.
- Set alpha = 1/2 so the retarded part contains only (T^11 - T^22) and (T^12 + T^21).
- Recombine into (T^11 - T^22 +- 2i T^12), each behaving like (x +- iy)^2, hence e^{+-2i theta}.
- Read off the propagator and h(k) proportional to (T - (1/2) eta T)/k^2; recognise the Einstein trace reversal.
- Close with the failure of renormalization and forward pointers.

## Concepts

### graviton

*definition · core* · also: quantum of the gravitational field, gravity-carrying particle

The hypothetical quantum of the weak gravitational field h_{mu nu}: a massless particle of spin 2 with exactly two helicity states (+2 and -2), whose virtual exchange between energy-momentum sources would mediate gravitational attraction.

**How introduced:** Motivated by Feynman's counterfactual civilization with QFT but no geometry; its mass is fixed by the 1/r Newtonian potential, its spin by the always-attractive nature of gravity, and its polarizations by an explicit decomposition of the exchange amplitude that mirrors the photon calculation.

**Prerequisites:** force-carrying particle, gravitational wave, weak-field limit, helicity

$$
m_{\rm graviton}=0
$$

$$
S=2
$$

$$
h=\pm2
$$

**Notes:** Not observed; the chapter carries a skip-sign for this reason.

**Where:** GA p.512 (pdf 529); GA §47.1 p.513 (pdf 530); GA §47.1 p.514 (pdf 531); GA §47.3 p.517 (pdf 534); GA §47.3 p.518 (pdf 535); GA p.519 (pdf 536)

### field-theoretic route to gravity

*historical-idea · introduced* · also: Feynman's parallel-world approach, particle-physics approach to gravitation, spin-2 field approach

The programme of describing gravity as a massless spin-2 field on flat spacetime, coupled to energy-momentum and quantized like other gauge fields, rather than starting from curved spacetime geometry.

**How introduced:** Framed as the question Feynman posed in his gravitation course: what would physicists who knew QFT but not GR do? A margin note says this was an early, informative but ultimately unsuccessful path to quantum gravity.

**Prerequisites:** Lagrangian field theory, weak-field limit

**Notes:** Classically, consistent self-coupling of a spin-2 field is known to rebuild full GR (not discussed in the book).

**Where:** GA p.512 (pdf 529); GA §47.3 p.518 (pdf 535)

### weak-field limit

*convention · revisited* · also: linearized gravity, flat-background perturbation

Treating the metric as Minkowski plus a small symmetric perturbation h_{mu nu}, keeping only first-order terms and raising and lowering all indices with eta.

**How introduced:** Stated at the outset as the arena for the chapter, carried over from Chapters 45-46.

**Prerequisites:** metric perturbation

$$
g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu}
$$

**Where:** GA p.512 (pdf 529)

### force-carrying particle

*principle · developed* · also: exchange particle, mediator, gauge boson (for photon)

A particle whose virtual exchange between two sources produces their interaction; the carrier's mass sets the range and its spin sets the tensor character and sign of the force.

**How introduced:** Credited to Yukawa and highlighted as the one key notion of Section 47.1, then connected to the classical picture of sources arranging a field that acts back on sources.

**Prerequisites:** virtual particle

**Where:** GA §47.1 p.512 (pdf 529); GA §47.1 p.513 (pdf 530)

### virtual particle

*definition · revisited* · also: off-shell particle, off mass-shell particle

An intermediate quantum that does not satisfy the free dispersion relation p.p = -m^2; energy-time uncertainty allows such states to exist briefly, so they appear only as internal lines of a process.

**How introduced:** Recalled from Chapter 28 in a margin note that uses Delta E Delta t ~ hbar to bound the lifetime and hence the range of an energy-E virtual quantum.

**Prerequisites:** mass shell, energy-time uncertainty relation

$$
\Delta E\,\Delta t\sim\hbar
$$

$$
\Delta t\lesssim\hbar/E
$$

**Notes:** The finite-range remark applies to massive carriers; massless carriers with arbitrarily small momentum transfer give infinite range.

**Where:** GA §47.1 p.513 (pdf 530)

### Yukawa potential

*solution-or-model · developed* · also: screened Coulomb potential

The static potential produced by exchanging a spinless particle of mass m, proportional to -e^{-alpha m r}/(4 pi r) with alpha = c/hbar in SI units, so the interaction dies off beyond roughly the Compton wavelength hbar/(mc).

**How introduced:** Example 47.1 writes it down and notes that it is the point-source solution of a static Klein-Gordon equation.

**Prerequisites:** Green's function, Klein-Gordon equation

$$
U(\vec r)\propto-\frac{e^{-\alpha m|\vec r|}}{4\pi|\vec r|}
$$

$$
(\nabla^2-\alpha^2m^2)U=\delta^{(3)}(\vec r)
$$

**Where:** GA §47.1 p.513 (pdf 530)

### range of a force and mediator mass

*principle · developed* · also: mass-range relation

A carrier of mass m produces an interaction screened by e^{-r/lambda} with lambda = hbar/(mc); only a massless carrier gives a long-range 1/r potential.

**How introduced:** Obtained by comparing the Yukawa and Poisson Green's-function equations in Example 47.1 and then applied to both Coulomb and Newtonian potentials.

**Prerequisites:** Yukawa potential, Poisson's equation as the massless limit

$$
\lambda=\hbar/(mc)
$$

**Where:** GA §47.1 p.513 (pdf 530)

### Green's function

*mathematical-object · introduced*

The response G of a linear differential operator to a delta-function source, L G = delta; solutions for general sources follow by superposition.

**How introduced:** Defined in a margin note to Example 47.1 so that potentials can be characterised by the operator they invert.

**Prerequisites:** delta function

$$
\hat L\,G=\delta
$$

**Where:** GA §47.1 p.513 (pdf 530)

### Klein-Gordon equation

*law · revisited* · also: static Klein-Gordon operator, screened Poisson equation

The relativistic wave equation for a free massive spin-0 field; in the static case it reduces to (nabla^2 - m^2) phi = source, whose point-source solution is the Yukawa potential.

**How introduced:** Cited from Chapter 40 in Example 47.1 as the equation any effective potential carried by spinless massive quanta must satisfy.

**Prerequisites:** Lagrangian field theory

$$
(\nabla^2-\alpha^2 m^2)U=\delta^{(3)}
$$

**Where:** GA §47.1 p.513 (pdf 530)

### Poisson's equation as the massless limit

*identity · developed* · also: Coulomb Green's function

Setting the carrier mass to zero turns the static Klein-Gordon Green's-function equation into Poisson's equation, whose solution is the unscreened 1/r potential.

**How introduced:** Second half of Example 47.1: the electromagnetic analogue is identified and the consistency condition m = 0 is drawn.

**Prerequisites:** Green's function, Yukawa potential

$$
\nabla^2V=\delta^{(3)}(\vec r)
$$

$$
V\propto1/|\vec r|
$$

**Where:** GA §47.1 p.513 (pdf 530)

### masslessness of the graviton

*principle · developed* · also: zero graviton mass

Because the Newtonian potential falls exactly as 1/r at long range, the carrier of gravity must have zero mass, just as the photon must for Coulomb's law.

**How introduced:** Stated right after Example 47.1 by transferring the photon argument to the potential Phi proportional to 1/r.

**Prerequisites:** range of a force and mediator mass

$$
\Phi(r)\propto1/r\Rightarrow m=0
$$

**Notes:** Modern bound (not in book): gravitational-wave propagation limits the mass to below about 1e-23 eV/c^2.

**Where:** GA §47.1 p.513 (pdf 530)

### spin of the mediator and sign of the force

*principle · core* · also: even-spin attraction rule, spin-statistics of exchange forces

Exchange of an odd-spin (e.g. spin-1) boson makes like charges repel and opposite charges attract, while exchange of an even-spin boson (0, 2, ...) gives a force of a single sign between all sources, which can be purely attractive.

**How introduced:** Asserted in Section 47.1 to choose the graviton's spin; the photon and graviton amplitudes later exhibit it through the opposite overall signs of the J^0 J^0 and T^00 T^00 terms.

**Prerequisites:** force-carrying particle

**Notes:** In the components, the difference comes from one factor eta_00 = -1 for a vector versus two for a tensor.

**Where:** GA §47.1 p.513 (pdf 530); GA §47.1 p.514 (pdf 531); GA §47.2 p.515 (pdf 532); GA §47.3 p.516 (pdf 533)

### rejection of scalar gravity

*historical-idea · mention* · also: spin-0 gravity

A spin-0 mediator couples only to the trace of T; it is ruled out as a complete theory of gravity because it gets the energetics wrong (and, as is well known, it would not bend light, whose energy-momentum tensor is traceless).

**How introduced:** Dismissed in one sentence with a pointer to Exercise 42.13.

**Prerequisites:** spin of the mediator and sign of the force, trace of the energy-momentum tensor

**Notes:** Exercise 42.13 (after Padmanabhan) builds a scalar theory in which the equivalence principle forces the metric into the conformally flat form g = (1 + Phi)^2 eta. Null curves are unchanged by a conformal factor, so such a theory cannot deflect light. This is the concrete failure behind the book's one-line dismissal.

**Where:** GA §47.1 p.514 (pdf 531)

### field rank and particle spin

*convention · developed* · also: spin-tensor correspondence

Massless spin 0, 1 and 2 quanta are described respectively by a scalar phi, a vector (1-form) A_mu and a symmetric rank-2 tensor h_{mu nu}; the rank of the source (density, current, energy-momentum) matches.

**How introduced:** Tabulated in a margin note and restated in the chapter summary as the reason photons have spin 1 and gravitons spin 2.

**Prerequisites:** tensor rank

$$
S=0:\ \phi,\quad S=1:\ A_\mu,\quad S=2:\ h_{\mu\nu}
$$

**Notes:** A rank-2 symmetric tensor also contains spin-0 (trace) pieces, which is exactly the complication handled in Example 47.3.

**Where:** GA §47.1 p.513 (pdf 530); GA p.519 (pdf 536)

### Feynman diagram

*technique · introduced*

A pictorial shorthand for a term in the perturbative expansion of a scattering amplitude, with external lines for incoming and outgoing particles or currents, vertices for couplings and internal lines for propagators.

**How introduced:** Introduced in Section 47.2 as momentum-space cartoons, with the lowest-order electron-electron exchange diagram in Fig. 47.1.

**Prerequisites:** scattering amplitude

**Where:** GA §47.2 p.514 (pdf 531); GA §47.2 p.515 (pdf 532)

### scattering amplitude

*physical-quantity · developed* · also: transition amplitude

The complex quantum amplitude whose squared modulus gives the probability that incoming states scatter into outgoing ones; at lowest order for exchange it is current times propagator times current.

**How introduced:** Motivated by asking how we know two electrons have interacted (their momenta change), then written schematically as current-propagator-current.

**Prerequisites:** Feynman diagram

$$
i\mathcal A=J_a^\mu D_{\mu\nu}J_b^\nu
$$

**Where:** GA §47.2 p.514 (pdf 531); GA §47.2 p.515 (pdf 532)

### interaction Lagrangian

*definition · introduced* · also: minimal coupling term, source coupling

The term in the Lagrangian density linking a field to its source: J^mu A_mu for electromagnetism and h_{mu nu} T^{mu nu} for weak-field gravity.

**How introduced:** Stated for electromagnetism in Section 47.2 and carried over by analogy to gravity in eqn 47.13.

**Prerequisites:** Lagrangian field theory

$$
\mathcal L=J^\mu A_\mu
$$

$$
\mathcal L=h_{\mu\nu}T^{\mu\nu}
$$

**Where:** GA §47.2 p.514 (pdf 531); GA §47.3 p.516 (pdf 533)

### momentum-space field equation

*technique · developed* · also: field of a source in k-space

Fourier transforming a gauge-fixed wave equation turns the d'Alembertian into -k^2, so the field of a source is simply the source divided by k^2.

**How introduced:** Used to get A = J_b/k^2 from the Lorenz-gauge equation and h proportional to T/k^2 by analogy.

**Prerequisites:** Fourier transform, Lorenz gauge

### circular polarization and helicity

**How introduced:** Photon case: writing J^1 = j cos phi, J^2 = j sin phi gives j e^{+-i phi}. Graviton case: each bracket in eqn 47.26 has the form (x +- iy)^2. Exercises 47.2-47.3 formalise both with rotation matrices.

### trace contamination and trace subtraction

*technique · core* · also: removing the spin-0 part, alpha = 1/2 correction

Because a symmetric rank-2 tensor contains a scalar trace, the naive amplitude -T_a.T_b/k^2 includes a spin-0 exchange and shows three transverse structures; adding alpha T_a T_b/k^2 with alpha = 1/2 cancels the spurious piece and leaves only helicity +-2 exchange.

**How introduced:** Motivated as an apparent third polarization, parametrised by a free constant alpha, and fixed in Example 47.3 by demanding exactly two terms in the retarded part.

**Prerequisites:** trace of the energy-momentum tensor, graviton exchange amplitude, energy-momentum conservation in momentum space

$$
\alpha\,T'\frac{1}{k^2}T
$$

$$
\alpha=\tfrac12

**Notes:** The same term also changes the instantaneous T^00 T^00 coefficient by a factor 1/2 (absorbed into G), which is what gives light twice the Newtonian coupling; the book does not comment on this.

**Where:** GA §47.3 p.517 (pdf 534); GA §47.3 p.518 (pdf 535)

### graviton polarizations

### non-renormalizability of perturbative quantum gravity

*phenomenon · introduced* · also: failure of perturbative quantum gravity

In the perturbative expansion of quantum gravity, divergences at higher orders cannot be absorbed into a finite number of redefined parameters (unlike QED or QCD), so the theory loses predictive power at high energy.

**How introduced:** A closing paragraph and a long margin note explain that the chapter's approach has been tried repeatedly without success, using graviton-graviton scattering as the illustration.

**Prerequisites:** scattering amplitude, Feynman diagram

$$
\mathcal A\sim G\left[1+GE^2+(GE^2)^2+\dots\right]

**Where:** GA §47.3 p.518 (pdf 535); GA p.519 (pdf 536)

### Planck-scale breakdown of the G E^2 expansion

*principle · introduced* · also: gravitational expansion parameter

Because Newton's constant has dimensions of inverse energy squared in natural units, successive orders in graviton scattering grow like (G E^2)^n, and the series fails once E approaches G^{-1/2}, the Planck energy.

**How introduced:** Given in margin note 12 with a comparison to Fermi's four-fermion theory of the weak interaction, where new physics appears at the analogous scale.

**Prerequisites:** non-renormalizability of perturbative quantum gravity

$$
E\sim G^{-1/2}

**Where:** GA §47.3 p.518 (pdf 535)

*historical-idea · mention* · also: effective field theory analogy

Fermi's contact theory of beta decay also has a dimensionful coupling and breaks down at high energy, where it is replaced by exchange of W and Z bosons; by analogy gravity may need new physics near its breakdown scale.

**How introduced:** Mentioned in margin note 12 with a pointer to Zee's QFT text.

*solution-or-model · mention*

A candidate quantum theory of gravity in which fundamental objects are extended strings; it follows a QFT-like route but softens the ultraviolet divergences of point-particle graviton exchange.

### Amplitude in propagator form · derivation-step · GA §47.3 p.518 (pdf 535)

$$
\mathcal A=T_a^{\sigma\tau}D_{\sigma\tau\mu\nu}T_b^{\mu\nu}
$$

Defines the graviton propagator by factoring the two sources out of (47.27).

**Symbols:** The reading copy garbles T_a as T^{i sigma tau}; the printed symbol is T' (source a).

### (47.28) Graviton propagator · central · GA §47.3 p.518 (pdf 535)

$$
D_{\sigma\tau\mu\nu}=-\frac12\frac{\eta_{\mu\sigma}\eta_{\nu\tau}+\eta_{\mu\tau}\eta_{\nu\sigma}-\eta_{\mu\nu}\eta_{\sigma\tau}}{k^2}
$$

Symmetrised tensor propagator whose last term implements the trace subtraction.

### (47.29) Field of a gravitating source (momentum-space weak-field Einstein equation) · central · GA §47.3 p.518 (pdf 535)

$$
h_{\mu\nu}(k)\propto\frac1{k^2}\left(T_{\mu\nu}-\tfrac12\eta_{\mu\nu}T\right)
$$

The trace-reversed source of the Einstein equation re-emerges from demanding pure spin-2 exchange.

### Graviton-graviton scattering series (margin note 12) · supporting · GA §47.3 p.518 (pdf 535)

$$
\mathcal A\sim G\left[1+GE^2+(GE^2)^2+\cdots\right]
$$

Perturbative expansion parameter G E^2 grows with energy; the series diverges once E reaches about G^{-1/2}.

**Symbols:** G Newton's constant (natural units), E energy

### (47.30) Summary: graviton exchange amplitude · supporting · GA p.519 (pdf 536)

$$
\mathcal A=-T_a^{\alpha\beta}\left(\frac{\eta_{\alpha\mu}\eta_{\beta\nu}}{k^2+i\varepsilon}\right)T_b^{\mu\nu}
$$

Restatement of (47.12) in the chapter summary (without the trace correction of 47.27).

### (47.31) Rotation matrix about z · supporting · GA p.519 (pdf 536)

$$
R(\theta)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&\sin\theta&0\\-\sin\theta&\cos\theta&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}
$$

Rotation about the propagation axis used to classify polarizations (Exercise 47.2).

### (47.32) Helicity transformation law · supporting · GA p.519 (pdf 536)

$$
R^i{}_j(\theta)(\epsilon_h)^j=e^{ih\theta}(\epsilon'_h)^i
$$

A helicity-h polarization vector changes only by a phase e^{i h theta} under rotation.

**Symbols:** h helicity; epsilon polarization vector

### (47.33) Rotation of polarization tensors · supporting · GA p.519 (pdf 536)

$$
(\epsilon'_\mu)_{ij}=R_i{}^m(\theta)R_j{}^n(\theta)(\epsilon_\mu)_{mn}
$$

Rank-2 polarization tensors take one rotation matrix per index (Exercise 47.3).

### (47.34) Rotated TT polarization components · supporting · GA p.519 (pdf 536)

$$
(\epsilon')_{11}=(\cos^2\theta-\sin^2\theta)\epsilon_{11}+2\sin\theta\cos\theta\,\epsilon_{12},\qquad(\epsilon')_{12}=-2\sin\theta\cos\theta\,\epsilon_{11}+(\cos^2\theta-\sin^2\theta)\epsilon_{12}
$$

Plus and cross amplitudes mix with angle 2 theta, so eps_11 +- i eps_12 carry helicity -+2 (sign depends on rotation convention).

### (47.35) Gravitational-wave power of a circular binary · supporting · GA p.519 (pdf 536)

$$
L=\frac{32G^4m_1^2m_2^2(m_1+m_2)}{5c^5a^5}

Quadrupole luminosity of two masses in circular orbit at separation a (Exercise 47.4).

**Symbols:** m1, m2 masses; a separation

## Worked examples

### Example 47.1 · intro · GA §47.1 p.513 (pdf 530)

**Problem:** Explain how the mass of a force carrier shows up in the static potential it produces, and what the Coulomb law implies for the photon mass.

**Method:** Identify the Yukawa potential as the point-source solution of (nabla^2 - alpha^2 m^2), a static Klein-Gordon equation; identify the Coulomb potential as the point-source solution of Poisson's equation; compare the two operators.

**Key insight:** Potentials are Green's functions of the carrier's field equation; the mass term produces exponential screening, so an exact 1/r law requires a massless carrier.

**Result:** m = 0 for the photon; applied later to Phi proportional to 1/r to conclude the graviton is massless.

**Concepts:** Yukawa potential, Green's function, Klein-Gordon equation, Poisson's equation as the massless limit, range of a force and mediator mass

### Example 47.2 · standard · GA §47.2 p.515 (pdf 532)

**Problem:** Decompose the tree-level photon exchange amplitude to identify its instantaneous part and its physical polarization content.

**Method:** Take k along z and write out the contraction; use k_mu J^mu = 0 to express J^3 through J^0; collect terms into a k^0-independent J^0 J^0 piece and a piece with the pole in -(k^0)^2 + (k^3)^2; Fourier transform the first; rewrite the transverse sum in a circular basis.

**Key insight:** Conservation removes the longitudinal current, so the propagating part involves only two transverse currents; the leftover charge-density term is Coulomb's law.

**Result:** A = J_a^0 J_b^0/(k^3)^2 - (J_a^1 J_b^1 + J_a^2 J_b^2)/k^2; the transverse part equals a sum over J^1 +- iJ^2 states.

**Concepts:** photon propagator, current conservation in momentum space, Coulomb (instantaneous) versus retarded decomposition, transverse photon polarizations, circular polarization and helicity

### Example · intro · GA §47.2 p.516 (pdf 533)

**Problem:** Show that the circular photon states found in Example 47.2 carry one unit of angular momentum along the propagation axis.

**Method:** Parametrise the transverse current as j(cos phi, sin phi); the two combinations become j e^{+i phi} and j e^{-i phi}; act with the angular momentum operator about z.

**Key insight:** Phase winding number under rotation equals the helicity.

**Result:** Helicity +-1 (with the book's L_z sign convention, e^{i phi} is labelled +1).

**Concepts:** circular polarization and helicity, helicity

### Example · standard · GA §47.3 p.516 (pdf 533)

**Problem:** Expand the uncorrected graviton exchange amplitude into components for k along z.

**Method:** Contract T_a^{alpha beta} eta_{alpha mu} eta_{beta nu} T_b^{mu nu}: each time index gives a factor -1, symmetric off-diagonal pairs double up, and divide by k^2 = -(k^0)^2 + (k^3)^2.

**Key insight:** The T^00 T^00 term has the opposite relative sign to the photon's J^0 J^0 term (two eta_00 factors instead of one), which is why tensor exchange attracts like sources.

**Result:** Eqn 47.15 with ten types of component products.

**Concepts:** graviton exchange amplitude, Newtonian limit of graviton exchange, spin of the mediator and sign of the force

### Example 47.3 · challenging · GA §47.3 p.517 (pdf 534)

**Problem:** Isolate the propagating part of the graviton amplitude and remove the spurious spin-0 contribution so that only two polarizations remain.

**Method:** Apply k_mu T^{mu nu} = 0 to eliminate all T^{3 nu}; group the k^0-independent instantaneous terms (47.19) and the retarded terms (47.20); add alpha times the product of traces and keep its transverse piece (47.21); choose alpha so that the retarded part contains only two independent structures; symmetrise T^12.

**Key insight:** A rank-2 source contains a scalar trace; a free coefficient tuned by counting polarizations cancels it, and the required value alpha = 1/2 is exactly Einstein's trace-reversal factor.

**Result:** Retarded part proportional to (1/2)(T^11 - T^22)_a(T^11 - T^22)_b + (1/2)(T^12 + T^21)_a(T^12 + T^21)_b; polarization tensors A^11 = -A^22 = 1/sqrt2 and A^12 = A^21 = 1/sqrt2.

**Concepts:** energy-momentum conservation in momentum space, trace contamination and trace subtraction, graviton polarizations, Coulomb (instantaneous) versus retarded decomposition

### Example · standard · GA §47.3 p.518 (pdf 535)

**Problem:** Confirm that the two graviton polarization structures correspond to helicity +-2.

**Method:** Rewrite the retarded bracket as a sum of products of (T^11 - T^22 +- 2iT^12) with complex conjugate partners (47.26); note each has the algebraic form xx - yy +- 2ixy = (x +- iy)^2 for a tensor built from a transverse vector, so it rotates with a doubled phase.

**Key insight:** Squaring a helicity-1 phase gives helicity 2; the rank of the tensor sets the winding number.

**Result:** Phases e^{+2i theta} and e^{-2i theta}; details deferred to Exercises 47.1 and 47.3.

**Concepts:** helicity, graviton polarizations, circular polarization and helicity

### Example 47.4 · standard · GA §47.3 p.518 (pdf 535)

**Problem:** From the trace-corrected amplitude, extract the graviton propagator and the field generated by a source.

**Method:** Write the amplitude as (-T_a.T_b + (1/2) T_a T_b)/k^2; factor it as T_a^{sigma tau} D_{sigma tau mu nu} T_b^{mu nu} with D symmetrised in each index pair; since the amplitude is also h_{mu nu} T_a^{mu nu}, read off h in terms of T_b.

**Key insight:** The requirement of pure spin-2 exchange reproduces the trace-reversed source of the Einstein equation without any geometry.

**Result:** D_{sigma tau mu nu} = -(1/2)(eta_{mu sigma} eta_{nu tau} + eta_{mu tau} eta_{nu sigma} - eta_{mu nu} eta_{sigma tau})/k^2 and h_{mu nu}(k) proportional to (T_{mu nu} - (1/2) eta_{mu nu} T)/k^2.

**Concepts:** graviton propagator, momentum-space weak-field Einstein equation, trace contamination and trace subtraction


## Analogies and intuitions

### A parallel civilization that invented quantum field theory before (or instead of) curved-spacetime geometry → field-theoretic route to gravity · strong · GA p.512 (pdf 529)

Frames the whole chapter: forget geometry and ask what particle-physics tools alone would suggest, leading to a massless spin-2 quantum.

**Where it breaks down:** The counterfactual only reaches the linear theory here; recovering full nonlinear GR from a spin-2 field requires self-coupling arguments not in the chapter, and the quantum version fails at high energy.

**App idea:** Opening 'alternate history' card: learner is given only Coulomb's law, Newton's law and the photon story, and must fill in a mediator table (mass, spin, polarizations) for gravity before the chapter reveals it.

### Photon exchange as a template for graviton exchange: the same calculation with one more index on the source → graviton exchange amplitude · strong · GA §47.1 p.514 (pdf 531)

Every step in 47.3 is announced as the rank-2 repetition of 47.2, so novelties (sign flip, trace term, doubled phase) stand out as consequences of the extra index.

**Where it breaks down:** Gravity is not just 'EM with an extra index': the graviton couples to its own energy, making the full theory nonlinear, and the trace problem has no photon analogue.

**App idea:** Side-by-side two-column derivation viewer that highlights in colour exactly where the rank-2 column differs from the rank-1 column.

### Sources shape the surrounding field, and that field in turn pushes the sources around → force-carrying particle · useful · GA §47.1 p.513 (pdf 530)

Connects the classical field pattern already met in electromagnetism and GR to the quantum exchange picture.

**Where it breaks down:** The classical picture has no notion of virtual particles or off-shell states; the quantum exchange picture is perturbative and not literally a sequence of events.

### Feynman diagrams as cartoons of momentum flow → Feynman diagram · useful · GA §47.2 p.514 (pdf 531)

Lets readers with no QFT treat the diagram as a bookkeeping picture of current-propagator-current.

**Where it breaks down:** Lines are not trajectories in space and the diagram is only one term in an expansion; internal lines are off-shell.

### Squaring a circular combination: (x + iy)(x + iy) = xx - yy + 2ixy → helicity · strong · GA §47.3 p.518 (pdf 535)

Shows why the graviton polarization combinations rotate with twice the phase of a vector's circular combination.

**Where it breaks down:** Treats the tensor as if built from one vector; general symmetric traceless tensors need the full rotation-matrix argument of Exercise 47.3.

**App idea:** Rotating-arrow clock: a phasor for x + iy turns by theta while a second phasor for its square turns by 2 theta; below, a ring of test masses deformed by the corresponding plus/cross mixture returns to itself after 180 degrees.

### Graviton perturbation theory behaves like Fermi's theory of the weak interaction → Planck-scale breakdown of the G E^2 expansion · useful · GA §47.3 p.518 (pdf 535)

Both have couplings with dimensions of inverse energy squared, so both break down at a characteristic energy; for the weak force new particles appeared there, suggesting new physics for gravity too.

**Where it breaks down:** The weak-interaction fix (W and Z bosons) is known; for gravity the replacement is unknown, and analogies to string theory are speculative.


## Misconceptions addressed

### The graviton is an observed, established particle like the photon. · GA p.512 (pdf 529)

**Correction:** It is hypothetical; no consistent quantum theory of gravity exists and single gravitons have never been detected. The chapter is marked skippable for this reason.

**Why tempting:** Popular accounts list the graviton alongside confirmed force carriers, and gravitational waves have been detected.

### Gravity could be mediated by a spin-1 particle just like electromagnetism. · GA §47.1 p.513 (pdf 530)

**Correction:** Vector exchange makes like charges repel; since all masses attract, the mediator must have even spin.

**Why tempting:** Gravitoelectromagnetism and the Coulomb-Newton similarity make gravity look like EM with a different charge.

### A scalar (spin-0) field is enough to describe gravity. · GA §47.1 p.514 (pdf 531)

**Correction:** Spin-0 gravity is too simple and makes wrong predictions (energetics; it also couples only to the trace of T and so would not deflect light).

**Why tempting:** Newtonian gravity is described by a single scalar potential Phi.

### A symmetric rank-2 tensor field automatically describes a particle with more than two physical polarizations (e.g. three transverse structures). · GA §47.3 p.517 (pdf 534)

**Correction:** The apparent third structure is the scalar trace; subtracting half the product of traces leaves exactly two helicity states.

**Why tempting:** Counting independent transverse components of a symmetric 2x2 block gives three.

### The Coulomb (or Newtonian) term in the amplitude means the force acts instantaneously, faster than light. · GA §47.2 p.515 (pdf 532)

**Correction:** Instantaneity is an artefact of splitting a covariant amplitude in a particular frame; the full amplitude is causal.

**Why tempting:** The term's Fourier transform literally contains delta(t_a - t_b).

### Gravity can be quantized perturbatively in the same way as QED. · GA §47.3 p.518 (pdf 535)

**Correction:** Graviton perturbation theory is non-renormalizable: higher orders grow like (G E^2)^n and the expansion fails near the Planck energy.

**Why tempting:** The lowest-order calculation in this chapter works smoothly and reproduces the weak-field Einstein equation.

### Feynman diagram lines show paths that particles actually follow through space. · GA §47.2 p.514 (pdf 531)

**Correction:** Diagrams are momentum-space symbols for terms in an amplitude; internal lines are off-shell virtual states, not trajectories.

**Why tempting:** The drawings look like spacetime pictures of particles bouncing.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### Virtual particles always give finite-range forces, so a force carried by virtual photons or gravitons should be short-ranged. · GA §47.1 p.513 (pdf 530)

**Correction:** The range scales as hbar/(mc); for massless carriers arbitrarily small momentum transfers are allowed, giving the infinite-range 1/r law.

**Why tempting:** The margin note on virtual particles says they must have finite range, without qualifying the massless case.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### Detecting gravitational waves with LIGO means gravitons have been detected. · GA p.519 (pdf 536)

**Correction:** LIGO measures a classical wave containing an astronomically large number of quanta; Exercise 47.4 shows single-graviton energies are tiny compared with any realistic radiated power.

**Why tempting:** For light, detecting waves and photons often go together.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### Recovering T - (1/2) eta T from graviton exchange means the full Einstein equation follows from this chapter. · GA §47.3 p.518 (pdf 535)

**Correction:** Only the linearized, flat-background equation is recovered; full GR needs the graviton's coupling to its own energy-momentum.

**Why tempting:** The authors present the trace-reversed form as the familiar Einstein structure.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*


## Thought experiments

### Gravity in a world without geometry · GA p.512 (pdf 529)

**Setup:** Physicists fluent in quantum field theory but ignorant of curved spacetime try to build a theory of gravity from the known facts: a 1/r potential, universal attraction, coupling to all energy.

**Lesson:** Those facts alone point to a massless, spin-2 carrier coupled to T^{mu nu}, and the linear field equation they reach coincides with weak-field GR.

**App idea:** Guided decision tree: each known fact about gravity prunes a grid of candidate mediators (mass 0 or not, spin 0-4) until one cell survives.

### Emitting a graviton by waving your arms · GA p.519 (pdf 536)

**Setup:** A person swings their arms back and forth; estimate the gravitational-wave power from the quadrupole formula and compare with the energy hbar omega of one graviton at that frequency.

**Lesson:** Everyday gravitational radiation is so feeble that producing even one graviton would take far longer than a human lifetime, illustrating why quantum gravity effects are unobservable in practice.

**App idea:** Estimator slider: choose moving mass, arm length and frequency; show radiated power, graviton energy and a 'time to first graviton' counter compared with human lifetimes and the age of the universe.

### Rotating the detector about the beam · GA §47.3 p.518 (pdf 535)

**Setup:** Rotate the transverse coordinate axes by theta about the propagation direction of a photon or graviton and track how its polarization components change.

**Lesson:** Linear polarizations mix, but circular combinations change only by a phase e^{i h theta}; h = +-1 for photons and +-2 for gravitons, so a graviton pattern repeats after 180 degrees and plus turns into cross at 45 degrees.

**App idea:** 3D beam with a draggable rotation dial; show an arrow (photon) and a ring of test masses (graviton) together with the phases of their circular components on complex-plane clocks.


## Applications and observations

- **Long-range versus short-range forces and mediator mass** (numerical-estimate, GA §47.1 p.513 (pdf 530)): Massless carriers give 1/r potentials (electromagnetism, Newtonian gravity); massive ones give Yukawa screening. As an illustration not in the book, pion exchange with mc^2 of about 140 MeV gives a nuclear-force range near 1.4 fm. Key numbers: Range = hbar/(mc) = hbar c/(mc^2); hbar c = 197 MeV fm (own illustration)
- **Upper bound on the graviton mass from gravitational-wave observations (not in book)** (observation, GA §47.1 p.513 (pdf 530)): A massive graviton would make gravitational waves dispersive; LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA compact-binary signals constrain this, consistent with the chapter's m = 0 argument. Added for tutor context. Key numbers: m_g below about 1.3e-23 eV/c^2 (GWTC-3 era bound; own addition)
- **Gravitational-wave luminosity and graviton emission rate of the Earth-Sun system (Exercise 47.4a)** (numerical-estimate, GA p.519 (pdf 536)): Plugging solar and Earth masses and 1 AU into the circular-binary power formula gives a tiny classical power; dividing by the energy of a graviton at twice the orbital frequency gives the emission rate. Values below are our own estimates. Key numbers: L ~ 200 W; omega_GW = 2 x 2 pi/yr ~ 4e-7 rad/s; hbar omega ~ 4e-41 J; about 5e42 gravitons per second (own estimates)
- **Time to emit one graviton by waving arms (Exercise 47.4b)** (numerical-estimate, GA p.519 (pdf 536)): Treating a few kilograms moving about a metre at a couple of hertz as a rotating quadrupole gives a radiated power of order 1e-45 W versus a graviton energy of order 1e-33 J. Our own rough estimate; the answer depends strongly on assumptions. Key numbers: Roughly 1e12 s, i.e. tens of thousands of years (own estimate)
- **Breakdown energy of perturbative quantum gravity** (numerical-estimate, GA §47.3 p.518 (pdf 535)): Margin note 12 places the failure of the G E^2 series at E ~ G^{-1/2} in natural units; restoring constants this is the Planck energy. Key numbers: E_Planck = sqrt(hbar c^5/G) ~ 1.2e19 GeV; Planck length ~ 1.6e-35 m (own conversion); Fermi-theory analogue near a few hundred GeV

## Historical notes

- **Hideki Yukawa (1935):** Proposed (in 1935; the year is not given in the book) that nuclear forces come from the exchange of a massive particle. This introduced the idea that interactions are themselves carried by particles and that the carrier's mass sets the range. — Supplies the exchange-particle principle on which the chapter's graviton argument rests. A margin note on pdf p.529 gives only his life dates (1907-1981); the reading-copy export dropped this note. (GA §47.1 p.512 (pdf 529))
- **Richard Feynman (1995):** Feynman's Caltech gravitation course (early 1960s) asked how a field theorist ignorant of geometry would describe gravity; the notes were published as Lectures on Gravitation. — The chapter explicitly follows Feynman's route, including the spin-2 amplitude and trace subtraction. (GA p.512 (pdf 529))
- **Richard Feynman, Abhay Ashtekar (2004):** Perturbative quantization of gravity along these lines was among the early approaches to quantum gravity and ultimately failed; the authors point to Ashtekar's historical review (arXiv gr-qc/0410054). — Places the chapter within the long, unfinished history of quantum gravity. (GA p.512 (pdf 529))

## Notation and conventions

- **Metric signature and k^2:** eta = diag(-1, 1, 1, 1), so for k = (k^0, 0, 0, k^3) the denominator is k^2 = -(k^0)^2 + (k^3)^2; on-shell massless quanta have k^2 = 0. — Many QFT texts use (+,-,-,-), which flips the sign of k^2 and of propagators. (GA §47.2 p.515 (pdf 532))
- **Weak-field index gymnastics:** Gravity is represented by the field h(x) on flat spacetime; all indices are raised and lowered with eta. (GA p.512 (pdf 529))
- **Bold k and momentum space:** Bold k denotes the four-momentum (wavevector) of the exchanged quantum; fields and amplitudes are written as functions of k after Fourier transforming. — Units with hbar = c = 1 are implicit in the QFT expressions; G then has dimensions of inverse energy squared. (GA §47.2 p.514 (pdf 531))
- **Source labels a and b:** Subscripts a and b label the two interacting particles or distributions (J_a, J_b, T_a, T_b); in eqn 47.16 and Example 47.4 a prime (T') is used for source a and bare T for source b, with T also denoting a trace. — The symbol T is overloaded (tensor versus its trace); a novice must read context. (GA §47.3 p.517 (pdf 534))
- **Sign of the amplitude:** With A = -J_a eta J_b/k^2, a positive static term means repulsion (Coulomb between like charges) and a negative static term means attraction (Newtonian gravity). (GA §47.2 p.515 (pdf 532))
- **Densities as time components:** J^0 = rho (charge density) and T^00 = rho (mass-energy density). (GA §47.3 p.516 (pdf 533))
- **Spin label S and helicity h:** S denotes the spin of the field (0 scalar, 1 vector/1-form, 2 symmetric tensor); h denotes helicity in the exercises, defined by the phase e^{i h theta} under rotation about the momentum. — The main text calls helicity states 'spin +-1' loosely. (GA §47.1 p.513 (pdf 530))
- **Angular momentum operator sign:** The text uses L_z = i d/dphi when assigning spin +-1 to j e^{+-i phi}. — Standard quantum mechanics uses L_z = -i hbar d/dphi, under which e^{i phi} has +1; the book's operator would assign the opposite sign. (GA §47.2 p.516 (pdf 533))
- **Symbol alpha:** alpha is a constant in the Yukawa exponent in Example 47.1 and, separately, the adjustable coefficient of the trace term in eqn 47.16. — Two unrelated meanings within four pages. (GA §47.3 p.517 (pdf 534))
- **Rotation matrices in the exercises:** R(theta) rotates about z with +sin theta in the (1,2) slot; index placement is treated loosely, with R^i_j = R_i^j assumed. — Sign of theta convention determines whether eps_11 + i eps_12 is labelled h = +2 or -2. (GA p.519 (pdf 536))
- **Tilde on A and D:** A-tilde denotes the electromagnetic potential as a 1-form and D-tilde the photon propagator tensor; the tilde follows the book's 1-form notation from earlier chapters. (GA §47.2 p.514 (pdf 531))

## Teaching gems

### Read the mediator's mass off the force law: potentials are Green's functions, and only a massless carrier gives an unscreened 1/r. · GA §47.1 p.513 (pdf 530)

**Why it works:** Turns an abstract particle property into something learners can see in a familiar potential, and applies identically to photons and gravitons.

**App idea:** Mass slider driving a Yukawa curve against a Coulomb curve on log-log axes, with the range hbar/(mc) marked and a real-world label (pion, W boson, photon, graviton bound).

### Explain why tensor exchange attracts where vector exchange repels by counting minus signs from eta_00: the J^0 J^0 term carries one, the T^00 T^00 term carries two. · GA §47.3 p.516 (pdf 533)

**Why it works:** Gives an elementary, checkable reason for the spin-sign rule that the text asserts, using only the component expansions already in eqns 47.8 and 47.15.

**App idea:** Index-counter widget: learner picks the rank of the source; the tool highlights each time index in the contraction and multiplies the -1 factors live, ending with an attract/repel badge.

### Do the photon first, then repeat identical steps for the graviton so that every new feature is exactly what the extra index brings. · GA §47.1 p.514 (pdf 531)

**Why it works:** Learners consolidate the easier case and then see a controlled difference instead of a wall of new algebra.

**App idea:** Two synchronized derivation columns (photon, graviton) stepped by one 'next move' button, with differences highlighted in colour.

### Use conservation in momentum space to kill the longitudinal components, then separate a k^0-independent (Coulomb or Newton) term from a propagating pole term that holds the physical polarizations. · GA §47.2 p.515 (pdf 532)

**Why it works:** Shows in one algebraic move why massless gauge particles have only transverse polarizations and how static forces sit inside the same amplitude.

**App idea:** 4x4 component grid of T (or 4-vector of J) where rows and columns light up as conservation eliminates them, and remaining cells are colour-coded instantaneous versus propagating.

### Introduce an unknown coefficient alpha for the trace term and fix it by demanding exactly two polarizations; alpha = 1/2 turns out to be Einstein's trace reversal. · GA §47.3 p.517 (pdf 534)

**Why it works:** The famous factor of one half is derived from a physical requirement rather than memorized, and it links quantum polarization counting to the classical field equation.

**App idea:** Alpha slider with a live count of independent retarded structures (3 for generic alpha, 2 at exactly 1/2), plus a readout of the resulting source term T - alpha eta T.

### Identify helicity by phase winding: x + iy rotates once, (x + iy)^2 rotates twice, so graviton states pick up e^{+-2i theta}. · GA §47.3 p.518 (pdf 535)

**Why it works:** A picture-level explanation of spin 2 that needs no group theory and connects to the 180-degree symmetry of the plus pattern.

**App idea:** Rotation dial about the beam axis showing a vector arrow, a plus/cross ring of test masses, and complex-plane clocks for the h = 1 and h = 2 phases.

### Recover a known classical equation from an entirely different route as a consistency check (momentum-space weak-field Einstein equation from spin-2 exchange). · GA §47.3 p.518 (pdf 535)

**Why it works:** Builds trust in both approaches and shows learners that GR's structure is not arbitrary.

**App idea:** Split view: left, the geometric chain (metric perturbation, linearized Ricci, trace reversal); right, the field-theory chain (amplitude, trace subtraction, propagator); both converge on the same boxed equation.

### Extension (not in the text but follows from eqn 47.27): because light has traceless T, its coupling to a static mass is twice that of slow matter with the same energy, the origin of GR's doubled light deflection, and a scalar theory would give none. · GA §47.3 p.518 (pdf 535)

**Why it works:** Gives the trace term an observable consequence and explains why spin 0 fails, closing a gap the book leaves to Exercise 42.13.

**App idea:** Toggle the probe between dust and a photon; the calculator evaluates -T_a.T_b + (1/2) T_a T_b and animates the deflection angle, with a spin-0 option that shows zero bending for light.

### Count gravitons emitted by the Earth-Sun system and by a person waving their arms. · GA p.519 (pdf 536)

**Why it works:** Huge and tiny numbers make vivid why gravitational waves are classical and single gravitons undetectable.

**App idea:** Scale explorer comparing radiated power, graviton energy and emission rate for arm-waving, Earth-Sun, Hulse-Taylor pulsar and a black-hole merger on a logarithmic ladder.


## Gaps and pitfalls

- **The photon helicity assignment uses L_z = i d/dphi, the opposite sign to the standard -i hbar d/dphi, so the stated +1/-1 labels for e^{i phi}/e^{-i phi} do not follow from the operator as written.** — A careful reader computing the eigenvalues gets the opposite signs and may doubt their understanding. *Suggestion:* Use L_z = -i hbar d/dphi and note that only the magnitude of helicity matters for the argument.
- **Conservation relations are printed with lower-index components (-k_0 J^0 + k_3 J^3 = 0 and k_0 T^{0 nu} = k_3 T^{3 nu}), but with signature (-,+,+,+) they are only correct for upper-index k.** — Sign errors when reproducing Examples 47.2-47.3. *Suggestion:* Write k^0 J^0 = k^3 J^3 and k^0 T^{0 nu} = k^3 T^{3 nu} explicitly with upper indices.
- **Just before eqn 47.16 the amplitude is quoted as T'(1/k^2)T without the minus sign of eqn 47.12; with that sign, alpha would need to be -1/2 rather than +1/2.** — Confusion about the sign of the trace correction. *Suggestion:* Keep the minus sign throughout: A = (-T_a.T_b + alpha T_a T_b)/k^2 with alpha = +1/2.
- **Eqn 47.25 lists A^11 = -A^22 = 1/sqrt2 and A^12 = A^21 = 1/sqrt2 on one line, which reads like a single tensor.** — Learners may think one polarization tensor has all four entries non-zero. *Suggestion:* Present two separate normalised tensors, e_plus = diag(1, -1)/sqrt2 and e_cross = offdiag(1, 1)/sqrt2, in the transverse block.
- **Eqn 47.11 writes h proportional to T/k^2 and eqn 47.29 writes h proportional to (T - (1/2) eta T)/k^2 using the same symbol h; the first is really the trace-reversed h-bar of Chapter 45.** — Apparent contradiction between two boxed-looking results in the same section. *Suggestion:* Label 47.11 as h-bar (Lorenz gauge) and 47.29 as h, and show that trace reversal maps one to the other.
- **Adding alpha T_a T_b/k^2 changes not only the retarded part but also the instantaneous T^00 T^00 coefficient (by a factor 1/2) and adds instantaneous pressure couplings; the text is silent on this and on the observable consequence (light bends twice as much as a Newtonian estimate).** — Learners may think the Newtonian term 47.14 is untouched, and miss the most physical payoff of the trace term. *Suggestion:* Show the static limit of eqn 47.27 for dust and for radiation, absorb the 1/2 into G, and derive the factor-of-2 light coupling.
- **Margin note 3 says virtual particles must have finite range, without qualifying that the range hbar/(mc) is infinite for massless carriers.** — Seems to contradict the use of massless photons and gravitons for infinite-range forces. *Suggestion:* State the range as hbar/(mc) and explain that massless exchange allows arbitrarily small momentum transfers.
- **The rule that odd spin repels like charges and even spin attracts is asserted without derivation, spin 4 and higher are not excluded, and spin 0 is dismissed only by a pointer to an exercise.** — Key selection steps feel like arguments from authority. *Suggestion:* Show the eta_00 sign counting, mention that coupling to the conserved rank-2 T singles out spin 2, and give the light-bending objection to scalar gravity.
- **The terms 'instantaneous' and 'retarded' are used loosely: eqn 47.19 labelled instantaneous still contains (k^0)^2/(k^3)^2, and the reason the pole term carries the physical polarizations (on-shell residue) is not stated.** — Learners cannot tell why only one part of the amplitude counts polarizations. *Suggestion:* Explain that physical particles live at the pole k^2 = 0, where k^0 = k^3, and that polarizations are read from the residue there.
- **The chapter recovers only the linearized field equation; the known result that a consistently self-coupled spin-2 field rebuilds full nonlinear GR is not mentioned.** — Learners may overestimate what was derived, or conversely think the field approach cannot reach GR at all. *Suggestion:* Add a short note that gravitons carry energy and must couple to themselves, and that iterating this classically reproduces Einstein's theory, while the quantum iteration is non-renormalizable.
- **Overloaded symbols: T for both the energy-momentum tensor and its trace, a prime to mark source a, and alpha used for both the Yukawa parameter and the trace coefficient; the reading copy also garbles T' in Example 47.4.** — Hard to parse equations 47.16 and the propagator step. *Suggestion:* Use T_a, T_b for tensors, tr T_a, tr T_b for traces, and a different letter (e.g. c_tr) for the trace coefficient.
- **The summary says the graviton has spin 2 because its source is a rank-2 tensor, and restates the uncorrected amplitude (47.30) rather than the trace-corrected 47.27.** — Oversimplifies (a rank-2 tensor also contains spin 0) and could lead learners to memorise the wrong amplitude. *Suggestion:* In the summary quote eqn 47.27 and say that rank 2 allows spin 2 once the trace part is removed.
- **Footnote 1 misspells Ashtekar as 'Ashketar'.** — Minor; makes the reference harder to find. *Suggestion:* Cite A. Ashtekar, arXiv:gr-qc/0410054.

## Tutor notes

- Say up front that the graviton is hypothetical and the chapter is optional; frame it as 'what would particle physics say about gravity?' rather than established physics.
- Opening questions: 'Why does the Coulomb force reach across the universe but the nuclear force stop at a nucleus?' and 'Electric charges can repel; can two masses ever repel? What might that tell us about the particle carrying gravity?'
- Order of explanation: exchange-particle idea -> mediator mass and range (Yukawa vs Coulomb) -> force sign and spin -> photon amplitude and its split -> photon polarizations and helicity -> graviton amplitude as the rank-2 replay -> trace problem and alpha = 1/2 -> helicity +-2 -> propagator and Einstein trace reversal -> why quantum gravity fails -> graviton-counting estimates.
- For novices, stay at the level of three facts (massless because 1/r, spin 2 because always attractive and coupled to energy-momentum, two polarizations like gravitational waves) and use the rotation picture: a spin-s pattern looks the same after 360/s degrees, so the plus pattern repeats after 180 degrees.
- For advanced learners, have them redo Example 47.3 themselves with upper-index conservation relations and then take the static limit of eqn 47.27 for dust and for light to find the factor of 2.
- Check for understanding: ask why the J^0 J^0 term is repulsive while T^00 T^00 is attractive (expected answer: one versus two factors of eta_00).
- Check for understanding: ask what goes wrong if alpha is left at zero (expected: an extra spin-0 structure, three apparent polarizations) and what the value 1/2 corresponds to in the Einstein equation.
- Check for understanding: ask how a helicity-2 polarization changes under a 90-degree rotation about the beam (expected: phase e^{+-i pi} = -1, so plus flips sign; at 45 degrees plus becomes cross).
- Common question 'Did LIGO detect gravitons?': no; it detected classical waves. Use Exercise 47.4 numbers (about 5e42 gravitons per second from Earth's orbit, each carrying about 4e-41 J) to show why individual quanta are invisible.
- Common question 'Why can't we just quantize gravity like light?': explain that G has dimensions of 1/energy^2 in natural units, so each order brings G E^2 and the series fails near the Planck energy, about 1e19 GeV; mention the Fermi-theory analogy.
- Common question 'Is the Coulomb term really instantaneous?': no; it is how the causal amplitude looks when split in one frame and one gauge.
- Common question 'Why not spin 4?': higher-spin massless fields cannot couple consistently to the conserved energy-momentum tensor at low energy; spin 2 is the unique match (beyond the book, state it lightly).
- Watch for learners conflating h with h-bar between eqns 47.11 and 47.29, and for index-position sign slips in the conservation relations.
- Link back to Chapter 46's plus/cross ring animations when discussing polarizations; the circular states here are complex combinations of those linear ones.
