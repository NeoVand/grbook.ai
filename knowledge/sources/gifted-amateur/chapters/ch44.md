# GA ch44 · Gauge fields

> Shows that the covariant derivative of general relativity has a twin in field theory: demanding that a complex scalar field be invariant under position-dependent phase rotations forces in a compensating 1-form (the electromagnetic potential), a gauge covariant derivative D = d + iqA that plays the role of the connection, and, via transport round a tiny loop, a field strength [D,D] = iqF that plays the role of the Riemann tensor, all pictured as a fibre bundle of phase circles over spacetime.

**Pages:** printed 476–484 · pdf 493–501 · **Difficulty:** math 3/5, conceptual 4/5, novice-friendliness 3/5

Algebra is light (product rule, commutators, second-order Taylor composition), but the chapter asks readers to hold three languages at once (GR connections, gauge fields, bundles) and leans on Chapters 7, 11, 35, 40 and 42. The loop derivation is heuristic and contains a commutator typo, the horizontal-transport condition drops a factor of psi, and fibre bundles are only pictured, not defined. Exercise 44.1 is markedly harder than the text, switching to a non-abelian convention without comment.

## Role in the book

Part VI turns from geometry to fields. Chapter 40 built Lagrangian field theory, Chapter 42 introduced the electromagnetic 4-potential and its gauge freedom, and Chapter 43 tied charge conservation to the Bianchi identity. This short chapter unifies those threads with the geometric machinery of Parts II and V (covariant derivative from Chapter 7, loop curvature from Chapters 11 and 35) by presenting gauge fields as connections on a bundle of internal spaces. It supplies the vocabulary of gauge freedom that Chapter 45 exploits for linearized gravity, that Chapter 46 uses to treat gravitational waves as waves in a gauge theory, and that Chapter 48 needs when an internal phase is promoted to an extra spacetime dimension.

## Learning objectives

- Reader can explain what an internal variable of a field is and why a phase rotation is not a rotation in physical space.
- Reader can describe a fibre bundle in terms of base space, fibre and bundle, and give the tangent bundle and the U(1) phase-circle bundle as examples.
- Reader can distinguish a global phase transformation from a local one and show that the complex scalar Lagrangian is invariant only under the global kind.
- Reader can carry out the product-rule calculation showing which extra terms a position-dependent phase generates in the kinetic term.
- Reader can define the gauge covariant derivative D_mu = partial_mu + iqA_mu and verify that D_mu psi picks up the same phase factor as psi when A shifts by -(1/q) partial_mu alpha.
- Reader can identify the compensating transformation of A with electromagnetic gauge freedom by setting chi = alpha/q.
- Reader can set up the parallel-transport analogy delta psi = -iqA_nu psi dx^nu versus delta v^mu = -Gamma^mu_{nu beta} v^beta dx^nu, and state which object plays the role of the connection.
- Reader can explain how transporting a field round an infinitesimal loop yields a change proportional to the commutator [D_mu, D_nu] times the loop area, and compute [D_mu, D_nu] = iqF_mu nu for the U(1) case.
- Reader can fill in the three-column dictionary linking general relativity (coordinate change, Gamma, Riemann), gauge theory (local phase change, A, iqF) and bundles (strained bundle, connection, curvature).
- Reader can state the limits of the analogy and recognise where later chapters (weak fields, gravitational waves, Kaluza-Klein) reuse gauge ideas.

## Teaching approach

A compact, analogy-driven chapter that never introduces a new mathematical tool without tying it to one the reader already owns. It opens and closes on the covariant derivative of relativity, uses pictures of arrows, knobs and hatched strips to make the invisible internal space concrete, stages a deliberate failure (local phase change spoils invariance) to motivate the gauge field, and then replays the Chapter 11 loop computation with D in place of the connection so that the Faraday tensor emerges as a curvature. A closing three-way dictionary table is the payoff. Rigour about bundles is deferred to Appendix C; the argument is heuristic but tightly sequenced.

**Style:** analogy-driven, physical-motivation-first, diagram-driven, spiral-revisit, conversational-informal, example-driven, geometric-first

**Narrative arc**

1. Recall: a vector's change on displacement has an intrinsic part and a basis-change part, which defined the covariant derivative (eqn 44.1).
2. Picture a complex field as a grid of phase arrows and rotate them all by one angle to illustrate an internal symmetry (Fig. 44.1).
3. Ask where the phase lives: not in spacetime but in a fibre above each event, introducing base space, fibre and bundle (Fig. 44.2, Example 44.1).
4. Make the fibre a circle so the phase becomes a knob at each point; a global transformation twists all knobs equally (Fig. 44.3).
5. Write the complex scalar field Lagrangian and confirm global U(1) invariance.
6. Pose the provocative question of letting the twist vary from point to point and compute the resulting non-invariance (Example 44.2).
7. Repair it by adding a 1-form gauge field with a compensating shift, building D_mu, and verify invariance (Example 44.3).
8. Recognise the compensating shift as the electromagnetic gauge transformation of Chapter 42 and name the construction a gauge theory.
9. Reinterpret: point-dependent shifts strain the bundle and A is the connection that accounts for the strain.
10. Return to parallel transport: write delta v for vectors (Example 44.4) and delta psi for the charged field, then build D as total change minus transport change, reading off A as the analogue of Gamma.
11. Generalise parallel transport to horizontal transport through the bundle (Example 44.5, Fig. 44.4).
12. Carry the field round an infinitesimal loop, expand to second order and extract the commutator times the area as the field strength (Examples 44.6-44.7).
13. Compare with the commutator of nabla giving Riemann (eqn 44.24) and summarise in a GR / gauge theory / bundle dictionary table.
14. Exercises extend the cube-of-loops Bianchi argument to a gauge field and ask for the scalar field's energy-momentum tensor.

**Signature moves**

- The same words-equation template, change of the object minus change attributable to the reference frame, is written for both nabla (eqn 44.13) and D (eqn 44.17), so the reader sees one idea applied twice.
- Break-then-repair: first show explicitly which cross terms a local phase introduces, then introduce exactly the field needed to cancel them.
- The same internal space is drawn four ways in the order the argument needs them: phase arrows on a grid (Fig. 44.1), tubes standing over a curve (Fig. 44.2), knob circles seen from above (Fig. 44.3), and a hatched strip crossed by a horizontal curve (Fig. 44.4).
- Replays the loop-transport curvature derivation from Chapter 11 almost line for line with D replacing the geometric covariant derivative.
- Grounds the unfamiliar bundle concept first in the tangent bundle the reader already knows, then isospin, then Newtonian spacetime as a bundle over time.
- Borrows a solid-state image (a strained crystal whose atoms are displaced by position-dependent amounts) to name the strained bundle.
- Ends on a compact three-column dictionary table rather than a prose recap.

## Section by section

### Chapter opening: two reasons a vector changes — p.476 (pdf 493)

After a Chesterton epigraph about change, the authors recall that when you move and see a vector's components alter, part of the alteration is real and part comes from the coordinate grid shifting underneath; that split defined the covariant derivative in Chapter 7. They announce that this split has a close cousin in field theories with local symmetries, where a derivative D_mu does the same job. A boxed pointer says gauge ideas will pay off for weak fields, gravitational waves and higher-dimensional theories, and a footnote explains the historical, rather unhelpful, origin of the word gauge.

**Concepts:** covariant derivative, connection coefficients, gauge covariant derivative

**Key moves**
- Restate nabla as change in the vector minus change due to the coordinate system, with eqn 44.1 as its component form.
- Flag the analogy between nabla_mu and a field-theory covariant derivative D_mu as the chapter's organising theme.

### §44.1 Fibre bundles and gauge invariance — p.476 (pdf 493)

Fields assign values to every spacetime point and a theory is specified by its Lagrangian; a transformation leaving the Lagrangian unchanged is a symmetry. Besides coordinates, fields can carry internal variables such as a phase, drawn as an arrow at each point; rotating every arrow by the same angle is an internal symmetry if the Lagrangian does not notice. Because the phase is not a direction in spacetime, the authors introduce the fibre bundle: a base space with a fibre of internal values attached to each point. Example 44.1 lists the tangent bundle, isospin and Newtonian spacetime as bundles. For a single-valued phase the fibre is a circle, pictured as a turnable knob. The complex scalar field Lagrangian is invariant under a constant phase shift (global U(1)), but Example 44.2 shows that a position-dependent shift generates extra derivative-of-alpha terms. Introducing a 1-form gauge field A with D_mu = partial_mu + iqA_mu and the compensating rule A -> A - (1/q) partial alpha makes D psi transform like psi, so the Lagrangian with D in place of partial is locally invariant (Example 44.3). Identifying chi = alpha/q shows the compensating rule is the electromagnetic gauge transformation of Chapter 42; the result is called a gauge theory, the basis of particle physics, superconductivity and quantum Hall physics. In bundle language, point-dependent shifts produce a strained bundle and A introduces the strain into the connection.

**Concepts:** internal variable, symmetry of a Lagrangian, internal symmetry, amplitude-phase representation of a complex field, fibre bundle, base space, fibre, tangent bundle, isospin, Newtonian spacetime as a bundle over time, U(1) circle fibre, complex scalar field theory, global U(1) phase symmetry, global transformation, local transformation, local gauge invariance, gauge field, gauge covariant derivative, gauge transformation, gauge theory, electric charge as gauge coupling, strained bundle, bundle connection

**Key moves**
- Separate spacetime dependence of a field from internal variables it carries, using the phase of rho e^{i theta} as the example.
- Place the internal variable in a fibre above each point, making the field a structure on a bundle over the base space.
- Use single-valuedness (theta ~ theta + 2 pi) to make the fibre a unit circle.
- Show global invariance of eqn 44.2 under eqn 44.3 because psi-dagger picks up the opposite phase.
- Compute the transformed kinetic term under a local phase and exhibit the non-cancelling terms (eqns 44.4-44.6).
- Introduce A_mu and D_mu (eqn 44.7) with a transformation law (eqn 44.8) chosen to cancel those terms; verify D psi -> e^{i alpha} D psi (eqn 44.9).
- Match the compensating law to A -> A - partial chi (eqn 44.11) with chi = alpha/q.
- Translate into bundle language: local shift = strained bundle, partial_mu compares neighbouring fibres, A supplies the connection.

### §44.2 Parallel transport and field strength — p.480 (pdf 497)

The naive difference psi(x+dx) - psi(x) mixes genuine change with a change of reference frame in the internal space. The authors restate the geometric covariant derivative as total change minus frame change and, in Example 44.4, reduce nabla_u v = 0 to the transport rule delta v = -Gamma v dx. By analogy they posit the transport rule delta psi = -iqA psi dx for the charged field and define D psi = d psi - delta psi, which reproduces D_mu = partial_mu + iqA_mu and shows A playing the role of Gamma (with a footnote noting the vector case needs extra indices). Example 44.5 recasts parallel transport as horizontal transport through the bundle, where D_mu psi = 0. Example 44.6 carries psi round an infinitesimal quadrilateral, expanding each leg to second order; first-order and square terms cancel, leaving a change equal to the loop area times the commutator [D_mu, D_nu], named the gauge field strength Q_mu nu. Example 44.7 evaluates it for U(1): iq(partial_mu A_nu - partial_nu A_mu) = iqF_mu nu, so the Faraday tensor is the curvature of the bundle. The commutator of nabla giving the Riemann tensor (Chapter 35) completes the parallel, summarised in a table matching coordinate transformation / local phase transformation / strained bundle, connection coefficient / gauge field / bundle connection, and Riemann curvature / iqF / bundle curvature. The section closes by noting that U(1) is the simplest of many gauge theories, all with field strength given by the commutator, and that Chapter 48 will try to house an internal parameter in an extra dimension.

**Concepts:** parallel transport of a vector, parallel transport of a charged field, covariant derivative, gauge covariant derivative, gauge field as connection, horizontal transport, holonomy of an infinitesimal loop, commutator of covariant derivatives as curvature operator, gauge field strength, Faraday tensor as U(1) field strength, bundle curvature, Riemann tensor as commutator of covariant derivatives, gravity-gauge theory dictionary, non-abelian gauge theory

**Key moves**
- Point out that d psi = psi(x+dx) - psi(x) compares values expressed in two different internal frames.
- Cancel the tangent vector in the transport equation to isolate delta v^mu = -Gamma^mu_{nu beta} v^beta dx^nu (eqn 44.15).
- Write the matching transport change delta psi = -iqA_nu psi dx^nu (eqn 44.16) and subtract it from d psi to obtain D_mu (eqns 44.17-44.18).
- Define horizontal transport by D_mu psi = 0.
- Expand transport along each of the four legs of a loop to second order, compose, and show only the antisymmetric cross term survives (eqn 44.20).
- Name [D_mu, D_nu] the field strength (eqn 44.21) and compute it for U(1) to get iqF_mu nu (eqn 44.23).
- Place eqn 44.23 beside [nabla_mu, nabla_nu] Z = R Z (eqn 44.24) and tabulate the three-way correspondence.

### Chapter summary and exercises — p.483 (pdf 500)

A three-point summary restates fibres and bundles, the gauge covariant derivative obtained from local phase invariance, and loop transport giving [D_mu, D_nu] = iqF as the counterpart of Riemann. Exercise 44.1 follows Ryder's approach to derive a Bianchi identity for a (generally non-abelian) gauge field by traversing loops round the faces of a small cube (Fig. 44.5) in three composite paths, ending with a cyclic sum of covariant derivatives of the field strength and its link to the Jacobi identity for commutators. Exercise 44.2, a short closing task on pdf page 501, asks for the energy-momentum tensor of the complex scalar field in flat space.

**Concepts:** gauge-field Bianchi identity, Jacobi identity, energy-momentum tensor of a complex scalar field

**Key moves**
- Decompose the closed cube surface into three composite loop paths (eqn 44.25).
- Accumulate the loop factors leg by leg to leading order in the coupling g to obtain eqn 44.26.
- Relate the vanishing cyclic sum to the Jacobi identity of the D operators (eqn 44.27).

## Concepts

### internal variable

*definition · core* · also: internal degree of freedom, internal coordinate

A quantity carried by a field at each spacetime point that is not a spacetime coordinate or direction, such as the phase of a complex field or an isospin label; transformations acting on it do not move anything in spacetime.

**How introduced:** Introduced by contrasting the spacetime dependence of psi(x) with the phase theta carried in the factor e^{i theta}, pictured as an arrow at each point in Fig. 44.1.

**Prerequisites:** classical field

$$
\psi(x)=\rho(x)\,e^{i\theta(x)}
$$

**Notes:** The arrow drawing can mislead: the arrow direction is not a spatial direction.

**Where:** GA §44.1 p.476 (pdf 493); GA §44.1 p.477 (pdf 494)

### symmetry of a Lagrangian

*definition · developed* · also: symmetry of a field theory

A transformation of the fields or coordinates under which the Lagrangian takes the same value, so the equations of motion derived from it are unchanged.

**How introduced:** Stated at the start of §44.1 as the criterion used for every transformation in the chapter.

**Prerequisites:** Lagrangian density, Euler-Lagrange equation for fields

**Where:** GA §44.1 p.476 (pdf 493)

### internal symmetry

*definition · introduced*

A symmetry whose transformation acts only on internal variables of the fields (for example rotating every phase), leaving spacetime points untouched.

**How introduced:** Defined as the case where rotating the phase arrows of Fig. 44.1 leaves the Lagrangian unchanged.

**Prerequisites:** internal variable, symmetry of a Lagrangian

**Where:** GA §44.1 p.477 (pdf 494)

### amplitude-phase representation of a complex field

*technique · introduced* · also: phasor picture, arrow picture of a complex field

Writing a complex field as a real non-negative amplitude times a unit phase factor and drawing the phase as the angle of an arrow at each point of space.

**How introduced:** Used in Fig. 44.1 to make phase rotations visible; later replaced by circles with a pointer (Fig. 44.3).

**Prerequisites:** internal variable

$$
\psi=\rho e^{i\theta}
$$

**Where:** GA §44.1 p.476 (pdf 493)

### fibre bundle

*mathematical-object · core* · also: bundle, an M worth of V's (Penrose)

A space B built by attaching a copy of a fibre space V to every point of a base manifold M, locally looking like M x V, so that a field with internal variables is a choice of point in the fibre above each base point.

**How introduced:** Motivated by the question of where the phase lives, since it is not part of spacetime; illustrated by tubes floating above a curve (Fig. 44.2) and backed by familiar examples in Example 44.1. The formal treatment is deferred to Appendix C.

**Prerequisites:** manifold, tangent space, internal variable

$$
\mathcal{B}\;\sim\;\mathcal{M}\text{ with a fibre }\mathcal{V}\text{ above each point}
$$

**Notes:** Local product structure and transition functions are not discussed in this chapter.

**Where:** GA §44.1 p.477 (pdf 494); GA §44.2 p.483 (pdf 500)

### base space

*definition · introduced* · also: base manifold

The manifold over which a bundle is built; here ordinary (3+1)-dimensional spacetime M, whose points label the fibres.

**How introduced:** Named while introducing the bundle; drawn as the curve or line labelled M in Figs 44.2 and 44.4.

**Prerequisites:** manifold

**Where:** GA §44.1 p.477 (pdf 494)

### fibre

*definition · core* · also: internal space

The space of possible values of the internal variables attached above a single point of the base space, such as the unit circle of phases or the tangent space at a point.

**How introduced:** Introduced as a space floating over each spacetime point; drawn as vertical tubes (Fig. 44.2) and as circles with pointers (Fig. 44.3).

**Prerequisites:** internal variable

**Where:** GA §44.1 p.477 (pdf 494)

### tangent bundle

*mathematical-object · revisited* · also: TM

The bundle whose fibre above each point P of a manifold is the tangent space T_P of vectors at P; a vector field is a choice of vector in each fibre.

**How introduced:** First bullet of Example 44.1, used to show the reader has already met a bundle when studying tangent vectors.

**Prerequisites:** tangent space, fibre bundle

$$
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{M}=\bigcup_{\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{P}}
$$

**Where:** GA §44.1 p.477 (pdf 494)

### isospin

*physical-quantity · mention* · also: isotopic spin

An internal variable of certain matter fields that acts like a switch rotating one particle identity into another (for example proton and neutron) at a given point, describable with fibres.

**How introduced:** Second bullet of Example 44.1, as a physics example of internal space beyond phase.

**Prerequisites:** internal variable, fibre

**Where:** GA §44.1 p.477 (pdf 494)

### Newtonian spacetime as a bundle over time

*solution-or-model · mention* · also: Galilean fibration

The pre-relativistic picture in which absolute time is a one-dimensional base and each instant carries its own three-dimensional space as the fibre.

**How introduced:** Third bullet of Example 44.1; a footnote adds that general relativity replaces this layered structure with a metric on a single manifold.

**Prerequisites:** fibre bundle

**Where:** GA §44.1 p.477 (pdf 494)

### U(1) circle fibre

*mathematical-object · core* · also: phase circle, knob picture

For a single-valued complex field the phase angle is only defined modulo 2 pi, so the fibre above each point is a unit circle, the group manifold of U(1); a phase transformation turns the pointer on each circle.

**How introduced:** Derived from single-valuedness and drawn as a grid of circles with pointers at different angles (Fig. 44.3), which the authors invite the reader to think of as knobs.

**Prerequisites:** fibre, amplitude-phase representation of a complex field

$$
\theta\sim\theta+2\pi
$$

**Where:** GA §44.1 p.477 (pdf 494)

### complex scalar field theory

*solution-or-model · core* · also: U(1) theory, charged scalar field

The flat-spacetime theory of a complex-valued scalar field whose Lagrangian is minus the squared modulus of its gradient minus a mass term m^2 |psi|^2 (eqn 44.2); the simplest theory with a U(1) phase symmetry.

**How introduced:** Chosen in flat space as the concrete model on which to test global versus local phase invariance.

**Prerequisites:** Lagrangian density, Klein-Gordon equation

$$
\mathcal{L}=-(\partial^\mu\psi)^\dagger(\partial_\mu\psi)-m^2\psi^\dagger\psi
$$

**Where:** GA §44.1 p.477 (pdf 494); GA §44.1 p.478 (pdf 495)

### global U(1) phase symmetry

*principle · developed* · also: global phase symmetry, global gauge symmetry

Invariance of a theory under multiplying the field everywhere by the same constant phase factor e^{i alpha}; for the complex scalar field it holds because psi-dagger acquires the inverse factor.

**How introduced:** Checked directly on eqn 44.2 with a footnote giving the cancellation between psi and psi-dagger.

**Prerequisites:** complex scalar field theory, internal symmetry

$$
\psi\to\psi e^{i\alpha},\quad \psi^\dagger\to\psi^\dagger e^{-i\alpha}
$$

**Notes:** Noether's theorem (Chapter 40) would associate a conserved charge with it; the chapter does not make this link.

**Where:** GA §44.1 p.478 (pdf 495)

### global transformation

*definition · introduced*

A transformation whose parameter has the same value at every spacetime point, e.g. adding the same alpha to every phase.

**How introduced:** Named after eqn 44.3; in the knob picture every knob is twisted by the same amount.

**Prerequisites:** internal symmetry

$$
\theta(x)\to\theta(x)+\alpha
$$

**Where:** GA §44.1 p.478 (pdf 495)

### local transformation

*definition · core* · also: position-dependent transformation

A transformation whose parameter alpha(x) is an arbitrary smooth function of spacetime position, so different points are rotated by different amounts.

**How introduced:** Posed as a what-if question immediately after global invariance is established.

**Prerequisites:** global transformation

$$
\theta(x)\to\theta(x)+\alpha(x)
$$

**Where:** GA §44.1 p.478 (pdf 495)

### local gauge invariance

*principle · core* · also: local phase symmetry, local U(1) invariance, gauge principle

The requirement that a theory be unchanged under local transformations of its internal variables; achieving it for a field with derivatives requires introducing a gauge field that compensates for the position dependence.

**How introduced:** Motivated by showing that the ordinary kinetic term fails the test (Example 44.2) and then restoring invariance with a covariant derivative (Example 44.3).

**Prerequisites:** local transformation, global U(1) phase symmetry

$$
\mathcal{L}[\psi e^{i\alpha(x)},A-\tfrac1q\partial\alpha]=\mathcal{L}[\psi,A]
$$

**Notes:** The book presents this as a repair; the stronger modern reading (demanding local invariance predicts electromagnetism) is implicit.

**Where:** GA §44.1 p.478 (pdf 495); GA §44.1 p.479 (pdf 496)

### gauge field

*physical-quantity · core* · also: gauge potential, 1-form gauge field A, compensating field

A 1-form field A = A_mu dx^mu introduced so that derivatives of a field can be made to transform covariantly under local internal transformations; it shifts by a gradient of the transformation parameter.

**How introduced:** Introduced as the fix for the non-invariance found in Example 44.2; later identified with the electromagnetic potential when q is the charge.

**Prerequisites:** local gauge invariance, 1-form, electromagnetic 4-potential

$$
\tilde A=A_\mu\,dx^\mu
$$

$$
A_\mu\to A_\mu-\frac1q\partial_\mu\alpha
$$

**Where:** GA §44.1 p.478 (pdf 495); GA §44.1 p.479 (pdf 496)

### gauge covariant derivative

*operation · core* · also: covariant field derivative, D_mu, minimal-coupling derivative

The derivative D_mu = partial_mu + iqA_mu acting on a charged field; it removes the part of the change due to the local phase convention, so under psi -> e^{i alpha} psi with the compensating shift of A it transforms as D_mu psi -> e^{i alpha} D_mu psi.

**How introduced:** Defined in eqn 44.7 to cancel the unwanted terms, verified in Example 44.3, and then re-derived in §44.2 as total change minus parallel-transport change, directly paralleling nabla.

**Prerequisites:** gauge field, covariant derivative

$$
D_\mu\psi=\partial_\mu\psi+iqA_\mu\psi
$$

$$
D_\mu\psi\to e^{i\alpha}D_\mu\psi
$$

**Where:** GA §44.1 p.478 (pdf 495); GA §44.1 p.479 (pdf 496); GA §44.2 p.481 (pdf 498)

### gauge transformation

*definition · developed* · also: gauge freedom

The simultaneous change psi -> e^{i alpha(x)} psi and A_mu -> A_mu - (1/q) partial_mu alpha; for the potential alone it is the familiar A -> A - partial chi that leaves Maxwell's equations and F unchanged.

**How introduced:** The compensating law of eqn 44.8 is matched to the electromagnetic gauge freedom of Chapter 42 by setting chi = alpha/q.

**Prerequisites:** gauge field, Faraday tensor

$$
A_\mu\to A_\mu-\partial_\mu\chi,\quad \chi=\alpha/q
$$

**Where:** GA §44.1 p.478 (pdf 495); GA §44.1 p.479 (pdf 496)

### gauge theory

*definition · core* · also: gauge field theory

A field theory made invariant under local internal transformations by replacing ordinary derivatives with gauge covariant derivatives built from gauge fields.

**How introduced:** Named after the complex scalar example is made locally invariant by the replacement partial -> D; said to underlie particle physics, superconductivity and topological phases.

**Prerequisites:** local gauge invariance, gauge covariant derivative

$$
\partial_\mu\to D_\mu
$$

**Where:** GA §44.1 p.479 (pdf 496); GA §44.2 p.483 (pdf 500)

### electric charge as gauge coupling

*physical-quantity · mention* · also: coupling constant q

The constant q in D_mu = partial_mu + iqA_mu that sets how strongly the field couples to the gauge field; interpreted as electric charge it makes A the electromagnetic potential.

**How introduced:** Mentioned in a footnote identifying the U(1) gauge field with electromagnetism.

**Prerequisites:** gauge covariant derivative

**Where:** GA §44.1 p.479 (pdf 496)

### strained bundle

*definition · introduced*

The authors' name for a bundle after a local transformation has shifted the value stored in each fibre by a different amount, by analogy with a strained solid whose atoms are displaced by position-dependent amounts.

**How introduced:** Introduced when translating the local phase change into bundle language, and listed in the dictionary table opposite coordinate transformation and local phase transformation.

**Prerequisites:** fibre bundle, local transformation

**Notes:** Book-specific terminology; a pure local transformation alone produces no curvature, so strain here is a gauge artefact.

**Where:** GA §44.1 p.479 (pdf 496); GA §44.2 p.483 (pdf 500)

### bundle connection

*mathematical-object · core* · also: connection on a fibre bundle, gauge field as connection

A rule for comparing the values in neighbouring fibres, i.e. for saying which change counts as no change when moving across the base; in U(1) gauge theory its components are iqA_mu, playing the role that Gamma plays for tangent vectors.

**How introduced:** Introduced verbally (partial_mu connects fibres; A introduces strain into the connection) and made precise by comparing delta psi = -iqA psi dx with delta v = -Gamma v dx.

**Prerequisites:** fibre bundle, connection coefficients, gauge field

$$
iqA_\nu\;\leftrightarrow\;\Gamma^\mu{}_{\nu\beta}
$$

**Where:** GA §44.1 p.479 (pdf 496); GA §44.2 p.480 (pdf 497); GA §44.2 p.481 (pdf 498)

### covariant derivative

*operation · revisited* · also: nabla, geometric covariant derivative

The derivative of a tensor field that subtracts from the raw component change the part caused by the basis changing with position, so the result is a tensor; for vectors (nabla_alpha v)^mu = partial_alpha v^mu + Gamma^mu_{alpha beta} v^beta.

**How introduced:** Recalled from Chapter 7 in words and components at the chapter opening and again at the start of §44.2 as the template for D.

**Prerequisites:** connection coefficients, tangent space

$$
(\nabla_\alpha v)^\mu=\partial_\alpha v^\mu+\Gamma^\mu{}_{\alpha\beta}v^\beta
$$

**Where:** GA p.476 (pdf 493); GA §44.2 p.480 (pdf 497)

### connection coefficients

*mathematical-object · revisited* · also: Christoffel symbols, Gamma

The coefficients Gamma^mu_{alpha beta} that encode how basis vectors change from point to point and hence how vector components shift under parallel transport.

**How introduced:** Used as the geometric partner of the gauge field in the analogy and in the dictionary table.

**Prerequisites:** covariant derivative

$$
\delta v^\mu=-\Gamma^\mu{}_{\nu\beta}v^\beta dx^\nu
$$

**Where:** GA §44.2 p.480 (pdf 497); GA §44.2 p.483 (pdf 500)

### parallel transport of a vector

*operation · revisited*

Moving a vector along a curve so that its covariant derivative along the tangent vanishes; its components then change only by the amount the connection dictates.

**How introduced:** Example 44.4 reduces nabla_u v = 0 to the component change delta v^mu = -Gamma^mu_{nu beta} v^beta dx^nu.

**Prerequisites:** covariant derivative, connection coefficients

$$
u^\nu\partial_\nu v^\mu+\Gamma^\mu{}_{\nu\beta}v^\beta u^\nu=0
$$

**Where:** GA §44.2 p.480 (pdf 497)

### parallel transport of a charged field

*operation · core* · also: gauge parallel transport

Moving the value of a charged field to a neighbouring point so that only the gauge connection changes it: delta psi = -iqA_nu psi dx^nu, i.e. an infinitesimal phase rotation set by A.

**How introduced:** Written down by direct analogy with the vector rule and then used to define D psi = d psi - delta psi.

**Prerequisites:** bundle connection, parallel transport of a vector

$$
\delta\psi=-iqA_\nu\psi\,dx^\nu
$$

**Where:** GA §44.2 p.480 (pdf 497); GA §44.2 p.481 (pdf 498)

### horizontal transport

*operation · introduced* · also: horizontal lift, horizontal curve

Transport through the bundle along a curve whose fibre value counts as unchanged according to the connection, characterised by D_mu psi = 0; the bundle analogue of parallel transport.

**How introduced:** Example 44.5 and Fig. 44.4, where a horizontal line with arrows crosses the hatched strip representing the bundle.

**Prerequisites:** bundle connection, gauge covariant derivative

$$
D_\mu\psi=0\iff\partial_\mu\psi=-iqA_\mu\psi
$$

**Where:** GA §44.2 p.481 (pdf 498)

### holonomy of an infinitesimal loop

*phenomenon · core* · also: loop transport, Wilson loop (infinitesimal)

The change in a field (or vector) after parallel transport round a small closed loop; to leading order it equals the loop's area element contracted with a curvature operator acting on the field.

**How introduced:** Example 44.6 revisits the Chapter 11 loop computation for psi on a quadrilateral ABCDA, expanding each leg to second order.

**Prerequisites:** parallel transport of a charged field, gauge covariant derivative

$$
\psi'_{\mathcal A}=(1+\delta x^\mu\Delta x^\nu[D_\mu,D_\nu])\psi_{\mathcal A}
$$

**Notes:** For U(1) the finite version is the phase factor exp(-iq times the loop integral of A), not written in the book.

**Where:** GA §44.2 p.481 (pdf 498); GA §44.2 p.482 (pdf 499)

### commutator of covariant derivatives as curvature operator

*identity · core* · also: curvature operator

The operator [D_mu, D_nu] (or [nabla_mu, nabla_nu]) contains no derivatives of the object it acts on; it is a linear map built from the connection that measures the failure of transport to be path independent, i.e. curvature.

**How introduced:** Emerges from the loop expansion and is emphasised as striking because a purely algebraic, linear operator results.

**Prerequisites:** holonomy of an infinitesimal loop

$$
[D_\mu,D_\nu]=D_\mu D_\nu-D_\nu D_\mu
$$

**Where:** GA §44.2 p.482 (pdf 499)

### gauge field strength

*physical-quantity · core* · also: field strength, Q_mu nu, bundle curvature operator

The commutator Q_mu nu = [D_mu, D_nu] of gauge covariant derivatives; it is gauge covariant (invariant for U(1)) and encodes the physical content of the gauge field.

**How introduced:** Named immediately after the loop result (eqn 44.21) and evaluated for U(1) in Example 44.7.

**Prerequisites:** commutator of covariant derivatives as curvature operator, gauge covariant derivative

$$
Q_{\mu\nu}=[D_\mu,D_\nu]
$$

**Where:** GA §44.2 p.482 (pdf 499)

### Faraday tensor as U(1) field strength

*identity · core* · also: electromagnetic field tensor as curvature

For D = partial + iqA the commutator is iq(partial_mu A_nu - partial_nu A_mu) = iqF_mu nu, so the electromagnetic field tensor is the curvature of the U(1) phase bundle.

**How introduced:** Direct computation in Example 44.7, then interpreted as the local strain of the bundle.

**Prerequisites:** gauge field strength, Faraday tensor

$$
[D_\mu,D_\nu]=iq(\partial_\mu A_\nu-\partial_\nu A_\mu)=iqF_{\mu\nu}
$$

**Where:** GA §44.2 p.482 (pdf 499)

### bundle curvature

*mathematical-object · developed* · also: curvature of a connection

The curvature 2-form of a bundle connection, obtained from the commutator of covariant derivatives; for the tangent bundle it is the Riemann tensor and for the U(1) phase bundle it is iqF.

**How introduced:** Named in the fibre-bundle column of the dictionary table after both commutators have been compared.

**Prerequisites:** bundle connection, gauge field strength

**Where:** GA §44.2 p.482 (pdf 499); GA §44.2 p.483 (pdf 500)

### Riemann tensor as commutator of covariant derivatives

*identity · revisited* · also: Ricci identity

In a coordinate frame with a torsion-free connection, commuting two covariant derivatives on a vector field produces no derivative terms, only the Riemann tensor contracted with the vector (eqn 44.24); Riemann is thus the curvature operator of the tangent bundle.

**How introduced:** Recalled from Chapter 35 to complete the analogy with eqn 44.23.

**Prerequisites:** Riemann curvature tensor, covariant derivative

$$
[\nabla_\mu,\nabla_\nu]Z^\rho=R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}Z^\sigma
$$

**Where:** GA §44.2 p.482 (pdf 499)

### gravity-gauge theory dictionary

*principle · core* · also: GR / gauge / bundle correspondence table

The correspondence coordinate transformation <-> local phase transformation <-> strained bundle; connection coefficient Gamma <-> gauge field A <-> bundle connection; Riemann curvature R <-> field strength iqF <-> bundle curvature.

**How introduced:** Presented as a three-column table closing §44.2.

**Prerequisites:** bundle connection, bundle curvature, Riemann tensor as commutator of covariant derivatives, Faraday tensor as U(1) field strength

**Notes:** The analogy is structural; the dynamics (Einstein-Hilbert versus Maxwell action) and the soldering of tangent fibres to spacetime are not discussed.

**Where:** GA §44.2 p.482 (pdf 499); GA §44.2 p.483 (pdf 500)

### non-abelian gauge theory

*solution-or-model · mention* · also: Yang-Mills theory

A gauge theory whose internal transformations form a non-commuting group, so the gauge field is matrix-valued and the field strength contains a commutator of potentials in addition to derivatives.

**How introduced:** Only alluded to: the chapter says many gauge theories exist with U(1) the simplest, and Exercise 44.1 uses a general field strength G_mu nu.

**Prerequisites:** gauge theory, gauge field strength

$$
[D_\mu,D_\nu]=-igG_{\mu\nu}
$$

**Where:** GA §44.2 p.483 (pdf 500); GA p.483 (pdf 500)

### gauge-field Bianchi identity

*identity · introduced* · also: Bianchi identity for gauge fields

The statement that the cyclically permuted sum of covariant derivatives of the gauge field strength vanishes, following from transport round the closed surface of a small cube or equivalently from the Jacobi identity of covariant derivatives.

**How introduced:** Exercise 44.1, adapted from Ryder and from the Chapter 43 gravitational argument.

**Prerequisites:** gauge field strength, holonomy of an infinitesimal loop

$$
D_\rho G_{\mu\nu}+D_\mu G_{\nu\rho}+D_\nu G_{\rho\mu}=0
$$

**Where:** GA p.483 (pdf 500); GA p.484 (pdf 501)

### Jacobi identity

*identity · mention*

For any three operators, the cyclic sum of nested commutators [X,[Y,Z]] + [Y,[Z,X]] + [Z,[X,Y]] vanishes identically.

**How introduced:** Invoked in Exercise 44.1(g) as the algebraic reason the cube calculation gives zero.

**Prerequisites:** commutator of covariant derivatives as curvature operator

$$
[D_\rho,[D_\mu,D_\nu]]+[D_\mu,[D_\nu,D_\rho]]+[D_\nu,[D_\rho,D_\mu]]=0
$$

**Where:** GA p.484 (pdf 501)

### energy-momentum tensor of a complex scalar field

*physical-quantity · mention*

The symmetric tensor T_mu nu describing energy density, momentum and stress of the complex scalar field, obtained from its Lagrangian by the methods of Chapter 40.

**How introduced:** Set as Exercise 44.2, a one-line task that sends the reader back to the Chapter 40 machinery using this chapter's model Lagrangian (eqn 44.2).

**Prerequisites:** complex scalar field theory, Hilbert energy-momentum tensor

**Where:** GA p.484 (pdf 501)

## Key equations

### (44.1) Geometric covariant derivative · central · GA p.476 (pdf 493)

$$
(\nabla_\alpha \boldsymbol{v})^\mu=\frac{\partial v^\mu}{\partial x^\alpha}+\Gamma^\mu{}_{\alpha\beta}v^\beta
$$

Component form of the vector covariant derivative: raw change of components plus a connection term correcting for the changing basis.

**Symbols:** v^mu vector components; Gamma connection coefficients

### Covariant derivative in words (opening) · supporting · GA p.476 (pdf 493)

$$
\begin{pmatrix}\text{Covariant}\\\text{derivative}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\text{Change}\\\text{in vector}\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}\text{Change due to}\\\text{coordinate system}\end{pmatrix}
$$

Verbal template that the chapter reuses for the gauge covariant derivative.

### Index conventions for derivatives (footnote 4) · supporting · GA §44.1 p.478 (pdf 495)

$$
\partial_\mu=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^\mu},\qquad \partial^\mu=\eta^{\mu\nu}\partial_\nu
$$

Shorthand for partial derivatives and their raised-index version using the Minkowski metric.

**Symbols:** eta Minkowski metric

### (44.2) Complex scalar field Lagrangian · central · GA §44.1 p.478 (pdf 495)

$$
\mathcal{L}=-(\partial^\mu\psi)^\dagger(\partial_\mu\psi)-m^2\psi^\dagger\psi
$$

Free charged scalar field theory in flat spacetime; the minus signs suit the (-,+,+,+) signature so the time-derivative term is positive.

**Symbols:** psi complex scalar field; dagger hermitian (complex) conjugate; m mass parameter

### (44.3) Global phase transformation · central · GA §44.1 p.478 (pdf 495)

$$
\psi(x)\to\psi(x)\,e^{i\alpha}
$$

Constant phase rotation of the field at every point; eqn 44.2 is invariant.

**Symbols:** alpha constant angle

### Conjugate field under global rotation (footnote 5) · derivation-step · GA §44.1 p.478 (pdf 495)

$$
\psi^\dagger\to\psi^\dagger e^{-i\alpha}
$$

The conjugate picks up the opposite phase, so every product psi-dagger psi is unchanged.

### (44.4) Derivative under local phase change · derivation-step · GA §44.1 p.478 (pdf 495)

$$
\partial_\mu\psi\to\partial_\mu\!\left(\psi e^{i\alpha(x)}\right)=e^{i\alpha(x)}\partial_\mu\psi+i\psi e^{i\alpha(x)}\partial_\mu\alpha=e^{i\alpha(x)}\{\partial_\mu+i[\partial_\mu\alpha(x)]\}\psi
$$

Product rule shows the derivative acquires an extra term proportional to the gradient of alpha, so it does not simply pick up the phase factor.

**Symbols:** alpha(x) position-dependent angle

### (44.5) Conjugate derivative under local phase change · derivation-step · GA §44.1 p.478 (pdf 495)

$$
\partial^\mu\psi^\dagger\to e^{-i\alpha(x)}\{\partial^\mu-i[\partial^\mu\alpha(x)]\}\psi^\dagger
$$

Complex-conjugate counterpart of eqn 44.4.

### (44.6) Transformed kinetic term · derivation-step · GA §44.1 p.478 (pdf 495)

$$
(\partial^\mu\psi^\dagger)(\partial_\mu\psi)-i\psi^\dagger(\partial^\mu\alpha)(\partial_\mu\psi)+i\psi(\partial^\mu\psi^\dagger)(\partial_\mu\alpha)+\psi^\dagger\psi(\partial^\mu\alpha)(\partial_\mu\alpha)
$$

The kinetic term after a local phase change contains three unwanted terms involving derivatives of alpha, proving local non-invariance.

### (44.7) Gauge covariant derivative · central · GA §44.1 p.478 (pdf 495)

$$
D_\mu\psi(x)=\partial_\mu\psi(x)+iqA_\mu(x)\psi(x)
$$

Derivative supplemented by a gauge-field term designed to absorb the gradient-of-alpha terms.

**Symbols:** A_mu gauge field components; q coupling (charge)

### (44.8) Compensating transformation of the gauge field · central · GA §44.1 p.478 (pdf 495)

$$
A_\mu(x)\to A_\mu(x)-\frac1q\partial_\mu\alpha(x)
$$

How A must shift when psi is rotated by alpha(x) so that D_mu psi transforms covariantly.

### (44.9) Covariance of D · central · GA §44.1 p.479 (pdf 496)

$$
D_\mu\psi\to e^{i\alpha}(\partial_\mu\psi)+i\psi e^{i\alpha}(\partial_\mu\alpha)+iqA_\mu\psi e^{i\alpha}-i\psi e^{i\alpha}(\partial_\mu\alpha)=e^{i\alpha}D_\mu\psi
$$

The gradient term from the product rule is exactly cancelled by the shift of A, so D psi rotates like psi itself.

### (44.10) Locally invariant Lagrangian · central · GA §44.1 p.479 (pdf 496)

$$
\mathcal{L}=-(D^\mu\psi)^\dagger(D_\mu\psi)-m^2\psi^\dagger\psi
$$

Replacing partial by D makes the complex scalar theory invariant under local U(1) transformations; this is the scalar field coupled to electromagnetism.

### (44.11) Electromagnetic gauge transformation · supporting · GA §44.1 p.479 (pdf 496)

$$
A_\mu(x)\to A_\mu(x)-\partial_\mu\chi(x)
$$

Gauge freedom of the 4-potential from Chapter 42, leaving F and Maxwell's equations unchanged.

**Symbols:** chi arbitrary scalar function

### Identification of gauge parameters · derivation-step · GA §44.1 p.479 (pdf 496)

$$
\chi(x)=\frac{\alpha(x)}{q}
$$

With this choice eqn 44.8 is exactly the electromagnetic gauge transformation, so A is the familiar potential.

### (44.12) Total change of a field · supporting · GA §44.2 p.480 (pdf 497)

$$
d\psi=\psi(x+dx)-\psi(x)
$$

Naive difference of field values at neighbouring points, which mixes real change with the change of internal reference frame.

### (44.13) Covariant derivative along u in words · supporting · GA §44.2 p.480 (pdf 497)

$$
\begin{pmatrix}\text{Covariant}\\\text{derivative of }\boldsymbol v\end{pmatrix}_{\boldsymbol u}=\begin{pmatrix}\text{total change}\\\text{in vector }\boldsymbol v\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}\text{change due to}\\\text{coordinate system}\end{pmatrix}
$$

Verbal definition of nabla_u v reused as the template for D.

### (44.14) Parallel transport condition in components · derivation-step · GA §44.2 p.480 (pdf 497)

$$
u^\nu\frac{\partial v^\mu}{\partial x^\nu}+\Gamma^\mu{}_{\nu\beta}v^\beta u^\nu=0
$$

nabla_u v = 0 written out: v is parallel transported along a curve with tangent u.

**Symbols:** u tangent vector of the path

### (44.15) Component change under parallel transport · central · GA §44.2 p.480 (pdf 497)

$$
\delta v^\mu=-\Gamma^\mu{}_{\nu\beta}v^\beta\,dx^\nu
$$

How much the components of a parallel-transported vector change over a displacement dx; entirely set by the connection.

### (44.16) Parallel transport change of a charged field · central · GA §44.2 p.480 (pdf 497)

$$
\delta\psi(x)=-iqA_\nu(x)\psi(x)\,dx^\nu
$$

Gauge analogue of eqn 44.15: transport rotates the phase by an amount set by A along the displacement.

### Connection analogy (footnote 7) · supporting · GA §44.2 p.480 (pdf 497)

$$
iqA_\nu(x)\;\leftrightarrow\;\Gamma^\mu{}_{\nu\beta}(x)
$$

The gauge field times iq plays the role of the connection; the vector case carries extra indices labelling which component is transported.

### (44.17) Gauge covariant differential · central · GA §44.2 p.481 (pdf 498)

$$
D\psi=d\psi-\delta\psi=d\psi+iqA_\mu\psi\,dx^\mu
$$

Covariant change of the field is total change minus the change attributable to transport.

### (44.18) Gauge covariant derivative recovered · central · GA §44.2 p.481 (pdf 498)

$$
\frac{D\psi}{dx^\mu}\equiv D_\mu\psi=\partial_\mu\psi+iqA_\mu\psi
$$

Dividing eqn 44.17 by dx^mu gives back eqn 44.7, now motivated geometrically.

### Horizontal transport condition (Example 44.5, corrected) · supporting · GA §44.2 p.481 (pdf 498)

$$
D_\mu\psi=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\partial_\mu\psi=-iqA_\mu\psi
$$

A field value is carried horizontally through the bundle when its gauge covariant derivative vanishes. The book prints the right-hand side without the factor psi.

### (44.19) First leg of the loop · derivation-step · GA §44.2 p.481 (pdf 498)

$$
\psi(x_{\mathcal B})=\psi(x_{\mathcal A})+D_\mu\psi(x_{\mathcal A})\Delta x^\mu+\tfrac12D_\mu D_\nu\psi(x_{\mathcal A})\Delta x^\mu\Delta x^\nu=\left(1+\Delta x^\mu D_\mu+\tfrac12\Delta x^\mu\Delta x^\nu D_\mu D_\nu\right)\psi(x_{\mathcal A})
$$

Second-order covariant Taylor expansion used as the transport operator from A to B.

**Symbols:** Delta x^mu displacement from A to B

### Second leg of the loop · derivation-step · GA §44.2 p.481 (pdf 498)

$$
\psi_{\mathcal C}=\left(1+\delta x^\mu D_\mu+\tfrac12\delta x^\mu\delta x^\nu D_\mu D_\nu\right)\psi_{\mathcal B}=\left[1+(\delta x^\mu+\Delta x^\mu)D_\mu+\left(\tfrac12\delta x^\mu\delta x^\nu+\delta x^\mu\Delta x^\nu+\tfrac12\Delta x^\mu\Delta x^\nu\right)D_\mu D_\nu\right]\psi_{\mathcal A}
$$

Composing two legs keeps second-order terms including the ordered cross term delta x Delta x D D.

**Symbols:** delta x^mu displacement from B to C

### Third leg of the loop · derivation-step · GA §44.2 p.482 (pdf 499)

$$
\psi_{\mathcal D}=\left(1-\Delta x^\mu D_\mu+\tfrac12\Delta x^\mu\Delta x^\nu D_\mu D_\nu\right)\psi_{\mathcal C}=\left[1+\delta x^\mu D_\mu+\tfrac12\delta x^\mu\delta x^\nu D_\mu D_\nu+(\delta x^\mu\Delta x^\nu-\Delta x^\mu\delta x^\nu)D_\mu D_\nu\right]\psi_{\mathcal A}
$$

Returning along -Delta x cancels the Delta x terms and leaves an antisymmetric cross term.

### (44.20) Change after transport round the loop · central · GA §44.2 p.482 (pdf 499)

$$
\psi'_{\mathcal A}=\left(1+\delta x^\mu\Delta x^\nu[D_\mu,D_\nu]\right)\psi_{\mathcal A}
$$

After the full circuit only the commutator term survives, multiplied by the loop's area element. The text defines the commutator with a typo (D_mu D_nu - D_mu D_nu); it should read D_mu D_nu - D_nu D_mu.

### (44.21) Gauge field strength · central · GA §44.2 p.482 (pdf 499)

$$
Q_{\mu\nu}=[D_\mu,D_\nu]
$$

Definition of field strength as the commutator of covariant derivatives.

### (44.22) Field strength for the complex scalar field · derivation-step · GA §44.2 p.482 (pdf 499)

$$
Q_{\mu\nu}=[D_\mu,D_\nu]=[(\partial_\mu+iqA_\mu),(\partial_\nu+iqA_\nu)]
$$

Setting up the U(1) commutator explicitly.

### (44.23) U(1) field strength is the Faraday tensor · central · GA §44.2 p.482 (pdf 499)

$$
[D_\mu,D_\nu]=iq(\partial_\mu A_\nu-\partial_\nu A_\mu)=iqF_{\mu\nu}
$$

Derivative terms cancel in the commutator, leaving iq times the electromagnetic field tensor: electromagnetism's F is the curvature of the phase bundle.

**Symbols:** F_mu nu Faraday tensor components

### (44.24) Riemann tensor from commuting covariant derivatives · central · GA §44.2 p.482 (pdf 499)

$$
[\nabla_\mu,\nabla_\nu]Z^\rho=R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}Z^\sigma
$$

Coordinate-frame expression recalled from Chapter 35; the geometric counterpart of eqn 44.23.

**Symbols:** Z vector field; R Riemann tensor

### Field strength convention in Exercise 44.1 · supporting · GA p.483 (pdf 500)

$$
[D_\mu,D_\nu]=-igG_{\mu\nu}
$$

Ryder-style convention with coupling g and a general (possibly matrix-valued) field strength G, differing in sign and symbol from eqn 44.23.

**Symbols:** g coupling; G general gauge field strength

### (44.25) Path decomposition of the cube surface · supporting · GA p.484 (pdf 501)

$$
(\mathcal{ABCDAPSRQPA})+(\mathcal{ADSPABQRCBA})+(\mathcal{APQBADCRSDA})
$$

Three composite closed paths that together traverse all six faces of the cube with consistent orientation.

**Symbols:** script letters label cube vertices in Fig. 44.5

### (44.26) Cube result · supporting · GA p.484 (pdf 501)

$$
1-ig\,\Delta V^{\rho\mu\nu}\left(D_\rho G_{\mu\nu}+D_\mu G_{\nu\rho}+D_\nu G_{\rho\mu}\right)
$$

Factor by which the field changes after all three paths; since the surface is closed it must equal 1, giving the gauge Bianchi identity.

**Symbols:** Delta V volume element of the cube

### (44.27) Jacobi identity for covariant derivatives · supporting · GA p.484 (pdf 501)

$$
[D_\rho,[D_\mu,D_\nu]]+[D_\mu,[D_\nu,D_\rho]]+[D_\nu,[D_\rho,D_\mu]]=0
$$

Algebraic identity that guarantees the cyclic sum in eqn 44.26 vanishes.

## Worked examples

### Example 44.1 · intro · GA §44.1 p.477 (pdf 494)

**Problem:** Give familiar structures that are fibre bundles.

**Method:** Lists three cases: tangent spaces glued over a manifold (tangent bundle), isospin as an internal switch on matter fields, and Newtonian spacetime as three-dimensional space attached over each instant of absolute time.

**Key insight:** The reader has already used a bundle (the tangent bundle) without naming it, so internal phase spaces are a generalisation rather than a novelty.

**Result:** Tangent bundle TM, isospin fibres, Newtonian space-over-time bundle.

**Concepts:** fibre bundle, tangent bundle, isospin, Newtonian spacetime as a bundle over time

### Example 44.2 · standard · GA §44.1 p.478 (pdf 495)

**Problem:** Test whether the complex scalar Lagrangian survives a position-dependent phase change psi -> psi e^{i alpha(x)}.

**Method:** Apply the product rule to partial_mu of the transformed field and its conjugate, multiply out the kinetic term and collect terms.

**Key insight:** Derivatives of alpha appear and do not cancel, so global invariance does not extend to local invariance.

**Result:** Kinetic term gains three extra pieces (eqn 44.6); the theory is not locally invariant.

**Concepts:** local transformation, complex scalar field theory, local gauge invariance

### Example 44.3 · standard · GA §44.1 p.479 (pdf 496)

**Problem:** Show that D_mu = partial_mu + iqA_mu with A_mu -> A_mu - (1/q) partial_mu alpha makes the Lagrangian locally invariant.

**Method:** Transform D_mu psi term by term; the product-rule gradient term cancels against the shift of A; substitute into the Lagrangian with D in place of partial.

**Key insight:** Designing the gauge field's transformation to cancel exactly the offending gradient makes D psi transform like psi.

**Result:** D_mu psi -> e^{i alpha} D_mu psi and eqn 44.10 is invariant.

**Concepts:** gauge covariant derivative, gauge field, local gauge invariance

### Example · intro · GA §44.1 p.479 (pdf 496)

**Problem:** Is the compensating field required by local phase invariance something already known?

**Method:** Compare eqn 44.8 with the electromagnetic gauge transformation eqn 44.11 and choose chi = alpha/q.

**Key insight:** The transformation law demanded by symmetry is precisely electromagnetic gauge freedom, so A is the electromagnetic potential and q the charge.

**Result:** Local U(1) invariance of a charged field forces coupling to electromagnetism.

**Concepts:** gauge transformation, gauge field, electric charge as gauge coupling

### Example 44.4 · intro · GA §44.2 p.480 (pdf 497)

**Problem:** Extract the component change of a parallel-transported vector.

**Method:** Write nabla_u v = 0 in components, cancel the tangent vector against dx along the path.

**Key insight:** Under transport the components change by an amount fixed entirely by the connection, which is the template for the gauge case.

**Result:** delta v^mu = -Gamma^mu_{nu beta} v^beta dx^nu (eqn 44.15).

**Concepts:** parallel transport of a vector, connection coefficients

### Example 44.5 · intro · GA §44.2 p.481 (pdf 498)

**Problem:** Describe parallel transport in bundle language.

**Method:** Picture fibres over the base and call a curve through the bundle horizontal if the value it carries has zero covariant change; express as D_mu psi = 0.

**Key insight:** Parallel transport generalises to horizontal transport defined by the connection, independent of any spacetime geometry.

**Result:** Horizontal transport condition D_mu psi = 0, i.e. partial_mu psi = -iqA_mu psi (book omits psi).

**Concepts:** horizontal transport, bundle connection

### Example 44.6 · challenging · GA §44.2 p.481 (pdf 498)

**Problem:** Find the change in a charged field after transport round an infinitesimal quadrilateral ABCDA with sides Delta x and delta x.

**Method:** Represent each leg by the second-order operator 1 + dx D + (1/2) dx dx DD, compose the four legs keeping all second-order terms, and watch linear and squared terms cancel.

**Key insight:** Only the ordered cross terms survive and combine into a commutator, so loop transport measures [D_mu, D_nu] times the area, exactly as in the Riemann derivation.

**Result:** psi'_A = (1 + delta x^mu Delta x^nu [D_mu, D_nu]) psi_A (eqn 44.20).

**Concepts:** holonomy of an infinitesimal loop, commutator of covariant derivatives as curvature operator

### Example 44.7 · standard · GA §44.2 p.482 (pdf 499)

**Problem:** Compute the gauge field strength for the complex scalar field.

**Method:** Expand the commutator of partial + iqA with itself acting on a test field; second-derivative terms and A-derivative cross terms cancel by symmetry.

**Key insight:** The commutator is a multiplication operator equal to iq times the antisymmetrised derivative of A, i.e. the Faraday tensor.

**Result:** [D_mu, D_nu] = iqF_mu nu (eqn 44.23).

**Concepts:** gauge field strength, Faraday tensor as U(1) field strength


## Analogies and intuitions

### Phase as an arrow at each point of a grid (phasor field) → internal variable · useful · GA §44.1 p.476 (pdf 493)

Makes the complex phase visible so that global rotations are literally all arrows turning together.

**Where it breaks down:** Invites the false reading that the arrow points in a physical direction; the book immediately stresses the rotation is in internal space.

**App idea:** Draw arrows in a visually distinct 'internal' colour and offer a toggle that lifts them off the plane into a separate layer to reinforce that they are not spatial.

### Each fibre as a knob that can be turned → U(1) circle fibre · strong · GA §44.1 p.477 (pdf 494)

Global transformation equals twisting every knob by one amount; local transformation equals twisting each knob by its own amount.

**Where it breaks down:** Knobs have an absolute zero mark; in gauge theory there is no preferred zero, only relative settings compared via a connection.

**App idea:** Knob board demo with a hidden-zero mode where the tick marks disappear and only connection-corrected differences are shown.

### Strained bundle compared to a strained solid → strained bundle · useful · GA §44.1 p.479 (pdf 496)

Position-dependent phase shifts are likened to atoms displaced by different amounts in a deformed crystal, motivating the need for a connection to track the strain.

**Where it breaks down:** Elastic strain is physical and costs energy; a pure local phase shift is a relabelling that leaves F = 0 and all physics unchanged. Only curvature (field strength) is the gauge-invariant analogue of real strain.

**App idea:** Side-by-side lattice: a uniformly displaced crystal, a non-uniformly displaced one, and a phase grid under local gauge change, with a curvature meter showing zero for the pure relabelling.

### An M worth of V's (Penrose's phrasing) → fibre bundle · useful · GA §44.1 p.477 (pdf 494)

Captures the construction of a bundle as one fibre attached for every point of the base.

**Where it breaks down:** Suggests a simple product M x V; nontrivial global twisting (e.g. the Mobius band) is not conveyed.

**App idea:** Toggle between a cylinder and a Mobius strip as bundles over a circle, both locally 'a circle worth of intervals'.

### Gauge field as the connection coefficients of internal space → bundle connection · strong · GA §44.2 p.480 (pdf 497)

delta psi = -iqA psi dx is set beside delta v = -Gamma v dx, with iqA_nu matching Gamma^mu_{nu beta}, and both covariant derivatives are written as total change minus frame change.

**Where it breaks down:** For U(1) the internal space is one-dimensional so the internal indices of Gamma collapse; Gamma can be computed from a metric whereas A is an independent field; the tangent bundle is tied to spacetime directions while an internal fibre is not.

**App idea:** Split-screen derivation where each line of the nabla derivation is paired with the corresponding D line, and hovering highlights matched symbols.

### Field strength as curvature, parallel to Riemann from loop transport → gauge field strength · strong · GA §44.2 p.482 (pdf 499)

The Chapter 11 loop argument is replayed with D so that F emerges as a curvature just as Riemann did; eqn 44.24 is placed beside eqn 44.23.

**Where it breaks down:** Gravitational field equations are linear in curvature while Maxwell's Lagrangian is quadratic in F; the Riemann tensor carries tangent-space indices that are also spacetime indices.

**App idea:** Loop-transport demo with two modes, a vector on a sphere and a phase on a U(1) bundle with a magnetic field, both showing change proportional to enclosed area times curvature.

### Tangent bundle as a bundle the reader already knows → fibre bundle · useful · GA §44.1 p.477 (pdf 494)

Vectors stored in a tangent space floating above each point prepare the idea of values stored in a fibre.

**Where it breaks down:** The tangent bundle is canonically tied to the manifold; internal bundles are extra structure.

### Isospin as an internal switch → isospin · weak · GA §44.1 p.477 (pdf 494)

Shows internal variables need not be phases: a switch can change a particle's identity at a point.

**Where it breaks down:** A switch suggests discrete settings, whereas isospin rotations are continuous SU(2) transformations.


## Misconceptions addressed

### Rotating the phase arrows of a complex field is a rotation of something in physical space. · GA §44.1 p.477 (pdf 494)

**Correction:** The phase lives in an internal space (a fibre) above each point; changing it moves nothing in spacetime.

**Why tempting:** The arrow drawing of Fig. 44.1 lies flat on the page like a spatial vector field.

### If a theory is symmetric under a constant phase change it is automatically symmetric under a position-dependent one. · GA §44.1 p.478 (pdf 495)

**Correction:** Derivatives of alpha(x) enter through the product rule and spoil invariance unless a gauge field is introduced.

**Why tempting:** At any single point a local transformation looks like a global one.

### The difference psi(x+dx) - psi(x) is an unambiguous measure of how the field changes. · GA §44.2 p.480 (pdf 497)

**Correction:** The two values are expressed relative to different internal reference choices, so a connection is needed to compare them; the covariant derivative removes the reference-frame part.

**Why tempting:** For ordinary real functions in Cartesian coordinates the plain difference works.

### The gauge potential A itself is a directly measurable physical field. · GA §44.1 p.479 (pdf 496)

**Correction:** A can be shifted by any gradient without physical consequences; the gauge-invariant content is in the field strength F = [D, D]/(iq), the curvature.

**Why tempting:** A appears explicitly in the Lagrangian and in D, so it looks as physical as psi.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### A non-zero gauge field means a non-zero electromagnetic field (or, a strained bundle is a curved bundle). · GA §44.2 p.482 (pdf 499)

**Correction:** A pure-gauge potential A = -(1/q) grad alpha produces F = 0: the bundle is strained by relabelling but flat, just as polar coordinates give non-zero Gamma in flat space.

**Why tempting:** The text says local strain is represented by the curvature, which can blur the difference between strain from relabelling and genuine curvature.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### Gauge ideas belong only to electromagnetism, or the analogy with gravity is just a coincidence of notation. · GA §44.2 p.483 (pdf 500)

**Correction:** U(1) is the simplest of many gauge theories, all with field strength given by a commutator of covariant derivatives, and the same structure (connection and curvature) underlies general relativity.

**Why tempting:** The only worked case is electromagnetism, and GR is usually taught through metrics rather than connections.

### The word gauge refers to some kind of measuring device or scale in modern field theory. · GA p.476 (pdf 493)

**Correction:** The term is a historical leftover (originally the thickness of wires and rails) that reached physics through Weyl's early attempt to unify gravity and electromagnetism; it now just means local internal symmetry freedom.

**Why tempting:** In everyday English a gauge is a measuring instrument.

### The general relativity / gauge theory correspondence is an exact identity, so gravity is just another Yang-Mills theory. · GA §44.2 p.483 (pdf 500)

**Correction:** The dictionary is structural: connection and curvature match, but the gravitational connection is tied to the metric and to spacetime directions, and the Einstein-Hilbert action is linear in curvature while gauge actions are quadratic in F.

**Why tempting:** The three-column table presents entries as one-to-one replacements.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*


## Thought experiments

### Twisting all knobs versus each knob separately · GA §44.1 p.478 (pdf 495)

**Setup:** Imagine a phase knob at every point of space. First turn every knob by the same angle, then turn each by its own smoothly varying angle, and ask whether the physics described by the Lagrangian notices.

**Lesson:** Only relative settings of neighbouring knobs enter derivatives; a uniform twist is invisible, but a non-uniform twist changes neighbour differences unless a connection records compensating offsets.

**App idea:** Knob board with a master dial, an individual-knob mode and a connection overlay, plus a live kinetic-energy readout.

### What does 'change' mean when moving a field from x to x + dx? · GA §44.2 p.480 (pdf 497)

**Setup:** Move from one event to a neighbouring one and compare the stored field values, as one would compare vector components across a curved coordinate grid.

**Lesson:** The raw difference contains a frame contribution; defining the transport change delta psi and subtracting it gives the covariant derivative.

**App idea:** Two neighbouring fibres with independently rotatable reference marks, showing the raw difference change with the marks while the covariant difference stays fixed.

### Carrying a charged field round a tiny loop · GA §44.2 p.481 (pdf 498)

**Setup:** Start at A with a field value, transport it along four short legs of a quadrilateral back to A and compare with the starting value.

**Lesson:** The mismatch is proportional to the enclosed area and to the field strength; with no field strength the value returns unchanged, whatever the potential.

**App idea:** Draw a loop in a plane with an adjustable magnetic field; animate the phase pointer riding along and show the final phase offset equal to q times flux.

### Going round every face of a cube · GA p.483 (pdf 500)

**Setup:** Traverse loops on all six faces of a small cube, stitched into three composite paths so connecting edges cancel.

**Lesson:** Because the faces make a closed surface the net effect must vanish, giving the cyclic Bianchi identity for the field strength.

**App idea:** Rotatable cube animation with a per-face tally that sums to zero.

## Notation and conventions

- **Metric signature:** (-,+,+,+), inferred from the overall minus sign on the kinetic term of eqn 44.2, which makes the time-derivative contribution positive. — Consistent with the rest of GA; particle-physics texts often use (+,-,-,-) with the opposite overall sign. (GA §44.1 p.478 (pdf 495))
- **Derivative shorthand:** partial_mu = partial/partial x^mu and partial^mu = eta^{mu nu} partial_nu (footnote 4). (GA §44.1 p.478 (pdf 495))
- **Hermitian conjugate:** psi^dagger denotes the complex conjugate of the scalar field, written with a dagger in anticipation of quantum fields. (GA §44.1 p.478 (pdf 495))
- **Gauge covariant derivative and transformation signs:** D_mu = partial_mu + iqA_mu, psi -> e^{i alpha} psi, A_mu -> A_mu - (1/q) partial_mu alpha, giving [D_mu, D_nu] = +iqF_mu nu with F_mu nu = partial_mu A_nu - partial_nu A_mu. — Exercise 44.1 switches to Ryder's convention [D_mu, D_nu] = -igG_mu nu; many texts use D = partial - iqA. (GA §44.1 p.478 (pdf 495))
- **Units:** Natural units with hbar = 1 are implicit: the coupling appears as qA without hbar or c. — Not stated in the chapter; inferred. (GA §44.1 p.478 (pdf 495))
- **Bold versus tilde-bold symbols:** Bold nabla for the geometric covariant derivative, plain D_mu for the gauge covariant derivative; 1-forms carry a tilde (A-tilde, F-tilde), vectors are bold (v, u, Z), R bold for Riemann. — The print occasionally drops the tilde on A. (GA §44.1 p.479 (pdf 496))
- **Bundle symbols:** Script M for base space, script V for fibre, script B for bundle, T_P for the tangent space at P, TM for the tangent bundle; script capitals label loop and cube vertices. (GA §44.1 p.477 (pdf 494))
- **Field strength symbol:** Q_mu nu = [D_mu, D_nu] denotes the gauge field strength operator; for U(1) it equals iqF_mu nu. — Q includes the factor iq, so 'field strength' in the table is iqF rather than F. (GA §44.2 p.482 (pdf 499))
- **Riemann index order in the commutator:** In eqn 44.24 the first Riemann index is the output component, the second is contracted with the transported vector, and the last two match the order of the commuted derivatives (the loop plane). — Coordinate frame, torsion-free connection. (GA §44.2 p.482 (pdf 499))

## Gaps and pitfalls

- **The commutator is defined in the text after eqn 44.20 as D_mu D_nu - D_mu D_nu, which is identically zero; it should be D_mu D_nu - D_nu D_mu.** — A careful reader may conclude the loop effect vanishes or distrust the derivation. *Suggestion:* State the correct definition and show how relabelling dummy indices turns the antisymmetric cross term left after the third leg into the commutator.
- **Example 44.5 writes the horizontal transport condition as partial_mu psi = -iqA_mu, missing the factor psi.** — Dimensional and structural confusion about whether the gauge field sets the derivative or the logarithmic derivative. *Suggestion:* Use partial_mu psi = -iqA_mu psi and note that the solution is psi = psi_0 exp(-iq integral of A dx).
- **The loop derivation substitutes D for partial in a Taylor series, evaluates operators at different points as if at A, and justifies dropping d psi by single-valuedness without explanation.** — Advanced learners may find the argument unconvincing; novices may not see why transport is a Taylor series at all. *Suggestion:* Supplement with the exact U(1) holonomy: transport with delta psi = -iqA psi dx multiplies psi by exp(-iq times the loop integral of A). For the loop that runs first along Delta x then along delta x, Stokes gives the integral as F_mu nu Delta x^mu delta x^nu, so the factor is 1 - iqF_mu nu Delta x^mu delta x^nu = 1 + iqF_mu nu delta x^mu Delta x^nu, which is eqn 44.20 with eqn 44.23 inserted (orientation matters for the sign).
- **The phrase 'change due to coordinate system' is reused for internal space, where what changes is the local phase convention, not spacetime coordinates.** — Learners may think gauge transformations are coordinate transformations of spacetime. *Suggestion:* Say 'change due to the choice of reference phase in each fibre' and contrast explicitly with spacetime coordinate changes in the first row of the dictionary.
- **The strained-bundle language suggests that local strain equals curvature, but a pure gauge transformation strains the bundle while leaving F = 0.** — Confusion between gauge artefacts and physical fields, mirroring the classic confusion of non-zero Christoffel symbols with curvature. *Suggestion:* Show A = -(1/q) grad alpha gives zero F and compare with polar coordinates in flat space, where Gamma is non-zero but Riemann vanishes.
- **No kinetic term for the gauge field is added; the chapter makes the matter Lagrangian invariant but does not say where Maxwell's equations come from.** — Learners may think the gauge field has no dynamics of its own. *Suggestion:* Add the gauge-invariant term -(1/4) F_mu nu F^mu nu from Chapter 42 and note it is built from the curvature, just as the Einstein-Hilbert action is built from R.
- **For U(1) the internal space is one-dimensional, so the analogy iqA_nu <-> Gamma^mu_{nu beta} loses the internal indices, and the non-abelian case that restores them is only hinted at.** — The analogy can look forced; Exercise 44.1 then silently uses a matrix-valued field strength with a different sign convention. *Suggestion:* Show an SU(2) example where A_nu is a 2x2 matrix, matching Gamma^mu_{nu beta} as a matrix in (mu, beta) for each nu, and flag the convention switch to -igG.
- **Limits of the gravity-gauge analogy (metric-determined, soldered connection; action linear in curvature; diffeomorphisms versus internal symmetries) are not discussed.** — Learners may over-generalise and believe gravity is simply a gauge theory of the same type as electromagnetism. *Suggestion:* Add a 'where the analogy breaks' panel after the dictionary table.
- **Natural units (hbar = 1) and the (-,+,+,+) signature are used without comment in the Lagrangian and in D.** — Readers coming from quantum mechanics may miss the factor q/hbar or be confused by overall signs relative to particle-physics texts. *Suggestion:* State D = partial + i(q/hbar)A and the signature once at the start.
- **Fig. 44.1 and Fig. 44.3 draw only the phase; the amplitude rho(x) is invisible (all arrows equal length), and neither picture shows the gauge field A that later compares neighbouring fibres.** — Learners may think the local-transformation problem concerns the arrows themselves rather than the comparison between neighbouring arrows, which is what the derivative (and hence A) cares about. *Suggestion:* When moving from Fig. 44.3 to eqn 44.7, add small links between neighbouring knobs labelled by q A_mu dx^mu, and show that a local twist changes the links by -d alpha while the covariant difference is unchanged.

## Tutor notes

- Open with: 'In Chapter 7, when a vector's components change as you move, what are the two possible reasons?' Then ask whether a complex number's phase could suffer from the same ambiguity.

- Check for understanding: 'If A_mu = -(1/q) partial_mu alpha for some function alpha, what is F?' Expected answer: zero, it is pure gauge. Follow with the analogy to Christoffel symbols in polar coordinates.
- Check for understanding: ask the learner to fill in the missing row entries of the GR / gauge / bundle table from memory.

- Common learner question: 'Is the phase arrow pointing somewhere in space?' Answer: no, it lives in a fibre; use the 3D bundle demo where fibres stick out in a separate direction.
- Common learner question: 'Why call it gauge?' Give the short history (wire gauge, Weyl's scale idea) and point to Chapter 45.
- Common learner question: 'Is gravity a gauge theory then?' Answer: structurally it has a connection and curvature, but the connection comes from the metric and relates to spacetime directions; the actions differ. Avoid overselling the table.
