# GA ch39 · Fluids as dry water

> Builds the viscosity-free fluid twice: first as a Newtonian continuum (continuity, convective derivative, Euler and hydrostatics, Bernoulli read as energy conservation), then as a consequence of a divergence-free stress-energy tensor, and finally as a relativistic perfect fluid whose baryon, entropy, energy and momentum laws come from projecting the conservation of T along and across the 4-velocity, ending in a relativistic Euler equation that says transverse pressure gradients push flow lines off geodesics.

**Pages:** printed 411–427 · pdf 428–444 · **Difficulty:** math 4/5, conceptual 3/5, novice-friendliness 3/5

Sections 39.1-39.2 are gentle undergraduate fluid mechanics (vector calculus plus first-law thermodynamics). Section 39.3 is short but its component argument is admittedly loose. Section 39.4 is index-heavy: Leibniz expansions, contractions with u, projection tensors and repeated use of u.u = -1, with key rules motivated rather than derived and several printing slips (dt for dtau, stray indices, a misprinted scalar-field equation of state, and u used for two different quantities). Exercises 39.4-39.9 require Part V machinery (Lie derivatives, forms) and connection coefficients.

## Role in the book

Opening chapter of Part VI (classical and quantum fields), placed right after the Part V geometry toolkit. The perfect-fluid stress-energy tensor had been used almost as a black box in Chapter 12 and for the cosmological fluid in Chapters 15-18; this chapter, marked as skippable on a first reading, supplies the missing fluid mechanics and thermodynamics behind it. It is a spiral revisit on the matter side of Einstein's equation: the same conservation law is unpacked into continuity, energy and Euler equations. It also plants results that later chapters pick up: the fluid Lagrangian density L = -rho and the free-energy form of the pressure (Chapter 40), equations of state including a scalar field (Chapter 41), and the Lie derivative from Chapter 33 as the natural comoving derivative (exercises).

## Learning objectives

- Reader can describe a fluid by a scalar density field and a velocity field, and explain why streamlines coincide with the paths of fluid elements only in steady flow.
- Reader can write local mass conservation both as the vanishing 4-divergence of the mass current J = (rho0, rho0 v) and as the 3D continuity equation.
- Reader can turn the surface integral of pressure over a fluid element into minus the volume integral of grad p and explain why uniform pressure gives no net force.
- Reader can convert rates of change between the comoving observer (d/dt) and the fixed observer (partial_t + v.grad), and interpret div v as the fractional rate of change of a parcel's volume.
- Reader can derive Euler's equation with and without gravity and specialize it to hydrostatic equilibrium and Pascal's law of pressure with depth.
- Reader can use the identity v x (curl v) = grad(v^2/2) - (v.grad)v to rewrite Euler's equation in general Bernoulli form, then reduce it to the steady-flow version along streamlines and the irrotational unsteady version valid throughout the fluid.
- Reader can work with specific internal energy, entropy and enthalpy, and derive p = rho0^2 du/drho0 for a dissipation-free fluid.
- Reader can substitute the slow-flow, flat-space perfect-fluid tensor (eqn 39.41) into the conservation law and recover both the continuity equation and Euler's equation.
- Reader can define the rest-frame scalars n, rho0, rho, p, T and s for a relativistic fluid and state rho = rho0(1 + u).
- Reader can state the relativistic dictionary (d/dt to D/dtau, v.grad to nabla_u, div v to div u) and use it to prove baryon conservation div(n u) = 0.
- Reader can contract the divergence of the perfect-fluid T with u_alpha to obtain nabla_u rho + (rho + p) div u = 0 and show it is the relativistic first law at constant entropy per baryon.
- Reader can build the projection tensor P = g + u x u, apply it to the divergence of T, derive the relativistic Euler equation (rho + p) nabla_u u = -P.grad p, and explain why dust flow lines are geodesics.
- Reader can obtain the pressure from an equation of state rho(n, s) or from a free energy per baryon a(n, T).
- Reader can (through the exercises) derive a sound-wave equation with c_s^2 = (dp/drho)_s and the t^{2/3} growing mode of density perturbations in an Einstein-de Sitter universe.

## Teaching approach

A do-it-twice chapter. Every idea is first built for an ordinary Newtonian inviscid fluid with vector calculus and elementary thermodynamics, then reinterpreted as a consequence of the vanishing divergence of T, then promoted to a covariant relativistic statement through a short dictionary of replacement rules. Almost all technical work sits in numbered Example boxes, the main text states and interprets the results, and margin notes carry the rules, unit restorations and side calculations. The geometric payoff comes at the end: the relativistic Euler equation read as the geodesic equation plus a transverse pressure-gradient force.

**Style:** physical-motivation-first, derivation-heavy, example-driven, spiral-revisit, components-first, conversational-informal

**Narrative arc**

1. Framing: fluids are a satisfying classical field theory; viscosity (the 'wet' part) is dropped because relativistic fluids are close to inviscid, and the chapter is flagged as optional background.
2. Kinematics: a fluid is a density scalar plus a velocity field viewed as the tangent field of a space-filling congruence of streamlines; caution that streamlines are not particle paths unless flow is steady; mass current and continuity; vorticity and irrotational flow.
3. Dynamics: the pressure force on an element as a closed surface integral turned into a volume integral of grad p, equated with mass times acceleration.
4. Frame bookkeeping: the total derivative belongs to a comoving observer; the chain rule gives the convective derivative for a fixed observer; a fixed-mass parcel shows div v is the fractional volume change rate.
5. Euler's equation for the fixed observer, gravity added, hydrostatics and Pascal's law as the static special case.
6. A vector identity trades the (v.grad)v term for vorticity, giving the most general Bernoulli form.
7. Thermodynamics per unit mass: first law, enthalpy, perfect fluid with no entropy production, pressure from internal energy.
8. Steady flow: Bernoulli becomes energy conservation (kinetic + potential + enthalpy) along each streamline; irrotational unsteady flow gives a version valid everywhere using a velocity potential.
9. Reframe: substitute the non-relativistic stress-energy tensor into its conservation law and watch continuity and Euler both drop out.
10. Relativistic setup: rest-frame scalars n, rho, p, T, s; the two Newtonian rules upgraded to nabla_u and div u = (1/V) dV/dtau; perfect-fluid T and its Leibniz-expanded divergence.
11. Five laws in sequence: baryon conservation, entropy (second law), energy (projection along u, matched to a relativistic first law), momentum (projection transverse to u, giving the relativistic Euler equation and the geodesic comparison with dust), and equation of state.
12. Closing pointer that the Lie derivative is the natural comoving derivative, left for the exercises, followed by a three-point summary.
13. Exercises push outward: Torricelli outflow, relativistic Bernoulli, sound waves, point-particle currents, relativistic hydrostatic equilibrium in Schwarzschild, Lie-derivative form of continuity and Euler, flux 2-form, and density perturbations in Einstein-de Sitter.

**Signature moves**

- Two conversion rules (derivative rule and divergence rule) are boxed in a margin note for the Newtonian fluid and then upgraded one-for-one into relativistic statements, so the relativistic section reads as a translation exercise.
- A fixed-mass parcel M = rho0 V gives the divergence of velocity a tangible meaning as fractional volume growth.
- The authors stress that the conservation of T yields more than energy conservation and then tame it by projecting along u (energy) and perpendicular to u (momentum).
- The relativistic Euler equation is laid next to the geodesic equation, with an italicized restatement that pressureless dust moves on geodesics.
- The normalization u.u = -1 is used repeatedly to kill terms, justified by the flat-space analogue v.dv = (1/2) d(v^2).
- The relativistic first law is obtained by bookkeeping the energy of a volume holding a fixed number N of baryons with V = N/n.
- Margin notes flag where each result is reused later (Lagrangian density L = -rho, free-energy form of pressure in Chapter 40) and list equations of state used elsewhere in the book.
- Light touches: a punning title, an epigraph about microbes in London drinking water, and gossip-style biographies of Pascal and the Bernoulli family.

### Chapter opening: describing a fluid — p.412 (pdf 429)

Announces the plan (Newtonian fluid in flat spacetime, then energy, then relativistic fluid) and justifies ignoring viscosity. A fluid is defined as matter that keeps deforming under shear; it is described by a mass-density scalar field and a velocity vector field. The velocity is the tangent field to a congruence of streamlines (flow lines), which only match actual element trajectories for steady flow. Local mass conservation is written as a vanishing divergence of the mass current, giving the continuity equation, and vorticity (curl of v) is defined with irrotational flow as its vanishing (the vorticity definition spills onto p.413 just before the 39.1 heading).

**Concepts:** fluid, perfect fluid, streamlines, steady flow, mass current, continuity equation, vorticity

**Key moves**
- Velocity field treated geometrically as tangents to a space-filling congruence of curves (Fig. 39.1).
- Warning that streamlines record instantaneous velocities, not particle histories, except in steady flow.
- Mass current J^mu = (rho0, rho0 v) with divergence zero expresses local mass conservation (39.1).
- Distinguishes rest-mass density rho0 from energy density rho c^2 (margin note 2).
- Defines vorticity omega = curl v and irrotational flow (39.2).

### §39.1 Euler's equation — p.413 (pdf 430)

Derives the Newtonian equation of motion of an inviscid fluid. Pressure acts inward on each element's surface; the gradient theorem turns this into minus the volume integral of grad p, and equating with mass times acceleration for arbitrary elements gives rho0 dv/dt = -grad p. The total derivative is identified as what a comoving observer measures, and the chain rule converts it into the convective derivative partial_t + v.grad for a fixed observer. Applied to continuity, this yields the comoving form drho0/dt = -rho0 div v, and a fixed-mass parcel shows div v = (1/V) dV/dt. Euler's equation follows, with gravity added as an extra acceleration; the static case gives the hydrostatic equation and Pascal's law. Finally a vector identity rewrites Euler in terms of vorticity and a gravitational potential, the most general form of Bernoulli's equation.

**Concepts:** pressure force on a fluid element, convective derivative, comoving and stationary observers, divergence rule, Euler's equation, hydrostatic equilibrium, Pascal's law, Bernoulli's equation, vorticity

**Key moves**
- F = -closed integral dS p = -integral d^3x grad p (39.3); no gradient, no net force (margin note 3).
- Integrand of integral d^3x (rho0 dv/dt + grad p) must vanish because the element is arbitrary (39.5-39.6).
- Example 39.1: chain rule dA^i/dt = partial_t A^i + v^j partial_j A^i defines the convective derivative (39.7-39.9).
- Example 39.2: continuity rewritten for the comoving observer; with dM/dt = 0 for M = rho0 V this gives div v = (1/V) dV/dt (39.10-39.11).
- Euler's equation for the stationary observer (39.12) and with gravity (39.13).
- Example 39.3: v = 0 gives grad p = rho0 g and p(z) = -rho0 g z + const (39.14-39.16).
- Example 39.4: v x omega = grad(v^2/2) - (v.grad)v converts Euler into the general Bernoulli form with g = -grad Phi (39.17-39.18).

### §39.2 Energy and Bernoulli's equation — p.415 (pdf 432)

Adds thermodynamics. The first law is written per unit mass using V -> 1/rho0, and enthalpy per unit mass h = u + p/rho0 is introduced because its natural variables are s and p. A perfect fluid produces no entropy, so du = -p d(1/rho0) and dh = dp/rho0, giving p = rho0^2 du/drho0. For steady flow the comoving rate of change equals the derivative along streamlines. Dotting the general Bernoulli form with v removes the vorticity term; for steady perfect-fluid flow the result is that kinetic energy plus potential plus enthalpy per unit mass is constant along each streamline, which shows Bernoulli is energy conservation. For irrotational flow a velocity potential phi with v = grad phi turns the whole equation into a gradient, so the unsteady Bernoulli combination (with partial_t phi) is uniform throughout the fluid rather than only along streamlines.

**Concepts:** first law of thermodynamics per unit mass, specific enthalpy, perfect fluid, isentropic flow, steady flow, Bernoulli's equation, velocity potential, irrotational flow

**Key moves**
- Unit-mass first law du = T ds - p d(1/rho0) and enthalpy form dh = T ds + dp/rho0 (39.20-39.21).
- Perfect fluid: ds = 0, so du = -p d(1/rho0), dh = dp/rho0 and p = rho0^2 du/drho0 (39.22-39.23).
- Example 39.5: entropy constant along the flow, ds/dt = (v.grad)s = 0 (39.24).
- Steady flow: d/dt = v.grad (39.25), so rho0 (v.grad)h = (v.grad)p (39.26).
- Example 39.6: dot Bernoulli with v, drop v.(v x omega) = 0, get v.grad(v^2/2 + Phi + h) = 0 (39.27-39.28).
- Reinterpretation: (1/2)v^2 + Phi + h, equivalently (1/2)v^2 + Phi + u + p/rho0, is conserved along streamlines (39.29-39.31).
- Example 39.7: with v = grad phi, omega = 0 and isentropic flow, the whole Bernoulli equation is grad of (v^2/2 + Phi + h + partial_t phi), which is therefore uniform in space (39.32-39.37).

### §39.3 Energy-momentum tensor — p.418 (pdf 435)

Shows that the conservation of stress-energy reproduces the Newtonian fluid equations. The relativistic perfect-fluid tensor from Chapter 12 is recalled; in flat spacetime with slow motion the energy density dwarfs the pressure, so the tensor keeps only a density-weighted product of velocities plus pressure times the Minkowski metric (39.41). Differentiating with v^mu = (1, v) and taking the time component gives an energy-flow (continuity-type) statement; the spatial components, after using mass conservation to cancel three terms, give Euler's equation. The authors admit the argument is untidy and promise a cleaner projection method in the relativistic section.

**Concepts:** energy-momentum tensor of a perfect fluid, conservation of energy-momentum, non-relativistic limit of the fluid stress tensor, continuity equation, Euler's equation

**Key moves**
- Key equation div T = 0 (39.38) as the universal source of matter-field dynamics.
- Energy density rho includes rest mass plus internal energy, rho c^2 = rho0 c^2 + rho0 u (margin note 11).
- Slow flow in flat spacetime: rho >> p/c^2 lets (rho + p) be replaced by rho in the tensor (39.40-39.41).
- Example 39.8: expand partial_mu T^{mu nu} with v^mu = (1, v) (39.42-39.43).
- Time component gives partial_t p = partial_t rho + div(rho v) (39.44).
- Spatial components minus a mass-conservation bracket give rho(partial_t v + v.grad v) = -grad p (39.45-39.46).
- Moral: a single tensor conservation law contains both mass-energy conservation and the equations of motion.

### §39.4 Relativistic fluids: variables, upgraded rules and the stress-energy tensor — p.420 (pdf 437)

Sets up the relativistic perfect fluid. Flow lines are integral curves of the unit timelike 4-velocity field, and the derivative along them is nabla_u; in general they are not geodesics, and the relativistic Euler equation will quantify the departure caused by pressure gradients. The fluids of interest are the cosmological fluid and stellar matter, made mostly of baryons. Rest-frame scalars are defined: baryon number density n, rest-mass density rho0 = m_b n, total energy density rho = rho0(1 + u), pressure p, temperature T and entropy per baryon s. The Newtonian derivative and divergence rules are upgraded to D/dtau = nabla_u and div u = (1/V) dV/dtau (Example 39.9), justified by flat-space component checks and the principle of covariance. The perfect-fluid T and its covariant conservation are written down, and Example 39.10 expands the divergence with the Leibniz rule.

**Concepts:** 4-velocity field, covariant directional derivative, relativistic divergence rule, baryon number density, total energy density, principle of covariance, energy-momentum tensor of a perfect fluid, fluid Lagrangian density

**Key moves**
- Flow-line tangents w = d/dtau normalized as u = w / sqrt(-g(w, w)) so u^2 = -1 (margin note 13).
- Scalar fields n, rho, p, T, s are frame independent but defined by comoving measurements (margin note 15).
- rho = rho0(1 + u) (39.48); margin note 16 gives the Lagrangian density L = -rho for later use.
- Example 39.9: in flat space nabla_u A = (u^t partial_t + u^i nabla_i) A mirrors the convective derivative; u^t = 1 non-relativistically motivates (1/V) dV/dtau = div u (39.50-39.52).
- T^{mu nu} = (rho + p) u^mu u^nu + p g^{mu nu}, rest-frame form diag(rho, p, p, p), and T^{mu nu}_{;nu} = 0 (39.53-39.54).
- Example 39.10: Leibniz expansion (rho + p)_{,beta} u^alpha u^beta + (rho + p)(u^alpha_{;beta} u^beta + u^alpha u^beta_{;beta}) + p_{,beta} g^{alpha beta} = 0 (39.55).

### 39.4 (cont.) Laws I-III: baryon, entropy and energy conservation — p.421 (pdf 438)

Baryon conservation d(nV)/dtau = 0 becomes div(n u) = 0 via the two upgraded rules (Example 39.11). Entropy in a comoving volume is nsV; with baryon conservation, the second law says entropy per baryon cannot decrease along flow lines, and a perfect fluid keeps it constant. Energy conservation is extracted by contracting the divergence of T with u_alpha, using u.u = -1 and u.nabla_u u = 0 (Example 39.12), giving nabla_u rho + (rho + p) div u = 0; with the volume rule and baryon conservation this becomes drho/dtau = ((rho + p)/n) dn/dtau (Example 39.13). A relativistic first law, obtained by writing the energy of a volume holding N baryons as rho N/n, is drho = ((rho + p)/n) dn + nT ds, which at ds = 0 reproduces the projected equation.

**Concepts:** baryon number conservation, entropy per baryon conservation, second law of thermodynamics, projection of the conservation law along the 4-velocity, relativistic first law of thermodynamics, normalization of the 4-velocity

**Key moves**
- Example 39.11: 0 = dn/dtau + (n/V) dV/dtau = u.grad n + n div u = div(n u) (39.59-39.60).
- Margin note 21: div u = -(1/rho0) drho0/dtau (printed with dt).
- d(nsV)/dtau >= 0 plus d(nV)/dtau = 0 gives ds/dtau >= 0; perfect fluid has equality (39.62).
- Energy law from u_alpha T^{alpha beta}_{;beta} = 0 (39.63).
- Example 39.12: u.nabla_u u = (1/2) nabla_u(u^2) = 0 removes the acceleration term, leaving -rho_{,beta} u^beta - (rho + p) u^beta_{;beta} = 0 (39.64-39.68).
- Example 39.13: rule (ii) and baryon conservation give drho/dtau - ((rho + p)/n) dn/dtau = 0 (39.69-39.70).
- First law with V = N/n: d(rho N/n) = -p d(N/n) + T d(Ns), hence drho = ((p + rho)/n) dn + nT ds (39.71-39.73).

### 39.4 (cont.) Laws IV-V: momentum, the relativistic Euler equation and equations of state — p.423 (pdf 440)

Momentum conservation is the part of the divergence of T perpendicular to u, isolated with the projection tensor P_{alpha mu} = g_{alpha mu} + u_alpha u_mu. Example 39.14 applies P and obtains (rho + p) nabla_u u = -P.grad p, the relativistic Euler equation. Its left side is the relativistic convective acceleration of the flow lines; comparing with the geodesic equation shows that only pressure gradients transverse to u make flow lines non-geodesic, so uniform-pressure fluids and pressureless dust move on geodesics. A margin list gives the equations of state used in the book (vacuum, radiation, dust, homogeneous scalar field). Example 39.15 obtains pressure from rho(n, s) and from a free energy a(n, T), the latter being the form used in Chapter 40. A closing remark names the Lie derivative as the natural comoving derivative, developed in the exercises.

**Concepts:** projection tensor, relativistic Euler equation, geodesic equation, dust, equation of state, pressure from an equation of state, Lie derivative along the flow

**Key moves**
- Perpendicular part of a vector: v + (u.v)u, matching P_{alpha mu} v^mu (margin note 24, 39.74-39.75).
- Momentum law P_{alpha mu} T^{mu nu}_{;nu} = 0 (39.76).
- Example 39.14: projecting with P leaves the acceleration term, the pressure gradient and the pressure change along u (39.77), which assemble into the relativistic Euler equation (39.80).
- Non-relativistic limit rho >> p/c^2 recovers Euler; convective derivative replaced by nabla_u.
- Geodesic comparison: transverse pressure gradients are the only cause of non-geodesic flow; dust is geodesic.
- Example 39.15: p(n, s) = n (partial rho/partial n)_s - rho and p(n, T) = n^2 (partial a/partial n)_T (39.81-39.84).

### Chapter summary and exercises — p.425 (pdf 442)

Three-point summary: fluids are described by a velocity field or its integral curves; Euler (motion) and Bernoulli (energy) are both contained in the vanishing divergence of T; relativistic Euler measures departure from geodesic motion due to transverse pressure gradients. Nine exercises follow on pages 425-427, ranging from Torricelli's outflow speed to perturbation growth in an Einstein-de Sitter universe.

**Concepts:** Euler's equation, Bernoulli's equation, relativistic Euler equation

**Key moves**
- Summary ties motion and energy equations back to a single tensor conservation law.

## Concepts

### classical field theory

*definition · mention* · also: pre-quantum field theory

A description of physics by fields defined over spacetime that obey deterministic equations, relativistic but not quantized.

**How introduced:** Stated in the Part VI opener as one of the two viewpoints (with differential geometry) the part will use; the fluid is presented as a particularly satisfying example.

**Where:** GA p.411 (pdf 428); GA p.412 (pdf 429)

### fluid

*definition · introduced* · also: continuum matter

Matter that keeps deforming for as long as a shear stress acts on it, modelled as a continuum whose elements are labelled by position and carried along by a flow.

**How introduced:** Opening definition, followed immediately by the field description: a scalar mass density and a vector velocity field over space and time.

$$
\rho_0(t,\vec x),\ \vec v(t,\vec x)
$$

**Where:** GA p.412 (pdf 429)

### perfect fluid

*definition · core* · also: inviscid fluid, ideal fluid, dry water

A fluid with no viscosity and no heat conduction, so no dissipation or entropy production; in its rest frame it is fully characterized by an energy density and an isotropic pressure.

**How introduced:** Viscosity is deliberately dropped at the start (the joke of the title), justified because the fluids of relativity are essentially inviscid; in section 39.2 the no-dissipation condition ds = 0 is made the operational definition.

**Prerequisites:** fluid, entropy

$$
\mathrm{d}s=0
$$

$$
T^{\mu\nu}=(\rho+p)u^\mu u^\nu+p\,g^{\mu\nu}
$$

**Notes:** Chapter 12 used the tensor; here the thermodynamic meaning (no entropy production) is added.

**Where:** GA p.412 (pdf 429); GA §39.2 p.416 (pdf 433); GA §39.4 p.421 (pdf 438)

### streamlines

*definition · core* · also: flow lines, integral curves of the velocity field, congruence of flow curves

The family of curves, one through every point, whose tangent at each point is the instantaneous fluid velocity there; in relativity, the integral curves of the 4-velocity field.

**How introduced:** Velocity is introduced as a tangent field to a space-filling congruence (Fig. 39.1); in 39.4 the flow lines become timelike curves with tangent d/dtau normalized to u.

**Prerequisites:** fluid, vector field

$$
\boldsymbol u=\boldsymbol w/\sqrt{-g(\boldsymbol w,\boldsymbol w)}
$$

**Notes:** Newtonian streamlines are a snapshot picture; relativistic flow lines are worldlines in spacetime.

**Where:** GA p.412 (pdf 429); GA §39.4 p.420 (pdf 437)

### steady flow

*definition · developed* · also: stationary flow

Flow in which no field quantity changes with time at a fixed position, so partial_t of anything vanishes; streamlines then coincide with the paths of fluid elements.

**How introduced:** First used in the caution that streamlines equal trajectories only for steady flow; formalized in 39.2 where it reduces the convective derivative to v.grad.

**Prerequisites:** streamlines, convective derivative

$$
\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}=\vec v\cdot\vec\nabla
$$

**Where:** GA p.412 (pdf 429); GA §39.2 p.416 (pdf 433)

### mass current

*physical-quantity · developed* · also: mass flux 4-vector, rest-mass current

The 4-vector J with components (rho0, rho0 v): time component the mass density, spatial part the mass flux density.

**How introduced:** Built to express local mass conservation as a vanishing 4-divergence; exercise 39.4 generalizes to a distributional current for point particles.

**Prerequisites:** fluid

$$
J^\mu=(\rho_0,\rho_0\vec v)
$$

$$
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol J=0
$$

**Where:** GA p.412 (pdf 429); GA p.426 (pdf 443)

### continuity equation

*law · core* · also: local mass conservation, conservation of mass

The statement that the rate of change of mass density at a point equals minus the divergence of the mass flux there, so mass in any volume changes only by flow through its boundary.

**How introduced:** Derived from the mass current; Example 39.2 rewrites it for the comoving observer as drho0/dt = -rho0 div v; section 39.3 recovers it from the stress-energy tensor; exercise 39.6 recasts it as a Lie-derivative statement.

**Prerequisites:** mass current, convective derivative

$$
\partial_t\rho_0+\vec\nabla\cdot(\rho_0\vec v)=0
$$

$$

### pressure force on a fluid element

*principle · developed* · also: pressure gradient force

The net force from surrounding fluid on an element equals minus the integral of pressure over its outward-directed surface, which by the gradient theorem is minus the volume integral of grad p; only pressure differences push.

### convective derivative

*operation · core* · also: material derivative, total derivative, substantive derivative, Lagrangian derivative

The rate of change of a quantity following a fluid element, equal to the local rate at fixed position plus the directional derivative along the velocity: d/dt = partial_t + v.grad.

**How introduced:** Motivated by noticing that the d/dt in Newton's law belongs to an observer carried with the fluid; Example 39.1 derives it by the chain rule; relabelled as the 'derivative rule' in margin note 6; upgraded to nabla_u in 39.4.

### comoving and stationary observers

*convention · core* · also: Lagrangian and Eulerian viewpoints, comoving frame, stationary frame, static frame

Two ways of recording fluid behaviour: an observer carried with a fluid element measures total time derivatives, while an observer at fixed position measures partial derivatives; equations are translated between them with the convective derivative.

**How introduced:** Introduced when interpreting d/dt in Euler's equation, explicitly tied back to comoving observers from the cosmology chapters; relativistically the comoving observer uses proper time along a flow line.

**Prerequisites:** convective derivative

### divergence rule

*identity · core* · also: divergence of velocity as fractional volume change, rule (ii)

The divergence of the velocity field equals the fractional rate of change of the volume of a comoving fluid element: div v = (1/V) dV/dt.

### Euler's equation

### hydrostatic equilibrium

## Worked examples

### Example 39.1 · intro · GA §39.1 p.413 (pdf 430)

**Problem:** Relate the rate of change of a vector field seen by an observer drifting with the fluid to rates measured at a fixed point.

**Method:** Write the differential of a component A^i in terms of partial derivatives in t and x^j, divide by dt, and identify dx^j/dt with the fluid velocity.

**Key insight:** The comoving rate is local change plus transport along the velocity; the extra piece is a directional derivative along streamlines.

**Result:** dA^i/dt = \partial_t A^i + v^j \partial_j A^i, giving d/dt = \partial_t + \vec v\cdot\vec\nabla

**Concepts:** convective derivative, comoving and stationary observers

### Example 39.2 · intro · GA §39.1 p.414 (pdf 431)

**Problem:** Express mass conservation in the comoving frame and extract the meaning of div v.

**Method:** Expand the divergence of rho0 v with the product rule, group partial_t rho0 + v.grad rho0 into the convective derivative, then combine with the constancy of a parcel's mass M = rho0 V.

**Key insight:** Density following the flow drops exactly as fast as the parcel's volume grows, so div v is a fractional volume growth rate.

**Result:** d\rho_0/dt = -\rho_0\,\vec\nabla\cdot\vec v;\ \vec\nabla\cdot\vec v = (1/V)\,dV/dt

**Concepts:** continuity equation, divergence rule

### Example 39.3 · intro · GA §39.1 p.415 (pdf 432)

**Problem:** Find the pressure distribution in a fluid at rest under uniform gravity.

**Method:** Set v = 0 in Euler's equation with gravity, write Cartesian components with g along -z, integrate in z.

**Key insight:** Static fluids need a pressure gradient that exactly holds up their weight; horizontal pressure is uniform.

**Result:** \vec\nabla p = \rho_0\vec g;\ p(z) = -\rho_0 g z + \text{const}

**Concepts:** hydrostatic equilibrium, Pascal's law

### Example 39.4 · standard · GA §39.1 p.415 (pdf 432)

**Problem:** Rewrite Euler's equation so that the nonlinear advection term is replaced by a gradient and a vorticity term.

**Method:** Apply the double cross product identity to v x (curl v), substitute for (v.grad)v, and replace g by -grad Phi.

**Key insight:** Euler's equation already contains an energy-like gradient grad(v^2/2 + Phi); what is left over is the vorticity force v x omega.

**Result:** \partial_t\vec v + \vec\nabla(\tfrac12 v^2+\Phi) + \vec\nabla p/\rho_0 - \vec v\times\vec\omega = 0

**Concepts:** Bernoulli's equation, vorticity, Euler's equation

### Example 39.5 · intro · GA §39.2 p.416 (pdf 433)

**Problem:** State how the entropy of a perfect fluid behaves in comoving and fixed frames.

**Method:** Combine ds/dt = 0 for a non-dissipative fluid with the convective derivative and an assumption of no explicit time dependence of s.

**Key insight:** Entropy is carried unchanged along streamlines, so its gradient has no component along the flow.

**Result:** ds/dt = (\vec v\cdot\vec\nabla)s = 0

**Concepts:** isentropic flow, perfect fluid, convective derivative

### Example 39.6 · standard · GA §39.2 p.417 (pdf 434)

**Problem:** Derive the familiar streamline form of Bernoulli's equation for steady perfect-fluid flow.

**Method:** Dot the general Bernoulli form with v so the vorticity term drops (v is perpendicular to v x omega), set partial_t = 0, and replace (v.grad p)/rho0 by v.grad h using the steady perfect-fluid enthalpy relation.

**Key insight:** Projecting a vector equation onto the velocity leaves only the part that describes energy along the streamline.

**Result:** \vec v\cdot\vec\nabla(\tfrac12v^2+\Phi+h) = 0 \Rightarrow \tfrac12v^2+\Phi+u+p/\rho_0 = \text{const on streamlines}

**Concepts:** Bernoulli's equation, specific enthalpy, steady flow

### Example 39.7 · standard · GA §39.2 p.418 (pdf 435)

**Problem:** Find a Bernoulli law for unsteady, isentropic, irrotational flow.

**Method:** Take the spatial gradient of the enthalpy first law, drop grad s, set omega = 0 and v = grad phi in the general Bernoulli form, and collect every term under one gradient.

**Key insight:** Because the full gradient (not just its component along v) vanishes, the Bernoulli combination is the same at every point in the fluid, not merely along each streamline.

**Result:** \tfrac12v^2+\Phi+h+\partial_t\phi = \text{const in space}

**Concepts:** Bernoulli's equation, velocity potential, irrotational flow, isentropic flow

### Example 39.8 · standard · GA §39.3 p.419 (pdf 436)

**Problem:** Show that the conservation of the non-relativistic fluid stress-energy tensor gives both a conservation law and Euler's equation.

**Method:** Insert the slow-flow tensor (39.41) into the flat-space conservation law, expand by the product rule with v^mu = (1, v), and examine the nu = t and nu = i components separately; in the spatial part use mass conservation to cancel a bracket multiplying v^i.

**Key insight:** One tensor equation holds four scalar equations: its time component is a conservation statement and its space components are the momentum equation.

**Result:** \partial_t p = \partial_t\rho + \vec\nabla\cdot(\rho\vec v);\ \rho(\partial_t\vec v + \vec v\cdot\vec\nabla\vec v) = -\vec\nabla p

**Concepts:** conservation of energy-momentum, non-relativistic limit of the fluid stress tensor, Euler's equation, continuity equation

### Example 39.9 · standard · GA §39.4 p.420 (pdf 437)

**Problem:** Justify the relativistic versions of the derivative rule and the divergence rule.

**Method:** For rule (i), write nabla_u A in flat space with vanishing connection and expand into u^t partial_t + u^i partial_i, noting u = (gamma, gamma v). For rule (ii), observe u^t = dt/dtau is close to 1 for slow flow and propose div u = (1/V) dV/dtau, then extend to curved spacetime by covariance.

**Key insight:** Proper time replaces coordinate time and the spacetime directional derivative absorbs the separate time and advection pieces of the convective derivative.

**Result:** D\boldsymbol A/d\tau = \nabla_{\boldsymbol u}\boldsymbol A;\ (1/V)\,dV/d\tau = \nabla\cdot\boldsymbol u

**Concepts:** covariant directional derivative, relativistic divergence rule, principle of covariance

### Example 39.10 · standard · GA §39.4 p.421 (pdf 438)

**Problem:** Expand the covariant divergence of the perfect-fluid stress-energy tensor.

**Method:** Apply the Leibniz rule to (rho + p) u^alpha u^beta + p g^{alpha beta}, using metric compatibility so g^{alpha beta}_{;beta} = 0.

**Key insight:** This single expanded form is the parent of both the energy and momentum equations, obtained later by different projections.

**Result:** (\rho+p)_{,\beta}u^\alpha u^\beta + (\rho+p)(u^\alpha{}_{;\beta}u^\beta + u^\alpha u^\beta{}_{;\beta}) + p_{,\beta}g^{\alpha\beta} = 0

**Concepts:** energy-momentum tensor of a perfect fluid, conservation of energy-momentum

### Example 39.11 · intro · GA §39.4 p.422 (pdf 439)

**Problem:** Prove the covariant baryon conservation law from the comoving statement d(nV)/dtau = 0.

**Method:** Expand the comoving derivative with the product rule, convert dn/dtau with rule (i) into u.grad n and (1/V) dV/dtau with rule (ii) into div u, then recognize the product rule for div(n u).

**Key insight:** The two translation rules turn a statement about one parcel into a local field equation valid in any coordinates.

**Result:** \nabla\cdot(n\boldsymbol u) = 0,\ (nu^\alpha)_{;\alpha} = 0

**Concepts:** baryon number conservation, covariant directional derivative, relativistic divergence rule

### Example 39.12 · challenging · GA §39.4 p.422 (pdf 439)

**Problem:** Extract the energy equation by contracting the expanded divergence of T with u_alpha.

**Method:** Multiply (39.55) by u_alpha, use u^alpha u_alpha = -1 to simplify each term, cancel the pressure terms, and show the leftover u_alpha u^alpha_{;beta} u^beta equals (1/2) nabla_u(u.u) = 0.

**Key insight:** The normalization of u makes the 4-acceleration orthogonal to u, so projection along u sees only density change and expansion.

**Result:** \nabla_{\boldsymbol u}\rho + (\rho+p)\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol u = 0

**Concepts:** projection of the conservation law along the 4-velocity, normalization of the 4-velocity

### Example 39.13 · standard · GA §39.4 p.423 (pdf 440)

**Problem:** Rewrite the projected energy equation in terms of baryon density.

**Method:** Replace div u by (1/V) dV/dtau, then use d(nV)/dtau = 0 to trade dV/V for -dn/n.

**Key insight:** Energy density rises under compression at a rate set by rho + p per baryon, which is exactly what the first law predicts at fixed entropy.

**Result:** d\rho/d\tau - ((\rho+p)/n)\,dn/d\tau = 0

**Concepts:** projection of the conservation law along the 4-velocity, baryon number conservation, relativistic first law of thermodynamics

### Example · standard · GA §39.4 p.423 (pdf 440)

**Problem:** Show that the projected energy equation is the relativistic first law of thermodynamics (worked in the main text after Example 39.13).

**Method:** Apply d(energy) = -p dV + T dS to a comoving volume holding N baryons, with energy rho N/n, volume N/n and entropy Ns; cancel the common N and rearrange.

**Key insight:** Counting energy per fixed set of baryons, rather than per fixed volume, gives thermodynamics that follows the flow automatically.

**Result:** d\rho = ((p+\rho)/n)\,dn + nT\,ds

**Concepts:** relativistic first law of thermodynamics, baryon number density

### Example 39.14 · challenging · GA §39.4 p.424 (pdf 441)

**Problem:** Derive the relativistic Euler equation from the part of the conservation law orthogonal to u.

**Method:** Contract (39.55) with P_{alpha mu} = g_{alpha mu} + u_alpha u_mu; terms proportional to u^mu are annihilated, the acceleration term survives because it is already orthogonal to u, and the pressure term becomes grad p plus its component along u times u. Rewrite in index-free form with the (2,0) projector.

**Key insight:** Only pressure gradients lying in the fluid's local rest space accelerate the flow, and the inertia resisting them is rho + p rather than rho.

**Result:** (\rho+p)\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}\boldsymbol u = -\boldsymbol P\cdot\nabla p

**Concepts:** relativistic Euler equation, projection tensor, geodesic equation

### Example 39.15 · standard · GA §39.4 p.424 (pdf 441)

**Problem:** Obtain the pressure from two kinds of equation of state: rho(n, s) and a free energy a(n, T).

**Method:** Differentiate the relativistic first law with respect to n at fixed s to get p = n (partial rho/partial n)_s - rho; for a free energy with natural variables n and T, the pressure is a first derivative, p = n^2 (partial a/partial n)_T.

**Key insight:** Pressure is always a derivative of an energy-like potential with respect to density, which is why pressure gradients encode how the equation of state resists compression.

**Result:** p(n,s) = n(\partial\rho/\partial n)_s - \rho;\ p(n,T) = n^2(\partial a/\partial n)_T

**Concepts:** pressure from an equation of state, equation of state


## Analogies and intuitions

### 'Dry water': a fluid with the wetness (viscosity) removed. → perfect fluid · useful · GA p.412 (pdf 429)

The title and opening make the idealization memorable: keep density, pressure and flow, discard internal friction, because relativistic fluids such as the cosmic fluid and stellar matter behave that way to good approximation.

**Where it breaks down:** Real water is strongly affected by viscosity near boundaries; turbulence, drag and shear stresses are absent from this model, and a relativistic perfect fluid also has no heat conduction.

**App idea:** Side-by-side pipe flow: viscous profile (parabolic) versus perfect-fluid plug flow, with a viscosity slider that morphs one into the other.

### The comoving observer of cosmology drifting with a fluid parcel versus an observer fixed in place. → convective derivative · strong · GA §39.1 p.413 (pdf 430)

The authors link the total derivative in Newton's law to the comoving observer already met in the cosmology chapters, then relativistically to proper time along a flow line.

**Where it breaks down:** In cosmology the comoving frame is special for symmetry reasons; in a general fluid each element has its own comoving frame and there is no global comoving coordinate system.

**App idea:** Split screen: a thermometer fixed on a bridge and one riding a boat in a river with a temperature gradient; both readouts plotted, with the difference displayed as v.grad T.

### In flat space v.dv/dx = (1/2) d(v^2)/dx, so a vector of fixed length is perpendicular to its rate of change. → normalization of the 4-velocity · useful · GA §39.4 p.423 (pdf 440)

Used as the template for u.nabla_u u = (1/2) nabla_u(u^2) = 0 in the energy projection.

**Where it breaks down:** In Minkowski geometry perpendicular means Lorentz-orthogonal, which in a spacetime diagram does not look like a right angle.

**App idea:** Spacetime diagram showing a curved worldline with its unit 4-velocity and 4-acceleration drawn Lorentz-orthogonal, with a toggle to a Euclidean circle picture for comparison.

### Bernoulli's constant as an energy budget per unit mass: kinetic plus gravitational plus enthalpy. → Bernoulli's equation · strong · GA §39.2 p.417 (pdf 434)

After deriving the steady-flow form, the authors read each bracket term as an energy, turning a rewritten Euler equation into energy conservation along streamlines.

**Where it breaks down:** The budget is per streamline for steady flow; different streamlines can carry different constants unless the flow is irrotational.

**App idea:** Stacked bar chart riding along a streamline through a narrowing pipe: kinetic, potential and pressure bars trade height while their total stays fixed.

### Dust as a swarm of independent free-falling particles, each on its own geodesic. → dust · strong · GA §39.4 p.424 (pdf 441)

Emphasized after the relativistic Euler equation: with no pressure there is nothing to push elements off their free-fall paths.

**Where it breaks down:** Real cosmic matter has small pressures and velocity dispersion; dust flow lines can cross (caustics), where the fluid description breaks down.

**App idea:** Particle cloud in a gravitational field with a pressure slider: at zero pressure each particle traces a geodesic; raising pressure adds outward forces and the trajectories peel away from the geodesic ghosts.

### Removing the component of a vector along a direction, as in ordinary vector geometry, to build a spatial projector. → projection tensor · useful · GA §39.4 p.423 (pdf 440)

Margin note 24 writes the perpendicular part as v - (u.v)u/(u.u), which with u.u = -1 becomes v + (u.v)u, the action of P.

**Where it breaks down:** The sign flip relative to Euclidean projection comes from the timelike normalization and is easy to get wrong.

**App idea:** Interactive 1+1 diagram: drag a vector v and a unit timelike u; the display splits v into its part along u and its part in u's rest space.

### The cosmological fluid and stellar interiors, not fast water, as the relativistic fluids of interest. → perfect fluid · useful · GA §39.4 p.420 (pdf 437)

Recalibrates the learner's image of 'fluid' before defining baryon density, energy density and entropy per baryon.

**Where it breaks down:** Neutron-star matter and the early-universe plasma can have significant viscosity or heat flux in some regimes.


## Misconceptions addressed

### Streamlines show the paths that fluid particles follow. · GA p.412 (pdf 429)

**Correction:** Streamlines are tangent to the instantaneous velocity; they coincide with particle paths only when the flow is steady.

**Why tempting:** In the steady flows usually drawn (pipes, wings) the two coincide, and the name 'flow line' suggests a trajectory.

### A high pressure on a fluid element pushes it, even if the pressure is the same all around. · GA §39.1 p.413 (pdf 430)

**Correction:** Only pressure differences exert a net force; for uniform pressure the surface forces cancel and grad p = 0.

**Why tempting:** Everyday language treats pressure as a push in a direction.

### d/dt and partial/partial t mean the same thing for a fluid field. · GA §39.1 p.413 (pdf 430)

**Correction:** d/dt follows a fluid element; partial_t is taken at a fixed position; they differ by v.grad.

**Why tempting:** For a single particle's position there is only one time derivative, and notation is often used loosely.

### Bernoulli's equation is an independent law separate from Newton's laws for fluids. · GA §39.2 p.417 (pdf 434)

**Correction:** In its general form it is Euler's equation rearranged; in steady perfect-fluid flow it expresses energy conservation per unit mass along streamlines.

**Why tempting:** It is usually taught as a stand-alone formula about pressure and speed.

### The Bernoulli constant is the same everywhere in any flow. · GA §39.2 p.418 (pdf 435)

**Correction:** For steady flow it is constant only along each streamline; a single constant throughout the fluid requires irrotational flow (where the unsteady version holds everywhere).

**Why tempting:** Textbook problems often compare points on different streamlines without comment.

### Conservation of the stress-energy tensor only expresses energy conservation. · GA §39.3 p.419 (pdf 436)

**Correction:** Its time-like projection gives energy conservation, while its spatial projection gives the equations of motion (Euler); both are contained in one tensor law.

**Why tempting:** The phrase 'conservation law' suggests a single conserved scalar.

### Every element of a fluid in general relativity falls freely along a geodesic. · GA §39.4 p.424 (pdf 441)

**Correction:** Flow lines are geodesics only when there is no pressure gradient orthogonal to u; pressure gradients accelerate fluid elements off geodesics (that is how stars are held up).

**Why tempting:** GR is summarized as 'free particles follow geodesics', and dust models dominate cosmology courses.

### The energy density rho of a relativistic fluid is just its rest-mass density. · GA §39.4 p.420 (pdf 437)

**Correction:** rho includes internal (thermal, compressional) energy: rho = rho0(1 + u); pressure also contributes to inertia through rho + p.

**Why tempting:** In Newtonian fluid mechanics rho0 is the only density that appears.

### Scalars like n, rho and p depend on which observer measures them. · GA §39.4 p.420 (pdf 437)

**Correction:** They are scalar fields, identical for all observers at an event; they are defined as what a comoving observer would measure.

**Why tempting:** Density does change under boosts if one means the time component of a current rather than the rest-frame scalar.

### Pressure only matters for motion, not for inertia. · GA §39.4 p.424 (pdf 441)

**Correction:** In the relativistic Euler equation the inertia resisting a pressure gradient is rho + p; pressure also appears in the relativistic hydrostatic equation (exercise 39.5) as an extra source.

**Why tempting:** In the Newtonian limit p << rho c^2, so pressure's inertial role is invisible in everyday fluids.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### A perfect fluid's entropy cannot change anywhere in time. · GA §39.2 p.416 (pdf 433)

**Correction:** Entropy per unit mass is constant following each element; at a fixed point it can still change if fluid with different entropy flows past, unless the flow is steady or entropy is uniform.

**Why tempting:** The text states partial_t s = 0 for a perfect fluid, which is only true under extra assumptions.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*


## Thought experiments

### Riding observer versus fixed observer · GA §39.1 p.413 (pdf 430)

**Setup:** One observer floats with a small fluid parcel while another stays at a fixed location; both record how velocity (or density) changes with time.

**Lesson:** The two rates differ by transport along the flow; Newton's law is simplest for the rider, while field equations are written for the fixed observer.

**App idea:** Draggable probe in a flow animation that can be pinned or released; live plots of both readings plus their difference.

### Tagging a fixed mass of fluid · GA §39.1 p.414 (pdf 431)

**Setup:** Mark a blob containing mass M and follow it as it is carried and deformed by the flow; its density and volume change but M does not.

**Lesson:** Density decrease following the flow equals fractional volume increase, giving div v = (1/V) dV/dt; relativistically, the baryon count nV is fixed.

**App idea:** A dyed blob advected by a user-chosen field with a live readout of its area and of the integrated divergence inside it.

### Uniform-pressure element · GA §39.1 p.413 (pdf 430)

**Setup:** Imagine a small fluid element immersed in fluid whose pressure is the same everywhere around it.

**Lesson:** Surface forces cancel, so no net force acts; only a pressure gradient accelerates fluid.

### Pressureless dust versus a fluid with a pressure gradient · GA §39.4 p.424 (pdf 441)

**Setup:** Two fluids share an initial flow in the same spacetime: one with zero pressure, one with pressure varying across the flow.

**Lesson:** Dust elements trace geodesics; in the second fluid the transverse pressure gradient bends flow lines away from geodesics by an amount inversely proportional to rho + p.

**App idea:** Worldline plot with geodesic ghost paths and a pressure-gradient slider showing deviations growing.

### Compressing a box of N baryons · GA §39.4 p.423 (pdf 440)

**Setup:** Hold fixed a set of N baryons in a comoving volume V = N/n and squeeze it adiabatically.

**Lesson:** Work -p dV raises the energy rho V, so rho climbs at rate (rho + p)/n per unit increase in n; this is the relativistic first law and the energy equation.

**App idea:** Piston animation with sliders for equation of state (dust, radiation); plots rho versus n with the (rho + p)/n slope.


## Applications and observations

- **Pressure increase with depth (Pascal's law)** (numerical-estimate, GA §39.1 p.415 (pdf 432)): Hydrostatic balance gives a linear pressure rise with depth in a uniform liquid. Our own estimate for water shows the scale. Key numbers: Own estimate: rho0 g = 1000 kg m^-3 x 9.8 m s^-2, about 9.8 kPa per metre, so roughly one extra atmosphere per 10.3 m of water.
- **Outflow speed from a reservoir (Torricelli, exercise 39.1)** (numerical-estimate, GA p.425 (pdf 442)): Bernoulli along a streamline from the free surface to an outlet pipe at the bottom, with equal (atmospheric) pressure at both ends and negligible surface speed. Key numbers: v = sqrt(2g(y2 - y1)); own estimate: a 5 m head gives about 9.9 m/s.
- **Cosmological fluid and its equations of state** (astrophysical-system, GA §39.4 p.424 (pdf 441)): The fluids of interest in relativity are the matter-energy filling the Universe and matter in stars, mostly baryons. The margin list gives the equations of state used in the cosmology chapters and later inflation discussion. Key numbers: dust p = 0; radiation p = rho/3; vacuum energy p = -rho; homogeneous scalar field w = (phidot^2/2 - V)/(phidot^2/2 + V).
- **Relativistic hydrostatic equilibrium of stars (exercise 39.5)** (astrophysical-system, GA p.426 (pdf 443)): Conservation of T for a static perfect fluid in a Schwarzschild-type geometry gives dp/dr = -(rho + p) dPhi/dr; pressure itself adds to the effective inertia and gravitational pull, which matters for compact stars (our comment).
- **Sound speed in a relativistic fluid (exercise 39.3)** (numerical-estimate, GA p.426 (pdf 443)): Linearizing baryon conservation and relativistic Euler about a static state gives a wave equation for density perturbations with c_s^2 = (dp/drho)_s. Key numbers: Own estimate: radiation-like fluid p = rho/3 gives c_s = c/sqrt(3), about 0.577c; dust gives c_s = 0.
- **Growth of structure in an Einstein-de Sitter universe (exercise 39.9)** (numerical-estimate, GA p.427 (pdf 444)): Newtonian fluid equations for the Hubble flow, perturbed and written in comoving coordinates, give a growing mode t^{2/3} and a decaying mode 1/t for the density contrast. Key numbers: delta proportional to t^{2/3}, i.e. proportional to the scale factor a (own remark); so a density contrast grows only about a thousand-fold between recombination (a ~ 1/1100) and today, which is our own estimate.

## Notation and conventions

- **Metric signature and 4-velocity normalization:** Signature (-,+,+,+) so u^alpha u_alpha = -1; timelike tangents normalized by dividing by sqrt(-g(w, w)). (GA §39.4 p.420 (pdf 437))
- **Units:** Natural units c = 1 in the relativistic sections; energy density rho c^2 = rho0 c^2 + rho0 u when c is restored; non-relativistic limit written rho >> p/c^2. Exercise 39.9 also sets G = 1 in Phi = 2 pi rho r^2/3. — The limit is quoted with c even inside c = 1 formulas. (GA §39.3 p.419 (pdf 436))
- **rho0 versus rho:** rho0 is rest-mass density (in 39.4, rho0 = m_b n); rho is total energy density including internal energy. In section 39.3 the same letter rho is used for the energy density in the slow-flow T. (GA p.412 (pdf 429))
- **Lower-case specific variables:** u, s, h denote internal energy, entropy and enthalpy per unit mass in 39.2; in 39.4 s is entropy per baryon and a free energy per baryon. — Upper case U, S, H are extensive. (GA §39.2 p.416 (pdf 433))
- **Symbol u used twice:** u is both specific internal energy (rho = rho0(1 + u)) and the fluid 4-velocity (bold u, components u^mu) on the same pages. — Main source of confusion in 39.4; context and boldface are the only cues. (GA §39.4 p.420 (pdf 437))
- **Potentials and omegas:** Phi is gravitational potential (or a metric function in exercise 39.5); phi is velocity potential in 39.2 but a scalar field in the equation-of-state list; omega is vorticity while omega-tilde in exercise 39.6 is the volume 3-form. (GA §39.2 p.418 (pdf 435))
- **Derivative notation:** d/dt total (comoving) derivative; partial_t at fixed position; D/dtau comoving proper-time rate; nabla_u = u.nabla; comma for partial and semicolon for covariant derivatives, with div T = 0 written T^{mu nu}_{;nu} = 0. (GA §39.4 p.421 (pdf 438))
- **Vectors, 4-vectors and forms:** Arrowed letters are 3-vectors; bold letters are 4-vectors or tensors; a tilde marks a 1-form (u-tilde, v-tilde) or other form; bold d is the exterior derivative. (GA §39.4 p.424 (pdf 441))
- **Projection tensor:** P with components P_{alpha mu} = g_{alpha mu} + u_alpha u_mu (a (0,2) tensor) and its (2,0) version P^{alpha beta} = g^{alpha beta} + u^alpha u^beta used in P.grad p. (GA §39.4 p.423 (pdf 440))
- **Background values in perturbation exercises:** Subscript * marks constant equilibrium values (n_*, rho_*, p_*, s_*) and delta marks small perturbations; primed coordinates are comoving in exercise 39.9. (GA p.426 (pdf 443))

**Skills practiced**
- applying steady-flow Bernoulli between two points on a streamline

- **39.1** (intro): Use Bernoulli's theorem to get the speed of liquid leaving a pipe at the base of a tank in terms of the surface height and outlet height. — *Classic Torricelli result; a quick sanity check that the energy reading of Bernoulli is usable.* Skills: Bernoulli along a streamline
- **39.2** (standard): Prove a special-relativistic steady-flow Bernoulli law: the quantity gamma(rho + p)/rho0 is constant along streamlines, using the time component of the conservation law and rest-mass conservation. — *Shows how the Newtonian enthalpy per unit mass generalizes to (rho + p)/rho0 with a gamma factor.* Skills: gamma-factor bookkeeping, combining energy and mass conservation
- **39.3** (standard): Perturb n, rho, p, s and v about a static, uniform perfect fluid; derive linearized baryon conservation and Euler equations, use the first law to relate delta rho and delta n, and obtain a wave equation with c_s^2 = (dp/drho)_s. — *Gives physical meaning to the equation of state and to the inertial role of rho + p.* Skills: linearization, first law at fixed entropy, wave equation derivation
- **39.4** (challenging): For a point particle's mass current written as a proper-time integral of a 4D delta function, show that its covariant divergence vanishes using the sqrt(-g) divergence formula and a delta-function identity. — *Connects the continuum current to discrete particles, as Chapter 12 did for T.* Skills: distributions, covariant divergence identity
- **39.5** (challenging): Using Schwarzschild-type connection coefficients, show that a static perfect fluid satisfies dp/dr + (p + rho) dPhi/dr = 0. — *The relativistic hydrostatic equation behind the TOV equation for stellar structure; shows pressure adding to the gravitational source term.* Skills: connection coefficients, vielbein transformations, covariant divergence
- **39.6** (challenging): Write mass conservation geometrically: compute the Lie derivative of rho0 times the volume 3-form along v in components, check it with the Cartan-type formula for Lie derivatives of forms, and conclude (partial_t + Lie_v)(rho0 omega-tilde) = 0. — *Realizes the closing remark that the Lie derivative is the comoving observer's natural derivative; follows Schutz (1980).* Skills: Lie derivative of forms, exterior derivative, interior product
- **39.7** (standard): Rewrite the Newtonian Euler equation for the velocity 1-form using Lie and exterior derivatives. — *Shows the advection term splits into a Lie derivative and a gradient of v^2/2.* Skills: Lie derivative of 1-forms, index-free notation
- **39.8** (intro): For a uniform velocity field in Euclidean space, construct the 2-form that returns the fluid flux through the parallelogram spanned by two displacement vectors. — *Gives a concrete physical reading of a 2-form (flux through an oriented area), which prepares the mass 3-form of exercise 39.6.* Skills: building a 2-form from a vector field, geometric meaning of wedge products
- **39.9** (challenging): Treat the matter in an Einstein-de Sitter universe as a Newtonian fluid: check the Hubble flow against Euler, Friedmann and Poisson, perturb density, velocity and potential, move to comoving coordinates, and show the density contrast has modes growing as t^{2/3} and decaying as 1/t. — *Links fluid mechanics to cosmic structure formation and shows how slowly perturbations grow.* Skills: perturbation theory, coordinate change to comoving frame, ODE solution by power law

## Teaching gems

### A two-entry dictionary translates Newtonian fluid statements into relativistic ones: comoving d/dt becomes D/dtau and partial_t + v.grad becomes nabla_u; div v = (1/V) dV/dt becomes div u = (1/V) dV/dtau. · GA §39.4 p.420 (pdf 437)

**Why it works:** The relativistic section becomes a translation exercise from something the learner already trusts, and each relativistic law has an obvious Newtonian parent.

**App idea:** Two-column 'Rosetta stone' card that animates each Newtonian term morphing into its relativistic counterpart, with hover links to the flow sandbox and the spacetime worldline view.

### Give div v a body: follow a tagged parcel of fixed mass and read the divergence as the rate its volume swells. · GA §39.1 p.414 (pdf 431)

**Why it works:** Replaces an abstract operator with a visible deformation and sets up baryon conservation d(nV)/dtau = 0 directly.

**App idea:** Advect a dyed polygon in a 2D field; show its area growth rate next to the averaged divergence inside it; switch to compressible and incompressible presets.

### The conservation law for T 'says too much'; projecting it along u gives energy conservation and projecting it perpendicular to u gives the equation of motion. · GA §39.4 p.422 (pdf 439)

**Why it works:** Turns an opaque tensor identity into two physically named equations and teaches projection as a general GR technique (reused for 3+1 splits).

**App idea:** 1+1 or 2+1 spacetime diagram with a draggable 4-vector (the divergence of T) and the local u; the vector splits live into its u-parallel (energy) and rest-space (momentum) pieces.

### Read the relativistic Euler equation against the geodesic equation: pressure gradients across the flow are the only thing pushing fluid off free fall, so dust is geodesic. · GA §39.4 p.424 (pdf 441)

**Why it works:** Connects fluids to the central GR idea of geodesic motion and explains at once why cosmology uses dust and why stars need pressure.

**App idea:** Cloud of fluid elements falling toward a mass with a pressure slider and a gradient-profile editor; geodesic ghosts vs actual flow lines, with the acceleration arrow equal to -P.grad p/(rho + p).

### Dot Euler (in vorticity form) with v to kill the vorticity term and reveal Bernoulli as energy per unit mass conserved along streamlines. · GA §39.2 p.417 (pdf 434)

**Why it works:** Shows a familiar formula emerging from a single projection, and makes clear why it holds per streamline rather than everywhere.

**App idea:** Venturi-tube simulation with a bar chart of v^2/2, Phi and h following a chosen streamline; toggle a swirl to show streamlines carrying different constants.

### Bookkeep energy for a fixed set of N baryons (V = N/n) to derive the relativistic first law and see that it matches the projected conservation law. · GA §39.4 p.423 (pdf 440)

**Why it works:** Links geometry (projection of div T) to thermodynamics (first law) and explains why rho + p, the enthalpy density, appears.

**App idea:** Piston with selectable equations of state; plot rho against n and overlay the slope (rho + p)/n predicted by the energy equation.

### Use u.u = -1 as a term-killer, justified by the familiar v.dv = (1/2) d(v^2). · GA §39.4 p.423 (pdf 440)

**Why it works:** A one-line trick that removes much index clutter and makes the 4-acceleration's orthogonality to u memorable.

**App idea:** Worldline curvature explorer showing u and a = nabla_u u as Lorentz-orthogonal arrows, with a numeric u.a readout pinned at zero.

### Warn early that streamlines are velocity snapshots, not particle paths, unless the flow is steady. · GA p.412 (pdf 429)

**Why it works:** Prevents a stubborn misconception before it forms and prepares the relativistic view of flow lines as worldlines.

**App idea:** Unsteady flow animation with streamlines, pathlines and streaklines drawn in three colours that coincide only when 'steady' is toggled on.


## Gaps and pitfalls

- **Example 39.5 asserts partial_t s = 0 in the static frame for any perfect fluid, and concludes (v.grad)s = 0. A perfect fluid only guarantees ds/dt = 0 along the flow; partial_t s = 0 needs steady flow or uniform entropy.** — Learners may think entropy is frozen at every point in space, and misapply (v.grad)s = 0 to unsteady flows. *Suggestion:* State ds/dt = partial_t s + v.grad s = 0 as the perfect-fluid condition and add the steady-flow or homentropic assumption explicitly when dropping either term.
- **Symbol clash: u denotes specific internal energy (rho = rho0(1 + u)) and the 4-velocity on the same pages of 39.4; phi is both velocity potential and scalar field; omega is vorticity and a volume form.** — Index expressions like (rho + p) u^mu u^nu next to rho0(1 + u) invite algebra mistakes and confusion. *Suggestion:* In the course use epsilon or e for specific internal energy, keep u for 4-velocity, and use distinct symbols for velocity potential and inflaton.
- **The margin equation-of-state list prints the scalar-field case as p = [(phidot/2 + V)/(phidot/2 - V)] rho, with no square and swapped signs. The correct form is p = [(phidot^2/2 - V)/(phidot^2/2 + V)] rho.** — Sign errors would give the wrong inflation limit (the printed version does not give p close to -rho when the potential dominates). *Suggestion:* Use the corrected formula and check limits: kinetic domination gives p = rho, potential domination gives p = -rho.
- **Minor printing slips in 39.4: rest-frame T^{ij} = p delta^{ik} (should be delta^{ij}); margin eqn 39.61 uses dt where dtau is meant; the first line of 39.64 carries a stray 0; the relativistic volume rule is called the 'derivative rule' in the margin summary and in the main text although it is the divergence rule; eqn 39.7 writes v^j as a partial derivative. The text export also garbles u^t as u^i in 39.51 and T^{0 mu} in exercise 39.2 (printed correctly).** — Careful readers stall on the inconsistencies; rule names get mixed up. *Suggestion:* Present cleaned equations and keep the labels 'derivative rule' and 'divergence rule' fixed.
- **Exercise 39.9(d) prints the linearized equation of motion as '0 = [...] delta v + (adot/a) delta v = -grad delta Phi', chaining a zero with a nonzero right side.** — The equation as printed is self-contradictory unless grad delta Phi = 0. *Suggestion:* Read it as [partial_t + v.grad] delta v + (adot/a) delta v = -grad delta Phi.
- **Example 39.8's non-relativistic derivation is internally inconsistent. Replacing (rho + p) by rho while keeping p eta^{mu nu} makes T^{tt} = rho - p instead of approximately rho, and that stray -p is exactly what produces the partial_t p on the left of eqn 39.44. The book then reads that term as an energy source via partial_t p = rho0 partial_t h, which the approximations do not justify. The authors themselves call the argument untidy (margin note 12).** — Learners may not see which terms are genuinely leading order and may misidentify the time component as full energy conservation rather than, at leading order, mass conservation. *Suggestion:* Keep (rho + p) until the end: with u^mu = gamma(1, v) the time component gives partial_t[gamma^2(rho + p) - p] + div[gamma^2(rho + p) v] = 0, whose leading order is mass continuity and whose next order is the Newtonian energy equation. Alternatively go straight to the projection method of 39.4.
- **The relativistic divergence rule div u = (1/V) dV/dtau is proposed by analogy and extended by covariance rather than derived.** — Learners may treat it as a guess and distrust later steps (baryon conservation, energy equation) that depend on it. *Suggestion:* Derive it from the Lie derivative of the volume form (L_u of the spatial volume element equals (div u) times it), or in local rest-frame coordinates from the Jacobian of the flow.
- **The physical meaning of rho + p as inertial mass density (enthalpy density) is never spelled out, though it appears in the energy equation, relativistic Euler, and exercises 39.3 and 39.5.** — The key relativistic novelty, that pressure has inertia and gravitates, stays hidden behind algebra. *Suggestion:* Pause after (39.80) to interpret rho + p, compare with a box of hot gas being harder to accelerate, and connect to the (rho + p) term in the TOV equation.
- **The entropy law d(nsV)/dtau >= 0 is motivated by 'no heat flow between flow lines', which actually implies equality for reversible flow; the inequality is the general second law including dissipation that the chapter otherwise excludes.** — Confusion about whether perfect fluids can generate entropy. *Suggestion:* State: in general entropy per baryon cannot decrease (second law); for a perfect fluid (no viscosity or heat conduction) it is exactly conserved.
- **The free energy a(n, T) in Example 39.15 is introduced without definition.** — Readers cannot verify p = n^2 (partial a/partial n)_T. *Suggestion:* Define a = rho/n - Ts (free energy per baryon); then da = (p/n^2) dn - s dT follows from the relativistic first law.
- **Viscosity and heat flux are dropped entirely; there is no mention of imperfect-fluid terms in T or where they matter (neutron-star mergers, early-universe damping).** — Learners may assume every relativistic fluid is perfect. *Suggestion:* Add a short sidebar showing the structure of the viscous and heat-flux corrections to T and when they matter.
- **Bernoulli for irrotational unsteady flow is said to hold with a spatially constant right side, but the constant can depend on time.** — Minor, but can confuse when comparing states at different times. *Suggestion:* Write the right side as C(t) and note it can be absorbed into phi.

## Tutor notes

- Check first whether the learner wants the Newtonian fluid or only the relativistic payoff; the book marks this chapter as optional, and a GR-focused learner can start from the relativistic Euler equation, borrowing the Newtonian ideas only as needed.
- Opening question: 'If you watch a river from a bridge and from a drifting boat, do you measure the same rate of change of water temperature? Why not?' Use it to introduce the convective derivative before any equations.
- Explanation order that works: fields (rho0, v) -> streamlines vs paths -> continuity -> pressure force -> convective derivative -> Euler -> Bernoulli as energy -> div T = 0 contains both -> relativistic dictionary -> four laws by projection -> relativistic Euler vs geodesic.
- Check for understanding: ask why a submarine at great depth is not pushed sideways despite enormous pressure (only gradients matter), and why the Bernoulli constant can differ between streamlines.
- When reaching 39.4, flag the symbol clash immediately: u is both specific internal energy and 4-velocity. Suggest the learner rewrite rho = rho0(1 + epsilon).
- Before the projections, make sure the learner can split an ordinary vector into parts along and perpendicular to a unit vector, then redo it with u.u = -1 to see the sign change in P = g + u x u.
- Frame the relativistic Euler equation as 'geodesic equation plus pressure force'; ask: what holds a star up against free fall? What happens to the flow lines of dust?
- Common learner question: 'Why rho + p instead of rho?' Answer with inertia of pressure (enthalpy density) and point to exercise 39.5, where the same combination sources gravity in hydrostatic equilibrium.
- Common learner question: 'Why does one tensor equation give both energy and momentum laws?' Answer: four component equations; contracting with u picks the time-like one for the comoving observer, and P picks the three spatial ones.
- Correct the book's misprints proactively when quoting: scalar-field w = (phidot^2/2 - V)/(phidot^2/2 + V); rest-frame T^{ij} = p delta^{ij}; div u = -(1/rho0) drho0/dtau.
- For advanced learners, connect to Part V: the comoving derivative is the Lie derivative along the flow, and continuity is conservation of the mass 3-form (exercise 39.6).
- Useful quick numbers: about 1 atm per 10 m of water; radiation sound speed c/sqrt(3); matter-era density contrasts grow in proportion to the scale factor.
- Retrieval hook for voice tutoring: dust <-> geodesics; pressure gradient <-> non-geodesic flow; projection along u <-> energy; projection across u <-> momentum; d(nV)/dtau = 0 <-> baryon conservation.
