# GA ch40 · Lagrangian field theory

> Builds the Lagrangian machinery for classical fields (action, Lagrangian density, Euler-Lagrange equations, tested on a vibrating string and the Klein-Gordon field), carries it into curved spacetime with sqrt(-g) and semicolons, varies the Einstein-Hilbert action to recover Einstein's equation, defines the energy-momentum tensor as the metric derivative of the matter Lagrangian, derives Noether currents from Killing symmetries, and finally extracts the relativistic Euler equation and the perfect-fluid T from a fluid Lagrangian.

**Pages:** printed 428–444 · pdf 445–461 · **Difficulty:** math 5/5, conceptual 4/5, novice-friendliness 2/5

The flat-space part (40.1-40.3) is gentle and well paced. The Einstein-Hilbert variation needs fluent index work, three variation identities (one quoted without proof) and several printed label and sign slips must be corrected mentally. Noether's theorem is compressed, skipping the steps that make the Lie-derivative argument work. The fluid section is long, heuristic in its middle step, and explicitly optional. Physical motivation for choosing R as the gravitational Lagrangian is thin.

## Role in the book

Second chapter of Part VI. Part II (ch12, ch13) obtained T and the field equation by physical guesswork; this chapter replaces that guesswork with a systematic variational recipe, using the Part V toolkit (volume form of ch37, covariant derivative of ch34, Lie derivative and Killing vectors of ch33, the log-determinant exercise of ch34). It follows ch39 on fluids, whose Euler equation and perfect-fluid tensor it re-derives, and it is the launch pad for the field-theory chapters that follow: scalar-field inflation (ch41), the electromagnetic field (ch42), gauge fields (ch44), gravitational waves obeying a wave equation (ch46) and quantum gravity/string theory (ch49). It is a spiral revisit of Einstein's equation, now as an equation of motion from an action.

## Learning objectives

- Reader can write the action of a field theory as a spacetime integral of a Lagrangian density built from fields and their first derivatives, including several fields plus an interaction term.
- Reader can derive the field Euler-Lagrange equation by varying fields inside a region with fixed boundary values, integrating by parts and discarding the surface term.
- Reader can obtain the wave equation from the string Lagrangian and the Klein-Gordon equation from the free scalar Lagrangian, and read each Lagrangian term as kinetic, gradient or mass energy.
- Reader can transfer a flat-space field theory to curved spacetime by inserting sqrt(-g), replacing eta by g and commas by semicolons, and derive the curved-space Klein-Gordon equation.
- Reader can explain why varying the metric is harder than varying a matter field (the volume element and all index raising depend on g) and state the three variation rules with their proofs or sources.
- Reader can carry out the variation of the Einstein-Hilbert action term by term and show it yields the Einstein tensor, with the Ricci-variation term reducing to a boundary term.
- Reader can compute the Hilbert energy-momentum tensor from a matter Lagrangian in both index positions and apply it to a scalar field and the Maxwell field.
- Reader can justify the coupling alpha = 16 pi that turns the variational equation into G = 8 pi T in geometrized units.
- Reader can derive a Noether current from a symmetry generated by a Killing vector and assemble the canonical energy-momentum tensor from translation symmetry.
- Reader can explain why the canonical tensor is generally unsuitable as the source of gravity (non-symmetric, not covariant) and why a symmetric T is linked to angular-momentum conservation.
- Reader can set up the Lagrangian description of a perfect fluid (density field, flow-line congruence, conserved current, L = -rho) and follow how the relativistic Euler equation and T = (rho + p) v v + p g emerge.
- Reader can state why fluid elements with transverse pressure gradients do not move on geodesics.
- Reader can use the geodesic Lagrangian and its Hamiltonian to recover geodesic motion and identify conserved momenta from metric symmetries (exercises).

## Teaching approach

A recipe-driven chapter that sells the Lagrangian method as a mechanical procedure: supply the fields, turn the handle, and equations of motion and energy-momentum tensors come out. Every new rule is first run in flat space on a familiar system (string, free scalar), then promoted to curved spacetime by the comma-to-semicolon upgrade. The gravitational part is framed as a toolkit problem: three variation rules are stated and proven or cited before the Einstein-Hilbert algebra is attempted, and T is found by demanding that the combined variation reproduce G = 8 pi T. Noether's theorem is reached geometrically through Lie dragging along Killing fields. The chapter ends with a long, optional, step-numbered fluid derivation borrowed from Hawking and Ellis, explicitly flagged as skippable. Tone is informal; the only justification offered for choosing R as the gravitational Lagrangian is covariance plus the fact that it yields the right field equation.

**Style:** derivation-heavy, computational-recipe, example-driven, spiral-revisit, conversational-informal, components-first

**Narrative arc**

1. Motivation: earlier chapters had to guess T and the field equation; a Lagrangian field theory promises a systematic recipe, and Hilbert found one for gravity in 1915.
2. Inventory of the fields in play: matter fields (scalar, electromagnetic 1-form, fluid density) that build T, and the metric field g that sets curvature.
3. Recall particle mechanics (action as time integral of L) and upgrade to fields: Lagrangian density, action as a 4-volume integral, multiple fields plus interaction.
4. Model systems: string Lagrangian from kinetic and tension energies; free scalar Lagrangian read as kinetic minus gradient minus mass energy.
5. Formalism: vary the fields in a region with fixed boundary, integrate by parts, drop the surface term, get the field Euler-Lagrange equation.
6. Checks: wave equation drops out for the string; Klein-Gordon equation for the scalar.
7. Curved spacetime: sqrt(-g) volume element, covariant derivatives, semicolon form of Euler-Lagrange for scalar and vector fields; curved Klein-Gordon equation.
8. Gravity: total action S_EH + alpha S_m; explanation that the metric variation changes the measure so it must be done by hand; Hilbert's choice of R as the Lagrangian density.
9. Toolkit: Rule 1 (variation of inverse metric), Rule 2 (variation of sqrt(-g)), Rule 3 (Palatini identity), with matrix proofs for the first two.
10. Grind: vary S_EH term by term, reduce the delta R term to a boundary integral, obtain the vacuum Einstein equation.
11. Energy-momentum tensor: include matter, match to G = 8 pi T, read off T as the metric derivative of the matter Lagrangian, fix alpha = 16 pi, derive the all-down form.
12. Applications: scalar-field and Maxwell T; remark that gravity itself has no such tensor.
13. Noether's theorem: drag the Lagrangian along a Killing vector, use Euler-Lagrange to get a conserved current; translations give the canonical energy-momentum tensor, contrasted with the Hilbert one.
14. Showpiece: perfect fluid as a field theory; vary flow lines by a displacement field while conserving the current, obtain the relativistic Euler equation and, via the Hilbert recipe, the perfect-fluid T.
15. Summary of three boxed results and exercises extending to Hamiltonian formulations and the symmetry of T.

**Signature moves**

- Field and functional presented as 'machines' with inputs and outputs, and the whole Lagrangian formalism as a machine that outputs T.
- Every result first proven in flat space on a concrete system, then promoted to curved spacetime by the comma-to-semicolon rule with the remark that the guessed form is correct.
- Toolkit-before-algebra: the three variation rules are isolated, numbered and proven (or cited) before the Einstein-Hilbert variation, whose three terms are then treated in reverse order.
- Energy-momentum tensor is not derived from first principles but reverse-engineered: guess the definition that makes the variational equation match G = 8 pi T, then fix alpha.
- Physical reading of Lagrangian terms as energy densities (kinetic, gradient cost, cost of existing), checked against T_00.
- Noether's theorem phrased with the Part V geometric vocabulary: symmetry means a vanishing Lie derivative of the metric along a Killing field.
- Long derivation broken into labelled steps (Step 0 to Step V) with a margin box giving explicit permission to skip it.

## Section by section

### Chapter opening: why Lagrangians for GR — p.428 (pdf 445)

After a Lagrange epigraph boasting of mechanics without pictures, the chapter pictures a classical field as a device mapping spacetime positions to amplitudes and argues that GR, being a classical field theory, should admit a Lagrangian treatment. The payoff advertised is a systematic, non-ad-hoc route to the energy-momentum tensor and a re-derivation of Einstein's equation, the route Hilbert followed in 1915 just after Einstein. It separates the matter fields (which build T) from the metric field g and promises to couple them through a single Lagrangian.

**Concepts:** classical field, matter fields versus the metric field, Einstein-Hilbert action

**Key moves**
- Complains that T was previously assembled by educated guessing, which is unsatisfying given its role as the source of gravity.
- Frames the goal: feed fields in, get equations of motion and T out, and recover Einstein's equation.
- Splits the fields into matter fields and the single geometric (0,2) field g.

### §40.1 Matter fields — p.429 (pdf 446)

Positions GR relative to quantum field theory: particles are excitations of quantized fields, while GR keeps fields classical but starts from the same kind of classical field description. Example 40.1 catalogues the fields to appear: a real scalar, the electromagnetic 1-form gauge field, and the mass-density field of a perfect fluid. Locality is stressed: knowing a field at a point plus its equation of motion lets you predict it elsewhere, and the route to that equation begins with the action.

**Concepts:** quantum field theory picture of particles, real scalar field, electromagnetic gauge 1-form field, rest-mass density field

**Key moves**
- QFT quantizes classical fields into operators; GR stops at the classical stage.
- Lists scalar, 1-form and fluid-density fields as the chapter's cast.
- Local field values plus an equation of motion give predictive power, motivating the action.

### §40.2 Action and equations of motion — p.430 (pdf 447)

Recalls the particle action as a time integral of L(q, qdot) and upgrades it: L becomes a spatial integral of a Lagrangian density, the action a 4-volume integral of a density depending on the field and its first derivatives. The string (Fig. 40.1, Example 40.2) supplies a concrete density; the free scalar (Example 40.3) supplies the standard relativistic one. Several fields combine additively with an interaction term. Varying the fields in a region with fixed boundary values, integrating by parts and invoking the divergence theorem (Example 40.4) gives the field Euler-Lagrange equation, which reproduces the string wave equation and the Klein-Gordon equation (Examples 40.5, 40.6). A footnote warns that this simple variation assumes the integration measure is independent of the varied field, which fails for the metric.

**Concepts:** action functional, Lagrangian density, Lagrangian of a stretched string, real scalar field, interaction Lagrangian, principle of stationary action for fields, Euler-Lagrange equation for fields, boundary term in the variation, wave equation, d'Alembertian operator, Klein-Gordon equation

**Key moves**
- S = integral dt L becomes S = integral d^4x script-L.
- String: kinetic energy density from the time derivative of displacement, potential energy density from the slope times tension.
- Scalar Lagrangian read as kinetic minus gradient energy minus mass term.
- Total action = sum of free actions + interaction action.
- Variation: delta(phi_,mu) = (delta phi)_,mu, integrate by parts, surface term vanishes because delta phi = 0 on the boundary, integrand must vanish for arbitrary delta phi.
- Apply to string (wave speed sqrt(tension/density)) and to the scalar (Klein-Gordon).

### §40.3 Fields in curved spacetime — p.433 (pdf 450)

Promotes the matter-field formalism to curved spacetime: the volume element becomes sqrt(-g) d^4x (footnote derivation from the volume 4-form), partial derivatives become covariant ones, and the Euler-Lagrange equation is written with semicolons for scalars and, component by component, for vector fields. Footnotes remind the reader that derivative indices sit naturally downstairs, so contractions use g^{mu nu}, and that g_{mu nu} = 1/g^{mu nu} only for diagonal metrics. Example 40.7 derives the curved-space Klein-Gordon equation.

**Concepts:** invariant volume element, minimal coupling, covariant Euler-Lagrange equation, Klein-Gordon equation

**Key moves**
- d^4x -> sqrt(-g) d^4x.
- For scalars the gradient needs no connection, so the Lagrangian is unchanged in form.
- Guess the covariant Euler-Lagrange equation by replacing partial with covariant derivatives and assert it is right.
- Extend to vector components with semicolon notation.
- Curved Klein-Gordon: g^{mu nu} phi_{;nu mu} - m^2 phi = 0.

### §40.4 Motivating the Einstein equation — p.434 (pdf 451)

Introduces the total action S = S_EH + alpha S_m, where varying matter should give the right-hand side of Einstein's equation and varying geometry the left. Because the varied field is the metric itself, which changes the volume element and index raising, the variation must be done from first principles with respect to g_{mu nu}. Hilbert's Lagrangian density for geometry is the Ricci scalar, stated without proof but motivated by general covariance. Three rules are assembled: the inverse-metric variation with its minus sign (matrix proof, Example 40.8), the determinant variation (log-det identity, Example 40.9), and the Palatini identity (cited). Example 40.10 varies S_EH as three pieces: the sqrt(-g) and inverse-metric variations give -(R^{mu nu} - (1/2) g^{mu nu} R) delta g_{mu nu}, while the delta R_{mu nu} term becomes a total divergence that vanishes on the boundary. The vacuum Einstein equation results.

**Concepts:** Einstein-Hilbert action, variation with respect to the metric, gravitational coupling constant in the action, variation of the inverse metric, variation of the metric determinant, log-determinant identity, Palatini identity, tensor character of connection variations, vacuum Einstein equation from the action, Einstein tensor

**Key moves**
- Total action = geometry part + alpha times matter part.
- Metric variation alters sqrt(-g) d^4x, so no off-the-shelf Euler-Lagrange formula applies.
- Choose R as the gravitational Lagrangian density (general covariance, simplest scalar).
- Rule 1 from varying M M^-1 = I; Rule 2 from ln det M = Tr ln M; Rule 3 quoted.
- Write R = g^{sigma rho} R_{sigma rho} and vary each of sqrt(-g), g^{sigma rho}, R_{sigma rho}.
- Index consistency check confirms varying g^{sigma rho} in front of R_{sigma rho}.
- Palatini term, using metric compatibility, is a covariant divergence and hence a boundary term.
- Remaining integrand gives the Einstein tensor = 0.

### §40.5 Energy-momentum tensor — p.437 (pdf 454)

Adds the matter action back in (Example 40.11) and proposes T^{mu nu} = (2/sqrt(-g)) delta(sqrt(-g) L_m)/delta g_{mu nu} so that stationarity of the full action matches Einstein's equation; alpha = 16 pi then gives G = 8 pi T, and the full action is the integral of sqrt(-g)(R + 16 pi L_m). Expanding gives T^{mu nu} = 2 dL_m/dg_{mu nu} + g^{mu nu} L_m, and a footnote converts this to the more convenient T_{mu nu} = -2 dL_m/dg^{mu nu} + g_{mu nu} L_m. Example 40.12 applies it to the scalar field (T_00 is a positive sum of kinetic, gradient and mass energies) and to the Maxwell Lagrangian. The section closes by noting that the gravitational field itself has no energy-momentum tensor.

**Concepts:** Hilbert energy-momentum tensor, Einstein field equation, gravitational coupling constant in the action, energy-momentum tensor of a scalar field, Maxwell Lagrangian and electromagnetic stress tensor, non-localizability of gravitational energy

**Key moves**
- Vary total action with respect to g_{mu nu}; geometry gives the Einstein tensor term.
- Define T so that the matter part has the same form; set alpha = 16 pi.
- Use Rule 2 to expand sqrt(-g) derivative into the g^{mu nu} L_m term.
- Chain rule plus Rule 1 converts to derivative with respect to g^{mu nu} and all-down indices.
- Scalar: differentiate the single inverse metric; Maxwell: two inverse metrics give a factor 2.
- Check T_00 of scalar is a positive energy density.

### §40.6 Noether's theorem — p.438 (pdf 455)

States Noether's theorem (continuous symmetry implies conserved current) and derives it by treating the change of the Lagrangian as a Lie derivative along a vector xi, including the metric as a variable. Choosing xi to be a Killing field removes the metric term; using the Euler-Lagrange equation converts the rest into a total divergence, giving the Noether current J_N^alpha = (dL/d(d_alpha phi)) Lie_xi phi - xi^alpha L. Translations in Minkowski space (Example 40.13) give four currents that combine into the canonical energy-momentum tensor S^{alpha beta}, conserved but generally non-symmetric and not suited to curved spacetime, unlike the Hilbert T.

**Concepts:** Noether's theorem, continuous symmetry of the action, Lie derivative, Killing vector field, Noether current, canonical energy-momentum tensor, symmetry of the energy-momentum tensor and angular momentum conservation

**Key moves**
- Write the differential of L including a metric-variation term.
- Replace the variation by a Lie derivative along xi; for a scalar Lagrangian it is xi^alpha d_alpha L.
- Killing condition kills the metric term.
- Substitute Euler-Lagrange for dL/dphi and collect a total divergence.
- Translations give S^{alpha beta} with d_alpha S^{alpha beta} = 0.
- Contrast S with Hilbert T: equal for scalars (up to convention), different and non-symmetric in general.

### §40.7 The perfect fluid — p.440 (pdf 457)

Treats a non-viscous, adiabatic fluid as a field theory with a rest-mass density field and a congruence of timelike flow lines whose normalized tangent is the 4-velocity; the rest-mass current is conserved and the Lagrangian density is minus the total energy density, with pressure p = rho_0^2 du/drho_0. Varying the flow lines by a displacement field K (delta w = Lie_K w) while readjusting rho_0 to keep the current conserved, Example 40.14 (Steps 0-V, after Hawking and Ellis) finds delta rho_0, substitutes it into the action variation and integrates by parts to obtain the relativistic Euler equation (rho + p) vdot = -(g + v v) grad p, showing flow lines are not geodesics when transverse pressure gradients exist. Example 40.15 then finds how rho_0 depends on the metric (holding sqrt(-g) J fixed) and applies the Hilbert prescription to get T = (rho + p) v v + p g.

**Concepts:** perfect fluid (field-theoretic description), flow-line congruence and fluid 4-velocity, rest-mass current conservation, specific internal energy and pressure, flow-line variation by a displacement field, relativistic Euler equation, projection tensor orthogonal to 4-velocity, non-geodesic motion of fluid elements, densitized current, perfect fluid energy-momentum tensor

**Key moves**
- Fluid = density field + flow-line congruence; L_m = -rho_0(1 + u).
- Vary flow lines via Lie derivative along K, keep J conserved by adjusting rho_0.
- Step I-III: vary the conservation law, insert delta v, collect terms proportional to the divergence of v to solve for delta rho_0.
- Step IV-V: substitute into delta S, integrate by parts to isolate K_alpha, demand the coefficient vanish.
- Identify rho_0 (1 + d(rho_0 u)/d rho_0) = rho + p and rho_0 d_gamma(d(rho_0 u)/d rho_0) = d_gamma p to get Euler's equation.
- For T: sqrt(-g) J^alpha is metric-independent, so rho_0^2 = -J.J gives d rho_0/d g_{alpha beta}; insert into the Hilbert recipe.

### Chapter summary — p.443 (pdf 460)

Three boxed takeaways: the covariant component form of the Euler-Lagrange equation for matter fields, the Einstein-Hilbert action as the 4-volume integral of the Ricci scalar, and the Hilbert prescription for T^{mu nu} as a metric derivative of the matter Lagrangian plus g^{mu nu} L_m.

**Concepts:** covariant Euler-Lagrange equation, Einstein-Hilbert action, Hilbert energy-momentum tensor

**Key moves**
- Condenses the chapter into equations of motion, gravitational action, and energy-momentum recipe.

## Concepts

### classical field

*definition · core* · also: field as a machine

A function on spacetime assigning to each event a value (scalar, vector, 1-form or higher tensor), such as a matter amplitude or the metric, without quantization.

**How introduced:** Opening image: a device fed a spacetime position that returns the field amplitude there; GR is then classified as a classical field theory so the Lagrangian toolkit should apply.

**Prerequisites:** tensor field

**Where:** GA p.428 (pdf 445); GA §40.1 p.429 (pdf 446)

### matter fields versus the metric field

*definition · developed* · also: two classes of field in GR

GR's dynamical content splits into matter fields (scalar fields, the electromagnetic 1-form, fluid density and flow) whose energy content forms T, and the single geometric (0,2) tensor field g that encodes curvature; a Lagrangian formulation must contain and couple both.

**How introduced:** A reminder placed before the formalism, pairing the two sides of Einstein's equation with the two classes of field.

**Prerequisites:** classical field, metric tensor, energy-momentum tensor

**Where:** GA p.428 (pdf 445); GA p.429 (pdf 446)

### quantum field theory picture of particles

*principle · mention* · also: particles as field excitations

In quantum field theory each particle species is a quantized excitation of a field; the classical fields are promoted to operators with commutation relations that create and annihilate quanta.

**How introduced:** Used to explain why one starts from classical fields even in GR: QFT begins at the same classical stage and then quantizes, which GR does not do.

**Prerequisites:** classical field

**Where:** GA §40.1 p.429 (pdf 446)

### real scalar field

*mathematical-object · core* · also: free scalar field, Klein-Gordon field

A field phi(x) giving one real number per event; free and with mass parameter m its Lagrangian density (signature -+++) is -(1/2) g^{mu nu} d_mu phi d_nu phi - (1/2) m^2 phi^2, and its quanta are massive spin-0 particles.

**How introduced:** Catalogued in Example 40.1, given its Lagrangian in Example 40.3, and used throughout as the test case for Euler-Lagrange, curved spacetime, T and Noether currents.

**Prerequisites:** classical field, Lagrangian density

$$
\mathcal{L}_m=-\tfrac12 g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\phi\,\partial_\nu\phi-\tfrac12 m^2\phi^2
$$

**Notes:** Example 40.1 describes phi(x)^2 as a probability density for finding matter; that reading is not valid for a real relativistic classical field and should not be taught.

**Where:** GA §40.1 p.429 (pdf 446); GA §40.2 p.431 (pdf 448); GA §40.5 p.438 (pdf 455)

### electromagnetic gauge 1-form field

*mathematical-object · mention* · also: 4-potential, gauge field A

The 1-form field A whose exterior derivative F = dA encodes the electric and magnetic fields; quantized, its excitations are photons.

**How introduced:** Listed among matter fields in the opening and Example 40.1, with pointers to ch42 and ch44; its field-strength Lagrangian reappears in Example 40.12.

**Prerequisites:** differential forms, classical field

$$
F=\mathrm{d}A
$$

**Where:** GA p.428 (pdf 445); GA §40.1 p.429 (pdf 446); GA §40.5 p.438 (pdf 455)

### rest-mass density field

*physical-quantity · developed* · also: mass density field rho(x), rho_0

For a fluid, the scalar field rho_0(x) giving rest mass per unit proper volume in the fluid's rest frame, so that the mass in a small 3-volume is rho_0 times that volume.

**How introduced:** Listed in Example 40.1 as the field GR uses most and noted as absent from standard QFT; becomes one of two ingredients of the fluid Lagrangian in 40.7.

**Prerequisites:** classical field

**Where:** GA §40.1 p.429 (pdf 446); GA §40.7 p.440 (pdf 457)

### action functional

*definition · core* · also: action, S[phi]

A functional that assigns a single number (dimensions energy x time) to an entire history of a system: the time integral of the Lagrangian for particles, or the spacetime integral of the Lagrangian density for fields.

**How introduced:** Recalled from particle mechanics with N generalized coordinates, then upgraded by turning the Lagrangian into a spatial integral of a density.

**Prerequisites:** Lagrangian

$$
S=\int \mathrm{d}t\,L=\int \mathrm{d}^4x\,\mathcal{L}
$$

**Where:** GA §40.2 p.430 (pdf 447)

### Lagrangian density

*definition · core* · also: Lagrangian (shortened), script L

A local function of the fields and their first derivatives whose integral over space is the Lagrangian and over spacetime is the action; for simple fields it equals kinetic minus potential energy density.

**How introduced:** Defined through L = integral d^3x script-L, then used as the basic input of field theory; a footnote announces that 'Lagrangian' will mean the density from then on.

**Prerequisites:** action functional

$$
L=\int\mathrm{d}^3x\,\mathcal{L}
$$

$$
S[\phi]=\int\mathrm{d}^4x\,\mathcal{L}_m(\phi,\partial_\mu\phi)
$$

**Where:** GA §40.2 p.430 (pdf 447)

### Lagrangian of a stretched string

*solution-or-model · developed* · also: waves on a string

For a string of linear mass density rho and tension T, transverse displacement psi(x,t) has Lagrangian density (rho/2)(d_t psi)^2 - (T/2)(d_x psi)^2: kinetic energy of motion minus elastic energy stored in the slope.

**How introduced:** Example 40.2 with Fig. 40.1: kinetic and potential energies of a string element written as integrals, then combined into an action whose integrand is the density.

**Prerequisites:** Lagrangian density

$$
\mathcal{L}_m=\frac{\rho}{2}\left(\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}\right)^2-\frac{\mathcal{T}}{2}\left(\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}\right)^2
$$

**Where:** GA §40.2 p.431 (pdf 448); GA §40.2 p.432 (pdf 449)

### interaction Lagrangian

*definition · introduced* · also: L_int

The part of the total Lagrangian density containing products of different fields, which couples them; the total action for several fields is the sum of each free action plus the interaction action.

**How introduced:** Stated right after the scalar-field example as the rule for combining two fields phi and psi.

**Prerequisites:** Lagrangian density

$$
S=S[\phi]+S[\psi]+S_{\rm int}[\phi,\psi]
$$

**Where:** GA §40.2 p.431 (pdf 448)

### principle of stationary action for fields

*principle · core* · also: extremizing the action, Hamilton's principle for fields

The physically realized field configuration makes the first-order change in the action vanish for every small field variation that is zero on the boundary of the region considered.

**How introduced:** Carried over from particle mechanics; a footnote cautions that the simple version assumes the volume element does not change when the field is varied, which is exactly what fails for gravity.

**Prerequisites:** action functional

$$
\delta S=\int\mathrm{d}^4x\,\delta\mathcal{L}=0
$$

**Where:** GA §40.2 p.431 (pdf 448); GA §40.2 p.432 (pdf 449); GA §40.4 p.434 (pdf 451)

### Euler-Lagrange equation for fields

*law · core* · also: field equation of motion from a Lagrangian

The partial differential equation dL/dphi = d_mu [dL/d(d_mu phi)] satisfied by any field that makes the action stationary; the particle version's total time derivative becomes a spacetime divergence.

**How introduced:** Derived in Example 40.4 by varying inside a region D, using the commutation of variation and differentiation, integrating by parts, discarding the surface term and demanding the integrand vanish.

**Prerequisites:** principle of stationary action for fields, boundary term in the variation

$$
\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_m}{\partial\phi}=\partial_\mu\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_m}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}
$$

**Where:** GA §40.2 p.432 (pdf 449); GA §40.6 p.439 (pdf 456)

### boundary term in the variation

*technique · developed* · also: surface term

A total-divergence contribution produced by integration by parts; by the divergence theorem it becomes an integral over the boundary of the region and vanishes when the variation is fixed to zero there.

**How introduced:** Appears in Example 40.4, again in Example 40.10 where the Palatini term reduces to one, and in footnote 21 where a symmetry is allowed to change the action by such a term.

**Prerequisites:** divergence theorem

**Where:** GA §40.2 p.432 (pdf 449); GA §40.4 p.436 (pdf 453); GA §40.6 p.438 (pdf 455)

### wave equation

*law · developed* · also: d'Alembert equation

The equation -d_t^2 psi + nabla^2 psi = 0 (unit wave speed), or d_x^2 psi = v^{-2} d_t^2 psi with v = sqrt(tension/density) on a string, describing non-dispersive propagation.

**How introduced:** Emerges from the string Lagrangian in Example 40.5; a footnote anticipates metric perturbations obeying it (gravitational waves, ch46).

**Prerequisites:** Euler-Lagrange equation for fields

-\partial_t^2\psi+\nabla^2\psi=0

v=\sqrt{\mathcal{T}/\rho}

**Where:** GA §40.2 p.432 (pdf 449)

### d'Alembertian operator

*operation · introduced* · also: box operator, partial squared

The invariant second-order operator d_mu d^mu = g^{mu nu} d_mu d_nu, equal to -d_t^2 + nabla^2 in Minkowski coordinates with signature (-+++).

**How introduced:** Introduced as shorthand when writing the wave and Klein-Gordon equations.

**Prerequisites:** metric tensor

\partial^2=\partial_\mu\partial^\mu=-\partial_t^2+\nabla^2

### Klein-Gordon equation

*law · developed* · also: KG equation

The relativistic field equation of a free scalar with mass parameter m: (-d^2 + m^2) phi = 0 in flat spacetime, and g^{mu nu} phi_{;nu mu} - m^2 phi = 0 in curved spacetime.

**How introduced:** Obtained as the Euler-Lagrange equation of the free scalar Lagrangian in Example 40.6, then re-derived with covariant derivatives in Example 40.7.

**Prerequisites:** real scalar field, Euler-Lagrange equation for fields, covariant derivative

(-\partial^2+m^2)\phi=0

$$
g^{\mu\nu}\phi_{;\nu\mu}-m^2\phi=0

### invariant volume element

*definition · revisited* · also: sqrt(-g) d^4x, covariant measure

The coordinate-independent 4-volume element sqrt(-g) d^4x, g being the determinant of the metric components; it is the volume 4-form evaluated on coordinate displacements and makes the action a scalar.

**How introduced:** Footnote 10 builds it from the volume 4-form; it replaces d^4x in every curved-spacetime action, and its dependence on g is the central complication in varying the metric.

**Prerequisites:** volume form, metric determinant

$$
\mathrm{d}^4x\to\sqrt{-g}\,\mathrm{d}^4x
$$

**Where:** GA §40.3 p.433 (pdf 450); GA §40.4 p.434 (pdf 451)

### minimal coupling

*principle · developed* · also: comma-goes-to-semicolon rule, partial-to-covariant upgrade

The prescription for moving a flat-space field theory into curved spacetime: replace eta by g, partial by covariant derivatives and d^4x by sqrt(-g) d^4x, with no explicit curvature terms added.

**How introduced:** Foreshadowed in footnote 4 (commas become semicolons) and applied in 40.3 by guessing the covariant Euler-Lagrange equation and declaring the guess correct.

**Prerequisites:** covariant derivative, invariant volume element

$$
\phi_{,\mu}\to\phi_{;\mu}
$$

**Notes:** The book does not use the name 'minimal coupling' nor mention that the rule is ambiguous in principle (e.g. a term proportional to R phi^2 could be added).

**Where:** GA §40.2 p.430 (pdf 447); GA §40.3 p.433 (pdf 450)

### covariant Euler-Lagrange equation

*law · core* · also: semicolon Euler-Lagrange equation

Curved-spacetime field equations dL/dphi = (dL/dphi_{;mu})_{;mu} for a scalar and dL/dA^nu = (dL/dA^nu_{;mu})_{;mu} for each component of a vector field.

**How introduced:** Guessed from the flat version by the semicolon upgrade in 40.3, extended to vector components, and boxed again in the chapter summary.

**Prerequisites:** Euler-Lagrange equation for fields, minimal coupling

$$
\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_m}{\partial A^\nu}=\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_m}{\partial A^\nu{}_{;\mu}}\right)_{;\mu}
$$

**Where:** GA §40.3 p.433 (pdf 450); GA p.443 (pdf 460)

### Einstein-Hilbert action

*definition · core* · also: S_EH, Hilbert action

The gravitational action S_EH = integral d^4x sqrt(-g) R, whose Lagrangian density is the Ricci scalar; added to 16 pi times the matter action (G = c = 1) and varied with respect to g_{mu nu}, it yields Einstein's field equation.

**How introduced:** Presented as Hilbert's solution to putting geometry into the Lagrangian formalism; justified only by general covariance and simplicity, with success as the real proof.

**Prerequisites:** Ricci scalar, invariant volume element, action functional

$$
S_{\rm EH}=\int\mathrm{d}^4x\sqrt{-g}\,R
$$

$$
S=\int\mathrm{d}^4x\sqrt{-g}\,[R+16\pi\mathcal{L}_m]
$$

**Notes:** The boundary (Gibbons-Hawking-York) term needed for a well-posed variation is not discussed.

**Where:** GA §40.4 p.434 (pdf 451); GA §40.4 p.436 (pdf 453); GA §40.5 p.437 (pdf 454); GA p.443 (pdf 460)

### variation with respect to the metric

*technique · developed* · also: metric variation

Computing the change in an action under a small change delta g_{mu nu} of the metric components; since g also enters sqrt(-g), every raised index and the connection, it must be carried out explicitly rather than by quoting the matter-field Euler-Lagrange formula.

**How introduced:** Motivated in 40.4 by the observation that changing g changes the spacetime intervals and the volume element itself.

**Prerequisites:** invariant volume element, variation of the inverse metric, variation of the metric determinant

$$
\delta S=\int\mathrm{d}^4x\,\frac{\partial(\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L})}{\partial g_{\mu\nu}}\,\delta g_{\mu\nu}=0
$$

**Notes:** The book insists on varying g_{mu nu} rather than g^{mu nu}; in fact both give equivalent equations because the two variations are related by Rule 1.

**Where:** GA §40.4 p.434 (pdf 451); GA §40.5 p.437 (pdf 454)

### gravitational coupling constant in the action

*convention · introduced* · also: alpha, 16 pi normalization

The relative weight alpha between gravitational and matter actions; with G = c = 1 and T defined by the Hilbert prescription, alpha = 16 pi makes the variational equation read G_{mu nu} = 8 pi T_{mu nu} (in SI the gravitational term carries c^4/(16 pi G)).

**How introduced:** Introduced as an undetermined constant in S = S_EH + alpha S_m, fixed in Example 40.11 by matching to Einstein's equation.

**Prerequisites:** Einstein-Hilbert action, Einstein field equation

$$
\alpha=16\pi
$$

**Where:** GA §40.4 p.434 (pdf 451); GA §40.5 p.437 (pdf 454)

### variation of the inverse metric

*identity · developed* · also: Rule 1

delta g^{sigma rho} = -g^{sigma mu} g^{rho nu} delta g_{mu nu}: because g^{mu alpha} g_{alpha nu} = delta^mu_nu is fixed, the variation of the inverse picks up a minus sign and two inverse metrics.

**How introduced:** Stated as the first of three tricks and proven in Example 40.8 by varying the matrix identity M M^-1 = I.

**Prerequisites:** metric tensor, matrix inverse

$$
\delta g^{\sigma\rho}=-g^{\sigma\mu}\,\delta g_{\mu\nu}\,g^{\nu\rho}
$$

**Where:** GA §40.4 p.435 (pdf 452)

### variation of the metric determinant

*identity · developed* · also: Rule 2, Jacobi's formula

delta sqrt(-g) = (1/2) sqrt(-g) g^{mu nu} delta g_{mu nu}, which follows from delta(det M) = det M Tr(M^{-1} delta M).

**How introduced:** Second trick; proven in Example 40.9 from the log-determinant identity.

**Prerequisites:** log-determinant identity, metric determinant

$$
\delta\sqrt{-g}=\tfrac12\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}\delta g_{\mu\nu}
$$

**Where:** GA §40.4 p.435 (pdf 452)

### log-determinant identity

*identity · introduced* · also: ln det M = Tr ln M

For an invertible matrix, the log of the determinant equals the trace of the matrix logarithm (both are the sum of log-eigenvalues), so delta ln det M = Tr(M^{-1} delta M).

**How introduced:** Quoted with proof deferred to an exercise or to ch34 Exercise 34.3; used in Example 40.9.

**Prerequisites:** matrix inverse

$$
\delta\det M=\det M\,\mathrm{Tr}(M^{-1}\delta M)
$$

**Where:** GA §40.4 p.435 (pdf 452)

### Palatini identity

*identity · developed* · also: Rule 3

delta R_{alpha beta} = nabla_mu(delta Gamma^mu_{alpha beta}) - nabla_beta(delta Gamma^mu_{alpha mu}): the first-order change in the Ricci tensor is built from covariant derivatives of the (tensorial) change in the connection.

**How introduced:** Third trick, quoted without derivation (reference to Zee 2013), then used in Example 40.10 to turn the Ricci-variation term into a boundary integral.

**Prerequisites:** Ricci tensor, connection coefficients, tensor character of connection variations

$$
\delta R_{\alpha\beta}=\nabla_\mu(\delta\Gamma^\mu{}_{\alpha\beta})-\nabla_\beta(\delta\Gamma^\mu{}_{\alpha\mu})
$$

**Where:** GA §40.4 p.435 (pdf 452); GA §40.4 p.436 (pdf 453)

### tensor character of connection variations

*theorem · mention* · also: delta Gamma is a tensor

The Christoffel symbols are not tensor components, but the difference of two connections, and hence an infinitesimal variation delta Gamma, transforms as a (1,2) tensor, so its covariant derivatives are meaningful.

**How introduced:** Credited to Palatini in a biographical footnote.

**Prerequisites:** connection coefficients

**Where:** GA §40.4 p.435 (pdf 452)

### vacuum Einstein equation from the action

*law · developed* · also: stripped-down Einstein equation

Stationarity of the Einstein-Hilbert action alone gives R^{mu nu} - (1/2) g^{mu nu} R = 0, the field equation of spacetime without matter (equivalent to R_{mu nu} = 0 in four dimensions).

**How introduced:** Conclusion of Example 40.10 after the three variation rules are applied.

**Prerequisites:** Einstein-Hilbert action, Palatini identity, Einstein tensor

$$
R^{\mu\nu}-\tfrac12 g^{\mu\nu}R=0
$$

**Notes:** Vacuum does not mean flat: Schwarzschild and gravitational waves solve this equation.

**Where:** GA §40.4 p.436 (pdf 453)

### Einstein tensor

*mathematical-object · revisited* · also: G^{mu nu}

The symmetric, divergence-free tensor G^{mu nu} = R^{mu nu} - (1/2) g^{mu nu} R forming the geometric side of Einstein's equation.

**How introduced:** Here it emerges automatically from the metric variation of sqrt(-g) R, rather than being engineered for conservation as in ch13.

**Prerequisites:** Ricci tensor, Ricci scalar

$$
G^{\mu\nu}=R^{\mu\nu}-\tfrac12 g^{\mu\nu}R
$$

**Where:** GA §40.4 p.436 (pdf 453); GA §40.5 p.437 (pdf 454)

### Einstein field equation

*law · revisited* · also: G = 8 pi T

G_{mu nu} = 8 pi T_{mu nu} in geometrized units: the condition that the total action integral sqrt(-g)(R + 16 pi L_m) be stationary under metric variations.

**How introduced:** Recovered in Example 40.11 by combining the Einstein-Hilbert variation with the matter variation.

**Prerequisites:** Einstein tensor, Hilbert energy-momentum tensor

$$
G_{\mu\nu}=8\pi T_{\mu\nu}

**Where:** GA §40.5 p.437 (pdf 454)

### Hilbert energy-momentum tensor

*definition · core* · also: metric energy-momentum tensor, Hilbert stress tensor

T^{mu nu} = (2/sqrt(-g)) delta(sqrt(-g) L_m)/delta g_{mu nu} = 2 dL_m/dg_{mu nu} + g^{mu nu} L_m, equivalently T_{mu nu} = -2 dL_m/dg^{mu nu} + g_{mu nu} L_m: the response of the matter Lagrangian to a change of the metric, symmetric by construction and automatically covariant.

**How introduced:** Reverse-engineered in Example 40.11 by requiring the matter variation to supply the right-hand side of Einstein's equation; the all-down version follows in footnote 19 by the chain rule and Rule 1.

**Prerequisites:** variation with respect to the metric, variation of the metric determinant, variation of the inverse metric

$$
T^{\mu\nu}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta(\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}_m)}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}}
$$

$$
T_{\mu\nu}=-2\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_m}{\partial g^{\mu\nu}}+g_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{L}_m
$$

**Where:** GA §40.5 p.437 (pdf 454); GA §40.7 p.442 (pdf 459); GA p.443 (pdf 460)

### energy-momentum tensor of a scalar field

*solution-or-model · developed* · also: scalar field T

T_{mu nu} = d_mu phi d_nu phi - g_{mu nu}[(1/2) g^{alpha beta} d_alpha phi d_beta phi + (1/2) m^2 phi^2]; in flat space T_00 = (1/2) phidot^2 + (1/2)(grad phi)^2 + (1/2) m^2 phi^2, a positive energy density.

**How introduced:** First application of the Hilbert prescription (Example 40.12): only one inverse metric appears in the Lagrangian, so the derivative is immediate.

**Prerequisites:** Hilbert energy-momentum tensor, real scalar field

$$
T_{\mu\nu}=\phi_{;\mu}\phi_{;\nu}-g_{\mu\nu}\left(\tfrac12 g^{\alpha\beta}\phi_{;\alpha}\phi_{;\beta}+\tfrac12 m^2\phi^2\right)
$$

**Where:** GA §40.5 p.438 (pdf 455)

### Maxwell Lagrangian and electromagnetic stress tensor

*solution-or-model · introduced* · also: electromagnetic energy-momentum tensor

The electromagnetic Lagrangian is minus one quarter of the Faraday tensor fully contracted with itself through two inverse metrics; the Hilbert recipe turns it into a symmetric, traceless stress tensor made of F contracted with F on one index, minus a quarter of the metric times the invariant F-squared (Heaviside-Lorentz-type normalization).

**How introduced:** Second part of Example 40.12, with a pointer to ch42; the two inverse metrics in the contraction produce the factor that cancels the 1/4.

**Prerequisites:** Hilbert energy-momentum tensor, electromagnetic gauge 1-form field

$$
T_{\alpha\gamma}=F_{\alpha\beta}F_{\gamma\delta}g^{\beta\delta}-\tfrac14 g_{\alpha\gamma}F_{\sigma\rho}F_{\mu\nu}g^{\sigma\mu}g^{\rho\nu}
$$

**Where:** GA §40.5 p.438 (pdf 455)

### non-localizability of gravitational energy

*principle · mention* · also: no energy-momentum tensor for gravity

General relativity supplies no local, covariant energy-momentum tensor for the gravitational field itself; just as there is no gravitational force, gravitational energy cannot be assigned point by point.

**How introduced:** One-paragraph remark closing 40.5 after the matter-field examples.

**Prerequisites:** equivalence principle, Hilbert energy-momentum tensor

**Where:** GA §40.5 p.438 (pdf 455)

### Noether's theorem

*theorem · core* · also: symmetry-conservation theorem

Each continuous symmetry of an action (a transformation leaving it unchanged up to a boundary term) implies a current with vanishing divergence, and hence a conserved quantity.

**How introduced:** Derived in 40.6 by dragging the Lagrangian along a vector field, treating the change as a Lie derivative, choosing a Killing field to remove the metric term and using the Euler-Lagrange equation.

**Prerequisites:** Euler-Lagrange equation for fields, Lie derivative, Killing vector field

$$
\partial_\alpha\left[\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\alpha\phi)}\mathcal{L}_\xi\phi-\xi^\alpha\mathcal{L}\right]=0
$$

**Where:** GA §40.6 p.438 (pdf 455); GA §40.6 p.439 (pdf 456)

### continuous symmetry of the action

*definition · introduced* · also: symmetry

A continuous family of transformations of the fields and coordinates under which the action is unchanged, possibly up to a surface term.

**How introduced:** Defined in 40.6 as the input to Noether's theorem; the footnote allows a surface-term change.

**Prerequisites:** action functional

**Where:** GA §40.6 p.438 (pdf 455)

### Lie derivative

*operation · revisited* · also: Lie drag

The derivative measuring how a tensor field changes when transported along the flow of a vector field; acting on a scalar it is simply the directional derivative along that vector, and acting on a vector it is the commutator of the two fields, which can be written with covariant derivatives because the connection terms cancel.

**How introduced:** Used twice: as the variation of the Lagrangian under a symmetry in 40.6, and as the change in the flow-line tangent under a displacement field in 40.7.

**Prerequisites:** vector field, covariant derivative

$$
\mathcal{L}_\xi f=\xi^\alpha\partial_\alpha f
$$

$$
(\mathcal{L}_K w)^\alpha=w^\alpha{}_{;\beta}K^\beta-K^\alpha{}_{;\beta}w^\beta

**Where:** GA §40.6 p.439 (pdf 456); GA §40.7 p.440 (pdf 457); GA §40.7 p.441 (pdf 458)

### Killing vector field

*mathematical-object · revisited* · also: isometry generator

A vector field along which the metric does not change, Lie_xi g = 0; in Minkowski space the constant translations (and Lorentz generators) are examples.

**How introduced:** Recalled from Part V as the geometric expression of symmetry; choosing xi Killing removes the metric-variation term in the Noether derivation.

\mathcal{L}_\xi\eta_{\alpha\beta}=0

### Noether current

*definition · developed* · also: J_N

For a symmetry generated by a Killing vector xi acting on a scalar field, J_N^alpha = [dL/d(d_alpha phi)] Lie_xi phi - xi^alpha L, whose divergence vanishes on solutions.

**How introduced:** Read off from the bracket in the conservation equation derived in 40.6; specialized to an x-translation in Example 40.13.

### canonical energy-momentum tensor

*definition · developed* · also: Noether energy-momentum tensor, S^{alpha beta}

S^{alpha beta} = [dL/d(d_alpha phi)] d^beta phi - g^{alpha beta} L, the four Noether currents of spacetime translations stacked together, obeying d_alpha S^{alpha beta} = 0; in general it is neither symmetric nor defined covariantly in curved spacetime.

**How introduced:** Assembled after Example 40.13 and contrasted with the Hilbert tensor as unsuitable for Einstein's equation.

**Prerequisites:** Noether current, Killing vector field

### symmetry of the energy-momentum tensor and angular momentum conservation

*principle · introduced* · also: symmetric T

In flat spacetime the angular-momentum flux, formed by multiplying position with the energy-momentum tensor and antisymmetrizing, has zero divergence only when the energy-momentum tensor is symmetric in its two indices; a symmetric source is therefore required.

**How introduced:** Pointed to in footnote 24 and turned into Exercise 40.4.

### perfect fluid (field-theoretic description)

*solution-or-model · core* · also: Lagrangian perfect fluid

A viscosity-free, adiabatic fluid described by a rest-mass density field and a congruence of timelike flow lines, with conserved rest-mass current and Lagrangian density equal to minus its total energy density, L_m = -rho_0(1 + u).

**How introduced:** Opens 40.7 by recasting ch39's fluid as two fields; the Lagrangian is then varied in two ways, by moving flow lines (equation of motion) and by changing the metric (T).

**Prerequisites:** rest-mass density field, flow-line congruence and fluid 4-velocity, rest-mass current conservation

\mathcal{L}_m=-\rho=-\rho_0(1+u)
$$

**Where:** GA §40.7 p.440 (pdf 457)

### flow-line congruence and fluid 4-velocity

*definition · developed* · also: stream lines, velocity field v

A family of non-crossing timelike curves filling spacetime, one per fluid element; the tangent w = d/dtau normalized as v = w/sqrt(-g(w,w)) is the 4-velocity with g(v,v) = -1.

**How introduced:** Introduced as the second ingredient of the fluid field theory.

v=\frac{w}{[-g(w,w)]^{1/2}}

### rest-mass current conservation

*law · developed* · also: continuity equation, J^alpha_{;alpha} = 0

The current J = rho_0 v has zero covariant divergence, equivalently d_alpha(sqrt(-g) J^alpha) = 0: rest mass is neither created nor destroyed along the flow.

**How introduced:** Imposed as a constraint that is preserved while flow lines are varied; its divergence form with sqrt(-g) is used to get the metric dependence of rho_0.

**Prerequisites:** covariant divergence, rest-mass density field

## Worked examples

### Example 40.1 · intro · GA §40.1 p.429 (pdf 446)

**Problem:** Catalogue the kinds of classical field the chapter will use.

**Method:** Describe what each field takes in and returns: a real scalar, a vector or 1-form (the electromagnetic potential), and the perfect-fluid density.

**Key insight:** Fields differ in the type of value they output at each event; the fluid density is the field GR uses most but is not standard in QFT.

**Result:** Scalar field (spin-0 quanta), electromagnetic 1-form (photons), fluid mass-density field.

**Concepts:** real scalar field, electromagnetic gauge 1-form field, rest-mass density field

### Example 40.2 · intro · GA §40.2 p.431 (pdf 448)

**Problem:** Write the action and Lagrangian density for transverse waves on a string of given density and tension.

**Method:** Integrate kinetic energy density of the moving element and potential energy density from stretching (proportional to slope squared) along the string, form T - V and integrate in time.

**Key insight:** The Lagrangian of a continuous system is an integral of a local density depending on the field's time and space derivatives.

**Result:** L_m = (rho/2)(psi_t)^2 - (T/2)(psi_x)^2.

**Concepts:** Lagrangian of a stretched string, Lagrangian density, action functional

### Example 40.3 · intro · GA §40.2 p.431 (pdf 448)

**Problem:** Interpret the free scalar field Lagrangian.

**Method:** Expand the contraction with the Minkowski metric and read each term physically.

**Key insight:** The three terms are the energy of change in time, the cost of spatial variation, and a cost for the field having any nonzero value.

**Result:** L_m = (1/2) phi_t^2 - (1/2)(grad phi)^2 - (1/2) m^2 phi^2.

**Concepts:** real scalar field, Lagrangian density

### Example 40.4 · standard · GA §40.2 p.432 (pdf 449)

**Problem:** Derive the field Euler-Lagrange equation from stationarity of the action in flat spacetime.

**Method:** Vary phi inside a region D with delta phi = 0 on its boundary; commute variation and derivative; integrate by parts; convert the total divergence to a boundary integral that vanishes; require the remaining integrand to vanish for arbitrary delta phi.

**Key insight:** The time derivative of the particle Euler-Lagrange equation becomes a spacetime divergence, and the boundary condition on the variation is what kills the surface term.

**Result:** dL/dphi = (dL/dphi_{,mu})_{,mu}.

**Concepts:** Euler-Lagrange equation for fields, boundary term in the variation, principle of stationary action for fields

### Example 40.5 · intro · GA §40.2 p.432 (pdf 449)

**Problem:** Find the equation of motion of the string.

**Method:** Apply the field Euler-Lagrange equation with derivatives with respect to psi_x and psi_t.

**Key insight:** The wave equation and wave speed follow with essentially no physics input beyond the energies.

**Result:** T psi_xx = rho psi_tt, wave speed v = sqrt(T/rho).

**Concepts:** wave equation, Lagrangian of a stretched string

### Example 40.6 · intro · GA §40.2 p.432 (pdf 449)

**Problem:** Find the equation of motion of the free scalar field in Minkowski space.

**Method:** Differentiate the Lagrangian with respect to phi and d_mu phi, substitute into the Euler-Lagrange equation.

**Key insight:** A relativistic wave equation with a mass term, the Klein-Gordon equation, emerges.

**Result:** (-d^2 + m^2) phi = 0.

**Concepts:** Klein-Gordon equation, d'Alembertian operator

### Example 40.7 · standard · GA §40.3 p.433 (pdf 450)

**Problem:** Derive the scalar field equation in curved spacetime.

**Method:** Write the Lagrangian with covariant derivatives and g^{mu nu}; differentiate with respect to phi_{;mu}; take the covariant divergence using metric compatibility.

**Key insight:** The flat recipe carries over unchanged once commas become semicolons and the metric is kept explicit.

**Result:** g^{mu nu} phi_{;nu mu} - m^2 phi = 0.

**Concepts:** covariant Euler-Lagrange equation, Klein-Gordon equation, minimal coupling

### Example 40.8 · intro · GA §40.4 p.435 (pdf 452)

**Problem:** Prove the rule for varying the inverse metric (Rule 1).

**Method:** Vary the matrix identity M M^-1 = I, solve for delta(M^-1), and identify M with g_{mu nu}.

**Key insight:** Because the product with the inverse is fixed, the inverse must change oppositely: the essential minus sign.

**Result:** delta g^{alpha delta} = -g^{alpha beta} delta g_{beta gamma} g^{gamma delta}.

**Concepts:** variation of the inverse metric

### Example 40.9 · standard · GA §40.4 p.435 (pdf 452)

**Problem:** Prove the rule for varying sqrt(-g) (Rule 2).

**Method:** Differentiate ln det M = Tr ln M to get delta det M = det M Tr(M^-1 delta M), then insert the metric and take the square root.

**Key insight:** The determinant responds to a change in the matrix through the trace of M^-1 delta M.

**Result:** delta sqrt(-g) = (1/2) sqrt(-g) g^{mu nu} delta g_{mu nu}.

**Concepts:** variation of the metric determinant, log-determinant identity

### Example 40.10 · challenging · GA §40.4 p.436 (pdf 453)

**Problem:** Vary the Einstein-Hilbert action with respect to the metric.

**Method:** Write R = g^{sigma rho} R_{sigma rho}; split the variation into delta R, delta g^{sigma rho} and delta sqrt(-g) pieces; treat them in reverse order with Rule 2 and Rule 1; use the Palatini identity and metric compatibility to write the delta R piece as a covariant divergence; discard it as a boundary term.

**Key insight:** Only the variations of the metric in the measure and in the contraction contribute; the curvature variation is a pure boundary term, and the survivors assemble into the Einstein tensor.

**Result:** delta S_EH = -integral sqrt(-g) (R^{mu nu} - (1/2) g^{mu nu} R) delta g_{mu nu}, so G^{mu nu} = 0 in vacuum.

**Concepts:** Einstein-Hilbert action, Palatini identity, Einstein tensor, vacuum Einstein equation from the action

### Example 40.11 · standard · GA §40.5 p.437 (pdf 454)

**Problem:** Obtain the energy-momentum tensor and Einstein's equation from the combined gravity-plus-matter action.

**Method:** Vary S_EH + alpha S_m with respect to g_{mu nu}; use the previous result for the geometric part; choose T^{mu nu} as 2/sqrt(-g) times the metric derivative of sqrt(-g) L_m so both terms have the same form; fix alpha.

**Key insight:** T is whatever quantity the matter Lagrangian contributes when the metric is varied; the normalization 16 pi is set purely by matching to G = 8 pi T.

**Result:** alpha = 16 pi; S = integral sqrt(-g)(R + 16 pi L_m); T^{mu nu} = 2 dL_m/dg_{mu nu} + g^{mu nu} L_m.

**Concepts:** Hilbert energy-momentum tensor, gravitational coupling constant in the action, Einstein field equation

### Example 40.12 · standard · GA §40.5 p.438 (pdf 455)

**Problem:** Compute the energy-momentum tensors of a scalar field and of the electromagnetic field.

**Method:** Apply T_{mu nu} = -2 dL_m/dg^{mu nu} + g_{mu nu} L_m, noting one inverse metric in the scalar Lagrangian and two in the Maxwell Lagrangian; check the scalar T_00 in flat space.

**Key insight:** The recipe works mechanically; the positive T_00 confirms physical sense.

**Result:** Scalar: T = d phi d phi - g[(1/2)(d phi)^2 + (1/2) m^2 phi^2], T_00 = (1/2)phidot^2 + (1/2)(grad phi)^2 + (1/2) m^2 phi^2. EM: T = F F g - (1/4) g F F.

**Concepts:** energy-momentum tensor of a scalar field, Maxwell Lagrangian and electromagnetic stress tensor

### Example 40.13 · standard · GA §40.6 p.439 (pdf 456)

**Problem:** Find the Noether current for translations in x.

**Method:** Take xi = e_x, which is Killing in Minkowski space, so Lie_xi phi = d_x phi, and insert into the Noether current formula.

**Key insight:** Translation invariance yields momentum-type currents; the four translations together form the canonical energy-momentum tensor.

**Result:** J_N^alpha = [dL/d(d_alpha phi)] d_x phi - delta^alpha_x L; combined: S^{alpha beta} with d_alpha S^{alpha beta} = 0.

**Concepts:** Noether current, canonical energy-momentum tensor, Killing vector field

### Example 40.14 · challenging · GA §40.7 p.440 (pdf 457)

**Problem:** Derive the relativistic Euler equation of a perfect fluid from its Lagrangian.

**Method:** Step 0: write delta S in terms of delta rho_0. Step I: vary the conservation law. Step II: express delta v via the Lie derivative of w along the displacement K and the normalization. Step III: substitute, integrate along flow lines, collect terms with the divergence of v to solve for delta rho_0. Step IV: substitute into delta S. Step V: integrate by parts to isolate K_alpha and set its coefficient to zero, then use p = rho_0^2 du/drho_0.

**Key insight:** Treating the flow lines as the varied field (with mass conservation enforced) turns a fluid into a field theory; the Euler equation and non-geodesic motion drop out of stationarity.

**Result:** (rho + p) vdot^alpha = -p_{,beta}(g^{alpha beta} + v^beta v^alpha).

**Concepts:** relativistic Euler equation, flow-line variation by a displacement field, rest-mass current conservation, non-geodesic motion of fluid elements

### Example 40.15 · challenging · GA §40.7 p.442 (pdf 459)

**Problem:** Derive the perfect-fluid energy-momentum tensor from the fluid Lagrangian.

**Method:** Note the metric enters only through rho_0; hold the densitized current sqrt(-g) J fixed (from the determinant form of the divergence); write rho_0^2 as minus J.J and vary it with respect to g_{alpha beta} using Rule 2; insert into the Hilbert recipe and identify rho and p.

**Key insight:** The metric dependence of the density is fixed by particle-number conservation, and pressure appears through du/drho_0.

**Result:** T^{alpha beta} = (rho + p) v^alpha v^beta + p g^{alpha beta}.

**Concepts:** perfect fluid energy-momentum tensor, densitized current, Hilbert energy-momentum tensor

### Example · standard · GA §40.6 p.439 (pdf 456)

**Problem:** Derive Noether's theorem for a scalar field in Minkowski space using a Killing vector.

**Method:** Write the differential of L including a metric term; replace variations by Lie derivatives along xi; the Killing condition removes the metric term; replace dL/dphi with the Euler-Lagrange expression; recognize a total divergence.

**Key insight:** Symmetry is geometric (vanishing Lie derivative of the metric) and conservation follows from on-shell rearrangement into a divergence.

**Result:** d_alpha[(dL/d(d_alpha phi)) Lie_xi phi - xi^alpha L] = 0.

**Concepts:** Noether's theorem, Lie derivative, Killing vector field

### Example · standard · GA §40.5 p.437 (pdf 454)

**Problem:** Convert the Hilbert prescription to the all-down form with derivatives with respect to g^{mu nu} (footnote 19).

**Method:** Chain rule for d/dg_{mu nu} via g^{alpha beta}, insert Rule 1, then lower both indices with the metric and contract Kronecker deltas.

**Key insight:** Raising or lowering what you differentiate with respect to flips the sign of the derivative term.

**Result:** T_{mu nu} = -2 dL_m/dg^{mu nu} + g_{mu nu} L_m.

**Concepts:** Hilbert energy-momentum tensor, variation of the inverse metric


## Analogies and intuitions

### A field is a machine: put in a spacetime position, get out an amplitude. → classical field · useful · GA p.428 (pdf 445)

Opens the chapter and 40.1 so that fields feel like concrete functions rather than abstractions.

**Where it breaks down:** Says nothing about what type of value comes out or how it transforms; the metric field is also such a machine but plays a very different role.

**App idea:** Probe tool: click any event on a spacetime grid and see the value of a scalar field, a vector, or a metric ellipse displayed there.

### Lagrangian field theory as a turn-the-handle machine: feed in matter fields and a Lagrangian, and the equations of motion and T(x) come out. → Hilbert energy-momentum tensor · useful · GA p.428 (pdf 445)

Motivates the chapter against earlier ad-hoc constructions of T.

**Where it breaks down:** The machine still needs a guessed Lagrangian (and the choice of R for gravity is not derived); the output T depends on conventions.

**App idea:** Pipeline widget: drop a Lagrangian card (scalar, Maxwell, fluid) into a hopper and watch symbolic output cards for the field equation and T appear step by step.

### Dictionary from particle mechanics to fields: coordinates q_i become field values phi(x), the discrete index i becomes the continuous position, sums become integrals, and the Lagrangian becomes a spatial integral of a density. → Lagrangian density · strong · GA §40.2 p.430 (pdf 447)

Used to upgrade the particle action and Euler-Lagrange equation to fields with maximal resemblance.

**Where it breaks down:** Fields have spatial derivatives with no particle analogue, which is where the divergence in the field equation comes from.

**App idea:** Beads-to-string morph: a chain of N coupled beads with its discrete Lagrangian continuously refines into a string as N grows, with the sum turning into an integral on screen.

### Terms in the scalar Lagrangian as energy costs: time variation is kinetic energy, spatial variation costs gradient energy, and the mass term is a cost for the field existing at all. → real scalar field · strong · GA §40.2 p.431 (pdf 448)

Read directly off the Minkowski form and later checked by the positive T_00.

**Where it breaks down:** Energy is T_00 (a sum), whereas the Lagrangian is a difference; the 'cost of existing' picture fails for potentials with negative curvature (e.g. inflaton or Higgs-like potentials).

**App idea:** Three sliders scale a wave packet's frequency, wavelength and amplitude; bar gauges show kinetic, gradient and mass contributions to both L and T_00.

### Dragging the Lagrangian along a vector and comparing with the original, as a way to test for symmetry. → Noether's theorem · useful · GA §40.6 p.439 (pdf 456)

The variation under a symmetry is recast as a Lie derivative along xi.

**Where it breaks down:** Only symmetries generated by vector fields (spacetime symmetries) fit this picture; internal symmetries like phase rotations need a different variation.

**App idea:** Flow-field overlay: a Minkowski grid with a translation or boost Killing field; dragging a field configuration along it leaves the action unchanged, and a flux counter shows the Noether current through a box staying balanced.

### Integrating along flow lines 'undoes' covariant derivatives, like integrating a derivative along a curve. → flow-line variation by a displacement field · weak · GA §40.7 p.441 (pdf 458)

Used in Step III of Example 40.14 to justify moving semicolons by integration by parts.

**Where it breaks down:** Only derivatives along the flow direction are directional derivatives along the lines; the contracted indices in the fluid derivation are not all along the flow, so the picture is heuristic.

### Varying the metric changes the ruler with which the action is measured. → variation with respect to the metric · useful · GA §40.4 p.434 (pdf 451)

Explains why metric variation needs special care: it alters intervals and the volume element sqrt(-g) d^4x.

**Where it breaks down:** Metaphor does not by itself tell you the rules; the three explicit variation identities do the work.

**App idea:** Deformable grid: stretch a patch of a 2D metric and watch the area element and the integral of a fixed field over the patch change, contrasting with varying the field on a fixed grid.


## Misconceptions addressed

### Gravity can be handled like any matter field: write a Lagrangian and plug it into the standard Euler-Lagrange formula. · GA §40.4 p.434 (pdf 451)

**Correction:** The varied field is the metric, which also sits in the volume element and in every index contraction, so the variation must be done explicitly with the rules for delta g^{mu nu}, delta sqrt(-g) and delta R_{mu nu}.

**Why tempting:** The matter-field recipe worked so smoothly for strings and scalars.

### Raising the indices of a variation is as harmless as raising indices of a tensor: delta g^{mu nu} = g^{mu alpha} g^{nu beta} delta g_{alpha beta}. · GA §40.4 p.435 (pdf 452)

**Correction:** There is an extra minus sign, delta g^{mu nu} = -g^{mu alpha} g^{nu beta} delta g_{alpha beta}, because the inverse must compensate a change in the matrix.

**Why tempting:** Index gymnastics usually carries no signs.

### The component g_{mu nu} is simply 1/g^{mu nu}. · GA §40.3 p.433 (pdf 450)

**Correction:** Only for a diagonal metric; in general g^{mu nu} is the matrix inverse.

**Why tempting:** Most metrics met so far (Schwarzschild, FRW) are diagonal.

### The gravitational field carries a local energy-momentum tensor just like the electromagnetic field. · GA §40.5 p.438 (pdf 455)

**Correction:** GR gives no covariant local energy density for gravity; the Hilbert recipe applies to matter fields only.

**Why tempting:** Gravitational waves transport energy, and every other field has a T.

### The Noether energy-momentum tensor from translation symmetry is the tensor that sources Einstein's equation. · GA §40.6 p.439 (pdf 456)

**Correction:** The canonical tensor is generally non-symmetric and not generally covariant; the symmetric Hilbert tensor from metric variation is the correct source (they coincide, up to sign convention, for a scalar field).

**Why tempting:** Both are conserved and both are called energy-momentum tensors.

### Every element of a fluid falls freely along a geodesic. · GA §40.7 p.442 (pdf 459)

**Correction:** Pressure gradients orthogonal to the flow accelerate fluid elements; only pressureless dust moves geodesically.

**Why tempting:** The equivalence principle is often summarized as 'everything follows geodesics'.

### Varying the action with respect to g^{mu nu} instead of g_{mu nu} would give a different or wrong field equation. · GA §40.4 p.434 (pdf 451)

**Correction:** The two variations are related by Rule 1, so the resulting equations are equivalent (one is the other with indices lowered); the choice is a matter of convenience, and the book itself uses g^{mu nu} derivatives for T_{mu nu}.

**Why tempting:** The book states it is important to vary g_{mu nu} because the metric is naturally a (0,2) tensor.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### The vacuum equation G = 0 means no gravity. · GA §40.4 p.436 (pdf 453)

**Correction:** Vacuum means no matter source; curvature can still be present, as in Schwarzschild spacetime or gravitational waves.

**Why tempting:** The book calls it a 'stripped-down' Einstein equation, suggesting something trivial.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### A real classical scalar field phi describes the probability of finding a particle, with phi^2 as a probability density. · GA §40.1 p.429 (pdf 446)

**Correction:** A real relativistic field has no positive-definite conserved probability density; its physical content is its energy-momentum. Probability interpretations belong to quantum states, not classical field amplitudes.

**Why tempting:** Non-relativistic wavefunction language is familiar, and Example 40.1 phrases the scalar field this way.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### The action is kinetic energy minus potential energy. · GA §40.2 p.430 (pdf 447)

**Correction:** The Lagrangian is kinetic minus potential energy; the action is its integral over time (or the density's integral over spacetime).

**Why tempting:** The book itself makes this slip in a sentence in 40.2.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*


## Thought experiments

### Wiggling a field inside a box with clamped walls · GA §40.2 p.432 (pdf 449)

**Setup:** Change the field by a small amount only inside a finite spacetime region, keeping it fixed on the region's boundary, and ask how the action changes.

**Lesson:** Requiring no first-order change for every such wiggle is equivalent to the local Euler-Lagrange equation; the clamped boundary is what removes the surface term.

**App idea:** Heat-map of a 1+1D field with a draggable rectangular region; learner paints a bump variation inside it and a gauge shows delta S, which is zero to first order only for the true solution.

### Varying the metric instead of the field · GA §40.4 p.434 (pdf 451)

**Setup:** Keep all matter fields fixed but deform the metric slightly inside a region, so distances, volumes and index contractions change.

**Lesson:** The response of geometry gives the Einstein tensor and the response of matter defines T; balancing them is Einstein's equation.

**App idea:** 2D grid with a metric-ellipse at each point; learner stretches the metric locally and two meters show the change in the integral of R and the change in the matter action.

### Sliding a field configuration along a symmetry · GA §40.6 p.439 (pdf 456)

**Setup:** Translate a solution of the scalar field equation along a Killing direction of Minkowski space and compare the Lagrangian before and after.

**Lesson:** Because the metric is unchanged by the slide, the action is unchanged, and on solutions this forces a conserved current (momentum or energy).

**App idea:** Wave packet in a box with a translation handle; a flux meter at the box walls shows momentum entering equals momentum change inside.

### Nudging the flow lines of a fluid · GA §40.7 p.440 (pdf 457)

**Setup:** Displace every point of every flow line of a perfect fluid by a vector field K while re-adjusting the density so no mass is created or destroyed.

**Lesson:** Stationarity of the fluid action under such nudges produces the relativistic Euler equation, and the density must change by the divergence-like expression in eqn 40.89.

**App idea:** Streamline plot of a 2D flow with a brush that applies a displacement field K; streamlines deform, density shading updates to keep flux through a moving box constant, and an action meter responds.

## Historical notes

- **David Hilbert, Albert Einstein (1915):** Hilbert found a variational (action-based) derivation of the gravitational field equations, now the Einstein-Hilbert action, just after Einstein reached the field equations by a less systematic route. — Shows two complementary routes to GR: physical reasoning (Einstein) and an action principle (Hilbert), the latter being the template for modern field theory. (GA p.428 (pdf 445))
- **David Hilbert (1900):** Hilbert presented a list of mathematical problems that shaped twentieth-century research. — Biographical context for Hilbert's influence. (GA p.428 (pdf 445))
- **Albert Einstein, Hans Ohanian, Remo Ruffini (1907-1915):** Einstein developed GR over eight years; Ohanian and Ruffini remark that the theory arrived about two decades before the relativistic gauge-field methods that make it natural had matured. — Frames this chapter's field-theory methods as the natural modern language of GR; points to Pais and Cheng for the history. (GA p.428 (pdf 445))
- **Joseph-Louis Lagrange:** Epigraph from Mecanique Analytique, in which Lagrange boasts that his mechanics needs no figures, only uniform algebraic procedure. — Sets the tone of the chapter: a mechanical, algebraic recipe rather than geometric pictures (ironic in a book that elsewhere relies on diagrams). (GA p.428 (pdf 445))
- **Attilio Palatini:** Showed that variations of the connection coefficients are components of a tensor, leading to the identity for the variation of the Ricci tensor that bears his name. — Makes the Einstein-Hilbert variation tractable by reducing the curvature variation to a boundary term. (GA §40.4 p.435 (pdf 452))
- **Emmy Noether:** Noether's theorem linking continuous symmetries to conservation laws (named in 40.6 with dates 1882-1935). — Underlies energy, momentum and angular momentum conservation and the canonical energy-momentum tensor. (GA §40.6 p.438 (pdf 455))
- **Stephen Hawking, George Ellis (1973):** Their variational derivation of perfect-fluid dynamics is the model for Example 40.14, with steps they omitted filled in. — Credits the source of the chapter's most technical derivation. (GA §40.7 p.440 (pdf 457))

## Notation and conventions

- **Metric signature:** (-,+,+,+): the free scalar Lagrangian -(1/2)(d phi)^2 gives +(1/2)phidot^2 - (1/2)(grad phi)^2, and fluid velocity satisfies g(v,v) = -1. — Particle physics texts with (+,-,-,-) flip the sign of the kinetic term and of the Hilbert T formula. (GA §40.2 p.431 (pdf 448))
- **Units:** Geometrized units G = c = 1, so the coupling in the action is 16 pi and Einstein's equation reads G = 8 pi T. (GA §40.5 p.437 (pdf 454))
- **Comma and semicolon derivatives:** A comma before an index marks a partial derivative and a semicolon a covariant derivative; for vector components the connection term is added for an upper index and subtracted for a lower index. — The chapter's slogan: commas become semicolons in curved spacetime. (GA §40.2 p.430 (pdf 447))
- **Order of indices on second covariant derivatives:** Footnote 13 writes phi_{;mu nu} = (d_nu phi)_{;mu}, i.e. the last index is differentiated first, the opposite of the common convention (phi_{;mu})_{;nu}. — Irrelevant for scalars on a torsion-free connection, but a trap for vectors. (GA §40.3 p.433 (pdf 450))
- **Lagrangian symbols:** script-L_m matter density, script-L_EH geometric density (= R), script-L_int interaction; 'Lagrangian' means density from 40.2 onward. (GA §40.2 p.430 (pdf 447))
- **Lie derivative symbol:** A script L with subscript vector (printed like a pound sign), e.g. Lie_xi phi, Lie_K w. — Visually close to the Lagrangian density symbol; watch context in eqns 40.68-40.72. (GA §40.6 p.439 (pdf 456))
- **S for two objects:** S denotes both the action and the canonical energy-momentum tensor S^{alpha beta}. — Distinguish by indices. (GA §40.6 p.439 (pdf 456))
- **Variation versus partial derivative:** delta/delta g_{mu nu} and partial d/dg_{mu nu} are used interchangeably for the metric derivative of the Lagrangian density; no functional-derivative subtleties are tracked. — Valid when L_m depends on g but not on its derivatives, as for all examples here. (GA §40.5 p.437 (pdf 454))
- **Metric determinant:** g = det g_{mu nu}, negative in Lorentzian signature; the volume element is sqrt(-g) d^4x. (GA §40.3 p.433 (pdf 450))
- **Ricci contraction:** R_{nu beta} = R^mu_{nu mu beta}, contracting the first and third Riemann indices; R = g^{alpha beta} R_{alpha beta}. — Consistent with ch11; other books contract differently and change signs. (GA §40.4 p.436 (pdf 453))
- **Rules 1-3:** Book-specific labels: Rule 1 inverse-metric variation (40.37), Rule 2 determinant variation (40.41), Rule 3 Palatini identity (40.43). — Example 40.10 cites the equation numbers of Rules 1 and 2 the wrong way round. (GA §40.4 p.435 (pdf 452))
- **Matrix notation:** Underlined M denotes the matrix of metric components, with M_{mu nu} = g_{mu nu} and (M^-1) entries g^{mu nu}. (GA §40.4 p.435 (pdf 452))
- **Fluid symbols:** rho_0 rest-mass density, u specific internal energy, rho = rho_0(1+u) total energy density, p pressure, w flow-line tangent d/dtau, v normalized 4-velocity, vdot^alpha = v^alpha_{;beta} v^beta acceleration, K displacement field, J = rho_0 v current. (GA §40.7 p.440 (pdf 457))
- **Electromagnetic Lagrangian normalization:** L = -(1/4) F F with no 4 pi, i.e. Heaviside-Lorentz-type units. — Gaussian-unit texts carry an extra 1/(4 pi) (or 1/(16 pi)). (GA §40.5 p.438 (pdf 455))
- **Overloaded letter T:** T denotes the string's kinetic energy in Example 40.2, a calligraphic T is the string tension, and bold T (components T^{mu nu}) is the energy-momentum tensor. — Keep the three apart when moving from the string example to the Hilbert tensor; the tension carries a script font in eqns 40.7 and 40.21. (GA §40.2 p.431 (pdf 448))

## Gaps and pitfalls

- **In Example 40.10 the text says delta I_3 uses 'Rule 2 from eqn 40.37' and delta I_2 uses 'Rule 1 (eqn 40.41)'; the equation numbers are swapped (Rule 1 is 40.37, Rule 2 is 40.41).** — A reader checking references finds the wrong identity and may doubt the derivation. *Suggestion:* Present the rules with their correct numbers and tag each derivation line with the rule used.
- **Eqn 40.49 is labelled delta I_2 but is the Palatini (Ricci-variation) term delta I_1.** — Confuses which term is being discarded as a boundary term. *Suggestion:* Relabel as delta I_1 and state explicitly that delta I_1 vanishes.
- **Sign inconsistency: eqn 40.50 gives the geometric variation as minus the Einstein tensor, eqn 40.53 quotes it with a plus sign, and eqn 40.55 then carries a compensating minus in front of the T term; the final alpha = 16 pi and G = 8 pi T are correct.** — Careful readers cannot reproduce 40.55 from 40.53 and 40.54; the sign of T appears arbitrary. *Suggestion:* Write delta S = integral sqrt(-g) [-G^{mu nu} + (alpha/2) T^{mu nu}] delta g_{mu nu} explicitly, then set alpha = 16 pi.
- **In Example 40.15 two sign slips cancel: eqn 40.93 should read dL/dg = -(1 + u + rho_0 u') d rho_0/dg, and eqns 40.96-40.98 should give d rho_0/d g_{alpha beta} = -(1/2) rho_0 (v v + g). The final T is correct.** — A learner redoing the derivation gets T with the wrong sign for one term and cannot find the error. *Suggestion:* Use the physical check that enlarging spatial metric components at fixed particle number lowers the density, fixing the minus sign, and keep the chain-rule sign in 40.93.
- **The canonical tensor S^{alpha beta} in eqn 40.75, with the chapter's scalar Lagrangian and (-+++) signature, equals -T^{alpha beta}; the text calls them 'the same' for scalar fields.** — Learners computing S^{00} find a negative energy density. *Suggestion:* State the sign explicitly or define S with the opposite overall sign so that S = T for the scalar field.
- **The Noether derivation omits two needed facts: the Lie derivative commutes with the gradient, Lie_xi(d_mu phi) = d_mu(Lie_xi phi), and a Killing field is divergence-free so xi^alpha d_alpha L = d_alpha(xi^alpha L).** — The jump from eqn 40.69 to 40.71 looks like magic. *Suggestion:* Insert both lines; for translations (constant xi) both are trivial, which is a good first case.
- **Step III of Example 40.14 solves for delta rho_0 by collecting terms multiplying the divergence of v and demanding they vanish, justified by integrating 'along flow lines'; footnote eqn 40.87 misplaces indices (it should produce v^alpha_{;beta} K^beta_{;alpha}) and the needed swap of covariant-derivative order ignores curvature terms.** — The argument is not a valid derivation as written; learners may copy the method. *Suggestion:* Derive delta rho_0 from the Lie-dragged conserved current directly (delta(sqrt(-g) J) = Lie derivative of the densitized current) or quote the result, noting it is standard in Hawking and Ellis.
- **Section 40.2 says the action is the difference between kinetic and potential energy; that is the Lagrangian.** — Blurs the distinction between Lagrangian and action at the moment it is being built. *Suggestion:* Correct in teaching: L = T - V, S = integral L dt.
- **Example 40.1 describes the real scalar field as a probability amplitude with phi^2 as probability density, and says classical field amplitudes typically describe mass-energy density.** — Mixes nonrelativistic quantum wavefunction ideas with classical relativistic fields; a real Klein-Gordon field has no positive conserved probability density. *Suggestion:* Describe the classical scalar field by its energy-momentum tensor, and keep probability interpretations for quantum states.
- **The claim that one must vary with respect to g_{mu nu} rather than g^{mu nu} is presented as important; the two are equivalent via Rule 1, and the chapter itself later differentiates with respect to g^{mu nu}.** — Learners may think standard texts that vary g^{mu nu} are wrong. *Suggestion:* Present both as equivalent; choose whichever makes the Lagrangian's metric dependence simplest.
- **The Einstein-Hilbert action is given without justification beyond covariance, and the boundary term is treated as vanishing although delta Gamma involves derivatives of delta g that need not vanish on the boundary (the Gibbons-Hawking-York term is not mentioned). The cosmological constant as the other allowed term is also not mentioned.** — Leaves learners unable to answer 'why R?' or 'why not R + constant?'. *Suggestion:* Explain that R is the only scalar built from the metric with at most second derivatives (Lovelock) besides a constant, which gives Lambda; mention the boundary term for advanced learners.
- **Minor printed typos: eqn 40.24 writes the last Laplacian term as d^2/dx^2 instead of d^2/dz^2 and omits phi in d_mu d^mu; eqn 40.85's middle line has K^alpha_{;beta} w^alpha for K^alpha_{;beta} w^beta; T_00 in 40.63 uses mu as a dummy index twice; 'energy-moment tensor' on p.438.** — Small but distracting for careful readers. *Suggestion:* Silently correct in app content.
- **In the reading copy the tangent to flow lines appears as d/dr and eqn 40.25's surrounding text shows a comma where the book has a semicolon; the printed page has w = d/dtau and phi_{;mu}.** — OCR-based study copies can mislead. *Suggestion:* Use w = d/dtau and phi_{;mu}.
- **The statement that sqrt(-g) J^alpha is unchanged under metric variation is presented as following from the divergence formula; it is actually an additional assumption (particle number flux held fixed).** — Learners may think it is derivable and search for a proof. *Suggestion:* Present it as the physical constraint that the number of particles crossing each coordinate cell is fixed when the metric is varied.
- **The chapter never derives Maxwell's equations from the Lagrangian it quotes, nor explains why the Lagrangian is written in terms of F rather than A.** — The EM example feels like an unexplained input. *Suggestion:* Point forward to ch42 and show the vector Euler-Lagrange equation applied to A gives the inhomogeneous Maxwell equations.

## Tutor notes

- Opening question: 'Earlier we guessed the energy-momentum tensor for dust and fluids. Could there be a machine that just tells us T?' Then introduce the action as that machine.
- Before any fields, check the learner can derive the Euler-Lagrange equation for one particle; if not, spend time there (GA ch02) since every later step mirrors it.
- Teach the string first with Fig. 40.1 as a concrete anchor, then the scalar field, then curved spacetime, then gravity; do not start with the Einstein-Hilbert action.
- Check for understanding: ask why the boundary term vanishes and what would go wrong without the fixed-boundary condition.
- When reaching gravity, ask what is special about varying the metric (it changes the ruler and the volume element) before revealing the three rules.
- Have the learner verify Rule 1 on a 2x2 diagonal metric by hand; the minus sign becomes obvious when one entry is inverted.
