# GA ch50 · The Big-Bang singularity

> Sketches, in Geroch's style, a cosmological singularity theorem: a Cauchy slice guarantees maximal timelike curves, maximal curves must be normal geodesics free of focal points, gravity plus an energy condition forces the normal congruence to focus within proper time 3/c0, so no event can lie farther than 3/c0 from the slice; run backwards in an expanding Friedmann universe this makes every past-directed timelike curve run out of spacetime, which is the Big Bang.

**Pages:** printed 547–553 · pdf 564–570 · **Difficulty:** math 4/5, conceptual 4/5, novice-friendliness 2/5

Only seven pages, but it compresses a research-level theorem. The local calculation (Examples 50.1-50.2) needs confident index gymnastics with the Ricci identity and projection tensors; the global ingredients (Cauchy surfaces, domains of dependence, compactness, focal points) are asserted rather than developed, and the logic runs by contradiction. Several small printed slips (weak versus strong energy condition, 'left' for 'right', a sign in note 5, a garbled summary bullet) can confuse careful readers.

## Role in the book

The final chapter of the book and a deliberate bookend: Part III (Chapters 15-19) found a = 0 in explicit Friedmann solutions but left open whether that was an artefact of perfect homogeneity. Here the tools of Part V (covariant derivative commutators and the Ricci tensor from Chapter 35, projection tensors from Chapter 39) and the energy conditions of Chapter 13 are combined into a global, symmetry-light argument that a past singularity is generic. It closes the book by returning to the beginning of the Universe, previews the global (causal-structure) methods of Penrose, Hawking and Geroch that the book otherwise omits, and uses the exercises to introduce the Raychaudhuri equation, the standard engine of singularity theorems.

## Learning objectives

- Reader can state the question the chapter answers: whether the initial singularity of Friedmann models survives without exact homogeneity and isotropy.
- Reader can explain, with a Euclidean sketch, why the shortest path from a point to a surface must hit the surface at a right angle and why a normal geodesic that has crossed a neighbour is no longer shortest.
- Reader can translate those Euclidean facts into Lorentzian ones, replacing shortest by longest proper time, and say why the sign of the metric causes the swap.
- Reader can build the unit normal geodesic congruence of a spacelike hypersurface from a proper-time function phi and prove from u_mu = grad phi that the congruence is geodesic and has symmetric u_{mu;nu}.
- Reader can define the convergence c = -u^mu_{;mu} and interpret its sign as volume shrinking or growing along the flow.
- Reader can reproduce the derivation of dc/dtau = u^{nu;alpha}u_{alpha;nu} + R_{mu nu}u^mu u^nu using the Ricci identity and the geodesic property.
- Reader can split u_{alpha;nu} into trace and trace-free parts with the projection tensor and prove u^{nu;alpha}u_{alpha;nu} >= c^2/3.
- Reader can identify the energy condition actually needed for R_{mu nu}u^mu u^nu >= 0 (the strong energy condition, rho + 3p >= 0 for a perfect fluid) and note that the text labels it the weak condition.
- Reader can integrate dc/dtau >= c^2/3 to show convergence must diverge by tau = 3/c0 and explain why this means a caustic forms.
- Reader can assemble the proof by contradiction: maximal curves exist (Cauchy surface) yet cannot exist beyond 3/c0 (focusing), so spacetime cannot extend that far.
- Reader can compute c = -3 a-dot/a for comoving dust, explain why the argument fails to the future but applies to the past, and evaluate the past bound 3/c0 = 1/H0.
- Reader can explain what 'singularity' means in this argument (timelike curves that cannot be continued within a smooth manifold) and how that differs from a statement about infinite density.
- Reader can derive the Raychaudhuri equation with expansion, shear and rotation (exercises) and show focusing follows when rotation vanishes and the strong energy condition holds.

## Teaching approach

A compressed proof sketch of a singularity theorem, told in the book's chatty register. The authors avoid the full causal-structure apparatus by stating two 'geometric facts' in ordinary Euclidean 3-space, flipping them to Lorentzian form with a single word change (longest for shortest), and then doing the one genuinely local calculation (how the convergence of the normal congruence evolves) inside two boxed Examples. An inequality from a trace/trace-free split plus an energy condition gives a Riccati-type differential inequality whose finite-time blow-up delivers a contradiction. The payoff is staged as disappointment and rescue: the result seems useless for an expanding universe until time is run backwards. The book ends on a literary flourish, with the Universe (and the text) starting with a bang, while the standard Raychaudhuri machinery is left to the exercises.

**Style:** geometric-first, derivation-heavy, spiral-revisit, conversational-informal

**Narrative arc**

1. Motivating question: is the Big Bang of Robertson-Walker/Friedmann models a symmetry artefact or a robust prediction?
2. Assumption laid down: take a constant-cosmic-time slice that is a Cauchy surface; compactness of causal curves then guarantees a longest timelike curve from the slice to any event in its domain of dependence.
3. Warm-up in Euclidean space: the shortest route from a point to a surface meets it orthogonally; a normal geodesic that has crossed another cannot be shortest because the corner can be cut (the crossing is a conjugate point, recalling Chapter 8).
4. Transfer to spacetime: straightest timelike lines are longest, so the same facts hold with longest in place of shortest.
5. Model: a proper-time-from-S function phi, its gradient u, proof that u is a unit geodesic field, and definition of the convergence c.
6. Formalism, Example 50.1: differentiate c along u, trade derivative order via the Ricci identity, integrate by parts, and drop the geodesic term to get a square term plus a Ricci term.
7. Formalism, Example 50.2: project out the trace with P and show the square term is at least c^2/3.
8. Physics input: the trace-reversed Einstein equation plus an energy condition make the Ricci term non-negative, giving dc/dtau >= c^2/3 (gravity focuses).
9. Check/consequence: integrate to show c diverges by tau = 3/c0; interpret as a caustic where normal geodesics cross.
10. Logical clincher: existence of longest curves versus their impossibility past 3/c0 forces the conclusion that no event lies farther than 3/c0 from S.
11. Application: for Friedmann dust c = -3 a-dot/a is negative, so the theorem fails to the future; reversing time makes it apply to the past.
12. Interpretation: past-directed timelike curves must end within 3/c0 because they hit a point excised from the smooth manifold; this is the Big-Bang singularity, and the book closes.
13. Exercises extend to the full Raychaudhuri equation with shear and rotation and the strong energy condition.

**Signature moves**

- Proxy geometry: argue the key extremal facts with pictures in flat Euclidean space, then reverse the inequality for Lorentzian signature instead of proving them in spacetime.
- Gradient trick: define the congruence as the gradient of a proper-time function so that symmetry of u_{mu;nu}, the geodesic property and zero rotation all come for free, which lets the text skip shear-free/rotation bookkeeping that the exercises then restore.
- Heavy algebra quarantined in boxed Examples followed by a 'pause and interpret' paragraph that reads each term physically (volume shrinking by Ricci, a positive square).
- Identities needed for the decomposition are listed in a footnote as numbered 'facts' and pushed into Exercise 50.1, turning text gaps into guided homework.
- Contradiction as the engine: two statements that cannot both hold (maximal curves exist; maximal curves cannot exist beyond 3/c0) are reconciled only by cutting spacetime short.
- Disappointment-then-rescue staging in §50.3: the result looks inapplicable to an expanding universe until time is reversed.
- Literary bookends: a Lucretius epigraph about the heavens having a birth and a closing line that inverts a famous poetic ending, marking the end of the book.

## Section by section

### Chapter opening: is the Big Bang an artefact? — p.547 (pdf 564)

Frames the chapter's question (is the Friedmann initial singularity physical or an idealization) and announces the answer: under mild assumptions a past singularity is realistic and perhaps unavoidable. Introduces a constant-cosmic-time slice assumed to be a Cauchy surface, together with its domain of dependence, and asserts that a longest timelike curve then exists from the slice to any event in that domain. Margin notes credit Geroch's lecture notes for the form of the argument, point to Penrose, Wald and Hawking-Ellis, and justify the existence claim by compactness of the space of causal curves.

**Concepts:** Big-Bang singularity, Cauchy surface, domain of dependence, existence of a maximal timelike curve, compactness of the space of causal curves, global methods in general relativity

**Key moves**
- Pose the artefact-versus-reality question about the Friedmann singularity.
- Assume the present slice is a Cauchy surface.
- Invoke compactness to guarantee a proper-time-maximizing curve from the slice to any event in its domain of dependence.

### §50.1 Facts about Euclidean geometry — p.547 (pdf 564)

Uses ordinary 3-space as a rehearsal. Among all geodesics from a point to a surface S, the shortest must meet S at a right angle, since a slanted one can be beaten by a perpendicular one (Fig. 50.1); these are called orthogonal or normal geodesics. Next, if two normal geodesics cross at a point r, then the one continuing past r to a point q cannot be the shortest route from q to S: follow it to near r, cut across to the other geodesic and ride that down (Fig. 50.2). The crossing is identified with the conjugate points of Chapter 8, beyond which a trajectory stops being a minimum.

**Concepts:** orthogonality of the shortest path to a surface, normal geodesics, conjugate point, corner-cutting argument

**Key moves**
- Shortest path to a surface is a normal geodesic (first-variation fact).
- A normal geodesic continued past a crossing with another normal geodesic is not minimizing, by rounding the corner.
- Tie the crossing point to the conjugate-point criterion of Chapter 8.

### §50.2 Orthogonal geodesics in spacetime — p.548 (pdf 565)

Moves to spacetime, where timelike geodesics maximize proper time, so the Euclidean facts carry over with longest replacing shortest. A proper-time-from-S function phi is introduced, and its gradient defines a unit timelike field u whose covariant derivative is symmetric; this makes u automatically geodesic, the tangent to all normal geodesics. The convergence c = -div u is defined. Example 50.1 differentiates c along u, uses the Ricci identity and the geodesic property to get a square of u_{alpha;nu} plus a Ricci term; Example 50.2 uses the projection tensor to show the square is at least c^2/3. The trace-reversed Einstein equation and an energy condition make the Ricci term non-negative, giving dc/dtau >= c^2/3, whose integral blows up by tau = 3/c0: a caustic forms and normal geodesics must cross. Combined with the two geometric facts, this contradicts the existence of maximal curves unless no event lies farther than 3/c0 from S along a timelike curve.

**Concepts:** maximal proper time of timelike geodesics, proper-time function from a hypersurface, hypersurface-orthogonal geodesic congruence, symmetry of the second covariant derivative of a scalar, convergence of a geodesic congruence, Ricci identity, Ricci tensor, projection tensor orthogonal to the 4-velocity, trace and trace-free decomposition of the velocity gradient, trace inequality for the velocity gradient, trace-reversed Einstein equation, weak energy condition, strong energy condition, focusing inequality, focusing time bound, caustic, existence of a maximal timelike curve

**Key moves**
- Swap shortest for longest because of the Lorentzian sign of the metric.
- Set u_mu = grad_mu phi with u.u = -1; symmetry of u_{nu;mu} gives u^mu u_{nu;mu} = (1/2)(u.u)_{;nu} = 0, so u is geodesic.
- Define c = -u^mu_{;mu}.
- Example 50.1: Ricci identity contracted on alpha and mu converts u^alpha_{;alpha nu} into u^alpha_{;nu alpha} minus a Ricci term; integrate by parts; the geodesic term vanishes.
- Example 50.2: write u_{alpha;nu} = -(c/3)P + trace-free part; cross terms vanish because the trace-free part is orthogonal to P; drop the non-negative trace-free square.
- Use R_{mu nu}u^mu u^nu = 8 pi (T - (1/2) g T)u u >= 0 to reach dc/dtau >= c^2/3.
- Integrate: c(tau) >= 3/(3/c0 - tau), diverging by tau = 3/c0.
- Divergent convergence means neighbouring normal geodesics cross (caustic), so they stop being maximal.
- Contradiction with the Cauchy-surface existence result: events more than 3/c0 from S cannot exist.

### §50.3 Our Universe — p.551 (pdf 568)

Applies the result to a near-Friedmann perfect-fluid universe. A comoving volume grows as a^3, so the convergence of the comoving dust world lines is c = -3 a-dot/a, negative for an expanding universe; the theorem therefore says nothing about the future. Reversing the time direction flips the sign, so with S the present slice no past-directed timelike curve can be longer than 3/c0. Because the theory lives on a smooth manifold, the place such curves would reach is not part of the manifold: it is a point that has to be excised (Fig. 50.4). That missing boundary is the initial Big-Bang singularity, which the authors present as inevitable for universes like ours, closing the book.

**Concepts:** Friedmann-Robertson-Walker model, comoving volume element, convergence of comoving Friedmann world lines, time reversal of the focusing argument, smooth manifold assumption, timelike geodesic incompleteness, Big-Bang singularity, cosmological singularity theorem

**Key moves**
- Read convergence as minus the fractional rate of change of comoving volume, V proportional to a^3, giving c = -3 a-dot/a.
- Observe the future-directed argument fails because c < 0 in an expanding universe.
- Reverse time: past-directed convergence is +3 a-dot/a > 0, so the bound applies to the past.
- Interpret the bound as timelike curves leaving the smooth manifold: the singular point is removed from spacetime.

### Chapter summary and exercises — p.552 (pdf 569)

Four summary bullets restate the chain Cauchy surface, 3/c0 bound, singularity, Big Bang. Three exercises follow: properties of the projection tensor (including the spacelike-normal version), a guided derivation of the Raychaudhuri equation for a timelike geodesic congruence with its decomposition into expansion, shear and rotation (following Zee), and a proof that with zero rotation and the strong energy condition the congruence is focused.

**Concepts:** projection tensor orthogonal to the 4-velocity, velocity gradient tensor B, expansion, shear and rotation decomposition, Raychaudhuri equation, strong energy condition, focusing inequality

**Key moves**
- Recast the chapter's calculation in the conventional Raychaudhuri language with theta = -c.
- Show the shear square is non-negative when the tensor is purely spatial and rotation vanishes.
- Combine with the strong energy condition to get d theta/d tau <= -theta^2/3.

## Concepts

### Big-Bang singularity

*phenomenon · core* · also: initial singularity, cosmological singularity

The past boundary of an expanding universe at which every past-directed timelike world line comes to an end after finite proper time, because the events it would reach cannot belong to a smooth spacetime manifold.

**How introduced:** Posed at the outset as either an artefact of Friedmann symmetry or a real event, and established at the end by running the focusing argument backwards in time from today's slice; pictured as a hole cut out of a drawn sheet (Fig. 50.4).

**Prerequisites:** cosmological singularity theorem, timelike geodesic incompleteness, Friedmann-Robertson-Walker model

$$
\tau_{\rm past} \le 3/c_0 = 1/H_0
$$

**Notes:** The argument establishes incompleteness of past timelike curves; that curvature or density diverges there is known from the explicit Friedmann solutions, not from this theorem.

**Where:** GA p.547 (pdf 564); GA §50.3 p.551 (pdf 568); GA p.552 (pdf 569)

### cosmological singularity theorem

*theorem · core* · also: Hawking's cosmological singularity theorem, focusing theorem for a Cauchy slice

If spacetime is globally hyperbolic with Cauchy surface S, the strong energy condition holds, and the convergence of the future-directed unit normals to S is everywhere at least c0 > 0, then no future-directed timelike curve from S has proper length exceeding 3/c0; the time-reversed version applies to expanding universes and bounds the past.

**How introduced:** Never named as a theorem; built as a chain of plausibility arguments: existence of maximal curves, Euclidean normal-geodesic facts transplanted to spacetime, the convergence evolution equation, integration of the inequality, and contradiction.

**Prerequisites:** Cauchy surface, existence of a maximal timelike curve, normal geodesics, conjugate point, focusing inequality, strong energy condition

$$
\frac{dc}{d\tau}\ge\frac{c^2}{3}\ \Rightarrow\ \tau\le\frac{3}{c_0}
$$

**Notes:** The book follows Geroch's lecture-note version; the full statement needs c0 to be a uniform lower bound on S, which the text leaves implicit.

**Where:** GA p.547 (pdf 564); GA §50.2 p.550 (pdf 567); GA §50.2 p.551 (pdf 568); GA p.552 (pdf 569)

### global methods in general relativity

*technique · mention* · also: global techniques, causal structure methods

Arguments about the whole causal structure of a spacetime (Cauchy surfaces, spaces of causal curves, compactness, maximal curves) as opposed to local tensor calculations at a point.

**How introduced:** Named in the first margin note as the generalized form of the chapter's techniques, with Penrose, Wald and Hawking-Ellis as references.

**Prerequisites:** Cauchy surface, compactness of the space of causal curves

**Where:** GA p.547 (pdf 564)

### Cauchy surface

*definition · introduced*

A spacelike hypersurface met exactly once by every inextendible timelike curve, so that data on it determine the whole spacetime; a spacetime possessing one is called globally hyperbolic.

**How introduced:** Introduced as an assumption about the slice of fixed cosmic time, characterized loosely by its predictive power over the future, and used only to guarantee that maximal timelike curves exist.

**Prerequisites:** domain of dependence

**Notes:** The text's description ('determines some future surface') is closer to the domain of dependence than to the precise definition.

**Where:** GA p.547 (pdf 564); GA §50.2 p.551 (pdf 568); GA p.552 (pdf 569)

### domain of dependence

*definition · mention* · also: D(S), Cauchy development

The set of events p such that every inextendible causal curve through p crosses the surface S; conditions at such events are fixed entirely by data on S.

**How introduced:** Mentioned alongside the Cauchy surface as the region in which the longest-curve guarantee holds.

**Prerequisites:** Cauchy surface

**Where:** GA p.547 (pdf 564)

### compactness of the space of causal curves

*theorem · mention* · also: compactness

In a globally hyperbolic region, the set of timelike and null curves joining an event to a Cauchy surface is compact in a suitable topology, so proper length, being upper semicontinuous, attains a maximum.

**How introduced:** Given in a margin note as the technicality underlying the existence of longest curves, glossed as 'no curves escape to infinity' with a pointer to Appendix C.

**Prerequisites:** Cauchy surface

**Notes:** The note says length is continuous; strictly it is upper semicontinuous, which still suffices.

**Where:** GA p.547 (pdf 564)

### existence of a maximal timelike curve

*theorem · core* · also: longest timelike curve, maximal curve

For any event p in the domain of dependence of a Cauchy surface S there is a causal curve from S to p of greatest proper time; it is a timelike geodesic meeting S orthogonally with no focal point between S and p.

**How introduced:** Asserted at the chapter start as a consequence of the Cauchy property, then reused as one horn of the final contradiction.

**Prerequisites:** Cauchy surface, compactness of the space of causal curves, maximal proper time of timelike geodesics

**Where:** GA p.547 (pdf 564); GA §50.2 p.550 (pdf 567); GA §50.2 p.551 (pdf 568); GA p.552 (pdf 569)

### orthogonality of the shortest path to a surface

*theorem · developed* · also: first-variation condition for point-to-surface distance

A curve of minimal length from a point to a surface must be a geodesic that meets the surface at a right angle; otherwise sliding its foot along the surface shortens it.

**How introduced:** Stated in Euclidean 3-space with Fig. 50.1, where a slanted straight line is shown to be beaten by a perpendicular one.

**Prerequisites:** geodesic

**Where:** GA §50.1 p.547 (pdf 564)

### normal geodesics

*definition · core* · also: orthogonal geodesics, geodesics normal to a hypersurface

Geodesics that leave a hypersurface along its unit normal; for a spacelike hypersurface in spacetime they are timelike geodesics starting at right angles to the slice.

**How introduced:** Named in §50.1 for Euclidean surfaces and carried into spacetime in §50.2, where they become the world lines whose convergence is studied.

**Prerequisites:** geodesic, orthogonality of the shortest path to a surface

**Where:** GA §50.1 p.547 (pdf 564); GA §50.2 p.548 (pdf 565); GA §50.2 p.550 (pdf 567)

### corner-cutting argument

*technique · developed* · also: rounding the corner

If a candidate extremal path to a surface has a kink, or can be replaced by a kinked path of equal length, smoothing the kink strictly improves it (shorter in Euclidean space, longer in proper time for timelike curves), so the candidate was not extremal.

**How introduced:** Illustrated with Fig. 50.2: two normal geodesics cross at r, and a path that follows one to near r, shortcuts across, and follows the other to S beats the first.

**Prerequisites:** normal geodesics

**Notes:** For timelike curves the reverse triangle inequality means smoothing a kink lengthens proper time; the book leaves this Lorentzian version implicit.

**Where:** GA §50.1 p.548 (pdf 565)

### conjugate point

*definition · revisited* · also: focal point of a hypersurface

A point along a geodesic where an infinitesimally neighbouring geodesic from the same starting point (or normal to the same surface, for a focal point) meets it again; past it the geodesic is no longer a local extremum of length.

**How introduced:** Recalled from the Chapter 8 discussion of when a stationary action is a true minimum, and identified with the crossing point r of two normal geodesics.

**Prerequisites:** geodesic, normal geodesics

**Notes:** For curves starting on a surface the standard name is focal point; the book uses conjugate point for both.

**Where:** GA §50.1 p.548 (pdf 565); GA §50.2 p.550 (pdf 567)

### maximal proper time of timelike geodesics

*principle · revisited* · also: longest lines are straightest, twin-paradox principle

Among nearby timelike curves joining two events, the geodesic accumulates the most proper time, because acceleration reduces elapsed proper time in Lorentzian signature.

**How introduced:** Recalled at the start of §50.2 to justify replacing shortest with longest in every Euclidean statement.

**Prerequisites:** proper time, geodesic

$$
\tau=\int\sqrt{-g_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu}\,d\lambda
$$

**Where:** GA §50.2 p.548 (pdf 565)

### proper-time function from a hypersurface

*mathematical-object · developed* · also: phi(x), Gaussian normal time, distance function from S

A scalar field phi whose value at an event is the proper time elapsed along the normal geodesic from S to that event; its level sets are the hypersurfaces a fixed proper time away from S.

**How introduced:** Introduced to measure how far along the normal geodesics each event lies, drawn as a stack of labelled sheets phi = 0, 1, 2, 3 (Fig. 50.3).

**Prerequisites:** normal geodesics, proper time

$$
u_\mu=\nabla_\mu\phi
$$

**Notes:** That grad phi has unit norm is the Gauss lemma, assumed rather than proved.

**Where:** GA §50.2 p.548 (pdf 565)

### hypersurface-orthogonal geodesic congruence

*mathematical-object · core* · also: gradient velocity field, normal congruence

A family of timelike geodesics filling a region, one through each event, whose unit tangent field is (up to sign) the gradient of a scalar, so the flow is everywhere orthogonal to a family of hypersurfaces and has zero rotation.

**How introduced:** Built by setting the velocity 1-form equal to grad phi, raising the index with the metric, and showing the resulting field parallel-transports itself.

**Prerequisites:** proper-time function from a hypersurface, symmetry of the second covariant derivative of a scalar, geodesic equation

$$
u_\mu=\nabla_\mu\phi,\quad u^\mu u_\mu=-1,\quad u^\mu u_{\nu;\mu}=0
$$

**Where:** GA §50.2 p.548 (pdf 565)

### symmetry of the second covariant derivative of a scalar

*identity · developed* · also: torsion-free symmetry of phi_{;mu nu}

For a torsion-free connection the second covariant derivatives of a scalar commute, so the covariant derivative of a gradient 1-form is a symmetric tensor.

**How introduced:** Shown in a margin note by writing the covariant derivative of the gradient and noting the connection coefficients are symmetric in their lower indices.

**Prerequisites:** connection coefficients, covariant derivative

$$
\phi_{;\mu\nu}=\partial_\nu\partial_\mu\phi-\Gamma^\alpha{}_{\nu\mu}\partial_\alpha\phi=\phi_{;\nu\mu}
$$

**Where:** GA §50.2 p.548 (pdf 565)

### convergence of a geodesic congruence

*physical-quantity · core* · also: c, minus the expansion scalar, -theta

The scalar c = -u^mu_{;mu}, minus the divergence of the tangent field; it equals minus the fractional rate of change, per unit proper time, of the volume of a small comoving blob of the congruence, so c > 0 means world lines are drawing together.

**How introduced:** Defined right after showing the normal field is geodesic, as the quantity whose evolution along the flow decides whether the congruence focuses.

**Prerequisites:** hypersurface-orthogonal geodesic congruence, covariant derivative

$$
c=-\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol u=-u^\mu{}_{;\mu}
$$

$$
c=-\dot{\mathcal V}/\mathcal V
$$

**Notes:** Conventional literature uses theta = u^mu_{;mu} = -c, as the exercises do.

**Where:** GA §50.2 p.548 (pdf 565); GA §50.2 p.550 (pdf 567); GA §50.3 p.551 (pdf 568)

### Ricci identity

*identity · revisited* · also: commutator of covariant derivatives on a vector

The failure of second covariant derivatives of a vector field to commute is measured by the Riemann tensor contracted with that vector, so swapping derivative order costs a curvature term.

**How introduced:** Quoted from eqn 35.13 and contracted on the first index with the differentiation index to bring in the Ricci tensor.

**Prerequisites:** Riemann curvature tensor, covariant derivative

$$
u^\alpha{}_{;\nu\mu}-u^\alpha{}_{;\mu\nu}=R^\alpha{}_{\beta\mu\nu}u^\beta
$$

**Where:** GA §50.2 p.549 (pdf 566)

### Ricci tensor

*mathematical-object · revisited* · also: R_{mu nu}

The contraction R_{beta nu} = R^alpha_{beta alpha nu} of the Riemann tensor; contracted twice with a 4-velocity it measures the rate at which curvature shrinks a small ball of freely falling particles.

**How introduced:** Appears from contracting the Ricci identity; interpreted in the text as the piece of curvature that makes volumes contract.

**Prerequisites:** Riemann curvature tensor

$$
R_{\beta\nu}=R^\alpha{}_{\beta\alpha\nu}
$$

**Where:** GA §50.2 p.549 (pdf 566); GA §50.2 p.550 (pdf 567)

### projection tensor orthogonal to the 4-velocity

*mathematical-object · revisited* · also: transverse projection operator, P_{alpha beta}, spatial metric h_{alpha beta}

The symmetric tensor P_{alpha beta} = g_{alpha beta} + u_alpha u_beta, which removes the component of a vector along the unit timelike u and acts as the metric on the 3-space orthogonal to u; its trace is 3.

**How introduced:** Imported from Chapter 39 to separate the velocity gradient into trace and trace-free parts; its properties are listed in a footnote and assigned as Exercise 50.1.

**Prerequisites:** metric tensor, 4-velocity

$$
P_{\alpha\beta}=g_{\alpha\beta}+u_\alpha u_\beta
$$

$$
P^{\alpha\beta}P_{\alpha\beta}=3
$$

$$
P^{\alpha\beta}u_{\alpha;\beta}=-c
$$

**Where:** GA §50.2 p.549 (pdf 566); GA p.552 (pdf 569)

### trace and trace-free decomposition of the velocity gradient

*technique · developed* · also: trace/traceless split

Writing the symmetric, spatial tensor u_{alpha;nu} as its isotropic part -(c/3)P_{alpha nu} plus a remainder with zero trace; the two parts are orthogonal under full contraction.

**How introduced:** Example 50.2 uses it to turn the awkward contracted square in the convergence equation into c^2/3 plus a non-negative piece.

**Prerequisites:** projection tensor orthogonal to the 4-velocity, convergence of a geodesic congruence

$$
u_{\alpha;\nu}=-\tfrac{c}{3}P_{\alpha\nu}+\left(u_{\alpha;\nu}+\tfrac{c}{3}P_{\alpha\nu}\right)
$$

**Where:** GA §50.2 p.549 (pdf 566)

### trace inequality for the velocity gradient

*theorem · core* · also: shear-square inequality

For the symmetric spatial velocity gradient of a hypersurface-orthogonal geodesic congruence, the full contraction u^{nu;alpha}u_{alpha;nu} is at least c^2/3, with equality exactly when the shear vanishes.

**How introduced:** Result of Example 50.2: after the split, the trace-free square is dropped because it cannot be negative.

**Prerequisites:** trace and trace-free decomposition of the velocity gradient

$$
(u^{\nu;\alpha})(u_{\alpha;\nu})\ge\frac{c^2}{3}
$$

**Notes:** In an eigenbasis this is the statement that the sum of squares of three numbers is at least one third of the square of their sum.

**Where:** GA §50.2 p.549 (pdf 566); GA §50.2 p.550 (pdf 567)

### trace-reversed Einstein equation

*law · revisited*

The field equation rewritten so the Ricci tensor is given directly by matter: R_{mu nu} = 8 pi (T_{mu nu} - (1/2) g_{mu nu} T) in units G = c = 1 without a cosmological constant.

**How introduced:** Used, with velocity components contracted in, to turn the geometric Ricci term into a statement about matter.

**Prerequisites:** Ricci tensor, energy-momentum tensor

$$
R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu=8\pi\left(T_{\mu\nu}-\tfrac12 g_{\mu\nu}T\right)u^\mu u^\nu
$$

**Where:** GA §50.2 p.550 (pdf 567)

### weak energy condition

*principle · mention* · also: WEC

The requirement that every observer measure a non-negative energy density, T_{mu nu}u^mu u^nu >= 0 for all timelike u.

**How introduced:** Cited from Chapter 13 (where it appears only in a margin note, as a looser version of the dominant condition) as the reason the contracted Ricci term is non-negative, although that term really contains the trace-reversed stress tensor.

**Prerequisites:** energy-momentum tensor

$$
T_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu\ge0
$$

**Notes:** Book error: the weak condition does not imply R_{mu nu}u^mu u^nu >= 0; the strong energy condition (Exercise 50.3) is what is needed.

**Where:** GA §50.2 p.550 (pdf 567)

### strong energy condition

*principle · introduced* · also: SEC, timelike convergence condition

The requirement (T_{mu nu} - (1/2) T g_{mu nu}) u^mu u^nu >= 0 for every timelike u, equivalently R_{mu nu}u^mu u^nu >= 0; for a perfect fluid it means rho + 3p >= 0 and rho + p >= 0. It encodes gravity being attractive for timelike geodesics.

**How introduced:** Introduced by name only in Exercise 50.3 as the condition that guarantees focusing; implicitly it is what the §50.2 argument requires.

**Prerequisites:** trace-reversed Einstein equation, energy-momentum tensor

$$
T_{\mu\nu}v^\mu v^\nu\ge\tfrac12 T v^\mu v_\mu
$$

$$
\rho+3p\ge0
$$

**Notes:** Violated by a positive cosmological constant (rho + 3p = -2 rho_Lambda) and by an inflaton during slow roll.

**Where:** GA §50.2 p.550 (pdf 567); GA p.553 (pdf 570)

### focusing inequality

*theorem · core* · also: attractive gravity focuses world lines, Raychaudhuri inequality

Along a hypersurface-orthogonal timelike geodesic congruence satisfying the strong energy condition, the convergence grows at least as fast as c^2/3 per unit proper time.

**How introduced:** Obtained by combining the result of Example 50.1 with the trace inequality and the energy condition; read physically as gravity pulling world lines together.

**Prerequisites:** convergence of a geodesic congruence, trace inequality for the velocity gradient, strong energy condition, Ricci tensor

$$
\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}c=\frac{dc}{d\tau}\ge\frac{c^2}{3}
$$

$$
\frac{D\theta}{d\tau}\le-\frac13\theta^2
$$

**Where:** GA §50.2 p.550 (pdf 567); GA p.553 (pdf 570)

### focusing time bound

*theorem · core* · also: 3/c0 bound, finite-time blow-up of convergence

If the initial convergence at S is c0 > 0, the focusing inequality forces c to become infinite no later than proper time tau = 3/c0 along each normal geodesic.

**How introduced:** Obtained by integrating the differential inequality with c(0) = c0, in the same way as the elementary blow-up of dy/dx = y^2.

**Prerequisites:** focusing inequality

$$
c(\tau)\ge\frac{3}{3/c_0-\tau}
$$

$$
\tau_{\max}=3/c_0
$$

**Where:** GA §50.2 p.550 (pdf 567); GA §50.2 p.551 (pdf 568); GA p.552 (pdf 569)

### caustic

*phenomenon · introduced* · also: focal envelope

The locus where neighbouring members of a congruence of curves meet, so that the convergence of the congruence diverges; familiar from optics as the bright envelope of focused light rays.

**How introduced:** Named to interpret the divergence of c: world lines are forced to cross, which is harmless in itself but spoils maximality.

**Prerequisites:** focusing time bound, conjugate point

**Where:** GA §50.2 p.550 (pdf 567)

### proof by contradiction via maximal curves

*technique · core* · also: the contradiction argument

The logical core of the theorem: a Cauchy surface guarantees a maximal curve to every event, yet a maximal curve must be a normal geodesic without crossings, and focusing forces crossings by 3/c0; hence no event can lie farther than 3/c0 from S.

**How introduced:** Assembled in the closing paragraphs of §50.2, with the key conclusion highlighted in a shaded box and then restated in plainer language.

**Prerequisites:** existence of a maximal timelike curve, corner-cutting argument, focusing time bound

**Where:** GA §50.2 p.550 (pdf 567); GA §50.2 p.551 (pdf 568)

### Friedmann-Robertson-Walker model

*solution-or-model · revisited* · also: Friedmann universe, Robertson-Walker space filled with perfect fluid

Homogeneous, isotropic cosmological spacetime with metric determined by a scale factor a(t) and curvature index, sourced by a perfect fluid according to the Friedmann equations.

**How introduced:** Recalled in §50.3 as a good description of our Universe, to which the abstract theorem is applied.

**Prerequisites:** scale factor, perfect fluid

**Where:** GA p.547 (pdf 564); GA §50.3 p.551 (pdf 568)

### comoving volume element

*physical-quantity · revisited* · also: comoving 3-volume V

The proper volume of a small region carried along with the cosmic fluid; in a Robertson-Walker universe it scales as the cube of the scale factor.

**How introduced:** Used dimensionally to identify the convergence of the fluid world lines with minus the fractional rate of change of volume.

**Prerequisites:** scale factor

$$
\mathcal V\propto a(t)^3
$$

**Where:** GA §50.3 p.551 (pdf 568)

### convergence of comoving Friedmann world lines

*physical-quantity · developed* · also: c = -3H

For comoving dust in a Friedmann model the convergence is c = -3 a-dot/a = -3H, negative when the universe expands; the corresponding past-directed convergence is +3H.

**How introduced:** Derived from V proportional to a^3 and the reading of c as minus the fractional volume change rate.

**Prerequisites:** convergence of a geodesic congruence, comoving volume element, Hubble parameter

$$
c=-\frac{3\dot a}{a}=-3H
$$

**Where:** GA §50.3 p.551 (pdf 568)

### Hubble parameter

*physical-quantity · mention* · also: H, a-dot/a

The fractional expansion rate H = a-dot/a of the scale factor; its present value H0 sets the Hubble time 1/H0.

**How introduced:** Not named in the chapter, but the convergence formula c = -3 a-dot/a is three times it with a minus sign.

**Prerequisites:** scale factor

$$
H=\dot a/a
$$

**Where:** GA §50.3 p.551 (pdf 568)

### time reversal of the focusing argument

*technique · developed* · also: playing time backwards

Reversing the time orientation turns an expanding congruence (c < 0 to the future) into a converging one (c > 0 to the past), so the future-directed focusing theorem becomes a bound on how far back timelike curves extend.

**How introduced:** Offered as the rescue after admitting the theorem cannot constrain an expanding universe's future.

**Prerequisites:** convergence of comoving Friedmann world lines, cosmological singularity theorem

**Where:** GA §50.3 p.551 (pdf 568)

### smooth manifold assumption

*convention · revisited* · also: spacetime as a smooth manifold

General relativity models spacetime as a smooth manifold on which fields are differentiable everywhere; places where that fails are not points of spacetime at all.

**How introduced:** Invoked to explain where the curves traced back 3/c0 go: to a place that cannot be in the manifold, which must be cut out.

**Where:** GA §50.3 p.551 (pdf 568)

### timelike geodesic incompleteness

*definition · core* · also: curves running out of spacetime, excised singular point

The existence of timelike geodesics that cannot be extended beyond a finite proper time within the spacetime manifold; the standard modern definition of a singular spacetime.

**How introduced:** Conveyed pictorially as a timelike curve followed backwards that reaches a hole in the manifold and cannot continue (Fig. 50.4).

**Prerequisites:** smooth manifold assumption, geodesic

**Notes:** The book says 'point'; for Friedmann models the boundary is a whole spacelike surface, reached by all comoving world lines at once.

**Where:** GA §50.3 p.551 (pdf 568)

### Raychaudhuri equation

*law · introduced*

The evolution equation for the velocity gradient of a timelike geodesic congruence; its trace says the expansion rate decreases because of its own square, the shear squared and the Ricci term along u, while only rotation can counteract the decrease.

**How introduced:** Introduced in Exercise 50.2 following Zee's derivation, presented as the conventional form of the §50.2 calculation.

**Prerequisites:** velocity gradient tensor B, expansion, shear and rotation decomposition, Ricci identity

$$
(\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}\boldsymbol B)_{\mu\nu}=-B_{\mu\alpha}B^\alpha{}_\nu-R_{\beta\mu\alpha\nu}u^\beta u^\alpha
$$

$$
\frac{D\theta}{d\tau}=-\tfrac13\theta^2-\sigma_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu}+\omega_{\mu\nu}\omega^{\mu\nu}-R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu
$$

**Where:** GA p.552 (pdf 569); GA p.553 (pdf 570)

### velocity gradient tensor B

*mathematical-object · introduced* · also: B_{mu nu} = u_{mu;nu}

The tensor B_{mu nu} = u_{mu;nu} of a geodesic congruence; acting on a connecting vector it gives the rate of change of that vector along the flow, and it is orthogonal to u in both slots.

**How introduced:** Defined in Exercise 50.2 via connecting vectors W_i between neighbouring geodesics labelled by sigma^i.

**Prerequisites:** covariant derivative, geodesic deviation

$$
(\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}\boldsymbol W_i)^\mu=B^\mu{}_\nu W_i^\nu
$$

$$
u^\mu B_{\mu\nu}=0=B_{\mu\nu}u^\nu
$$

**Where:** GA p.552 (pdf 569)

### expansion, shear and rotation decomposition

*technique · introduced* · also: theta, sigma, omega

Splitting B_{mu nu} into its trace theta (isotropic volume change), symmetric trace-free part sigma_{mu nu} (shape distortion at fixed volume) and antisymmetric part omega_{mu nu} (twisting of neighbouring world lines).

**How introduced:** Given in Exercise 50.2 as the conventional split; the main text's gradient field has omega = 0 automatically.

**Prerequisites:** velocity gradient tensor B, projection tensor orthogonal to the 4-velocity

$$
B_{\mu\nu}=\sigma_{\mu\nu}+\tfrac13\theta P_{\mu\nu}+\omega_{\mu\nu}
$$

**Where:** GA p.552 (pdf 569); GA p.553 (pdf 570)

### zero rotation of a hypersurface-orthogonal congruence

*theorem · introduced* · also: irrotational congruence, omega = 0

A geodesic congruence whose tangent is a gradient (orthogonal to a family of hypersurfaces) has vanishing rotation tensor, which removes the only term in the Raychaudhuri equation that opposes focusing.

**How introduced:** Implicit in the symmetry of u_{nu;mu} used in §50.2; made explicit as the hypothesis of Exercise 50.3.

**Prerequisites:** hypersurface-orthogonal geodesic congruence, expansion, shear and rotation decomposition

$$
\omega_{\mu\nu}=0
$$

**Where:** GA §50.2 p.548 (pdf 565); GA p.553 (pdf 570)

## Key equations

### Normal velocity field from the proper-time function · supporting · GA §50.2 p.548 (pdf 565)

$$
u_\mu=\nabla_\mu\phi,\qquad u^\mu u_\mu=-1
$$

The congruence of normal geodesics is described by the gradient of the proper-time-from-S function, normalized as a 4-velocity.

**Symbols:** phi proper time from S along the normal geodesic; u_mu components of the velocity 1-form

### Symmetry of the gradient's covariant derivative (margin note 3) · derivation-step · GA §50.2 p.548 (pdf 565)

$$
\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x^\mu}\right)_{;\nu}=\frac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial x^\nu\partial x^\mu}-\Gamma^\alpha{}_{\nu\mu}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x^\alpha}
$$

Both terms are symmetric in mu and nu for a torsion-free connection, so u_{mu;nu} = u_{nu;mu}.

**Symbols:** Gamma connection coefficients

### (50.1) Normal field is geodesic · supporting · GA §50.2 p.548 (pdf 565)

$$
(\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}\tilde{\boldsymbol u})_\nu=u^\mu u_{\nu;\mu}=u^\mu u_{\mu;\nu}=\tfrac12(u^\mu u_\mu)_{;\nu}=0
$$

Symmetry lets the index order be swapped, turning the acceleration into the gradient of the constant norm, which vanishes; raising nu gives nabla_u u = 0.

**Symbols:** tilde u velocity 1-form; semicolon covariant derivative

### (50.2) Convergence · central · GA §50.2 p.548 (pdf 565)

$$
c=-\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol u=-u^\mu{}_{;\mu}
$$

Minus the divergence of the congruence; positive when neighbouring world lines approach.

**Symbols:** c convergence (not the speed of light)

### (50.3) · derivation-step · GA §50.2 p.548 (pdf 565)

$$
\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}c=u^\nu\nabla_\nu c=-u^\nu u^\alpha{}_{;\alpha\nu}
$$

Rate of change of convergence along the flow, written with the divergence differentiated again.

### (50.4) Ricci identity (eqn 35.13) · supporting · GA §50.2 p.549 (pdf 566)

$$
u^\alpha{}_{;\nu\mu}-u^\alpha{}_{;\mu\nu}=R^\alpha{}_{\beta\mu\nu}u^\beta
$$

Commuting covariant derivatives of a vector produces curvature.

**Symbols:** R^alpha_{beta mu nu} Riemann tensor

### (50.5) · derivation-step · GA §50.2 p.549 (pdf 566)

$$
u^\alpha{}_{;\nu\alpha}-u^\alpha{}_{;\alpha\nu}=R^\alpha{}_{\beta\alpha\nu}u^\beta=R_{\beta\nu}u^\beta
$$

Contracting alpha with mu converts the commutator into the Ricci tensor.

**Symbols:** R_{beta nu} Ricci tensor

### (50.6) · derivation-step · GA §50.2 p.549 (pdf 566)

$$
\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}c=-u^\nu u^\alpha{}_{;\nu\alpha}+R_{\beta\nu}u^\nu u^\beta
$$

Derivative order swapped at the cost of a Ricci term.

### (50.7) · derivation-step · GA §50.2 p.549 (pdf 566)

$$
\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}c=-(u^\nu u^\alpha{}_{;\nu})_{;\alpha}+(u^\nu{}_{;\alpha})(u^\alpha{}_{;\nu})+R_{\beta\nu}u^\nu u^\beta
$$

Product rule (integration by parts) moves the outer derivative off u^alpha; the first term contains the geodesic acceleration.

### (50.8) · supporting · GA §50.2 p.549 (pdf 566)

$$
\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}c=(u^\nu{}_{;\alpha})(u^\alpha{}_{;\nu})+R_{\beta\nu}u^\nu u^\beta
$$

Geodesic property kills the total-derivative term, leaving a velocity-gradient square plus curvature. The text says the vanishing term is 'on the left' though it is on the right.

### (50.9) Convergence evolution equation · central · GA §50.2 p.549 (pdf 566)

$$
\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}c=(u^{\nu;\alpha})(u_{\alpha;\nu})+R_{\beta\nu}u^\nu u^\beta
$$

Convergence changes along the flow because of the self-contracted velocity gradient and the Ricci curvature; this is the trace of the Raychaudhuri equation for zero rotation, written in terms of c.

### Transverse projection tensor · supporting · GA §50.2 p.549 (pdf 566)

$$
P_{\alpha\beta}=g_{\alpha\beta}+u_\alpha u_\beta
$$

Metric on the 3-space orthogonal to u.

**Symbols:** P projection tensor

### (50.10) Trace/trace-free split · derivation-step · GA §50.2 p.549 (pdf 566)

$$
u_{\alpha;\nu}=-\frac{c}{3}P_{\alpha\nu}+\left(u_{\alpha;\nu}+\frac{c}{3}P_{\alpha\nu}\right)
$$

Isotropic part plus a remainder whose trace vanishes.

### Projection identities (margin note 5) · supporting · GA §50.2 p.549 (pdf 566)

$$
\tfrac{c}{3}P_{\alpha\nu}g^{\alpha\nu}=c,\quad g^{\alpha\nu}\left(u_{\alpha;\nu}+\tfrac{c}{3}P_{\alpha\nu}\right)=-c+c=0,\quad P^{\alpha\beta}P_{\alpha\beta}=3,\quad P^{\alpha\beta}u_{\alpha;\beta}=-c
$$

Facts that make the cross terms in 50.11 vanish and fix the coefficient c^2/3. The note calls the first term's trace c; with the minus sign in 50.10 its trace is -c.

### (50.11) · derivation-step · GA §50.2 p.549 (pdf 566)

$$
(u^{\nu;\alpha})(u_{\alpha;\nu})=\left[-\tfrac{c}{3}P^{\nu\alpha}+\left(u^{\nu;\alpha}+\tfrac{c}{3}P^{\nu\alpha}\right)\right]\left[-\tfrac{c}{3}P_{\alpha\nu}+\left(u_{\alpha;\nu}+\tfrac{c}{3}P_{\alpha\nu}\right)\right]=\frac{c^2}{3}+\left(u^{\nu;\alpha}+\tfrac{c}{3}P^{\nu\alpha}\right)\left(u_{\alpha;\nu}+\tfrac{c}{3}P_{\alpha\nu}\right)
$$

The square separates into the isotropic contribution c^2/3 and the square of the trace-free (shear) part.

### (50.12) Trace inequality · central · GA §50.2 p.549 (pdf 566)

$$
(u^{\nu;\alpha})(u_{\alpha;\nu})\ge\frac{c^2}{3}
$$

The shear square is non-negative because it lives in the positive-definite 3-space orthogonal to u.

### (50.13) Contracted trace-reversed Einstein equation · supporting · GA §50.2 p.550 (pdf 567)

$$
u^\mu u^\nu R_{\mu\nu}=8\pi\left(T_{\mu\nu}-\tfrac12 g_{\mu\nu}T\right)u^\mu u^\nu
$$

Converts the curvature term into matter; for a perfect fluid it equals 4 pi (rho + 3p).

**Symbols:** T trace of the energy-momentum tensor; G = c = 1

### (50.14) Focusing inequality · central · GA §50.2 p.550 (pdf 567)

$$
\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}c\ge\frac{c^2}{3}
$$

With a non-negative Ricci term, convergence grows at least quadratically: gravity focuses world lines.

### (50.15) · supporting · GA §50.2 p.550 (pdf 567)

$$
\frac{dc}{d\tau}\ge\frac{c^2}{3}
$$

Same inequality with the directional derivative written as a proper-time derivative along each geodesic.

**Symbols:** tau proper time from S

### (50.16) Blow-up bound · central · GA §50.2 p.550 (pdf 567)

$$
c(\tau)\ge\frac{3}{3/c_0-\tau}
$$

Integrating from c(0) = c0 > 0 shows convergence must become infinite no later than tau = 3/c0.

**Symbols:** c0 convergence on S

### Maximal proper distance from S · central · GA §50.2 p.551 (pdf 568)

$$
\tau\le\frac{3}{c_0}
$$

No event can be reached from S along a timelike curve of proper time longer than 3/c0. The shaded statement that no longest curve exists beyond 3/c0 is on printed p.550; the italic conclusion that spacetime cannot extend that far opens p.551.

### (50.17) Convergence of an expanding Friedmann universe · central · GA §50.3 p.551 (pdf 568)

$$
c=-\frac{3\dot a(t)}{a(t)}
$$

Comoving world lines diverge to the future; reversing time gives past convergence 3 a-dot/a.

**Symbols:** a scale factor, dot = d/dt

### Past bound in Hubble units (not printed; follows from 50.16-50.17) · supporting · GA §50.3 p.551 (pdf 568)

$$
c_0=3H_0\ \Rightarrow\ \tau_{\rm past}\le\frac{3}{c_0}=\frac{1}{H_0}
$$

The chapter's bound on the age of an SEC-obeying universe is the Hubble time, matching the tangent-line estimate of Chapter 18.

**Symbols:** H0 present Hubble parameter

### (50.18) Projection tensor (index-free) · supporting · GA p.552 (pdf 569)

$$
\boldsymbol P(\;,\;)=\boldsymbol g(\;,\;)+\tilde{\boldsymbol u}(\;)\otimes\tilde{\boldsymbol u}(\;)
$$

Slot-machine form of the projector orthogonal to a velocity u (Exercise 50.1); the printed ~u( , ) second factor has an extra slot.

### Projector orthogonal to a unit spacelike vector · supporting · GA p.552 (pdf 569)

$$
\boldsymbol P=\boldsymbol g(\;,\;)-\tilde{\boldsymbol n}(\;)\otimes\tilde{\boldsymbol n}(\;)
$$

The sign flips because n.n = +1 rather than -1 (Exercise 50.1e).

**Symbols:** n unit spacelike vector

### (50.19) Evolution of connecting vectors · supporting · GA p.552 (pdf 569)

$$
(\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}\boldsymbol W_i)^\mu=B^\mu{}_\nu W_i^\nu,\qquad B_{\mu\nu}=u_{\mu;\nu}
$$

Because connecting vectors are Lie dragged, their rate of change along the congruence is B acting on them.

**Symbols:** W_i connecting vectors; B velocity gradient

### (50.20) Expansion scalar · supporting · GA p.552 (pdf 569)

$$
\theta=P^{\mu\nu}B_{\mu\nu}=u^\mu{}_{;\mu}
$$

Trace of B, the fractional volume growth rate; theta = -c.

### (50.21) Shear tensor · supporting · GA p.552 (pdf 569)

$$
\sigma_{\mu\nu}=\tfrac12(B_{\mu\nu}+B_{\nu\mu})-\tfrac13\theta P_{\mu\nu}
$$

Symmetric trace-free part: volume-preserving distortion.

### (50.22) Rotation tensor · supporting · GA p.552 (pdf 569)

$$
\omega_{\mu\nu}=\tfrac12(B_{\mu\nu}-B_{\nu\mu})
$$

Antisymmetric part: twisting of neighbouring world lines.

### (50.23) · supporting · GA p.552 (pdf 569)

$$
B_{\mu\nu}=\sigma_{\mu\nu}+\tfrac13\theta P_{\mu\nu}+\omega_{\mu\nu}
$$

Full decomposition of the velocity gradient.

### (50.24) Raychaudhuri equation (tensor form) · central · GA p.552 (pdf 569)

$$
(\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}\boldsymbol B)_{\mu\nu}=-B_{\mu\alpha}B^\alpha{}_\nu-R_{\beta\mu\alpha\nu}u^\beta u^\alpha
$$

How the whole velocity gradient changes along a geodesic congruence. Checked: consistent with the Riemann convention of eqn 50.4.

### (50.25) · derivation-step · GA p.553 (pdf 570)

$$
g^{\mu\nu}B_{\mu\alpha}B^\alpha{}_\nu=\sigma_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu}+\tfrac13\theta^2-\omega_{\mu\nu}\omega^{\mu\nu}
$$

Trace of the square splits into shear, expansion and rotation pieces; rotation enters with the opposite sign.

### (50.26) Raychaudhuri equation (scalar form) · central · GA p.553 (pdf 570)

$$
\frac{D\theta}{d\tau}=-\tfrac13\theta^2-\sigma_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu}+\omega_{\mu\nu}\omega^{\mu\nu}-R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu
$$

Expansion decreases because of expansion squared, shear and Ricci focusing; only rotation resists. Equivalent to 50.9 when omega = 0 and theta = -c.

### (50.27) · supporting · GA p.553 (pdf 570)

$$
\frac{D\theta}{d\tau}\le-\tfrac13\theta^2-R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu
$$

With zero rotation the shear square, being spatial, can only help focusing.

### (50.28) Strong energy condition · central · GA p.553 (pdf 570)

$$
T_{\mu\nu}v^\mu v^\nu\ge\tfrac12 T\,v^\mu v_\mu\quad\text{for all timelike }\boldsymbol v
$$

Equivalent to R_{mu nu}v^mu v^nu >= 0; guarantees focusing of the congruence.

## Worked examples

### Example · standard · GA §50.2 p.548 (pdf 565)

**Problem:** Show that the unit field built from the gradient of the proper-time function is geodesic.

**Method:** Use symmetry of the covariant derivative of a gradient to swap indices in the acceleration, recognize half the derivative of the constant norm u.u = -1, and raise the free index with a metric-compatible connection.

**Key insight:** Being a unit-norm gradient is enough to force geodesic motion; no equation of motion needs to be solved.

**Result:** nabla_u u = 0, so u is tangent to all the normal geodesics.

**Concepts:** hypersurface-orthogonal geodesic congruence, symmetry of the second covariant derivative of a scalar

### Example 50.1 · challenging · GA §50.2 p.548 (pdf 565)

**Problem:** Find how the convergence c of the normal congruence changes along its own world lines.

**Method:** Write the directional derivative of c as a double covariant derivative of u; use the Ricci identity (eqn 35.13) contracted on the derivative index to reorder the derivatives, producing a Ricci term; apply the product rule to pull out a term containing the geodesic acceleration, which vanishes.

**Key insight:** Reordering covariant derivatives is exactly where curvature enters, and the Ricci tensor (not the full Riemann tensor) controls volume change.

**Result:** dc/dtau = u^nu_{;alpha} u^alpha_{;nu} + R_{beta nu}u^nu u^beta (eqns 50.8-50.9).

**Concepts:** convergence of a geodesic congruence, Ricci identity, Ricci tensor

### Example 50.2 · challenging · GA §50.2 p.549 (pdf 566)

**Problem:** Bound from below the contracted square of the velocity gradient in terms of the convergence.

**Method:** Split u_{alpha;nu} into -(c/3)P plus a trace-free remainder; multiply out, use P^{alpha beta}P_{alpha beta} = 3 and the orthogonality of P to the trace-free part so cross terms vanish; discard the non-negative square of the trace-free part.

**Key insight:** An isotropic part carries all of the trace, and any anisotropy (shear) only adds a positive amount, just as the mean square of three numbers exceeds the square of their mean.

**Result:** u^{nu;alpha}u_{alpha;nu} >= c^2/3 (eqn 50.12).

**Concepts:** trace and trace-free decomposition of the velocity gradient, projection tensor orthogonal to the 4-velocity, trace inequality for the velocity gradient

### Example · intro · GA §50.2 p.550 (pdf 567)

**Problem:** Solve the focusing inequality with the initial convergence c0 on S.

**Method:** Rewrite dc/dtau >= c^2/3 as d(-1/c)/dtau >= 1/3, integrate from 0 to tau, and invert for positive c.

**Key insight:** A quadratic growth law blows up in finite time, so an initial positive convergence guarantees a caustic within a fixed proper time.

**Result:** c(tau) >= 3/(3/c0 - tau), infinite by tau = 3/c0.

**Concepts:** focusing inequality, focusing time bound, caustic

### Example · standard · GA §50.2 p.550 (pdf 567)

**Problem:** Combine the geometric facts with the focusing bound to limit how far spacetime extends from S.

**Method:** Assume an event p lies more than 3/c0 from S along a timelike curve; the Cauchy property supplies a maximal curve to p; maximal curves must be normal geodesics without crossings; focusing forces a crossing before 3/c0; contradiction.

**Key insight:** The singularity is inferred, not computed: two guaranteed properties are incompatible unless the spacetime ends.

**Result:** No event is farther than 3/c0 from S along any timelike curve.

**Concepts:** proof by contradiction via maximal curves, existence of a maximal timelike curve, corner-cutting argument

### Example · intro · GA §50.3 p.551 (pdf 568)

**Problem:** Evaluate the convergence for comoving matter in a Friedmann universe and decide whether the theorem applies.

**Method:** Treat c as minus the fractional rate of change of a comoving volume, use V proportional to a^3, then reverse the time orientation.

**Key insight:** Expansion means divergence to the future but convergence to the past, so the theorem limits the past.

**Result:** c = -3 a-dot/a; past-directed c0 = 3H0, so past timelike curves are shorter than 3/c0 = 1/H0 (the last equality is our step, not printed).

**Concepts:** convergence of comoving Friedmann world lines, time reversal of the focusing argument, Big-Bang singularity


## Analogies and intuitions

### Shortest path from a point to a wall in ordinary space → existence of a maximal timelike curve · strong · GA §50.1 p.547 (pdf 564)

Everyday Euclidean facts (arrive perpendicular; do not continue past a crossing) are established first and then carried to spacetime by swapping shortest for longest.

**Where it breaks down:** The swap of inequality is justified only by appeal to the twin-paradox principle; in Lorentzian geometry there is no 'shortest' timelike curve at all, and kinks lengthen rather than shorten proper time.

**App idea:** Split-screen: Euclidean point-to-surface minimizer on the left, Minkowski point-to-slice maximizer on the right, driven by the same draggable handles.

### Cutting the corner of an L-shaped route → corner-cutting argument · useful · GA §50.1 p.548 (pdf 565)

Once two normal geodesics cross, the traveller can shortcut near the crossing and ride the other geodesic home, showing the first is not optimal.

**Where it breaks down:** The book does not compare the lengths of the two geodesics below the crossing, which the full argument needs.

**App idea:** Let the learner draw their own shortcut near the crossing and watch the length counter drop.

### Optical caustic of focused light rays → caustic · useful · GA §50.2 p.550 (pdf 567)

The book names the blow-up of c a caustic, an envelope of the congruence that forces its members to cross. The optical picture of light rays piling up on an envelope is our gloss; the book does not mention optics.

**Where it breaks down:** Light rays focus by refraction or reflection; here world lines focus because matter curves spacetime, and the rays are timelike.

**App idea:** Show a coffee-cup caustic beside a congruence of world lines focusing under a matter-density slider.

### Gravity as a converging lens for world lines → focusing inequality · strong · GA §50.2 p.550 (pdf 567)

The book reads the inequality as gravity being attractive and drawing world lines together along their length. The lens wording is our gloss on that reading.

**Where it breaks down:** Only true if the strong energy condition holds; dark energy acts like a diverging lens.

**App idea:** Lens slider from converging (ordinary matter) to diverging (cosmological constant) acting on a bundle of world lines.

### A sheet with a hole cut out → timelike geodesic incompleteness · useful · GA §50.3 p.551 (pdf 568)

A curve drawn on a sheet ends at a dashed excised region, conveying that the singular point is not part of spacetime and the curve cannot be continued.

**Where it breaks down:** Suggests a single point in space; the Friedmann singularity is reached by all comoving observers at the same cosmic time and is better pictured as a missing edge of the sheet.

**App idea:** Morph the hole into a torn-off bottom edge as the learner switches from a point-mass picture to a Friedmann picture.

### Stacked sheets of constant distance → proper-time function from a hypersurface · useful · GA §50.2 p.548 (pdf 565)

Level sets of phi are drawn as parallel layers with the velocity arrow crossing them, like contour layers or wavefronts.

**Where it breaks down:** Layers stay smooth only until the congruence focuses; after that phi is not single-valued.

**App idea:** Huygens-style wavefront propagation from a curved sheet showing layers cusping at the caustic.

### Running the cosmic film backwards → time reversal of the focusing argument · strong · GA §50.3 p.551 (pdf 568)

Expansion seen in reverse is contraction, so a theorem about converging world lines applies to the past of an expanding universe.

**Where it breaks down:** Reversing time changes no physics: the energy condition is time-symmetric. What must be redone is the bookkeeping, recomputing c with past-directed normals and applying the maximal-curve existence result to the past of the slice rather than its future.

**App idea:** Play/rewind button on a comoving-galaxy animation with a convergence gauge that flips sign.

### Mean square versus square of the mean → trace inequality for the velocity gradient · strong · GA §50.2 p.549 (pdf 566)

Our framing of Example 50.2: the three principal expansion rates have a sum fixed by c, and their squares are smallest when all three are equal.

**Where it breaks down:** Not stated this way in the book; requires the tensor to be symmetric and spatial.

**App idea:** Three sliders for principal rates with fixed sum; a bar for the sum of squares never dips below c^2/3.


## Misconceptions addressed

### The Big Bang singularity is merely an artefact of assuming a perfectly homogeneous and isotropic universe. · GA p.547 (pdf 564)

**Correction:** The focusing argument uses only a Cauchy slice, an energy condition and positive past convergence everywhere; no exact symmetry is required, so small inhomogeneities cannot remove the past boundary.

**Why tempting:** In Newtonian collapse, exact symmetry makes everything meet at one point, and slight asymmetry lets particles miss each other.

### Geodesics in spacetime are the shortest paths, as in ordinary geometry. · GA §50.2 p.548 (pdf 565)

**Correction:** Timelike geodesics maximize proper time; every Euclidean 'shortest' statement becomes 'longest' in spacetime.

**Why tempting:** Geodesics are introduced as straight lines, which are shortest in school geometry.

### When the convergence of a congruence becomes infinite, spacetime itself is singular there. · GA §50.2 p.550 (pdf 567)

**Correction:** Infinite convergence only means neighbouring world lines cross, forming a caustic, which happens in perfectly smooth spacetime; the singularity follows from the contradiction with the existence of maximal curves, not from the caustic.

**Why tempting:** A quantity blowing up usually signals a physical singularity.

### Since galaxies are flying apart, the focusing theorem shows gravity is not attractive in cosmology or says nothing about our Universe. · GA §50.3 p.551 (pdf 568)

**Correction:** The world lines diverge toward the future, so the theorem gives no future bound, but the same world lines converge toward the past, where the theorem applies.

**Why tempting:** The derivation assumed converging world lines, and our Universe expands.

### Positive energy density (the weak energy condition) is enough to guarantee focusing. · GA §50.2 p.550 (pdf 567)

**Correction:** Focusing needs R_{mu nu}u^mu u^nu >= 0, the strong energy condition, which for a fluid is rho + 3p >= 0; a cosmological constant has positive energy density yet defocuses. The main text labels this the weak condition; Exercise 50.3 uses the correct one.

**Why tempting:** The contracted term looks like an energy density, and the text cites the weak condition.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### The theorem proves that density and curvature become infinite at the Big Bang. · GA §50.3 p.551 (pdf 568)

**Correction:** It proves only that past timelike curves end within finite proper time (incompleteness). Infinite density is a separate fact about Friedmann solutions; in principle curves could end for other reasons.

**Why tempting:** The word singularity evokes infinities, and the Friedmann solutions do have them.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### The Big Bang happened at a single point in space, like the excised dot in the figure. · GA §50.3 p.551 (pdf 568)

**Correction:** In a Friedmann model every comoving world line reaches the singularity at the same cosmic time; it is a spacelike boundary of the whole universe, not a location within an existing space.

**Why tempting:** Fig. 50.4 shows a small hole, and 'explosion' imagery suggests a centre.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### A longest timelike curve between a surface and an event always exists. · GA p.547 (pdf 564)

**Correction:** Existence relies on global hyperbolicity (a Cauchy surface) and compactness of the space of causal curves; in spacetimes with holes or without Cauchy surfaces maximal curves can fail to exist, which is exactly what the argument exploits.

**Why tempting:** Among a family of curves it seems obvious one must be longest.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*


## Thought experiments

### Racing to a wall from beyond a crossing · GA §50.1 p.548 (pdf 565)

**Setup:** Two perpendicular routes leave a curved wall and cross at r; a traveller at q farther along one route wants the quickest way back to the wall.

**Lesson:** Beyond a crossing the straight normal route is beaten by a corner-cut combination, so normal geodesics stop being optimal after they cross.

**App idea:** Game where the learner must find the best route to a curved wall from points placed before and after a focal point.

### Following a world line backwards into a hole · GA §50.3 p.551 (pdf 568)

**Setup:** Starting on today's cosmic slice, trace any observer's history backwards in proper time.

**Lesson:** After at most 3/c0 the curve reaches a place that is not in the smooth manifold, so it cannot be continued: the Big-Bang singularity.

**App idea:** Rewind animation with a proper-time odometer that stops at the spacetime edge, next to the 1/H0 bound.

### Dust cloud with an initial squeeze · GA §50.2 p.550 (pdf 567)

**Setup:** (Our construction, not in the book.) A ball of freely falling dust starts with its world lines converging at rate c0 on some slice, in a region obeying the strong energy condition.

**Lesson:** Gravity only amplifies the squeeze: the ball's world lines must meet within proper time 3/c0, whatever its shape (shear speeds this up).

**App idea:** 3D dust ball with adjustable initial convergence, shear and matter density; timer to collapse compared with 3/c0.


## Applications and observations

- **Past singularity of our Universe** (astrophysical-system, GA §50.3 p.551 (pdf 568)): Our Universe is modelled as close to a perfect-fluid Friedmann model; with the present slice as S and time reversed, every past timelike curve must end within finite proper time, so an initial singularity is expected. Key numbers: c = -3 a-dot/a; past bound tau <= 3/c0
- **Hubble-time value of the bound (our estimate)** (numerical-estimate, GA §50.3 p.551 (pdf 568)): With past convergence c0 = 3H0 the bound is 1/H0. This is our computation, not printed in the chapter. A flat matter-only model has age 2/(3H0), comfortably inside the bound. Key numbers: H0 = 67.4 km/s/Mpc gives 1/H0 = 14.5 Gyr; H0 = 70 gives 14.0 Gyr; matter-only age 2/(3H0) = 9.7 Gyr; measured age about 13.8 Gyr (our estimates)
- **Accelerating expansion and dark energy versus the energy condition (our note)** (observation, GA §50.2 p.550 (pdf 567)): A positive cosmological constant has p = -rho, so rho + 3p = -2 rho < 0 and the strong energy condition fails; the chapter's hypothesis is therefore violated in the present dark-energy era and during inflation. The measured age still happens to satisfy t0 < 1/H0, and later theorems (e.g. Borde-Guth-Vilenkin) address past incompleteness without that energy condition. Not discussed in the book. Key numbers: t0 H0 about 0.95 for Planck-like parameters (our estimate)

## Notation and conventions

- **Metric signature:** (-,+,+,+): a unit timelike velocity has u^mu u_mu = -1. (GA §50.2 p.548 (pdf 565))
- **Units:** Geometric units G = c = 1, so the field equation carries 8 pi. — The letter c is reused for convergence; do not confuse it with the speed of light. (GA §50.2 p.550 (pdf 567))
- **Convergence c versus expansion theta:** Main text uses c = -u^mu_{;mu} (positive when focusing); the exercises use theta = u^mu_{;mu} = -c. — Most GR literature uses theta; translate signs when comparing 50.9 with 50.26. (GA §50.2 p.548 (pdf 565))
- **Order of repeated covariant derivatives:** u^alpha_{;nu mu} means differentiate with respect to nu first, then mu; with this order the commutator equals +R^alpha_{beta mu nu}u^beta (eqn 35.13). — Index order must be tracked when contracting to the Ricci tensor R_{beta nu} = R^alpha_{beta alpha nu}. (GA §50.2 p.549 (pdf 566))
- **Covariant directional derivative:** nabla_u, written D/dtau along a proper-time-parametrized curve. (GA §50.2 p.550 (pdf 567))
- **Vectors and 1-forms:** Bold letters for vectors (u), bold with tilde for 1-forms (u-tilde); index-free slot notation g( , ) in Exercise 50.1. (GA §50.2 p.548 (pdf 565))
- **Projection tensor:** P_{alpha beta} = g_{alpha beta} + u_alpha u_beta for timelike u; g - n n for unit spacelike n. — Other texts call it h_{alpha beta}. (GA §50.2 p.549 (pdf 566))
- **Congruence symbols:** phi proper time from S, S the Cauchy slice, tau proper time along a normal geodesic, c0 convergence at S; in exercises B_{mu nu} = u_{mu;nu}, sigma shear, omega rotation, W_i connecting vectors (following Zee). (GA p.552 (pdf 569))

## Gaps and pitfalls

- **The text says the weak energy condition makes R_{mu nu}u^mu u^nu non-negative, but that term is (T - (1/2)gT)uu, whose positivity is the strong energy condition (the one Exercise 50.3 correctly names).** — Learners will believe positive energy density suffices and be puzzled that a cosmological constant (positive rho) accelerates expansion. *Suggestion:* State the strong energy condition in §50.2, compute rho + 3p for dust, radiation and Lambda, and show which obey it.
- **Non-negativity of the trace-free square in Example 50.2 is asserted without saying why; it holds because that tensor is symmetric and orthogonal to u, so it lives in a positive-definite 3-space.** — In Lorentzian signature contractions can be negative, so the claim looks unjustified. *Suggestion:* Go to the rest frame of u, show the tensor has only spatial components, and write the contraction as a sum of squares.
- **The unit norm of grad phi (Gauss lemma) is taken for granted, and u_mu = grad_mu phi with u.u = -1 actually makes phi decrease along u, a sign glossed over.** — Careful readers find that dphi/dtau = u^mu u_mu = -1, apparently contradicting phi being elapsed proper time. *Suggestion:* Use u_mu = -grad_mu phi for future-directed u, and note the sign does not affect symmetry or the geodesic result.
- **The theorem needs c0 to be a positive lower bound on the convergence everywhere on S; the text treats c0 as a single number without saying so.** — It is unclear why a universe with some locally diverging regions would still be singular. *Suggestion:* Define c0 = inf over S of the convergence and note that for a near-Friedmann universe this is close to 3H0 everywhere.
- **The corner-cutting argument is given only in Euclidean space; the Lorentzian version (smoothing a kink in a timelike curve increases proper time) and the case analysis comparing the two geodesics below the crossing are skipped. Infinite convergence implying an actual crossing (focal point) is also asserted.** — The key step 'crossing implies not maximal' may feel like hand-waving in spacetime. *Suggestion:* Show the reverse triangle inequality on a Minkowski diagram, then cite the focal-point theorem from Wald or Hawking-Ellis.

## Tutor notes

- Open with: 'Friedmann models all start at a = 0, but they are perfectly smooth and symmetric. If the real Universe is lumpy, could matter have missed itself and bounced?' Let the learner guess before revealing that a symmetry-free argument exists.
- Before any tensors, check two warm-up facts: what is the shortest path from a point to a curved wall, and which of two twins ages more. The whole proof rests on combining these.
- Recommended order: (1) Euclidean normals and corner cutting; (2) longest instead of shortest; (3) proper-time function and its gradient; (4) define c and interpret its sign as volume shrinking; (5) the ODE dc/dtau >= c^2/3 and its blow-up, taken as given first; (6) the contradiction; (7) the cosmological application and time reversal; (8) only then return to Examples 50.1-50.2 for the derivation.
- Check for understanding: ask why a caustic by itself is not a singularity. Good answer: crossing world lines are allowed; the problem is that crossing destroys maximality, which the Cauchy surface demands.
- Check: ask the learner to compute c for an expanding Friedmann universe and say whether the theorem bounds the future or the past, and why.
- Numerical anchor: have the learner plug c0 = 3H0 and H0 of about 70 km/s/Mpc into 3/c0 to get about 14 Gyr, then compare with the 13.8 Gyr age.
- Correct the energy-condition slip proactively: the needed condition is rho + 3p >= 0 (strong), not rho >= 0 (weak). Ask what happens for a cosmological constant.
- For the trace inequality, use three numbers: if three principal expansion rates must add to -c, the sum of their squares is smallest when they are equal, giving c^2/3. Then show shear only adds to it.
- Common learner question: 'Does this prove the density was infinite?' Answer: it proves world lines cannot be extended to the past beyond a finite proper time; the infinities come from the explicit models and may be modified by quantum gravity.
- Common learner question: 'Where did the Big Bang happen?' Use Fig. 50.4 with care: the singularity is not a place inside space; in Friedmann models every comoving observer's past ends at the same cosmic time.
- Common learner question: 'Why does rotation matter?' Point to Exercise 50.2: rotation is the only Raychaudhuri term that resists focusing, and gradient flows have none.
- Advanced learners: connect to the Raychaudhuri equation in theta notation, to Penrose's null version for black holes, and to why inflation and dark energy evade the hypotheses.
