# GA ch49 · From classical to quantum gravity

> A guided tour of candidate routes beyond classical GR: compact extra dimensions and the Planck scale, the classical Nambu-Goto string reduced to a wave equation by gauge choices, cosmic strings as topological defects, Wheeler-style superspace, loop quantum gravity's quantized tetrahedron, anti-de Sitter spacetime and its boundary, closing with the Lambda-CDM picture of our Universe.

**Pages:** printed 527–546 · pdf 544–563 · **Difficulty:** math 3/5, conceptual 4/5, novice-friendliness 3/5

Mathematically the chapter is mostly moderate: dimensional analysis, separation of variables, vector projection, induced metrics and one careful Lagrangian reduction (the string, which is the hardest algebra and has printed slips). Conceptually it is demanding because it jumps between six research programmes in twenty pages, each with its own vocabulary (branes, supersymmetry, superspace, spin networks, holography), and most sections describe rather than derive. A novice can follow the extra-dimension, Planck-scale, tetrahedron and AdS-bounce calculations; the Einstein-Hamilton-Jacobi equation and AdS/CFT are name-checked without enough support to be understood, and Section 49.8 relies on the cosmology chapters.

## Role in the book

The penultimate chapter looks outward from the classical theory built over the whole book. It picks up the extra-dimension and gauge-field thread of the previous (Kaluza-Klein) chapter and the effective-field-theory view of gravity as non-renormalizable, then shows how tools the reader already owns (action principles, induced metrics from Appendix D, reparametrization freedom from Chapters 8-9, phase transitions from Chapter 41, maximally symmetric spacetimes from Chapters 15-19) can be pointed at open research questions. It is deliberately a survey that ends in humility: each approach is shown to be promising but untested, and the ending sets up the final chapter on the Big Bang singularity.

## Learning objectives

- Reader can solve the Schrodinger equation on a strip with one compact direction and explain why extra dimensions of small radius a only show up at energies of order hbar^2/(2 m a^2).
- Reader can build the Planck length, time and mass from G, c and hbar and convert the Planck length into an energy scale of about 10^19 GeV, comparing it with LHC and cosmic-ray energies.
- Reader can argue from Gauss's law that gravity falls off as 1/r^(d-1) in d spatial dimensions and relate the higher-dimensional coupling G^(D) to the measured G through the volume of the compact dimensions.
- Reader can explain why replacing point vertices with string world sheets softens the short-distance divergences of quantum field theory.
- Reader can write the Nambu-Goto action as tension times world-sheet area using the induced metric, identify the world-sheet momenta and state the resulting equations of motion.
- Reader can use static gauge and a velocity-perpendicular, energy-weighted choice of sigma to reduce the string equation of motion to a one-dimensional wave equation.
- Reader can describe how domain walls and vortices arise when a symmetry-breaking phase transition picks different vacua in different regions, and how stacking vortices yields a cosmic string that lenses light into double images.
- Reader can describe superspace as the space of all 3-geometries with an amplitude on each, and state the role of the Einstein-Hamilton-Jacobi equation.
- Reader can reproduce the heuristic that localizing a particle below the Planck length would form a black hole, and explain how angular-momentum-like commutators for tetrahedron face-area vectors quantize area and volume in loop quantum gravity.
- Reader can write the de Sitter and anti-de Sitter hyperboloid embeddings, contrast their signatures, topologies and curvature signs, and pass between them with alpha^2 -> -alpha^2.
- Reader can derive the conformal form of the AdS3 line element, locate the timelike boundary at psi = pi/2 (or w = 0 in Poincare coordinates), and show that massive particles turn back at w = b while light reaches the boundary.
- Reader can summarize the Lambda-CDM timeline and the measured density fractions, and list its unsolved problems (dark matter identity, dark energy, inflation, lithium-7, Hubble tension).

## Teaching approach

A survey chapter that reuses the book's own toolkit rather than importing new formalism. Each research programme is presented through one concrete, solvable calculation (a quantum particle on a cylinder, dimensional analysis for Planck units, a string action reduced to a wave equation, a tetrahedron treated as a set of angular momenta, a particle bouncing off the AdS boundary), wrapped in candid commentary about what is untested. The string section is the only sustained derivation and deliberately mirrors the point-particle action line by line; the later sections become progressively more descriptive, and the chapter ends by stepping back from speculation to the observationally anchored Lambda-CDM model.

**Style:** survey-overview, example-driven, analogy-driven, conversational-informal, derivation-heavy, spiral-revisit, diagram-driven, physical-motivation-first

**Narrative arc**

1. Motivating problem: three of the four interactions are unified as quantum gauge theories, but gravity resists; start from the previous chapter's extra-dimension idea
2. Quick side route (Coleman footnote): a tiny bit of quantum mechanics already gives gravitational redshift by rescaling time with the potential
3. Why hidden dimensions stay hidden: particle in a well with one compactified direction has new levels only at energies set by the tiny radius
4. Where the new physics lives: Planck units from dimensional analysis, the 10^19 GeV gap to accelerators
5. Consequence for gravity: Newton-Poisson law in D dimensions, force law 1/r^(d-1), G equals G^(5) divided by the compact circumference
6. String theory motivated by the divergence problem: point vertices versus smooth trouser-shaped world sheets; D-branes and closed strings
7. Formalism by analogy: point-particle length action -> world-sheet area action via the induced metric (Nambu-Goto)
8. Simplify by gauge choice: static gauge, transverse velocity, and a sigma parametrization that turns the equations into a wave equation; graviton as closed-string mode
9. Strings in relativity more broadly: supersymmetry and dark matter, M-theory's lack of falsifiable predictions, cosmic strings as vortex lines from early-Universe symmetry breaking, lensing signature
10. A conservative alternative: superspace of 3-geometries, Feynman phases and the Einstein-Hamilton-Jacobi equation, Planck-scale fuzziness of the metric
11. A radical alternative: loop quantum gravity, motivated by the mini-black-hole argument and built by quantizing tetrahedron area vectors like angular momentum
12. A geometric ingredient: de Sitter and anti-de Sitter hyperboloids, unrolling AdS, conformal coordinates reveal a boundary, Poincare coordinates show massive particles cannot reach it; holography and AdS/CFT named
13. Reality check: the Lambda-CDM timeline, measured Omega values, successes and open problems; hand-off to the Big Bang chapter

**Signature moves**

- Every speculative programme is anchored by one small calculation a reader can actually do with standard undergraduate tools
- The string is built by strict analogy with the relativistic particle: mass becomes tension, proper length becomes world-sheet area, affine reparametrization becomes gauge choice
- Gauge fixing is presented as the same trick as choosing a convenient world-line parameter earlier in the book, now exercised twice (tau and sigma)
- Quantum geometry is made concrete by mapping tetrahedron face-area vectors onto angular momentum operators, so familiar results (j(j+1) spectra, incompatible components) carry over
- de Sitter and anti-de Sitter are handled as a matched pair differing only by a sign flip of alpha^2 and an extra timelike embedding direction
- The AdS boundary is probed operationally by firing photons and massive particles at it and reading off an effective Newtonian potential
- Frequent candid caveats and humour (Coleman's warning about doing a thesis in GR, 'Or might not!') keep the speculative status of each theory explicit

## Section by section

### Chapter opening: gravity outside the Standard Model — p.527 (pdf 544)

Recalls that electromagnetism, the weak and the strong forces are combined in the Standard Model as quantum gauge theories while gravity is the holdout, and announces a tour of attempts to place gravity in the quantum world starting from extra dimensions. Two margin notes set the tone: gravity can be kept as a low-energy effective theory despite non-renormalizability, and a Coleman argument shows that inserting a gravitational potential into the quantum time-evolution operator is equivalent to rescaling time, giving gravitational redshift at once.

**Concepts:** Standard Model of particle physics, effective field theory of gravity, gravitational redshift from quantum time evolution

**Key moves**
- Gravity singled out as the one interaction not incorporated on the same footing as the others
- Effective-field caveat: non-renormalizable gravity is fine below a cutoff
- Coleman shortcut: H -> H(1+V) is the same as t -> (1+V)t, so clocks lower in the potential run slow

### §49.1 Extra dimensions — p.527 (pdf 544)

Asks why extra spatial dimensions, if they exist, have not been noticed. A particle in a box with one compactified direction shows that the new energy levels scale with the inverse square of the compact radius, so a tiny radius pushes them out of reach. Planck's natural units are built by dimensional analysis, giving lengths of 10^-35 m and energies of 10^19 GeV far beyond the LHC. The Poisson equation is then generalized to D spacetime dimensions: the force law steepens to 1/r^(d-1), and for a compact fifth dimension the measured G is the five-dimensional coupling diluted by the compact circumference, generalizing to G^(D)/G equal to the compact volume.

**Concepts:** compactified extra dimension, Kaluza-Klein tower of energy levels, Planck units, Planck energy, gravitational force law in d spatial dimensions, higher-dimensional Planck length

**Key moves**
- Separable solution on a strip with periodic y gives E_nl with an additive (l/a)^2 term; l = 0 reproduces the ordinary levels
- Small compact radius a << L means first new level sits at about hbar^2/(2 m a^2)
- Solve G, c, hbar for a length, time and mass scale
- Natural units: length inverse to energy, so probing 10^-35 m needs 10^19 GeV
- Poisson equation with dimension-dependent G^(D); Gauss-law scaling of force with r
- Integrate density over the compact circle: rho^(5) = rho^(4)/(2 pi a), so G = G^(5)/(2 pi a)

### §49.2 String theory — p.530 (pdf 547)

Introduces strings as structureless one-dimensional fundamental objects whose vibrations are the particles; strings may be open (with free ends or ends fixed on D-branes) or closed. The main selling point is that interactions are smeared over a smooth world sheet rather than concentrated at a vertex, easing the divergences of perturbative quantum field theory, and a closed string naturally contains a massless spin-2 mode. The classical theory is then built by analogy with the relativistic particle: a world sheet X^mu(tau, sigma), its induced 2x2 metric, and the Nambu-Goto action equal to minus the tension times the world-sheet area, from which world-sheet momenta and equations of motion follow.

**Concepts:** string (fundamental), open and closed strings, D-brane, world sheet, induced metric on the world sheet, Nambu-Goto action, string tension, world-sheet momentum currents, ultraviolet divergences and renormalization, graviton

**Key moves**
- Feynman vertex at a point versus smooth pair-of-pants world sheet: no single interaction point
- Recall point-particle action -m times proper length, parametrized by tau
- Promote world line to world sheet with two parameters tau and sigma
- Pull back eta_{mu nu} with the embedding to get gamma_{alpha beta} built from Xdot^2, Xdot.X', X'^2
- Action proportional to area integral of sqrt(-gamma), with tension T0 replacing mass
- Treat X^mu as fields on the (tau, sigma) plane; momenta P^tau_mu and P^sigma_mu; conservation-law form of the equations of motion

### §49.3 Parametrizing the string — p.532 (pdf 549)

Exploits the reparametrization freedom of the world sheet to simplify the equations. Static gauge sets tau equal to coordinate time, so constant-tau slices are snapshots of the string. Measuring position along the string by arc length s leads to the transverse velocity, the component of the string's velocity perpendicular to its tangent, and the action becomes a tension-weighted integral of the relativistic factor sqrt(1 - v_perp^2) along the string. Choosing sigma so that velocity is perpendicular to the string and so that ds/dsigma equals sqrt(1 - v_perp^2) collapses the equations of motion into the one-dimensional wave equation, whose modes, once quantized, would be particles including the graviton.

**Concepts:** gauge choice (world-sheet reparametrization), static gauge, transverse velocity of a string, string wave equation

**Key moves**
- Static gauge: tau = t, so dX^mu/dtau = (1, dX/dt) and dX^mu/dsigma = (0, dX/dsigma)
- Arc length s makes dX/ds a unit tangent; project velocity perpendicular to it
- Show the square-root argument equals (ds/dsigma)^2 (1 - v_perp^2)
- Action rewritten as -T0 times integral of ds sqrt(1 - v_perp^2): only transverse motion is physical
- Impose velocity orthogonal to tangent, then fix sigma by (ds/dsigma)(1 - v_perp^2)^(-1/2) = 1
- Result: d^2X/dt^2 = d^2X/dsigma^2

### §49.4 Strings in relativity — p.534 (pdf 551)

Surveys where strings meet gravitation. Superstrings are Planck-sized supersymmetric strings; supersymmetry pairs every boson with a fermion partner, and such partners are one candidate for dark matter, which is inferred only gravitationally. M-theory generalizes superstrings to branes but has not produced falsifiable predictions. Separately, cosmic strings are macroscopic defects formed in early-Universe phase transitions: a real scalar field with a double-well potential can form domain walls between regions in opposite vacua, and a complex field with a Mexican-hat potential forms vortices whose phase winds around a singular core; stacking vortex cores gives a line, the cosmic string, whose tension would split distant sources into double images by lensing. Black holes as strings is mentioned as a speculative link.

**Concepts:** superstring, supersymmetry, dark matter, M-theory, cosmic string, spontaneous symmetry breaking, domain wall, Mexican-hat potential, vortex (topological defect), gravitational lensing by a cosmic string

**Key moves**
- Supersymmetry operator swaps boson and fermion states; superpartner naming (photino, squark)
- Dark matter as possibly supersymmetric partners suppressed from interacting with ordinary matter
- Honest verdict: M-theory lacks testable predictions; Planck-scale force-law changes are unmeasurable
- Double-well cooling -> domains in +phi0 and -phi0 -> wall where the field interpolates
- Complex field: ring of degenerate minima; phase winding around a point forces a singular core
- Stack 2D vortices along a third direction to make a line defect = cosmic string
- Observable: conical space around the string produces double images

### §49.5 Superspace — p.536 (pdf 553)

Presents the view that a rigid spacetime is a classical approximation. Spacetime is sliced into spacelike 3-geometries; quantum mechanics requires enlarging this set to superspace, containing every possible 3-geometry with an amplitude on each. In a sum-over-histories picture each amplitude carries a phase set by the classical action, so the dynamics is governed by how surfaces of constant action propagate through superspace, described by the Einstein-Hamilton-Jacobi equation, which carries the same content as the Einstein field equation. Planck-scale metric fluctuations spread appreciable amplitude over many 3-geometries, limiting how far a smooth spacetime picture can be trusted. The approach is praised as conservative because it needs only the metric field.

**Concepts:** superspace (space of 3-geometries), 3+1 slicing of spacetime, sum over histories, Einstein-Hamilton-Jacobi equation, Hamilton-Jacobi equation, Planck-scale metric fluctuations

**Key moves**
- Deconstruct spacetime into stackable spacelike 3-surfaces
- Quantum version: superposition over all 3-geometries with amplitudes psi(3-geometry)
- Phase of each contribution is exp(iS/hbar); constructive interference where actions agree
- Wavefronts of constant S in superspace obey the Einstein-Hamilton-Jacobi equation
- Planck-scale fluctuations give many 3-geometries appreciable amplitude, so classical spacetime is only approximate

### §49.6 Loop quantum gravity — p.537 (pdf 554)

Motivates quantizing geometry itself. A heuristic shows that squeezing a particle into a region of size L gives it energy hbar c/L, whose horizon radius reaches L exactly at the Planck length, so sub-Planckian structure is hidden. Borrowing the logic that commutation relations cause quantization, a tetrahedron is described by four face-normal vectors whose magnitudes are face areas and which sum to zero; promoting them to operators with angular-momentum commutators scaled by a Planck-sized length squared makes each face area take discrete values proportional to sqrt(j(j+1)) and makes the orientation of faces uncertain. Volume commutes with closure, so tetrahedra carry discrete quanta of volume. Networks of such cells reduce to graphs whose closed paths are the loops. The section ends by stressing that LQG has no established semiclassical limit and no new predictions.

**Concepts:** loop quantum gravity, Planck-scale localization limit, quantum of area, quantum of volume, tetrahedron area vectors and closure, angular momentum algebra, spin network graph

**Key moves**
- Uncertainty: momentum at least hbar/L; ultra-relativistic energy at least hbar c/L
- Set horizon radius G E/c^4 equal to L: L = Planck length
- Tetrahedron encoded by four area vectors summing to zero; V^2 = (2/9) L1 x L2 . L3
- Postulate [L_a^i, L_b^j] = i delta_ab l0^2 epsilon^{ijk} L_a^k
- Carry over angular momentum results: area eigenvalues l0^2 sqrt(j(j+1)); only one Cartesian component sharp
- Joint eigenstates |j1 j2 j3 j4 v> give discrete volume
- Mesh of cells -> graph; loops are closed paths through nodes

### §49.7 Anti-de Sitter spacetime — p.539 (pdf 556)

Motivated by the quantum-mechanical particle in a box, looks for a spacetime that confines gravity. de Sitter spacetime is recalled as a hyperboloid in five-dimensional Minkowski space with positive constant curvature and topology R x S^3. Anti-de Sitter spacetime is the negative-curvature partner, a hyperboloid in a flat space with two timelike directions, topology S^1 x R^3, and closed timelike curves that are removed by unrolling the time circle. Global coordinates for AdS3 and a conformal rescaling show that the radial coordinate only reaches the equator, revealing a timelike boundary; Poincare coordinates show the boundary as flat Minkowski space of one lower dimension. Light reaches the boundary in finite coordinate time, but massive particles feel an effective repulsive potential and turn back, hinting at how bulk energy maps to a boundary scale, the germ of holography and AdS/CFT.

**Concepts:** de Sitter spacetime, anti-de Sitter spacetime, hyperboloid embedding, maximally symmetric spacetime, constant-curvature Riemann tensor, closed timelike curves, universal covering of AdS, global AdS coordinates, conformal compactification, conformal boundary of AdS, Poincare coordinates for AdS, holographic principle, AdS/CFT correspondence

**Key moves**
- dS: one-sheeted hyperboloid of radius alpha in (4+1)-dimensional Minkowski space; induced metric by eliminating the fifth coordinate
- Sphere <-> de Sitter via T^2 -> -T^2; maximal symmetry
- AdS: the analogous quadric in (3+2)-dimensional flat space with a second timelike axis U and negative right-hand side; dS <-> AdS via alpha^2 -> -alpha^2
- Time is a circle in the (T,U) plane: closed timelike loops; unroll to get R^4
- AdS3 global coordinates give -(1+r^2)dt^2 + dr^2/(1+r^2) + r^2 dtheta^2
- r = tan psi: conformal factor 1/cos^2 psi; psi only reaches pi/2 so there is a boundary
- Poincare coordinates: constant-w slices are Minkowski; boundary at w = 0
- Null ray returns in coordinate time 2 w0; massive geodesic has effective potential (b/w)^2 and turns at w = b

### §49.8 Our current best guess — p.542 (pdf 559)

Steps back to observational cosmology. COBE, WMAP and Planck measurements of the microwave background support the Lambda-CDM model: a flat Universe with about 5 per cent baryons, 26 per cent cold dark matter and 69 per cent dark energy, accelerating today. A timeline runs from inflation near 10^-32 s through hadron formation, baryon-antibaryon annihilation and baryogenesis, nucleosynthesis, recombination at 380,000 years, matter domination and dark-energy domination from about 5 Gyr ago, to an age of 13.80 Gyr. Agreement of Omega values from CMB acoustic peaks and deuterium abundance is the main reason for confidence, while lithium-7, baryogenesis, Hubble tension, the origin of inflation, and the nature of dark matter and dark energy remain open. The chapter closes by committing to the Big-Bang singularity as a robust feature, previewing the final chapter.

**Concepts:** Lambda-CDM model, cosmic microwave background, precision cosmology, density parameters, cold dark matter, dark energy, vacuum energy problem, inflation, baryogenesis, Big-Bang nucleosynthesis, recombination and last scattering, Hubble tension

**Key moves**
- CMB blackbody at 2.725 K with 10^-5 anisotropies, mapped by successive satellites
- Unpack Lambda (accelerating expansion from Type Ia supernovae) and CDM (galaxy halos, rotation curves)
- Budget: Omega_0 ~ 1 = 0.05 baryons + 0.26 CDM + 0.69 Lambda
- Zel'dovich: naive vacuum energy overshoots by about fifty orders of magnitude
- Chronological history of the Universe with key epochs
- Cross-check between CMB and deuterium as evidence; list of unresolved tensions
- Big-Bang singularity as the anchor for the final chapter

## Concepts

### Standard Model of particle physics

*solution-or-model · mention*

The quantum gauge field theory with gauge group SU(3) x SU(2) x U(1) that describes the strong, weak and electromagnetic interactions of quarks, leptons and the Higgs field; gravity is not included.

**How introduced:** Named in the opening as the great unification success of twentieth-century physics, setting up gravity as the one interaction left over.

**Prerequisites:** quantum field theory

**Notes:** The gauge group is not given in the chapter.

**Where:** GA p.527 (pdf 544)

### effective field theory of gravity

*principle · mention* · also: gravity as a low-energy effective theory

The view that general relativity, although non-renormalizable, can be quantized and used reliably for processes at energies well below a cutoff (roughly the Planck energy), above which an unknown more fundamental theory must take over.

**How introduced:** Margin note 1 recalls the previous chapter's effective-field stance to explain in what sense gravity can and cannot be fitted into the Standard Model framework.

**Prerequisites:** renormalization, Planck energy

**Where:** GA p.527 (pdf 544)

### gravitational redshift from quantum time evolution

*technique · mention* · also: Coleman's quantum argument for redshift

In a weak uniform field the rest-energy Hamiltonian acquires a potential term proportional to itself, H -> H(1+V) with V = gh (c = 1); since the evolution operator depends on the product Ht, this is equivalent to replacing t by (1+V)t, so clocks at lower potential tick more slowly.

**How introduced:** Offered in margin note 2 as a quick warm-up before the complicated material, crediting an argument of Sidney Coleman.

**Prerequisites:** gravitational redshift, time evolution operator

$$
\hat U = e^{-i\hat H t/\hbar} \to e^{-i\hat H(1+V)t/\hbar}
$$

**Notes:** Uses H = p^0 = m for a particle at rest in units with c = 1.

**Where:** GA p.527 (pdf 544)

### compactified extra dimension

*definition · developed* · also: curled-up dimension, compact dimension

A spatial direction that is periodic with a small period, e.g. identifying y with y + 2 pi a, so that space locally looks like ordinary space times a small circle (or a small compact manifold); physics is unchanged at scales much larger than a.

**How introduced:** Continues the Kaluza-Klein idea of the previous chapter; made concrete by turning a two-dimensional square well into a strip rolled into a cylinder of circumference 2 pi a.

**Prerequisites:** Kaluza-Klein theory, particle in a box

$$
(x, y) \sim (x, y + 2\pi a)
$$

**Where:** GA §49.1 p.527 (pdf 544); GA §49.1 p.528 (pdf 545)

### Kaluza-Klein tower of energy levels

*phenomenon · developed* · also: extra-dimensional excitations, Kaluza-Klein modes

For a quantum particle able to move around a compact circle of radius a, momentum around the circle is quantized in units of hbar/a, adding a ladder of energies proportional to l^2 hbar^2/(2 m a^2); the l = 0 states reproduce lower-dimensional physics and the first new state lies at an energy set by the tiny radius.

**How introduced:** Example 49.1 solves the Schrodinger equation on the rolled-up well and reads off which levels are new.

**Prerequisites:** compactified extra dimension, particle in a box

$$
E_{nl} = \frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\left[\left(\frac{n\pi}{L}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{l}{a}\right)^2\right]
$$

**Notes:** The book does not use the name Kaluza-Klein tower.

**Where:** GA §49.1 p.528 (pdf 545)

### Planck units

*convention · developed* · also: Planck scale, natural units of Planck

The unique length, time and mass built from G, c and hbar: l_P = sqrt(G hbar/c^3) ~ 1.6e-35 m, t_P = sqrt(G hbar/c^5) ~ 5.4e-44 s, m_P = sqrt(hbar c/G) ~ 2.2e-8 kg; they mark where quantum effects and gravity are expected to be simultaneously important.

**How introduced:** Historical framing (Descartes' wish for natural units, Planck's realization) followed by Example 49.2, which inverts the dimensional relations of the three constants.

**Prerequisites:** dimensional analysis

$$
\ell_P = (G\hbar/c^3)^{1/2}
$$

$$
t_P = (G\hbar/c^5)^{1/2}
$$

$$
m_P = (\hbar c/G)^{1/2}
$$

**Notes:** The printed dimensional relation for G in eqn 49.5 is missing a power of length (see gaps).

**Where:** GA §49.1 p.528 (pdf 545); GA §49.1 p.529 (pdf 546)

### Planck energy

*physical-quantity · introduced* · also: Planck energy scale

E_P = m_P c^2 ~ 1.22e19 GeV, the energy needed to probe distances of order the Planck length; it exceeds LHC beam energies by about fifteen orders of magnitude and the most energetic cosmic rays by seven or eight.

**How introduced:** Obtained in natural units by noting length is inverse to energy, with margin notes giving the direct m_P c^2 calculation and comparisons with the LHC and cosmic rays.

**Prerequisites:** Planck units, natural units

$$
E_P = m_P c^2 \approx 1.22\times 10^{19}\,\mathrm{GeV}
$$

**Where:** GA §49.1 p.529 (pdf 546)

### gravitational force law in d spatial dimensions

*law · introduced* · also: higher-dimensional Newtonian gravity, D-dimensional Poisson equation

With d spatial dimensions Newtonian gravity obeys a Poisson equation with a dimension-dependent coupling G^(D) and a d-dimensional mass density; Gauss's law spreads flux over a (d-1)-sphere, so the force falls off as 1/r^(d-1).

**How introduced:** Generalizes the familiar Poisson equation by asking what extra dimensions would do to gravity, then uses dimensional reasoning to get the force law.

**Prerequisites:** Poisson equation for gravity, Gauss's law

$$
\nabla^2\Phi^{(D)} = 4\pi G^{(D)}\rho
$$

$$
F \propto 1/r^{d-1},\ d = D-1
$$

**Notes:** The book says density is mass divided by the 'number of spatial dimensions'; it means mass per unit d-dimensional volume.

**Where:** GA §49.1 p.529 (pdf 546)

### relation between higher-dimensional and observed gravitational constants

*law · introduced* · also: dilution of gravity by compact volume

**How introduced:** Example 49.3 averages a five-dimensional density over the compact circle to reproduce the four-dimensional Poisson equation, then generalizes.

$$
\frac{G^{(D)}}{G} = \ell_c^{D-4} = V_c

**Notes:** This is the mechanism behind large-extra-dimension models in which the true gravity scale is much lower than the four-dimensional Planck scale; the book does not pursue that.

### Nambu-Goto action

*law · core* · also: Nambu-Goto string action, area action

The action for a relativistic string equal to minus the string tension times the proper area of its world sheet, S = -T0 integral dtau dsigma sqrt(-gamma), with gamma the determinant of the induced metric; it is invariant under reparametrizations of (tau, sigma).

**How introduced:** Constructed by analogy: a particle's action is -m times proper length of the world line, so a string's is -T0 times proper area of the world sheet.

**Prerequisites:** induced metric on the world sheet, relativistic point-particle action, string tension

$$
S = -T_0\int d\tau\, d\sigma\, \sqrt{(\dot X\cdot X')^2 - \dot X^2 X'^2}

### string wave equation

*law · introduced*

### constant-curvature Riemann tensor

*identity · revisited*

For a maximally symmetric space the full Riemann tensor is fixed by one constant: K = +1/alpha^2 for de Sitter and K = -1/alpha^2 for anti-de Sitter.

**How introduced:** Recalled in margin notes 25 and 26 from Chapters 15-16.

**Prerequisites:** Riemann curvature tensor

$$
R_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta} = \pm\frac{1}{\alpha^2}(g_{\mu\alpha}g_{\nu\beta} - g_{\mu\beta}g_{\nu\alpha})
$$

**Where:** GA §49.7 p.540 (pdf 557); GA §49.7 p.541 (pdf 558)

### closed timelike curves

*phenomenon · mention* · also: closed timelike loops

Future-directed timelike curves that return to their starting event, allowing an observer to meet their own past; they violate causality and are usually regarded as unphysical.

**How introduced:** Pointed out for the AdS hyperboloid, where global time runs around a circle in the (T, U) plane.

**Prerequisites:** anti-de Sitter spacetime

**Where:** GA §49.7 p.541 (pdf 558)

### universal covering of AdS

*technique · mention* · also: unrolled AdS

Removing AdS's closed timelike curves by letting the periodic global time coordinate run over the whole real line, which changes the topology from S^1 x R^3 to R^4 while leaving the local geometry untouched.

**How introduced:** Described informally as cutting the tube-shaped hyperboloid and unrolling it.

**Prerequisites:** closed timelike curves, anti-de Sitter spacetime

**Where:** GA §49.7 p.541 (pdf 558)

### global AdS coordinates

*convention · introduced*

For AdS3 (alpha = 1), T = sqrt(1+r^2) cos t, U = sqrt(1+r^2) sin t, X = r cos theta, Y = r sin theta, giving ds^2 = -(1+r^2) dt^2 + dr^2/(1+r^2) + r^2 dtheta^2.

**How introduced:** Given as the standard coordinates that make the unrolling explicit.

**Prerequisites:** anti-de Sitter spacetime

$$
ds^2 = -(1+r^2)dt^2 + \frac{dr^2}{1+r^2} + r^2 d\theta^2
$$

**Where:** GA §49.7 p.541 (pdf 558)

### conformal compactification

*technique · revisited* · also: conformal rescaling

Rewriting a metric as an overall positive factor times a simpler metric whose coordinates have finite range, so that infinity is brought to a finite coordinate location while light-cone structure is preserved.

**How introduced:** Revisited from Chapter 19 in Example 49.11 by substituting r = tan psi.

**Prerequisites:** conformal transformation

$$
ds^2 = \frac{1}{\cos^2\psi}(-dt^2 + d\psi^2 + \sin^2\psi\, d\theta^2)
$$

**Where:** GA §49.7 p.541 (pdf 558)

### conformal boundary of AdS

*phenomenon · core* · also: AdS boundary

The timelike surface at conformal latitude psi = pi/2 (r -> infinity), or w = 0 in Poincare coordinates, which light can reach in finite coordinate time and which is itself a flat spacetime of one lower dimension; AdS behaves like a box with reflecting walls.

**How introduced:** Discovered by noting that the latitude-like coordinate only reaches the equator, then visualized in Poincare coordinates (Fig. 49.13a).

**Prerequisites:** conformal compactification, anti-de Sitter spacetime

$$
\psi \in [0, \pi/2)
$$

**Where:** GA §49.7 p.541 (pdf 558); GA §49.7 p.542 (pdf 559)

### Poincare coordinates for AdS

*convention · developed* · also: Poincare patch

Coordinates in which AdS takes the form ds^2 = (-dt^2 + dx^2 + dw^2)/w^2, so each constant-w slice is conformal to Minkowski space and the boundary lies at w = 0; they cover only part of global AdS.

**How introduced:** Introduced to examine the boundary, with a pointer to the Poincare half-plane line element met earlier and a derivation in Exercise 49.12.

**Prerequisites:** anti-de Sitter spacetime

$$
ds^2 = \frac{1}{w^2}(-dt^2 + dx^2 + dw^2)
$$

**Where:** GA §49.7 p.542 (pdf 559); GA p.546 (pdf 563)

### holographic principle

*principle · mention* · also: holography

The proposal, associated with 't Hooft, that the physics of a gravitating region of d dimensions can be fully encoded by degrees of freedom on its (d-1)-dimensional boundary, as a hologram stores a 3D image on a 2D plate.

**How introduced:** Margin note 23, with AdS given as the vivid example; the massive-particle example hints how bulk depth encodes energy.

**Prerequisites:** conformal boundary of AdS

**Where:** GA §49.7 p.540 (pdf 557); GA §49.7 p.542 (pdf 559)

### AdS/CFT correspondence

*principle · mention* · also: gauge/gravity duality, Maldacena duality

Maldacena's conjecture that certain quantum gravity (string) theories on (d+1)-dimensional AdS are exactly equivalent to conformal field theories without gravity living on the d-dimensional boundary; the radial bulk direction corresponds to energy scale in the boundary theory.

**How introduced:** Named in the text and margin note 24 as the reason AdS attracts interest; the w = b turning point is offered as a hint of energy-to-depth mapping.

**Prerequisites:** holographic principle, anti-de Sitter spacetime

**Notes:** The margin note says it was first proposed for 'spin theory'; string theory is meant.

**Where:** GA §49.7 p.540 (pdf 557)

### Lambda-CDM model

*solution-or-model · introduced* · also: LCDM, standard model of cosmology, concordance model

The Big-Bang cosmological model with a cosmological constant (dark energy) and cold dark matter plus baryons and radiation, spatially flat to high precision, fitting CMB, supernova and nucleosynthesis data with Omega_B ~ 0.05, Omega_M ~ 0.26, Omega_Lambda ~ 0.69 and age 13.80 Gyr.

**How introduced:** Posed as the answer to which model describes our Universe, unpacked term by term, then told as a history and critically assessed.

### cosmic microwave background

Nearly isotropic blackbody radiation at 2.725 K released when the Universe became transparent about 380,000 years after the Big Bang; its 10^-5 temperature fluctuations encode cosmological parameters through acoustic oscillations.

The smooth component responsible for the present accelerating expansion, consistent with a cosmological constant; it is uniform in space, has very low density, but dominates the energy budget at about 69 per cent.

Naive estimates of the quantum vacuum energy density, which would act as a cosmological constant, exceed the observed dark-energy density by enormous factors, so why Lambda is so small is unexplained.

### inflation

*solution-or-model · mention* · also: cosmic inflation

An early phase of near-exponential expansion driven by a scalar field's potential energy, which flattens the Universe, seeds nearly scale-invariant fluctuations and gravitational waves, and ends by converting field energy into particles (reheating).

### Big-Bang nucleosynthesis

Formation of the light nuclei (mostly helium-4, with deuterium, helium-3 and lithium-7) during the first minutes; its predicted abundances depend on the baryon density and cross-check the CMB.

### recombination and last scattering

The disagreement between values of the Hubble constant inferred from the early Universe (about 67 km/s/Mpc) and measured locally (about 74 km/s/Mpc), unresolved at the time of writing.

## Key equations

### (49.1) Gravity as a time rescaling (Coleman) · supporting · GA p.527 (pdf 544)

$$
\hat U = e^{-i\hat H t/\hbar} \to e^{-i\hat H(1+V)t/\hbar}
$$

Adding the potential energy HV to the rest-energy Hamiltonian is the same as stretching the time parameter by (1+V), which is gravitational time dilation.

**Symbols:** H Hamiltonian (rest energy m, c = 1); V = gh gravitational potential; t time

### (49.2) Schrodinger equation on the 2D well · derivation-step · GA §49.1 p.528 (pdf 545)

$$
-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\left(\frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial y^2}\right) = E\psi
$$

Free-particle equation inside a well of length L in x with y periodic.

**Symbols:** psi wavefunction; m mass; E energy

### (49.3) Eigenfunctions · derivation-step · GA §49.1 p.528 (pdf 545)

$$
\psi = a_n\sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)\left[b_l\sin\left(\frac{ly}{a}\right) + c_l\cos\left(\frac{ly}{a}\right)\right]
$$

Separable solutions: standing wave in x times a periodic function around the compact circle.

**Symbols:** n, l integers; a compactification radius; L well length

### (49.4) Energy levels with a compact dimension · central · GA §49.1 p.528 (pdf 545)

$$
E_{nl} = \frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\left[\left(\frac{n\pi}{L}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{l}{a}\right)^2\right]
$$

l = 0 gives the usual 1D levels; any excitation around the circle costs at least hbar^2/(2ma^2), huge when a is tiny.

**Symbols:** as above

### (49.5) Constants in Planck units · derivation-step · GA §49.1 p.528 (pdf 545)

$$
G = \frac{\ell_P^3}{m_P t_P^2},\quad c = \frac{\ell_P}{t_P},\quad \hbar = \frac{m_P\ell_P^2}{t_P}
$$

Dimensional relations that, solved for the three scales, give the Planck units.

**Symbols:** l_P, t_P, m_P Planck length, time, mass

### (49.6) Planck units · central · GA §49.1 p.529 (pdf 546)

$$
\ell_P = \left(\frac{G\hbar}{c^3}\right)^{1/2} = 1.616\times10^{-35}\,\mathrm{m},\quad t_P = \left(\frac{G\hbar}{c^5}\right)^{1/2} = 5.391\times10^{-44}\,\mathrm{s},\quad m_P = \left(\frac{\hbar c}{G}\right)^{1/2} = 2.176\times10^{-8}\,\mathrm{kg}
$$

The natural scales where quantum mechanics and gravity meet.

**Symbols:** G Newton's constant; hbar reduced Planck constant; c speed of light

### (49.7) Poisson equation, 3+1 dimensions · supporting · GA §49.1 p.529 (pdf 546)

$$
\nabla^2\Phi^{(4)} = 4\pi G\rho

Newtonian gravity in ordinary spacetime.

**Symbols:** Phi^(D) potential in D spacetime dimensions; rho mass density

### (49.8) Poisson equation, D dimensions · supporting · GA §49.1 p.529 (pdf 546)

$$
\nabla^2\Phi^{(D)} = 4\pi G^{(D)}\rho

Same form with a dimension-dependent coupling; implies a force falling as 1/r^(d-1).

**Symbols:** G^(D) D-dimensional gravitational coupling; d = D - 1

### (49.9) Planck length in D dimensions · supporting · GA §49.1 p.529 (pdf 546)

$$
\left(\ell_P^{(D)}\right)^{D-2} = \frac{\hbar G^{(D)}}{c^3} = \ell_P^2\frac{G^{(D)}}{G}

The fundamental length when the gravitational coupling is the higher-dimensional one.

**Symbols:** l_P^(D) D-dimensional Planck length

### (49.10) Five-dimensional Poisson equation with a compact circle · derivation-step · GA §49.1 p.529 (pdf 546)

$$
\nabla^2\Phi^{(5)} = 4\pi G^{(5)}\rho^{(5)} = 4\pi\frac{G^{(5)}}{2\pi a}\rho^{(4)}

Mass spread uniformly around the circle has 5D density equal to the 4D density divided by the circumference.

**Symbols:** a radius of compact dimension

### (49.11) Measured G from 5D gravity · supporting · GA §49.1 p.529 (pdf 546)

\frac{G^{(5)}}{G} = 2\pi a = \ell_c

Observed gravity is weakened relative to the fundamental coupling by the size of the hidden dimension.

**Symbols:** l_c characteristic compact length

\frac{G^{(D)}}{G} = \ell_c^{D-4} = V_c

The ratio of couplings equals the volume of the compact dimensions.

S = -m\int d\tau\left(-g_{\mu\nu}\frac{dx^\mu}{d\tau}\frac{dx^\nu}{d\tau}\right)^{1/2}

Shorthand for world-sheet derivatives.

Recalled from Chapter 40 in margin note 11.

### (49.21)-(49.22) Momentum-like variables and Euler-Lagrange for X^mu · supporting · GA §49.2 p.532 (pdf 549)

\mathcal P^\nu_\mu = \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial X^\mu_{,\nu}},\qquad \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial X^\mu} = (\mathcal P^\nu_\mu)_{,\nu}

Each world-sheet direction nu = tau, sigma gets its own momentum current; X^mu does not appear undifferentiated, so the currents are conserved.

**Symbols:** nu world-sheet index; mu spacetime index

### (49.23)-(49.24) World-sheet momenta · derivation-step · GA §49.2 p.532 (pdf 549)

P^\tau_\mu = -\frac{T_0}{\sqrt{-\gamma}}\left[(\dot X\cdot X')X'_\mu - X'^2\dot X_\mu\right],\qquad P^\sigma_\mu = -\frac{T_0}{\sqrt{-\gamma}}\left[(\dot X\cdot X')\dot X_\mu - \dot X^2 X'_\mu\right]

Derivatives of the Lagrangian density with respect to Xdot and X'. The book writes the prefactor as -T0/L; differentiating the square root actually gives -T0/sqrt(-gamma), equivalently T0^2/L.

Conservation of world-sheet momentum currents; nonlinear in general.

### (49.47) Induced de Sitter line element · supporting · GA §49.7 p.540 (pdf 557)

$$
ds^2 = \left(\eta_{\mu\nu} - \frac{\eta_{\mu\alpha}\eta_{\nu\beta}X^\alpha X^\beta}{\eta_{\rho\sigma}X^\rho X^\sigma - \alpha^2}\right)dX^\mu dX^\nu
$$

Result of eliminating W; indices run 0-3. The printed equation omits the differentials dX^mu dX^nu and reuses mu nu as dummy indices in the denominator.

### (49.48) Constant-curvature Riemann tensor · supporting · GA §49.7 p.540 (pdf 557)

$$
R_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta} = K(g_{\mu\alpha}g_{\nu\beta} - g_{\mu\beta}g_{\nu\alpha}),\quad K = \alpha^{-2}\ \text{(de Sitter)}
$$

Maximal symmetry fixes curvature up to one constant.

**Symbols:** K sectional curvature

### (49.49) AdS Riemann tensor · supporting · GA §49.7 p.541 (pdf 558)

$$
R_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta} = -\frac{1}{\alpha^2}(g_{\mu\alpha}g_{\nu\beta} - g_{\mu\beta}g_{\nu\alpha})
$$

AdS has constant negative curvature.

### (49.50)-(49.51) AdS hyperboloid · central · GA §49.7 p.541 (pdf 558)

$$
-T^2 + X^2 + Y^2 + Z^2 - U^2 = -\alpha^2,\qquad ds^2 = -dT^2 + dX^2 + dY^2 + dZ^2 - dU^2
$$

AdS as a hyperboloid in (3+2)-dimensional flat space with two timelike directions.

**Symbols:** U second timelike embedding coordinate

### (49.52) Induced AdS line element · supporting · GA §49.7 p.541 (pdf 558)

$$
ds^2 = \left(\eta_{\mu\nu} - \frac{\eta_{\mu\alpha}\eta_{\nu\beta}X^\alpha X^\beta}{\eta_{\rho\sigma}X^\rho X^\sigma + \alpha^2}\right)dX^\mu dX^\nu
$$

Obtained from the de Sitter result by alpha^2 -> -alpha^2. The printed version omits the differentials.

### (49.53)-(49.54) Global AdS3 coordinates and metric · central · GA §49.7 p.541 (pdf 558)

$$
T = \sqrt{1+r^2}\cos t,\ U = \sqrt{1+r^2}\sin t,\ X = r\cos\theta,\ Y = r\sin\theta;\qquad ds^2 = -(1+r^2)dt^2 + \frac{dr^2}{1+r^2} + r^2 d\theta^2
$$

Coordinates on the AdS3 hyperboloid (alpha = 1); t is periodic on the hyperboloid and unrolled in the covering space.

### (49.55) Conformal AdS3 metric · central · GA §49.7 p.541 (pdf 558)

$$
ds^2 = \frac{1}{\cos^2\psi}\left(-dt^2 + d\psi^2 + \sin^2\psi\, d\theta^2\right),\quad r = \tan\psi
$$

AdS is conformal to half of a static cylinder; psi only reaches pi/2, revealing a boundary.

**Symbols:** psi latitude-like coordinate

### (49.56) Poincare AdS metric · central · GA §49.7 p.542 (pdf 559)

$$
ds^2 = \frac{1}{w^2}\left(-dt^2 + dx^2 + dw^2\right)
$$

Constant-w slices are flat Minkowski space up to scale; boundary at w = 0.

**Symbols:** w radial Poincare coordinate

### (49.57) Normalization of massive geodesic · derivation-step · GA §49.7 p.542 (pdf 559)

$$
\dot t^2 - \dot w^2 = w^2
$$

u.u = -1 for motion in the (t, w) plane of Poincare AdS; dots are proper-time derivatives.

### (49.58) Conserved energy · derivation-step · GA §49.7 p.542 (pdf 559)

$$
-\frac{1}{w^2}\frac{dt}{d\tau} = \text{const.},\qquad \dot t = \frac{w^2}{b}
$$

Time-translation Killing vector gives a conserved u_t; b is a constant length set by initial conditions. The reading copy loses the dot, printing t = w^2/b.

**Symbols:** b turning-point parameter

### (49.59) Effective radial motion · central · GA §49.7 p.542 (pdf 559)

$$
\left(\frac{\partial w}{\partial t}\right)^2 + \frac{b^2}{w^2} = 1
$$

Kinetic plus potential energy (b/w)^2 is constant; the diverging potential turns massive particles back at w = b before the boundary.

\Omega_0 = 0.999(2),\quad \Omega_B = 0.05,\quad \Omega_M = 0.26,\quad \Omega_\Lambda = 0.69

Measured composition of the Universe in units of the critical density.

### (49.60) Transverse speed squared (Exercise 49.4) · derivation-step · GA p.545 (pdf 562)

$$
v_\perp^2 = \left(\frac{\partial\vec X}{\partial t}\right)^2 - \left(\frac{\partial\vec X}{\partial s}\cdot\frac{\partial\vec X}{\partial t}\right)^2
$$

Pythagoras for the velocity split along and across the string.

### (49.63)-(49.64) Cosmic string interior and exterior metrics (Exercises 49.7-49.8) · supporting · GA p.545 (pdf 562)

$$
ds^2_{\rm in} = -dt^2 + r_0^2(d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\, d\phi^2) + dz^2,\qquad ds^2_{\rm out} = -dt^2 + r_0^2\left(\frac{\cos^2\theta}{\cos^2\theta_m}d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\, d\phi^2\right) + dz^2
$$

Interior is a spherical cap cross-section; exterior is locally flat but conical, with a circumference deficit.

**Symbols:** r0 constant radius; theta_m edge of string core

### (49.65)-(49.66) Tetrahedron volume (Exercise 49.10) · derivation-step · GA p.546 (pdf 563)

V = \tfrac16|\vec a\times\vec b\cdot\vec c|,\qquad V^2 = \tfrac29|\vec L_1\times\vec L_2\cdot\vec L_3|

Links the edge-vector and area-vector descriptions of a tetrahedron.

**Symbols:** a, b, c edge vectors from one vertex

### (49.67)-(49.70) Poincare embedding of AdS3 (Exercise 49.12) · derivation-step · GA p.546 (pdf 563)

$$
T = \frac{t}{w},\ X = \frac{x}{w},\ Y = \frac{x^2 - t^2 + w^2 - 1}{2w},\ W = \frac{x^2 - t^2 + w^2 + 1}{2w}

Explicit map from Poincare coordinates to the AdS3 hyperboloid (T^2 - X^2) + (W^2 - Y^2) = 1.

## Worked examples

### Example 49.1 · intro · GA §49.1 p.528 (pdf 545)

**Problem:** Find the energy levels of a particle in a well of length L whose second direction is rolled into a circle of circumference 2 pi a, and decide when the extra dimension would be noticed.

**Method:** Separate variables in the 2D Schrodinger equation: sine standing waves in x satisfy the walls, sines and cosines of ly/a satisfy periodicity in y; add the two kinetic contributions.

**Key insight:** The l = 0 sector is exactly the familiar 1D problem; anything that probes the extra dimension costs an energy set by 1/a^2, so a tiny circle is invisible at low energy.

**Result:** E_nl = (hbar^2/2m)[(n pi/L)^2 + (l/a)^2]; first new levels near hbar^2/(2 m a^2) when a << L.

**Concepts:** compactified extra dimension, Kaluza-Klein tower of energy levels

### Example 49.2 · intro · GA §49.1 p.528 (pdf 545)

**Problem:** Build natural units of length, time and mass from G, c and hbar.

**Method:** Write each constant's dimensions in terms of unknown scales l_P, m_P, t_P and solve the three simultaneous relations.

**Key insight:** Three constants with independent dimensions fix a unique set of units, and the numbers show quantum gravity lives at 10^-35 m.

**Result:** l_P = 1.616e-35 m, t_P = 5.391e-44 s, m_P = 2.176e-8 kg.

**Concepts:** Planck units

### Example 49.3 · intro · GA §49.1 p.529 (pdf 546)

**Problem:** If there are five dimensions with one compact circle of radius a, how is the measured G related to the fundamental five-dimensional coupling?

**Method:** Write the 5D Poisson equation, note that mass smeared around the circle has 5D density equal to the 4D density over 2 pi a, and match to the 4D Poisson equation.

**Key insight:** Gravity looks weak to us partly because its flux leaks into the compact volume; the ratio of couplings is that volume.

**Result:** G = G^(5)/(2 pi a), generalizing to G^(D)/G = l_c^(D-4).

**Concepts:** relation between higher-dimensional and observed gravitational constants, gravitational force law in d spatial dimensions

### Example 49.4 · intro · GA §49.2 p.530 (pdf 547)

**Problem:** Why do strings help with the infinities of quantum field theory?

**Method:** Qualitative comparison of a Feynman vertex, where fields meet at one point, with a closed-string splitting, where the world sheet is smooth everywhere.

**Key insight:** Divergences come from evaluating fields at coincident points; a smooth world sheet has no such point, and renormalization cannot rescue perturbative quantum gravity anyway.

**Concepts:** ultraviolet divergences and renormalization, world sheet, open and closed strings

### Example 49.5 · standard · GA §49.2 p.532 (pdf 549)

**Problem:** Derive the equations of motion from the Nambu-Goto Lagrangian density.

**Method:** Treat X^mu(tau, sigma) as four fields on a two-dimensional parameter space; define momentum densities as derivatives of L with respect to Xdot and X'; apply the field Euler-Lagrange equation, noting L has no explicit X dependence.

**Key insight:** The string dynamics is a conservation law for two momentum currents on the world sheet, exactly as field theory would have it.

**Result:** d P^tau_mu/d tau + d P^sigma_mu/d sigma = 0 with P^tau, P^sigma as in eqns 49.23-49.24.

**Concepts:** world-sheet momentum currents, Nambu-Goto action

### Example 49.6 · standard · GA §49.3 p.533 (pdf 550)

**Problem:** In static gauge, rewrite the Nambu-Goto integrand in terms of physically meaningful velocity.

**Method:** Introduce arc length s and the unit tangent dX/ds; project the coordinate velocity perpendicular to it; substitute the static-gauge components of Xdot and X' into the square-root argument and complete the algebra.

**Key insight:** Sliding along the string is unphysical; only the transverse speed matters, and each bit of string contributes like a relativistic particle with Lagrangian -T0 ds sqrt(1 - v_perp^2).

**Result:** (Xdot.X')^2 - Xdot^2 X'^2 = (ds/dsigma)^2 (1 - v_perp^2) and S = -T0 integral dt dsigma (ds/dsigma) sqrt(1 - v_perp^2).

**Concepts:** transverse velocity of a string, static gauge

### Example 49.7 · challenging · GA §49.3 p.534 (pdf 551)

**Problem:** Choose the sigma parametrization so the string equation of motion becomes as simple as possible.

**Method:** Impose that velocity be perpendicular to the string so v_perp is the full velocity; evaluate the momenta in this gauge; substitute into the Euler-Lagrange equation to get an intermediate equation; then fix sigma so ds/dsigma equals sqrt(1 - v_perp^2).

**Key insight:** Two successive gauge choices remove all the nonlinear prefactors, leaving the linear wave equation: the string's shape propagates as waves at the speed of light along the energy-weighted parameter.

**Result:** d^2X/dt^2 = d^2X/dsigma^2.

**Concepts:** string wave equation, gauge choice (world-sheet reparametrization)

### Example 49.8 · intro · GA §49.4 p.534 (pdf 551)

**Problem:** Could supersymmetry explain dark matter?

**Method:** Describe dark matter's purely gravitational evidence, introduce the supersymmetry operator that swaps bosons and fermions, name superpartners, and suggest they might be the dark matter if selection rules suppress their interactions.

**Key insight:** A symmetry that doubles the particle spectrum automatically supplies candidate invisible matter, but there is no experimental evidence for it.

**Concepts:** supersymmetry, dark matter

### Example 49.9 · standard · GA §49.4 p.535 (pdf 552)

**Problem:** How do cosmic strings form in the early Universe?

**Method:** Start with a real scalar whose potential becomes a double well on cooling; regions choose different minima, leaving walls. Move to a complex field with a Mexican-hat potential, where regions choose different phases and the phase can wind around a point, giving a vortex; stack vortices along a third direction.

**Key insight:** Topology of the vacuum manifold (two points versus a circle) decides which defects exist; a circle of vacua gives line-like defects in 3D.

**Result:** A cosmic string is the line through stacked vortex cores, where the phase gradient diverges.

**Concepts:** domain wall, Mexican-hat potential, vortex (topological defect), cosmic string

### Example 49.10 · challenging · GA §49.5 p.537 (pdf 554)

**Problem:** What governs the dynamics of quantum gravity in the superspace picture?

**Method:** Assign each 3-geometry a phase exp(iS/hbar) by analogy with Feynman's sum over histories; constructive interference singles out where action wavefronts line up; the propagation of those wavefronts is governed by the Einstein-Hamilton-Jacobi equation.

**Key insight:** The classical Hamilton-Jacobi form of GR already describes wavefronts in superspace, so the semiclassical quantum theory can be organized around it.

**Result:** Einstein-Hamilton-Jacobi equation (49.40), equivalent in content to Einstein's field equation.

**Concepts:** superspace (space of 3-geometries), Einstein-Hamilton-Jacobi equation, sum over histories

### Example · intro · GA §49.6 p.537 (pdf 554)

**Problem:** Estimate the smallest region in which a particle can be localized before gravity hides it.

**Method:** Uncertainty gives momentum at least hbar/L, so ultra-relativistic energy at least hbar c/L; treat that energy as mass and set its Schwarzschild radius equal to L.

**Key insight:** Quantum mechanics and gravity together forbid resolving distances below the Planck length, motivating discrete geometry.

**Result:** L ~ sqrt(G hbar/c^3) = l_P.

**Concepts:** Planck-scale localization limit, Planck units

### Example · standard · GA §49.6 p.538 (pdf 555)

**Problem:** Quantize a tetrahedron of space.

**Method:** Describe the tetrahedron by four area vectors obeying closure; postulate angular-momentum commutators for their components with a Planck-scale constant; import the angular momentum spectrum and incompatibility results; note volume commutes with areas and closure.

**Key insight:** Once geometry is described by vectors obeying the SU(2) algebra, area and volume quantization follow with no new mathematics.

**Result:** A_a = l0^2 sqrt(j_a(j_a+1)); states |j1 j2 j3 j4 v> with discrete volume.

**Concepts:** quantum of area, quantum of volume, tetrahedron area vectors and closure, angular momentum algebra

### Example · standard · GA §49.7 p.540 (pdf 557)

**Problem:** Obtain the metrics induced on the de Sitter and anti-de Sitter hyperboloids, and give AdS3 in global coordinates.

**Method:** Use the surface equation to eliminate one embedding coordinate and substitute its differential into the flat embedding metric; then parametrize the AdS3 hyperboloid with hyperbolic-circular coordinates.

**Key insight:** dS and AdS differ by one sign, alpha^2 -> -alpha^2, and a second time direction in the embedding; global coordinates expose AdS's periodic time.

**Result:** Eqns 49.47, 49.52 and ds^2 = -(1+r^2)dt^2 + dr^2/(1+r^2) + r^2 dtheta^2.

**Concepts:** hyperboloid embedding, de Sitter spacetime, anti-de Sitter spacetime, global AdS coordinates

### Example 49.11 · standard · GA §49.7 p.541 (pdf 558)

**Problem:** Put the AdS3 line element in conformal form and identify its boundary.

**Method:** Substitute r = tan psi so that dr^2/(1+r^2) = sec^2 psi dpsi^2 and 1 + r^2 = sec^2 psi, then factor out sec^2 psi.

**Key insight:** Infinite radial distance maps to psi = pi/2, only halfway round the would-be sphere, so conformally AdS is a solid cylinder with a timelike boundary.

**Result:** ds^2 = (1/cos^2 psi)(-dt^2 + dpsi^2 + sin^2 psi dtheta^2), with 0 <= psi < pi/2.

**Concepts:** conformal compactification, conformal boundary of AdS

### Example 49.12 · standard · GA §49.7 p.542 (pdf 559)

**Problem:** Send a light ray and a massive particle toward the AdS boundary in Poincare coordinates and see what happens.

**Method:** For light, the metric is conformally flat so null rays move at unit coordinate speed. For a massive particle, combine the normalization of the 4-velocity with the conserved quantity from the time-translation Killing vector, eliminate proper time, and read the result as an energy equation.

**Key insight:** The AdS geometry acts as a confining box: light reaches the boundary in finite coordinate time, whereas massive particles meet an infinite effective potential (b/w)^2 and turn back.

**Result:** Light returns after coordinate time 2 w0 (with a mirror at w = 0); massive particles obey (dw/dt)^2 + b^2/w^2 = 1 and turn at w = b.

**Concepts:** conformal boundary of AdS, Poincare coordinates for AdS, holographic principle


## Analogies and intuitions

### A square well rolled into a thin cylinder (a tutor might add the garden-hose image: a line from far away, a tube up close) → compactified extra dimension · strong · GA §49.1 p.528 (pdf 545)

The book's device is the rolled-up well of Fig. 49.1, which makes a hidden periodic direction concrete. The quantum energy levels show at what scale the tube becomes visible. The garden-hose image is not in the book.

**Where it breaks down:** Real compactifications may involve curved, multi-dimensional internal spaces with nontrivial topology, not a single flat circle.

**App idea:** Zoom-out animation where a tube becomes a line, synchronized with the energy ladder losing its l > 0 rungs off the top of the chart.

### A string is to a world sheet as a particle is to a world line; tension is to area as mass is to proper length → Nambu-Goto action · strong · GA §49.2 p.531 (pdf 548)

Every step of the string formalism is built by one-for-one replacement in the point-particle action, including the use of reparametrization freedom.

**Where it breaks down:** The world sheet has a two-dimensional reparametrization group and constraints with no particle analogue; quantization produces critical-dimension requirements that the analogy hides.

**App idea:** Two-column dictionary card that fills in as the tutor walks through the derivation (world line / world sheet, m / T0, length / area, tau / (tau, sigma)).

### Each element of string behaves like a relativistic particle of mass T0 ds moving with its transverse velocity → transverse velocity of a string · useful · GA §49.3 p.533 (pdf 550)

The static-gauge action takes the form -T0 integral ds sqrt(1 - v_perp^2), mirroring the particle Lagrangian -m sqrt(1 - v^2).

**Where it breaks down:** Neighbouring elements are coupled by tension, so they are not independent particles.

**App idea:** Highlight one string element with a moving gamma-factor readout as the string vibrates.

### A Feynman vertex as a sharp junction versus a closed string splitting smoothly (the familiar 'pair of trousers' picture) → ultraviolet divergences and renormalization · useful · GA §49.2 p.530 (pdf 547)

Fig. 49.3 sets a point interaction beside a tube that branches smoothly, and the text stresses that closed strings never meet at a point. The word 'trousers' is standard string-theory slang, not the book's.

**Where it breaks down:** The softening is not visible from the picture alone; the full argument requires modular invariance and loop amplitudes.

### The punt in the bottom of a wine bottle (the Mexican hat) → Mexican-hat potential · strong · GA §49.4 p.536 (pdf 553)

Fig. 49.8 draws a bottle and its upturned base to show a potential with a central hump and a circular trough of equal minima.

**Where it breaks down:** The picture shows only the potential, not the gradient energy that gives vortices their structure.

**App idea:** Marble-in-a-hat 3D toy linked to a phase dial.

### Stacking two-dimensional vortices so their cores line up into a curve → cosmic string · useful · GA §49.4 p.536 (pdf 553)

The book turns the planar vortex picture into a three-dimensional line defect by stacking copies along a third direction. Picturing it as a pile of coins is a tutor's addition.

**Where it breaks down:** Real cosmic strings can bend, loop and intercommute; stacking suggests a straight static line.

**App idea:** Extrude button in the winding-number explorer.

### Tetrahedron face-area vectors behave like angular momenta → quantum of area · strong · GA §49.6 p.538 (pdf 555)

By postulating the same commutators, the familiar j(j+1) spectrum and component incompatibility carry over to areas and face orientations.

**Where it breaks down:** It is a postulate chosen by analogy; the book does not derive it from quantizing general relativity, and the precise constant l0 depends on the Barbero-Immirzi parameter.

**App idea:** Show a spin-j vector cone next to a face normal cone with matching labels.

### A particle in a box for gravity → anti-de Sitter spacetime · useful · GA §49.7 p.539 (pdf 556)

Motivates AdS as a spacetime that confines gravitational physics, much as a box confines a quantum particle.

**Where it breaks down:** The walls are at infinite proper distance and massless signals do reach them; confinement depends on boundary conditions at the conformal boundary.

### de Sitter is a sphere in Minkowski space; flipping T^2 -> -T^2 swaps sphere and de Sitter, and alpha^2 -> -alpha^2 swaps de Sitter and AdS → maximally symmetric spacetime · strong · GA §49.7 p.540 (pdf 557)

Organizes three constant-curvature geometries as sign variations of one quadric.

**Where it breaks down:** Sign flips change causal structure and topology in ways that are not visible from the algebra alone.

**App idea:** Sign-switch panel morphing sphere, dS hourglass and AdS tube.

### Latitude that runs only from the north pole to the equator → conformal boundary of AdS · strong · GA §49.7 p.541 (pdf 558)

In conformal coordinates the radial variable behaves like latitude on a sphere but stops at the equator, which is the boundary.

**Where it breaks down:** The conformal picture is not the physical geometry; proper distances to the boundary are infinite.

**App idea:** Hemisphere-with-time cylinder where the equator glows as the boundary.

### AdS boundary encodes the bulk like a hologram encodes a 3D image on a 2D plate → holographic principle · useful · GA §49.7 p.540 (pdf 557)

Margin note 23 explains holography, and the w = b turning point is offered as a hint that bulk depth corresponds to energy.

**Where it breaks down:** The hint is only suggestive; the actual correspondence is a precise duality between quantum theories.

### Motion in a Newtonian potential (b/w)^2 → Poincare coordinates for AdS · strong · GA §49.7 p.542 (pdf 559)

The massive geodesic equation is rewritten as kinetic plus potential energy constant, making the turning point obvious.

**Where it breaks down:** t is coordinate time, not proper time, and the effective potential is gauge dependent.

**App idea:** Effective-potential plot with draggable energy line alongside the trajectory.


## Misconceptions addressed

### If extra dimensions existed we would already have noticed them. · GA §49.1 p.527 (pdf 544)

**Correction:** A compact dimension of tiny radius only affects states with energies of order hbar^2/(m a^2) or hbar c/a, far beyond experiments if a is near the Planck length.

**Why tempting:** We easily perceive three spatial dimensions, so it seems a fourth would be equally obvious.

### The Planck mass is an impossibly large or small mass that could never be relevant. · GA §49.1 p.529 (pdf 546)

**Correction:** The Planck mass is about 22 micrograms, macroscopic by particle standards; what is extreme is concentrating it into a Planck-length region, i.e. the Planck energy per particle.

**Why tempting:** Planck length and time are absurdly small, so learners assume all Planck quantities are.

### Renormalization tames quantum gravity just as it tames electromagnetism. · GA §49.2 p.530 (pdf 547)

**Correction:** Perturbative quantum gravity produces divergences that renormalization cannot absorb with finitely many parameters; it survives only as a low-energy effective theory.

**Why tempting:** Renormalization succeeds for every other Standard Model interaction.

### The parameters tau and sigma on a world sheet are physical time and position along the string. · GA §49.2 p.531 (pdf 548)

**Correction:** They are arbitrary labels; physics is invariant under reparametrization, and choosing tau = t or an energy-weighted sigma is a gauge choice for convenience.

**Why tempting:** The names are chosen to suggest time and length, and static gauge makes tau literally equal to t.

### Points on a string have a well-defined velocity including a component along the string. · GA §49.3 p.533 (pdf 550)

**Correction:** Only transverse motion is physical; sliding the labels along the string changes the apparent tangential velocity without changing the string's motion.

**Why tempting:** Material strings are made of identifiable atoms whose longitudinal motion is real.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### Cosmic strings are the fundamental strings of string theory blown up to large size. · GA §49.4 p.535 (pdf 552)

**Correction:** The cosmic strings discussed here are topological defects of ordinary field theory formed in symmetry-breaking transitions; they share only the name and line-like shape.

**Why tempting:** Both appear in the same section under the same word.

### Superspace in quantum geometrodynamics is related to supersymmetry. · GA §49.5 p.536 (pdf 553)

**Correction:** Here superspace means the space of all 3-geometries; the supersymmetric superspace of particle physics is an unrelated construction with extra fermionic coordinates.

**Why tempting:** The same word appears right after a section on supersymmetry.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### Loop quantum gravity says space is literally built out of tiny tetrahedra. · GA §49.6 p.539 (pdf 556)

**Correction:** The tetrahedron is only a convenient shape; because geometry is uncertain at the Planck scale one cannot conclude the microscopic structure is a tetrahedral network. What is claimed is discreteness of area and volume spectra.

**Why tempting:** The worked construction and pictures use tetrahedra and meshes.

### Anti-de Sitter spacetime is itself a theory of quantum gravity. · GA §49.7 p.540 (pdf 557)

**Correction:** AdS is a classical solution of Einstein's equation with negative Lambda; it is interesting as a stage for AdS/CFT, which relates gravity in the bulk to a quantum theory on the boundary.

**Why tempting:** It is discussed in a quantum gravity chapter and linked to holography.

### AdS necessarily contains closed timelike curves and is therefore unphysical. · GA §49.7 p.541 (pdf 558)

**Correction:** The closed timelike curves belong to the hyperboloid's periodic time; in the universal cover time is unrolled and causality is restored.

**Why tempting:** The embedding picture makes the time circle vivid.

### Nothing can reach the AdS boundary, so it is like an infinitely distant wall that no signal ever touches. · GA §49.7 p.542 (pdf 559)

**Correction:** Light reaches the boundary in finite coordinate time; massive particles cannot, turning back at w = b.

**Why tempting:** Proper distance to the boundary is infinite, which suggests it is unreachable for everything.

### Dark energy is dark matter, or both are just unseen ordinary matter. · GA §49.8 p.543 (pdf 560)

**Correction:** Baryons are about 5 per cent; cold dark matter (26 per cent) clusters into halos and is non-baryonic; dark energy (69 per cent) is smooth, uniform and drives acceleration.

**Why tempting:** Both are called dark and both are inferred only gravitationally.

### Quantum vacuum energy naturally explains the cosmological constant. · GA §49.8 p.543 (pdf 560)

**Correction:** Naive vacuum energy estimates overshoot the observed value by tens of orders of magnitude (Zel'dovich); why Lambda is so small is unexplained.

**Why tempting:** Vacuum energy is uniform and has the equation of state of a cosmological constant.


## Thought experiments

### Squeezing a particle into a Planck-sized box · GA §49.6 p.537 (pdf 554)

**Setup:** Localize a particle within ever smaller L; its momentum spread and energy grow as hbar c/L, while the horizon radius of that energy grows as G hbar/(c^3 L).

**Lesson:** At L ~ l_P the particle would be hidden behind its own horizon, so distances below the Planck length cannot be operationally resolved.

**App idea:** Log-log plot of localization energy (falling line) and horizon radius (rising line) versus box size, crossing at l_P, with a shrinking box animation that turns black at the crossing.

### Firing photons and massive particles at the AdS boundary · GA §49.7 p.542 (pdf 559)

**Setup:** From depth w0 in Poincare AdS send a light ray toward w = 0 with a mirror there, and separately launch a massive particle.

**Lesson:** Light makes the round trip in coordinate time 2 w0; massive particles feel a repulsive effective potential and turn back at w = b, so AdS acts like a box.

**App idea:** Boundary bounce demo described under Fig. 49.13(b).

### Quantum clock in a gravitational field (Coleman) · GA p.527 (pdf 544)

**Setup:** Put a quantum system at height h in a uniform field so its Hamiltonian gains a term proportional to itself.

**Lesson:** The phase evolution is the same as with time rescaled by (1+V), giving gravitational time dilation without any geometry.

**App idea:** Two phasors at different heights rotating at slightly different rates, with accumulated phase difference plotted.

### Measuring a tetrahedron at the Planck scale · GA §49.6 p.539 (pdf 556)

**Setup:** Try to determine all components of all four face-area vectors of a tiny tetrahedron whose area vectors obey angular-momentum commutators.

**Lesson:** Only one Cartesian component per face can be sharp, so face orientations shimmer and geometry is fuzzy at the Planck scale.

**App idea:** Quantum tetrahedron demo with normal cones.


## Applications and observations

- **Planck energy versus accelerator and cosmic-ray energies** (numerical-estimate, GA §49.1 p.529 (pdf 546)): The Planck energy is compared with the LHC beam energy and the highest cosmic-ray energies to show that Planck-scale physics is experimentally out of reach. Key numbers: E_P = 1.22e28 eV = 1.22e19 GeV; LHC about 7 TeV per beam; highest cosmic rays seven to eight orders of magnitude below E_P
- **Dark matter halos and rotation curves** (observation, GA §49.4 p.534 (pdf 551)): Dark matter is inferred from stellar orbital speeds in galaxies; direct-detection experiments assuming non-gravitational coupling have found nothing.
- **Lensing by cosmic strings** (astrophysical-system, GA §49.4 p.535 (pdf 552)): A cosmic string would produce double images of distant sources through its conical spacetime; none are confirmed.
- **CMB measurements (COBE, WMAP, Planck)** (observation, GA §49.8 p.543 (pdf 560)): Satellite measurements of the microwave background spectrum and anisotropy power spectrum underpin Lambda-CDM. Key numbers: T = 2.725 K; delta T/T ~ 1e-5; COBE result 1992, WMAP launch 2001, Planck launch 2009
- **Accelerating expansion from Type Ia supernovae** (observation, GA §49.8 p.543 (pdf 560)): Supernovae used as standardizable candles show the expansion is accelerating, requiring Lambda.
- **Cosmic energy budget** (data-analysis, GA §49.8 p.543 (pdf 560)): Combined data give the densities of baryons, cold dark matter and dark energy relative to critical. Key numbers: Omega_0 = 0.999(2); Omega_B = 0.05; Omega_M (CDM) = 0.26; Omega_Lambda = 0.69; vacuum-energy overshoot about 50 orders of magnitude (Zel'dovich)
- **Cosmic timeline in Lambda-CDM** (numerical-estimate, GA §49.8 p.543 (pdf 560)): Key epochs from inflation to dark-energy domination. Key numbers: Inflation ~1e-32 s; hadrons ~1e-5 s; baryon-antibaryon annihilation ~1 s; BBN 0.01 s to 20 min; a few baryons per billion photons; recombination ~380,000 yr; Lambda domination from ~5 Gyr ago; age 13.80(2) Gyr
- **Independent checks: CMB acoustic peaks and deuterium abundance** (data-analysis, GA §49.8 p.544 (pdf 561)): Parameters from CMB acoustic oscillations at 380,000 years agree with those from primordial deuterium made in the first minutes, giving confidence in Lambda-CDM.
- **Hubble tension and lithium problem** (observation, GA §49.8 p.544 (pdf 561)): Local and early-Universe Hubble constant determinations disagree, and lithium-7 abundance does not match prediction. Key numbers: H0 between about 67 and 74 km/s/Mpc
- **Scale of Kaluza-Klein excitations for a Planck-length circle (own estimate)** (numerical-estimate, GA §49.1 p.528 (pdf 545)): Our own estimate, not in the book: momentum quantization hbar c/a for a ~ l_P gives energies near the Planck energy, confirming Example 49.1's conclusion. Key numbers: hbar c/l_P ~ 1.2e19 GeV

## Historical notes

- **Rene Descartes, Max Planck (c. 1899):** Descartes speculated about natural units for the geometry of the Universe; about 250 years later Planck combined G, c and h into natural units. — Origin of the Planck scale as the meeting point of gravity, relativity and quantum theory. (GA §49.1 p.528 (pdf 545))
- **Sidney Coleman:** Argument that inserting a gravitational potential into the quantum evolution operator amounts to rescaling time. — Shows that gravitational redshift follows from minimal quantum reasoning. (GA p.527 (pdf 544))
- **Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet:** Dirichlet boundary conditions give D-branes their name. — Explains the terminology for surfaces on which open strings end. (GA §49.2 p.530 (pdf 547))
- **Yoichiro Nambu, Tetsuo Goto:** The string action proportional to world-sheet area bears their names. — Foundation of classical and quantum relativistic string dynamics. (GA §49.2 p.532 (pdf 549))
- **Gerard 't Hooft:** Proposed the holographic principle, that a volume's physics can be encoded on its boundary. — Conceptual basis for AdS/CFT. (GA §49.7 p.540 (pdf 557))
- **Juan Maldacena:** Proposed the AdS/CFT correspondence relating gravity in AdS to a conformal field theory on its boundary. — Most concrete realization of holography and a major research programme in quantum gravity. (GA §49.7 p.540 (pdf 557))
- **Willem de Sitter, Tullio Levi-Civita (1920s):** AdS spacetime was first discussed by de Sitter and discovered independently by Levi-Civita; both dS and AdS were then called de Sitter spacetimes. — Explains confusing historical naming. (GA §49.7 p.542 (pdf 559))
- **Sidney Coleman (1970):** Famously warned students against thesis research in GR because Einstein's last decades had led nowhere. — Humorous counterpoint showing how the field's prospects changed after 1970. (GA §49.7 p.542 (pdf 559))
- **Yakov Zel'dovich (1968):** Pointed out that quantum vacuum energy as a cosmological constant would exceed the critical density enormously. — First statement of the cosmological constant problem. (GA §49.8 p.543 (pdf 560))
- **History (1992, 2001, 2009):** COBE measured the CMB blackbody spectrum and anisotropies; WMAP (launched 2001) and Planck (launched 2009) mapped the fluctuations in increasing detail. — Transformed cosmology into a precision science supporting Lambda-CDM. (GA §49.8 p.543 (pdf 560))

## Notation and conventions

- **D and d:** D is the number of spacetime dimensions; d = D - 1 is the number of spatial dimensions. (GA §49.1 p.529 (pdf 546))
- **Superscript (D) on G, Phi, l_P:** Quantities belonging to D-dimensional gravity carry a parenthesized superscript; unadorned G and l_P are four-dimensional. (GA §49.1 p.529 (pdf 546))
- **Metric signature:** Mostly plus (-,+,+,+) throughout, so Xdot^2 is negative for a timelike world-sheet direction and u.u = -1. — The text says 'Euclidean space' when setting up the string target space but uses eta_{mu nu}. (GA §49.2 p.531 (pdf 548))
- **World-sheet notation:** X^mu(tau, sigma) = (T, X, Y, Z); dot for d/dtau, prime for d/dsigma; xi^1 = tau, xi^2 = sigma; gamma_{alpha beta} induced metric with determinant gamma; bold X for four-vectors, arrow X for 3-vectors. (GA §49.2 p.531 (pdf 548))
- **Units in string and AdS sections:** c = 1 in the string equations (v_perp dimensionless, wave speed one) and alpha = 1 in AdS3 coordinates; Planck unit numbers are in SI. — Natural units hbar = c = 1 are used for the energy-length conversion. (GA §49.3 p.533 (pdf 550))
- **(3)C and (3)R:** A pre-superscript 3 denotes a spatial 3-surface or a 3-dimensional curvature quantity. (GA §49.5 p.536 (pdf 553))
- **AdS dimension label:** AdS in d spacetime dimensions is written AdS^d (e.g. AdS^5, AdS^3). — Many references write AdS_d with a subscript. (GA §49.7 p.540 (pdf 557))
- **Curvature sign:** Riemann tensor for constant curvature written K(g g - g g) with K = +1/alpha^2 for de Sitter (R > 0) and -1/alpha^2 for AdS (R < 0). (GA §49.7 p.540 (pdf 557))
- **Einstein equation with Lambda:** For de Sitter, G_{mu nu} = -(R/4) g_{mu nu} with Lambda = R/4 = 3/alpha^2, i.e. G_{mu nu} + Lambda g_{mu nu} = 0. (GA §49.7 p.540 (pdf 557))
- **Omega labels:** Omega_0 total, Omega_B baryons, Omega_M cold dark matter, Omega_Lambda dark energy. — Omega_M normally denotes all matter (baryons plus CDM); the chapter uses it for CDM alone. (GA §49.8 p.543 (pdf 560))
- **Superpartner naming:** Fermionic partners of bosons take the suffix -ino with a tilde (photino); bosonic partners of fermions take the prefix s- (squark). — The book writes 'photonino'; the standard name is photino. (GA §49.4 p.535 (pdf 552))

## Teaching gems

### Hidden dimensions made quantitative with a particle-in-a-box on a cylinder: the familiar spectrum survives untouched and the new levels sit at an energy set by 1/a^2. · GA §49.1 p.528 (pdf 545)

**Why it works:** Uses a problem every physics student has solved to answer 'where are the extra dimensions?' with a number rather than a slogan.

**App idea:** Roll-up well with a live energy ladder and an LHC-energy line; shrinking a until the first l = 1 level passes the line.

### Gravity's strength diluted by extra volume: averaging mass around the compact circle gives G = G^(5)/(2 pi a) in one line. · GA §49.1 p.529 (pdf 546)

**Why it works:** Connects Gauss's-law flux spreading to why gravity might be weak, a surprising payoff from elementary reasoning.

**App idea:** Field-line flux animation in 3D space times a circle: close to a mass lines spread in all directions including the circle (1/r^3), far away they have filled the circle and spread only in 3D (1/r^2); plot of force versus r showing the crossover at r ~ a.

### Building the string by strict analogy with the relativistic particle (length to area, mass to tension, one parameter to two), then using gauge freedom to reach a wave equation. · GA §49.2 p.531 (pdf 548)

**Why it works:** Learners reuse action principles they already trust, and the end result is a familiar equation, so the new object feels earned rather than imposed.

**App idea:** Side-by-side derivation stepper: particle column and string column advance together, with the world line and world sheet drawn above each.

### Only transverse velocity is physical: after projecting out motion along the string, each bit of string has the Lagrangian of a relativistic particle. · GA §49.3 p.533 (pdf 550)

**Why it works:** Converts an abstract determinant into a picture of pieces of string with gamma factors, and shows why labels along the string are arbitrary.

**App idea:** Vibrating string with markers that the learner can slide along the string; the tangential velocity changes but the transverse arrows and energy readout stay fixed.

### Cosmic strings from vortices: the jump from double-well walls to Mexican-hat vortices to stacked vortex lines ties early-Universe cosmology to a picture of winding arrows. · GA §49.4 p.535 (pdf 552)

**Why it works:** Each step is a small visual extension, and topology (the phase must wind) explains why the defect cannot be smoothed away.

**App idea:** Winding-number explorer with an extrude button and a lensing view showing a background galaxy doubled by the string's deficit angle.

### Quantizing a tetrahedron by treating its face-area vectors as angular momenta. · GA §49.6 p.538 (pdf 555)

**Why it works:** Reuses the most familiar quantum algebra to make discrete area, discrete volume and fuzzy geometry concrete, while being honest that it is a postulate.

**App idea:** Quantum tetrahedron with snapping area values, a volume ladder and uncertainty cones on normals.

### The mini-black-hole argument: uncertainty plus horizon size gives the Planck length in two lines. · GA §49.6 p.537 (pdf 554)

**Why it works:** A back-of-envelope derivation that explains why the Planck length is a floor on measurable distance, not just a combination of constants.

**App idea:** Crossing-lines log-log plot with a shrinking box that collapses to a black hole.

### de Sitter and anti-de Sitter as one quadric with sign flips, and the AdS boundary found by noticing that a latitude-like coordinate stops at the equator. · GA §49.7 p.541 (pdf 558)

**Why it works:** Organizes three geometries into one family and uncovers a boundary through a vivid geometric observation instead of abstract conformal machinery.

**App idea:** Sign-flip hyperboloid morph plus a conformal cylinder view with the boundary highlighted at psi = pi/2.

### Probe a strange boundary by throwing things at it: photons reach the AdS edge, massive particles bounce off an effective 1/w^2 wall. · GA §49.7 p.542 (pdf 559)

**Why it works:** Turns an abstract geometric property into a concrete kinematic experiment using only Killing vectors and u.u = -1.

**App idea:** Boundary bounce simulator with an effective potential panel and adjustable b.


## Gaps and pitfalls

- **Eqn 49.5 prints G = l_P/(m_P t_P^2); the dimensions of G require l_P^3/(m_P t_P^2). The eqn 49.6 results are nonetheless correct.** — A student solving the printed system will not reproduce the Planck units and may doubt their algebra. *Suggestion:* Use the corrected relation, and have learners check dimensions of G (m^3 kg^-1 s^-2) before solving.
- **The text says mass density is mass divided by 'the number of spatial dimensions'; it means mass per unit d-dimensional volume.** — Garbled definition obscures why G must change dimension with D. *Suggestion:* State that rho^(D) has units of mass per length^d and derive the 1/r^(d-1) force explicitly from Gauss's law over a (d-1)-sphere.
- **Eqns 49.18, 49.19, 49.31 and the determinant expression print (X.X')^2 without the dot on the first X.** — The action as printed is not the world-sheet area and the derivation of v_perp will not work. *Suggestion:* Always write (Xdot.X')^2 - Xdot^2 X'^2 and point out the sign: for a timelike sheet this combination is positive.
- **Momenta in eqns 49.23-49.24 carry prefactor -T0/L, which is dimensionally and algebraically inconsistent; the correct factor is -T0 divided by the square root, equal to T0^2/L. Eqn 49.23 also has a stray comma, and the text around eqn 49.34 cites eqns 49.25 rather than 49.23-49.24.** — Learners verifying Exercise 49.6 get inconsistent signs and powers of T0. *Suggestion:* Define sqrt(-gamma) as its own symbol and differentiate carefully; compare with Zwiebach's treatment.
- **The string's target space is called Euclidean while the Minkowski metric eta is used.** — Confuses the sign of Xdot^2 and why sqrt(-gamma) is real. *Suggestion:* Say Minkowski spacetime and emphasize the mostly-plus signature.
- **The final wave equation is stated without its accompanying constraints (velocity orthogonal to the string and (dX/dt)^2 + (dX/dsigma)^2 = 1) or the open-string boundary conditions, and solutions are called 'simply plane waves'.** — Learners may think any solution of the wave equation is an allowed string motion. *Suggestion:* Present general solutions as left- plus right-movers subject to constraints; show a rotating rod or a pulsating closed loop as explicit allowed motions.
- **Supersymmetry is described as giving partners of the same mass, with no mention that it must be broken; the M in M-theory is glossed as brane dimension; the photon partner is called a photonino.** — Learners may repeat inaccurate claims about supersymmetry and M-theory. *Suggestion:* Note that unbroken SUSY is ruled out, that the M label is not standardized, and that the partner name is photino; mention LHC limits on superpartners.
- **Example 49.8's opening text is garbled in print (a sentence fragment about searches designed to detect dark matter appears out of place).** — Minor confusion when reading. *Suggestion:* Paraphrase: dark matter has resisted direct detection; one proposed solution is supersymmetry.
- **The Einstein-Hamilton-Jacobi equation appears with no derivation, no mention of the momentum constraint, and no connection to the Wheeler-DeWitt equation; footnote 18 cites eqn 49.39 where 49.40 is meant.** — The superspace section reads as a list of assertions a novice cannot check. *Suggestion:* Tutor should explain it by analogy with H(q, dS/dq) = E for a particle, and name the quantum version as the Wheeler-DeWitt equation, keeping expectations qualitative.
- **The LQG heuristic uses R = GM/c^2 for the Schwarzschild radius, dropping the factor 2.** — Students may memorize the wrong horizon radius. *Suggestion:* Say the estimate is order-of-magnitude and restate R_s = 2GM/c^2.
- **Printed induced-metric formulas 49.47 and 49.52 lack dX^mu dX^nu and reuse mu, nu as dummy indices in the denominator.** — A line element that equals a bracket of coefficients is meaningless and index clashes confuse summation. *Suggestion:* Write the full expression with separate dummy indices and derive it by eliminating W (or U) explicitly.
- **The chapter is a whirlwind; AdS/CFT, holography and M-theory are named but not explained, and several claims (black holes as strings, AdS/CFT in condensed matter) are presented without context.** — Learners may overestimate what they now understand or feel lost. *Suggestion:* Frame each section as 'one idea plus one calculation'; flag clearly what is speculative, and offer optional deeper modules.
- **Omega_M is used for CDM alone and a '3Li' appears among BBN products; Lambda-CDM numbers and the Hubble tension range are dated to around 2020.** — Mismatch with other sources that use Omega_M for all matter, and possibly outdated values. *Suggestion:* Clarify labels (Omega_c for CDM, Omega_m ~ 0.31 total matter, helium-3 rather than lithium-3) and pull current parameter values from the latest data release when teaching.

## Tutor notes

- Open with the puzzle: three forces are quantum gauge theories, gravity is not. Ask the learner what they think goes wrong if you quantize gravity like electromagnetism, and steer toward non-renormalizability and the Planck scale.
- Treat the chapter as a menu of five mini-lessons, each with one calculation: rolled-up well (extra dimensions), Planck units, string to wave equation, quantized tetrahedron, AdS boundary bounce. Let the learner choose depth after the Planck-scale lesson.
- Before the string section, check the learner can write the relativistic particle action and explain reparametrization invariance; if not, review world lines and proper time first.
- When presenting the Nambu-Goto action, write the correct form with Xdot in both places and warn that the printed book drops a dot; ask the learner to compute the area of a static straight string of length l over time T as a sanity check (answer: action -T0 l T).
- Check understanding of gauge choice by asking why sliding the sigma labels along the string cannot change physics, and what quantity is left invariant (transverse velocity).
- For cosmic strings, ask the learner to walk around a vortex and count how the arrow turns; then ask why the core must be singular. Clarify immediately that cosmic strings and fundamental strings are different things.
- For LQG, first have the learner reproduce the angular momentum facts (j(j+1) eigenvalues, only one component sharp) and then map each fact onto tetrahedron faces; stress that quantizing areas is a postulate in this presentation.
- For AdS, start with the sphere, then de Sitter, then AdS as sign changes; ask the learner to predict what happens to the topology of time when an extra timelike direction is added.
- In Example 49.12, have the learner derive (dw/dt)^2 + b^2/w^2 = 1 themselves from u.u = -1 and the Killing vector; ask where a massive particle turns around and whether a photon can reach w = 0.
- Common learner questions: 'Has string theory been tested?' (no direct tests); 'Is spacetime made of tetrahedra?' (no, the shape is incidental); 'Is AdS our Universe?' (no, our Lambda is positive); 'What is the difference between dark matter and dark energy?'; 'Why can't the LHC test quantum gravity?' (energy gap of about fifteen orders of magnitude).
- Keep the epistemic status explicit throughout: Lambda-CDM is observationally anchored; the quantum gravity approaches are unconfirmed. Coleman's 1970 warning is a good light-hearted closing, contrasted with how active the field became.
- Close Section 49.8 by linking to the final chapter: whatever replaces GR, the Big-Bang singularity problem remains, which is where quantum gravity is expected to matter.
