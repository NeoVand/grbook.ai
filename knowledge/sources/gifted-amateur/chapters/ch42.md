# GA ch42 · The electromagnetic field

> Builds electromagnetism as the simplest Lorentz-invariant coupling of a particle's displacement to a 1-form gauge field, extracts the antisymmetric Faraday tensor and the covariant Lorentz force, rewrites Maxwell's equations in index form, explores gauge freedom (Lorenz and Coulomb gauges, retarded potentials), computes the field's energy-momentum tensor, and finally recasts everything in the language of forms as F = dA, dF = 0 and d*F = *J.

**Pages:** printed 453–466 · pdf 470–483 · **Difficulty:** math 4/5, conceptual 3/5, novice-friendliness 2/5

The physics is familiar undergraduate electromagnetism, but the chapter moves quickly through index gymnastics, Lagrangian field theory, curved-space variation, gauge fixing and differential forms with Hodge duals. Many steps are asserted ('it can be checked') or left to exercises, several printed expansions contain sign or factor slips (42.4 prefactor, 2dF in 42.63, the B-derivative signs in 42.73, derivative variable in 42.29), and the geometric section assumes fluency with Part V. Readers without Chapters 32, 33, 37 and 40 fresh will struggle; readers who have them get a satisfying payoff.

## Role in the book

Fourth chapter of Part VI (Classical and quantum fields), coming after the fluids chapter (ch39), Lagrangian field theory (ch40) and inflation (ch41). It cashes in the Part V geometry (differential forms in Ch. 32, exterior derivative and dd = 0 in Ch. 33, Hodge duality in Ch. 37) on a physical theory the reader already knows, so that electromagnetism can serve as the template for gravity in what follows: charge conservation and the Bianchi identity (Ch. 43), gauge fields (Ch. 44), weak-field gravity and gravitational waves (Chs 45-46), which reuse the gauge fixing and retarded-potential ideas introduced here, and the graviton chapter (Ch. 47), which points back to the scalar-gravity exercise. It also supplies the electromagnetic stress-energy tensor that sources curvature (e.g. for charged black holes, Ch. 29).

## Learning objectives

- Reader can explain why the action q times the integral of A_sigma dx^sigma is the simplest Lorentz-invariant way for a field to act on a moving particle, and what the charge q measures.
- Reader can derive the covariant equation of motion m du_alpha/dtau = q F_{alpha beta} u^beta from the particle-plus-interaction action and identify F_{alpha beta} = A_{beta,alpha} - A_{alpha,beta}.
- Reader can show that the spatial part of the covariant force law reproduces q(E + v x B) and that antisymmetry of F keeps u.a = 0 so the rest mass is unchanged.
- Reader can write the Faraday tensor as a 4x4 antisymmetric matrix of E and B components with the book's sign conventions, in both (0,2) and (2,0) forms.
- Reader can obtain the homogeneous Maxwell equations from the cyclic (Bianchi) identity and the inhomogeneous ones from F^{mu nu}_{,nu} = J^mu, and upgrade both to curved spacetime.
- Reader can state the gauge transformation A -> A - d chi, prove E and B are unchanged (via dd = 0), and use it to impose the Lorenz condition and the further A_0 = 0 choice.
- Reader can show that in Lorenz gauge every component of A obeys a wave equation with source -J, and write the static and retarded-potential solutions.
- Reader can compute T^{00}, T^{0j} and T^{jk} of the electromagnetic field from T^{mu nu} = F^{mu alpha}F^nu_alpha - (1/4) eta^{mu nu} F^2 and recognize energy density, Poynting flux and Maxwell stress.
- Reader can decompose the Faraday 2-form into E dx^i wedge dt and B dx^j wedge dx^k pieces and picture a magnetic component as circulation in a tube of two basis 1-forms.
- Reader can feed a 4-velocity into one slot of F and extract component equations of motion, e.g. for a uniform magnetic field.
- Reader can form the Maxwell 2-form *F and the current 3-form *J with the Levi-Civita symbol and recover Gauss and Ampere-Maxwell from d*F = *J.
- Reader can explain why dF = 0 is automatic once F = dA and why the source equation needs the dual (metric-dependent) 2-form.

## Teaching approach

A double-pass chapter. The first pass is components-first and Lagrangian: guess the simplest coupling, crank the Euler-Lagrange equations, name the antisymmetric combination that appears as the Faraday tensor, and then relate it to the familiar E, B, potentials, Maxwell equations, gauge choices and stress-energy. The second pass (section 42.4) re-derives the same content in coordinate-free form language, so each Part V tool (wedge product, exterior derivative, dd = 0, Hodge star) is seen doing concrete physical work. Throughout, the authors frame electromagnetism as a rehearsal for gravity: a Wheeler-style slogan, a comma-to-semicolon upgrade to curved space, a gauge freedom that will reappear for gravitational waves, and retarded potentials that will return for the metric.

**Style:** components-first, derivation-heavy, spiral-revisit, example-driven, conversational-informal

**Narrative arc**

1. Slogan: recast Wheeler's matter/spacetime slogan as fields steering charges and charges steering fields, announcing EM as the model field theory for gravity.
2. Motivating question: what is the simplest Lorentz-invariant way a field can push a particle? Answer: couple the displacement dx^mu linearly to a 1-form A, with strength q.
3. Formalism (Example 42.1): vary the free-particle-plus-coupling action; the antisymmetric derivative combination of A emerges and is named the Faraday tensor; the equation of motion is written as dp/dtau = q F( , u) and upgraded to curved space.
4. Connection to known physics: identify A^0 with the electrostatic potential, define E and B from A, note gauge freedom, and display F_{mu nu} and F^{mu nu} as matrices of E and B.
5. Field dynamics: quote 3-vector Maxwell equations, form J^mu, show the cyclic identity gives the source-free pair and F^{mu nu}_{,nu} = J^mu the sourced pair; sketch the field Lagrangian (Example 42.2) and its curved-space version (Example 42.3).
6. Check/application: energy-momentum tensor of the field and its E, B components (Example 42.4), noting it is what couples to curvature.
7. Gauge freedom: rewrite the field equation in terms of A, remove the divergence term by the Lorenz choice, obtain wave equations, tighten further to A_0 = 0, and solve with static and retarded potentials (Examples 42.5-42.7), flagging the reuse for gravitational waves.
8. Geometric retelling: F = dA (Example 42.8), dF = 0 for free, E and B as components of a 2-form pictured as tubes (Fig. 42.2), force law as slot-filling (Example 42.9, helix), gauge invariance as dd = 0.
9. Close the loop: expand dF = 0 into div B and Faraday's law (Example 42.10), build the Maxwell 2-form *F and current 3-form *J by duality (Examples 42.11-42.12), and show d*F = *J reproduces the remaining pair (Example 42.13); forward pointer to charge conservation in Ch. 43.

**Signature moves**

- Derive the Faraday tensor rather than postulate it: it appears as the antisymmetric remainder when the Euler-Lagrange equation is applied to the displacement coupling.
- Present each result twice, once in indices and once as forms, so the learner sees that dF = 0 and the cyclic Bianchi identity are the same statement.
- Gauge invariance proved in one line by dd = 0 instead of by checking E and B separately.
- Use slot-filling language for the force: the 4-velocity is inserted into the second slot of the 2-form F.
- A compact E -> -B, B -> E rule for passing from F to its dual *F, making the duality operation concrete.
- Constant analogizing forward to gravity: Wheeler slogan, electromagnetic Bianchi identity, gauge fixing and retarded potentials each carry an explicit pointer to later gravitational chapters.
- Exercises that turn the same method on gravity (a scalar-field theory of gravitation in the style of Padmanabhan) to show why a scalar field alone fails.

## Section by section

### Chapter opening: electromagnetism as the model field theory — p.453 (pdf 470)

Two epigraphs, then a statement of purpose: redo electromagnetism with the geometric tools of Part V. Einstein's reliance on electromagnetism as a guide is noted, and Wheeler's gravitational slogan gets an electromagnetic twin in which fields steer charges and charges steer fields. The plan is announced: work in flat spacetime (a margin note reassures that valid tensor equations carry over to curved spacetime), first Lagrangian and components, then coordinate-free.

**Concepts:** electromagnetism as a field theory, Wheeler slogan for electromagnetism, comma-goes-to-semicolon rule

**Key moves**
- Pair the gravitational slogan with an electromagnetic one to set up a running analogy.
- Justify working in flat spacetime because tensor equations generalize.

### §42.1 Electric charge in a field — p.453 (pdf 470)

Charge is defined operationally as the property by which a particle couples to a field. Starting from the free massive-particle action, the authors propose the simplest Lorentz-symmetric interaction: the particle's displacement dx^mu contracted with a 1-form field A, weighted by q. Example 42.1 applies the Euler-Lagrange equations to the combined Lagrangian under proper-time parametrization; the derivative terms of A combine into an antisymmetric object, the Faraday tensor, giving m times the covariant acceleration equal to q F_{alpha beta} u^beta. This is written as dp/dtau = q F( , u), interpreted as the departure from geodesic motion, and upgraded to curved spacetime with a covariant derivative. Margin notes explain the factor 1/2 in the 2-form expansion, show that antisymmetry guarantees u.a = 0, and promise that the spatial components give the Lorentz force.

**Concepts:** electric charge, minimal coupling of a particle to a 1-form field, electromagnetic 4-potential, Faraday tensor, covariant Lorentz force law, antisymmetry of F and conservation of rest mass, comma-goes-to-semicolon rule

**Key moves**
- Free action S_m = -m integral sqrt(-ds^2) plus interaction q integral A_sigma dx^sigma.
- Euler-Lagrange: canonical momentum m u_alpha + q A_alpha; total tau derivative of A_alpha gives u^beta partial_beta A_alpha.
- Move that term to the right and relabel indices so the right side is q(partial_alpha A_beta - partial_beta A_alpha)u^beta.
- Name F_{alpha beta} = A_{beta,alpha} - A_{alpha,beta}; note antisymmetry allows writing F as a 2-form with a factor 1/2.
- Write the equation of motion as the momentum 1-form rate equal to q F with u in the second slot; F(u,u)=0 implies u.a = 0.
- Promote to curved space: D p/dtau = m nabla_u u = q F( , u).

### §42.2 Faraday tensor and Maxwell equations — p.455 (pdf 472)

Introduces A as the electromagnetic gauge field, raises its index with eta (so A_0 = -A^0 and A^0 is the electrostatic potential V), and defines E = -grad A^0 - dA/dt and B = curl A. A first look at gauge transformations shows E and B are unchanged by subtracting a gradient. The Faraday tensor is displayed as matrices F_{mu nu} and F^{mu nu} in terms of E and B. Maxwell's equations in natural units are quoted, the 4-current J^mu = (rho, J) is formed, and the equations are recast as the cyclic identity (homogeneous pair) and F^{mu nu}_{,nu} = J^mu (sourced pair). Example 42.2 recalls the field Lagrangian density -1/4 F F + J A from Chapter 40. Example 42.3 moves to curved space: F built with semicolons, Lagrangian with explicit inverse metrics, Euler-Lagrange giving the covariant divergence of F vanishing (source-free), and the cyclic identity with semicolons. The section closes with the electromagnetic energy-momentum tensor in flat and curved form, and Example 42.4 unpacks it into energy density, Poynting vector and Maxwell stress.

**Concepts:** electromagnetic 4-potential, electrostatic potential, electric and magnetic fields from potentials, gauge transformation, Faraday tensor, Maxwell's equations, 4-current, electromagnetic Bianchi identity, inhomogeneous Maxwell equations in tensor form, Maxwell Lagrangian density, Maxwell equations in curved spacetime, electromagnetic energy-momentum tensor, Poynting vector, Maxwell stress tensor

**Key moves**
- Map A_mu to A^mu with eta; identify A^0 with V.
- Define E and B from A and observe invariance under A_mu -> A_mu - partial_mu chi.
- Fill the matrix F_{mu nu} with E and B; raising both indices flips only the E entries.
- Set alpha, beta, gamma = 1,2,3 in the cyclic identity to get div B = 0; include a time index to get Faraday's law.
- Show F^{mu nu}_{,nu} = J^mu contains Gauss's law and the Ampere-Maxwell law.
- Curved space: covariant F, Lagrangian with g^{alpha gamma} g^{beta delta}, E-L gives F_{gamma delta;beta} g^{beta delta} = 0.
- Quote T^{mu nu} = F^{mu alpha}F^nu_alpha - (1/4)eta^{mu nu}F^2 and evaluate its components.

### §42.3 Gauge freedom — p.458 (pdf 475)

States the principle that no measurement can detect the replacement A_mu -> A_mu - chi_{,mu} and calls the choice of chi a gauge, suggesting it as a choice of language. Example 42.5 rewrites the sourced Maxwell equation as a divergence term minus the d'Alembertian of A, then chooses chi so that the divergence of A vanishes (Lorenz gauge), leaving a wave equation with source -J and, in vacuum, waves travelling at light speed. Example 42.6 shows residual freedom: a further shift by a function obeying the wave equation can set A_0 = 0, reducing the Lorenz condition to div A = 0 (the book calls this Coulomb gauge). Example 42.7 solves the static equations with 1/|x - x'| integrals over the source volume (Fig. 42.1), then inserts retarded time to obtain retarded potentials, and remarks that similar potential integrals will later describe the metric field produced by distributions of mass and energy; gauge choices of this kind are flagged as important for gravitational waves in Chapter 46.

**Concepts:** gauge freedom, gauge transformation, Lorenz gauge, residual gauge freedom, Coulomb gauge, d'Alembertian operator, electromagnetic wave equation, retarded time, retarded potentials, Green's function solution of Poisson's equation

**Key moves**
- Substitute F = dA into the sourced equation, which splits it into a gradient of the divergence of A minus the wave operator acting on A.
- Choose chi with partial^2 chi = partial^mu A_mu to kill the divergence.
- Get partial^2 A^mu = -J^mu; vacuum case is a wave equation at speed c.
- Use a second shift xi with partial^2 xi = 0 and partial_0 xi = A'_0 to reach A''_0 = 0 and div A'' = 0.
- Static limit reduces to Poisson equations solved by 1/(4 pi |x-x'|) integrals.
- Replace source time by t - |x-x'|/c to get retarded potentials.

### §42.4 Geometrical electromagnetism — p.460 (pdf 477)

The coordinate-free retelling. Taking the exterior derivative of the 1-form A yields the 2-form F (checked in components in Example 42.8), so F is exact and hence closed. The Faraday 2-form is expanded into E^i dx^i wedge dt and B^x dy wedge dz style terms, and Fig. 42.2 pictures a magnetic component circulating in a tube formed by two basis 1-forms. The force law becomes slot filling; Example 42.9 treats a uniform magnetic field along x and extracts component equations whose solutions are helices (Fig. 42.3). Gauge transformations become A -> A - d chi, and F is invariant because dd = 0. Maxwell's first pair is dF = 0; Example 42.10 expands it into div B = 0 and Faraday's law, and the vanishing of repeated-index wedge terms reproduces the cyclic Bianchi identity. The source must couple through the dual: the Maxwell 2-form *F (Example 42.11, with the rule E -> -B, B -> E and **F = -F) and the current 3-form *J (Example 42.12). Since dF is already zero, the only natural 3-form to equate to *J is d*F; Example 42.13 expands it, and matching components gives the second pair, d*F = *J. Charge conservation is deferred to Chapter 43.

**Concepts:** exterior derivative, Faraday 2-form, exact and closed forms, dd = 0 identity, 2-form picture of the magnetic field, covariant Lorentz force law, helical motion in a magnetic field, gauge transformation, Maxwell's equations in differential-form language, electromagnetic Bianchi identity, Hodge dual, Maxwell 2-form, current 3-form, electric-magnetic duality rotation

**Key moves**
- dA = partial_mu A_nu dx^mu wedge dx^nu; antisymmetrize to read off F_{mu nu}.
- F = dA implies dF = ddA = 0.
- Decompose F into electric (space-time) and magnetic (space-space) wedge pieces.
- Insert u into the second slot of B^x dy wedge dz to get dp_y/dtau = qB^x u^z and dp_z/dtau = -qB^x u^y.
- A -> A - d chi leaves F unchanged because dd chi = 0.
- Expand dF = 0 into four 3-form coefficients: div B and three components of Faraday's law.
- Build *F with the Levi-Civita symbol; observe E -> -B, B -> E.
- Build *J; argue d*F is the only nontrivial 3-form available; match coefficients to get Gauss and Ampere-Maxwell.

### Chapter summary — p.464 (pdf 481)

Boxed recap of four results: the charged-particle equation of motion with u in a slot of F, the Faraday tensor as the exterior derivative of the gauge field, Maxwell's equations as dF = 0 and d*F = *J, and the curved-space energy-momentum tensor of the field.

**Concepts:** covariant Lorentz force law, Faraday 2-form, Maxwell's equations in differential-form language, electromagnetic energy-momentum tensor

**Key moves**
- Collect the coordinate-free forms of the chapter's four results.

## Concepts

### electric charge

*physical-quantity · introduced* · also: coupling constant q

A scalar property of a particle that sets how strongly its world line couples to the electromagnetic 1-form field; in the action it multiplies the line integral of A along the path.

**How introduced:** Defined operationally at the start of 42.1 as whatever lets a particle feel a field, then made concrete as the prefactor of the interaction action.

**Prerequisites:** relativistic free-particle action

$$
S_em = q \int A_\sigma dx^\sigma
$$

**Notes:** The Eddington epigraph pokes fun at the circularity of defining charge and force in terms of each other.

**Where:** GA §42.1 p.453 (pdf 470); GA §42.1 p.454 (pdf 471)

### relativistic free-particle action

*definition · revisited* · also: length action

The action S = -m times the integral of the proper time along a world line; extremizing it gives straight (geodesic) motion.

**How introduced:** Recalled as the starting point to which an interaction term is added.

**Prerequisites:** proper time, Euler-Lagrange equations

$$
S_m = -m \int \sqrt{-ds^2}
$$

**Where:** GA §42.1 p.454 (pdf 471)

### minimal coupling of a particle to a 1-form field

*principle · core* · also: simplest Lorentz-invariant coupling, displacement coupling

The interaction in which the particle's infinitesimal displacement dx^mu is contracted with a 1-form field A_mu(x); the resulting line integral is Lorentz invariant and independent of how the path is parametrized.

**How introduced:** Posed as a question: what is the simplest Lorentz-symmetric way to let a field change a particle's motion? The answer is claimed to be the one Nature uses for electromagnetism.

**Prerequisites:** 1-form, relativistic free-particle action

$$
S = \int [ -m(-\eta_{\mu\nu} u^\mu u^\nu)^{1/2} + q A_\sigma u^\sigma ] d\tau
$$

**Notes:** Exercise 42.13 contrasts this with a scalar coupling (m + lambda phi) that multiplies the proper time instead.

**Where:** GA §42.1 p.454 (pdf 471)

### electromagnetic 4-potential

*mathematical-object · core* · also: gauge field, electromagnetic 1-form field A, vector potential A^mu

The 1-form field A = A_mu dx^mu whose components, with index raised, are (V, vector A); E and B are built from its derivatives, and it is defined only up to adding the gradient of a scalar.

**How introduced:** Enters as the field in the coupling term of 42.1; in 42.2 it is named the gauge field and related to the scalar and vector potentials.

**Prerequisites:** 1-form, raising and lowering indices

$$
\tilde{A} = A_\mu dx^\mu
$$

$$
A^\mu = (A^0, A^i),\; A_0 = -A^0,\; A_i = A^i
$$

**Where:** GA §42.1 p.454 (pdf 471); GA §42.2 p.455 (pdf 472); GA §42.2 p.456 (pdf 473)

### electrostatic potential

*physical-quantity · mention* · also: scalar potential V, A^0

The time component A^0 = V of the 4-potential with index up; its negative gradient contributes to E.

**How introduced:** Identified when A_mu is mapped to A^mu with the Minkowski metric.

**Prerequisites:** electromagnetic 4-potential

$$
V = A^0 = -A_0
$$

**Where:** GA §42.2 p.456 (pdf 473)

### electric and magnetic fields from potentials

*definition · developed* · also: E and B in terms of A

E = -grad V - dA/dt and B = curl A, expressing the observable 3-vector fields as derivatives of the 4-potential.

**How introduced:** Defined directly in 42.2 to link the abstract gauge field to familiar fields.

**Prerequisites:** electromagnetic 4-potential

$$
\vec E = -\nabla A^0 - \partial_t \vec A
$$

$$
\vec B = \nabla\times\vec A
$$

**Where:** GA §42.2 p.456 (pdf 473)

### Faraday tensor

*mathematical-object · core* · also: field-strength tensor, electromagnetic field tensor, F_{mu nu}

The antisymmetric (0,2) tensor F_{alpha beta} = partial_alpha A_beta - partial_beta A_alpha whose six independent components are the electric field (time-space entries) and magnetic field (space-space entries).

**How introduced:** Not postulated: it appears as the antisymmetric combination of derivatives of A that remains after applying the Euler-Lagrange equations to the coupled particle action (Example 42.1). Then displayed as a matrix of E and B.

**Prerequisites:** electromagnetic 4-potential, antisymmetric tensor, Euler-Lagrange equations

$$
F_{\alpha\beta} = A_{\beta,\alpha} - A_{\alpha,\beta}
$$

$$
F_{0i} = -E^i,\; F_{12} = B^z,\; F_{23} = B^x,\; F_{31} = B^y
$$

**Notes:** Raising both indices flips the sign of the E entries only.

**Where:** GA §42.1 p.455 (pdf 472); GA §42.2 p.456 (pdf 473)

### Faraday 2-form

*mathematical-object · core* · also: F tilde, field 2-form

The Faraday tensor viewed as a differential 2-form, F = (1/2) F_{alpha beta} dx^alpha wedge dx^beta = E^i dx^i wedge dt + B^x dy wedge dz + B^y dz wedge dx + B^z dx wedge dy; it equals the exterior derivative of A.

**How introduced:** Antisymmetry motivates rewriting the tensor with wedge products (42.15); in 42.4 it is derived as dA and decomposed into electric and magnetic pieces.

**Prerequisites:** Faraday tensor, wedge product, exterior derivative

$$
\tilde F = \tfrac12 F_{\alpha\beta} dx^\alpha\wedge dx^\beta = d\tilde A
$$

**Where:** GA §42.1 p.455 (pdf 472); GA §42.4 p.460 (pdf 477)

### covariant Lorentz force law

*law · core* · also: equation of motion of a charge, dp/dtau = q F( , u)

The rate of change of a charged particle's momentum 1-form with proper time equals q times the Faraday 2-form with the 4-velocity inserted in its second slot; in curved spacetime the derivative is covariant, so the right side measures the departure from geodesic motion.

**How introduced:** Result of Example 42.1, rewritten symbolically in 42.17, upgraded to curved space in a margin note, and used in slot-filling form in Example 42.9.

**Prerequisites:** Faraday tensor, 4-velocity, momentum 1-form, covariant derivative

$$
m \eta_{\mu\alpha} \frac{d^2x^\mu}{d\tau^2} = q F_{\alpha\beta} u^\beta
$$

$$
\frac{D\tilde p}{d\tau} = m\nabla_u \tilde u = q\tilde F(\,,u)
$$

**Notes:** Spatial components give the relativistic q(E + v x B) (Exercise 42.1).

**Where:** GA §42.1 p.455 (pdf 472); GA §42.4 p.461 (pdf 478)

### antisymmetry of F and conservation of rest mass

*identity · introduced* · also: u . a = 0 from F(u,u) = 0

Because F is antisymmetric, F(u,u) = 0, so the electromagnetic 4-force is orthogonal to the 4-velocity; hence u.a = 0 and the particle's rest mass stays constant.

**How introduced:** Pointed out in a margin note as a consistency condition required by relativity (Chapter 1).

**Prerequisites:** Faraday tensor, 4-acceleration

$$
\tilde F(u,u) = 0 \Rightarrow u\cdot a = 0
$$

**Where:** GA §42.1 p.455 (pdf 472)

### 4-current

*physical-quantity · developed* · also: current 4-vector J

The vector J with components (rho, J_x, J_y, J_z) combining charge density and current density; it is the source of the electromagnetic field.

**How introduced:** Assembled from rho and the 3-current appearing in Maxwell's equations.

**Prerequisites:** 4-vector

$$
J^\mu = (\rho, \vec J)
$$

**Notes:** A margin note inverts the slogan: J tells field lines where to go.

**Where:** GA §42.2 p.456 (pdf 473)

### Maxwell's equations

*law · core* · also: Maxwell equations of motion

The field equations of electromagnetism: div E = rho, div B = 0, curl E = -dB/dt, curl B = J + dE/dt (natural units), splitting into a source-free pair and a sourced pair.

**How introduced:** Quoted in 3-vector form, then rewritten in index notation (42.24, 42.25), in curved space (42.32-42.33), and in form language (42.61, 42.75).

**Prerequisites:** electric and magnetic fields from potentials, 4-current

$$
\nabla\cdot\vec E = \rho,\; \nabla\cdot\vec B = 0,\; \nabla\times\vec E = -\partial_t\vec B,\; \nabla\times\vec B = \vec J + \partial_t \vec E
$$

**Where:** GA §42.2 p.456 (pdf 473)

### electromagnetic Bianchi identity

*identity · core* · also: cyclic identity, homogeneous Maxwell equations in index form

The statement that summing the first derivatives of F over cyclic permutations of three indices gives zero; it is automatic when F comes from a potential and contains div B = 0 and Faraday's law.

**How introduced:** Stated in 42.2 with instructions for which index values give which Maxwell equation; re-derived in 42.4 as the component form of dF = 0.

**Prerequisites:** Faraday tensor, exterior derivative

$$
F_{\alpha\beta,\gamma} + F_{\gamma\alpha,\beta} + F_{\beta\gamma,\alpha} = 0
$$

**Notes:** Linked to the gravitational Bianchi identity of Chapter 13; the connection is developed in Chapter 43.

**Where:** GA §42.2 p.456 (pdf 473); GA §42.4 p.462 (pdf 479)

### inhomogeneous Maxwell equations in tensor form

*law · core* · also: F^{mu nu}_{,nu} = J^mu, sourced Maxwell equations

The divergence of the (2,0) Faraday tensor equals the 4-current; the time component is Gauss's law and the space components are the Ampere-Maxwell law.

**How introduced:** Stated after the Bianchi identity as the equation that reproduces the two current-dependent Maxwell equations.

**Prerequisites:** Faraday tensor, 4-current

$$
F^{\mu\nu}{}_{,\nu} = J^\mu
$$

**Where:** GA §42.2 p.456 (pdf 473)

### Maxwell Lagrangian density

*definition · developed* · also: -1/4 F F Lagrangian

The scalar density built from minus one quarter of the full contraction of F with itself plus the current contracted with the potential, whose field Euler-Lagrange equations are the sourced Maxwell equations.

**How introduced:** Recalled from Chapter 40 in Example 42.2 and generalized from the point-particle coupling; expanded in terms of A.

**Prerequisites:** Lagrangian field theory, Faraday tensor

$$
S = \int d^4x ( -\tfrac14 F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} + J^\mu A_\mu )
$$

**Where:** GA §42.2 p.457 (pdf 474)

### Maxwell equations in curved spacetime

*law · introduced* · also: covariant Maxwell equations

The curved-spacetime versions obtained by replacing partial with covariant derivatives: the covariant divergence of F vanishes (source-free) and the cyclic identity holds with semicolons.

**How introduced:** Example 42.3 builds F with semicolons, raises indices with g, applies Euler-Lagrange; a margin note gives the cyclic identity with semicolons.

**Prerequisites:** covariant derivative, Maxwell Lagrangian density, comma-goes-to-semicolon rule

$$
F_{\gamma\delta;\beta} g^{\beta\delta} = 0
$$

$$
F_{\alpha\beta;\gamma} + F_{\gamma\alpha;\beta} + F_{\beta\gamma;\alpha} = 0
$$

**Notes:** The Christoffel terms cancel in both A_{nu;mu} - A_{mu;nu} and the cyclic sum, a point the book does not make.

**Where:** GA §42.2 p.457 (pdf 474)

### comma-goes-to-semicolon rule

*technique · revisited* · also: minimal substitution to curved space

The recipe that a valid flat-space tensor equation written with partial derivatives becomes its curved-spacetime version when partial derivatives are replaced by covariant derivatives and eta by g.

**How introduced:** Invoked repeatedly: for the force law, for F, for the Lagrangian and Maxwell equations, and for T_{mu nu}.

**Prerequisites:** covariant derivative

**Where:** GA §42.1 p.455 (pdf 472); GA §42.2 p.457 (pdf 474)

### electromagnetic energy-momentum tensor

*physical-quantity · developed* · also: Maxwell stress-energy tensor, T^{mu nu} of the field

The symmetric tensor T^{mu nu} = F^{mu alpha} F^nu_alpha - (1/4) eta^{mu nu} F_{alpha beta} F^{alpha beta} (with g in curved spacetime) whose time-time part is the field energy density, time-space part the Poynting flux, and space-space part minus the Maxwell stress; it is the source of curvature for electromagnetic fields.

**How introduced:** Quoted using the Chapter 40 method, unpacked into E and B in Example 42.4, and given in curved-space (0,2) form; Exercises 42.11-42.12 derive it by symmetrizing the canonical tensor.

**Prerequisites:** Faraday tensor, energy-momentum tensor

$$
T^{00} = \tfrac12(E^2+B^2)
$$

$$
T^{0j} = (\vec E\times\vec B)^j
$$

$$
T^{jk} = -[E^jE^k + B^jB^k - \tfrac12(E^2+B^2)\delta^{jk}]
$$

**Where:** GA §42.2 p.458 (pdf 475)

### Poynting vector

*physical-quantity · introduced* · also: energy flux of the field, E x B

The vector E x B (times 1/mu_0 in SI) giving the field's energy flow per unit area per unit time; it equals T^{0j} in the book's units.

**How introduced:** Identified in Example 42.4 as the momentum-density components of T, with a biographical margin note.

**Prerequisites:** electromagnetic energy-momentum tensor

$$
S = E \times H = E \times B/\mu_0
$$

**Where:** GA §42.2 p.458 (pdf 475)

### Maxwell stress tensor

*physical-quantity · mention* · also: T^{jk} of the field

The spatial block of the electromagnetic energy-momentum tensor, describing momentum flux (pressure and shear) carried by the field.

**How introduced:** Computed without a name as the all-spatial components in Example 42.4.

**Prerequisites:** electromagnetic energy-momentum tensor

$$
T^{jk} = -[E^jE^k + B^jB^k - \tfrac12(E^2+B^2)\delta^{jk}]
$$

**Notes:** Book does not use this name; conventions differ by an overall sign across texts.

**Where:** GA §42.2 p.458 (pdf 475)

### gauge transformation

*operation · core* · also: A -> A - d chi

Replacement of the potential by A_mu - partial_mu chi for any smooth scalar chi; it leaves F, hence E and B, unchanged.

**How introduced:** First noted in 42.2 as a freedom in the potentials, elevated to a principle in 42.3, and proved in one line in 42.4 using dd = 0.

**Prerequisites:** electromagnetic 4-potential, dd = 0 identity

$$
A_\mu \to A_\mu - \partial_\mu\chi
$$

$$
\tilde A\to\tilde A - d\chi,\; d\tilde A \to d\tilde A - dd\chi = d\tilde A
$$

**Notes:** Exercise 42.3 uses the opposite sign of chi, which is equivalent.

**Where:** GA §42.2 p.456 (pdf 473); GA §42.4 p.461 (pdf 478)

### gauge freedom

*principle · core* · also: gauge invariance, choice of gauge

The principle that physical measurements depend only on gauge-invariant quantities, so the potential may be changed by any gradient; a particular choice of chi is a gauge and can be made to simplify calculations.

**How introduced:** Stated at the start of 42.3, likened to choosing a language that conveys the same message, and flagged as important for gravitational waves.

**Prerequisites:** gauge transformation

**Where:** GA §42.3 p.458 (pdf 475)

### d'Alembertian operator

*definition · introduced* · also: partial squared, wave operator

The Lorentz-invariant second-order operator partial^2 = -partial_t^2 + nabla^2 (signature -+++).

**How introduced:** Introduced as notation in a margin note to Example 42.5.

**Prerequisites:** Minkowski metric

$$
\partial^2 \equiv -\partial_t^2 + \nabla^2
$$

**Notes:** Sign is opposite to the box operator in many particle-physics texts.

**Where:** GA §42.3 p.458 (pdf 475)

### Lorenz gauge

*convention · core* · also: Lorentz gauge (misattribution), harmonic gauge (gravity analogue), de Donder gauge (gravity analogue)

The gauge condition partial_mu A^mu = 0, reachable by choosing chi to solve partial^2 chi = partial^mu A_mu; in it the Maxwell equations decouple into wave equations for each component of A.

**How introduced:** Motivated in Example 42.5 by wanting to kill the divergence term in the field equation written in terms of A.

**Prerequisites:** gauge transformation, d'Alembertian operator, inhomogeneous Maxwell equations in tensor form

$$
\partial_\mu A^\mu = 0
$$

$$
\partial^2 A^\mu = -J^\mu
$$

**Notes:** Margin note on naming: due to Ludvig Lorenz, not Hendrik Lorentz.

**Where:** GA §42.3 p.458 (pdf 475); GA §42.3 p.459 (pdf 476)

### residual gauge freedom

*definition · introduced* · also: restricted gauge transformations

The leftover gauge transformations that preserve a gauge condition; for the Lorenz condition these are shifts by functions xi satisfying partial^2 xi = 0.

**How introduced:** Example 42.6 notes the Lorenz condition does not fix A uniquely and uses the leftover freedom to impose a further condition.

**Prerequisites:** Lorenz gauge

$$
A'_\mu \to A'_\mu - \partial_\mu \xi,\; \partial^2\xi = 0
$$

**Where:** GA §42.3 p.459 (pdf 476)

### Coulomb gauge

*convention · introduced* · also: radiation gauge (when combined with A_0 = 0), transverse gauge

The gauge with div A = 0. In the book it is reached from Lorenz gauge by also setting A_0 = 0, which is only consistent in source-free regions (this combination is often called radiation gauge).

**How introduced:** Example 42.6 uses residual freedom with partial_0 xi = A'_0 to set A''_0 = 0, after which the Lorenz condition becomes div A = 0.

**Prerequisites:** residual gauge freedom, Lorenz gauge

$$
A''_0 = 0,\; \nabla\cdot\vec A'' = 0
$$

**Notes:** Pattern reused for transverse-traceless gauge in Chapter 46.

**Where:** GA §42.3 p.459 (pdf 476)

### electromagnetic wave equation

*law · developed* · also: vacuum wave equation for A

In Lorenz gauge and without sources each component of A obeys -partial_t^2 A + nabla^2 A = 0, so disturbances propagate at the speed of light as plane waves.

**How introduced:** Falls out of Example 42.5 once the divergence term is removed.

**Prerequisites:** Lorenz gauge, d'Alembertian operator

$$
-\partial_t^2 A^\mu + \nabla^2 A^\mu = 0
$$

$$
\phi = C e^{-i(\omega t - \vec k\cdot\vec x)}
$$

**Where:** GA §42.3 p.459 (pdf 476)

### Green's function solution of Poisson's equation

*technique · introduced* · also: static potentials, 1/r integral

The solution of nabla^2 A = -source as (1/4 pi) times the integral of the source divided by distance, summing contributions from each source element.

**How introduced:** Example 42.7, with geometry in Fig. 42.1 and identities supplied in Exercise 42.7.

**Prerequisites:** Poisson equation, Dirac delta

$$
A^0(\vec x) = \frac{1}{4\pi}\int d\mathcal V' \frac{\rho(\vec x')}{|\vec x - \vec x'|}
$$

**Where:** GA §42.3 p.459 (pdf 476)

### retarded time

*definition · introduced* · also: t_r

The earlier time t - |x - x'|/c at which a source element must be evaluated so that its influence, travelling at light speed, reaches the field point at time t.

**How introduced:** Introduced in Example 42.7 as the fix needed when static potentials are made time dependent.

**Prerequisites:** electromagnetic wave equation

$$
t_r = t - |\vec x - \vec x'|/c
$$

**Where:** GA §42.3 p.459 (pdf 476)

### retarded potentials

*solution-or-model · developed* · also: Lienard-Wiechert-type integrals (continuum)

The solution A^mu(t,x) = (1/4 pi) integral of J^mu(t_r, x')/|x - x'| over the source volume for the Lorenz-gauge wave equation with sources.

**How introduced:** Obtained in Example 42.7 by replacing the source time with retarded time; Exercise 42.7 verifies it solves the wave equation and Exercise 42.8 shows it transforms as a 4-vector.

**Prerequisites:** retarded time, Green's function solution of Poisson's equation, Lorenz gauge

$$
A^\mu(t,\vec x) = \frac{1}{4\pi}\int d\mathcal V' \frac{J^\mu(t_r,\vec x')}{|\vec x - \vec x'|}
$$

**Notes:** Promised to reappear for the metric perturbation sourced by masses (Chs 45-46).

**Where:** GA §42.3 p.459 (pdf 476); GA §42.3 p.460 (pdf 477)

### exterior derivative

*operation · revisited* · also: d operator

The derivative that maps a p-form to a (p+1)-form, d(omega_{a...} dx^a ...) = partial_mu omega_{a...} dx^mu wedge dx^a ..., and satisfies dd = 0.

**How introduced:** Applied to the 1-form A to produce F (Example 42.8), to F to produce the homogeneous Maxwell equations (Example 42.10), and to *F (Example 42.13).

**Prerequisites:** wedge product, differential form

$$
d\tilde A = \partial_\mu A_\nu\, dx^\mu\wedge dx^\nu
$$

**Where:** GA §42.4 p.460 (pdf 477); GA §42.4 p.462 (pdf 479)

### dd = 0 identity

*identity · developed* · also: nilpotency of d, Poincare identity

Applying the exterior derivative twice always gives zero, because mixed partial derivatives are symmetric while wedge products are antisymmetric.

**How introduced:** Used twice in 42.4: to show F = dA is automatically closed and to show gauge transformations leave F unchanged.

**Prerequisites:** exterior derivative

$$
dd = 0
$$

**Where:** GA §42.4 p.460 (pdf 477); GA §42.4 p.461 (pdf 478)

### exact and closed forms

*definition · mention* · also: exact implies closed

A form is exact if it is d of another form and closed if its exterior derivative vanishes; every exact form is closed.

**How introduced:** Recalled to note that F = dA is exact and therefore closed.

**Prerequisites:** exterior derivative, dd = 0 identity

$$
\tilde F = d\tilde A \Rightarrow d\tilde F = 0
$$

**Where:** GA §42.4 p.460 (pdf 477)

*technique · introduced* · also: field circulating in tubes, honeycomb picture

A visualization in which a 2-form is drawn as two families of intersecting surfaces; each magnetic component, e.g. B^x dy wedge dz, is pictured as circulation within the tubes cut out by the dy and dz surfaces, replacing field-line arrows.

**How introduced:** Built on the Chapter 32 image of a 2-form as intersecting planes and illustrated in Fig. 42.2.

**Where:** GA §42.4 p.460 (pdf 477)

### helical motion in a magnetic field

*phenomenon · introduced* · also: cyclotron motion, gyration

A charge in a uniform magnetic field circles around the field direction at fixed rate while drifting uniformly along it, tracing a helix.

**How introduced:** Solutions of the component equations from Example 42.9, sketched in Fig. 42.3 and proven in Exercise 42.9.

### Hodge dual

*operation · developed* · also: star operator, duality operation

The metric-dependent map that turns a p-vector or p-form into an (n-p)-form using the Levi-Civita tensor, e.g. (*F)_{alpha beta} = (1/2) epsilon_{mu nu alpha beta} F^{mu nu} in four dimensions.

**How introduced:** Recipe taken from Chapter 37 and applied to the (2,0) Faraday tensor and the current vector.

**Prerequisites:** Levi-Civita symbol, raising and lowering indices

$$
(\star F)_{\alpha\beta} = \tfrac12\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}F^{\mu\nu}
$$

$$
\star\star F = -F
$$

**Where:** GA §42.4 p.462 (pdf 479); GA §42.4 p.463 (pdf 480)

### Maxwell 2-form

*mathematical-object · core* · also: Maxwell tensor, dual Faraday tensor, *F

The Hodge dual of the Faraday tensor, a 2-form whose components are those of F with E replaced by -B and B replaced by E; its exterior derivative is set equal to the current 3-form.

**How introduced:** Needed because the source must couple to something other than F (whose d vanishes identically); computed component by component in Example 42.11.

**Prerequisites:** Hodge dual, Faraday tensor

$$
\star F = E^x dy\wedge dz + E^y dz\wedge dx + E^z dx\wedge dy + B^x dt\wedge dx + B^y dt\wedge dy + B^z dt\wedge dz
$$

**Where:** GA §42.4 p.462 (pdf 479); GA §42.4 p.463 (pdf 480)

### electric-magnetic duality rotation

*identity · introduced* · also: E -> -B, B -> E

The observation that dualizing F corresponds to the component swap E -> -B, B -> E, so applying it twice gives -F.

**How introduced:** Read off by comparing the matrices of *F and F in Example 42.11.

**Prerequisites:** Maxwell 2-form

$$
\vec E\to -\vec B,\; \vec B\to\vec E
$$

**Where:** GA §42.4 p.463 (pdf 480)

### current 3-form

*mathematical-object · introduced* · also: dual current, *J

The Hodge dual of the 4-current: a 3-form whose purely spatial component is the charge density and whose three time-containing components are minus the current density components; integrating it over a 3-volume counts charge, and it expresses charge conservation.

**How introduced:** Constructed in Example 42.12 as the object to which d*F must be equated; its role in charge conservation is deferred to Chapter 43.

**Prerequisites:** Hodge dual, 4-current

$$
(\star J)_{\alpha\beta\gamma} = \varepsilon_{\mu\alpha\beta\gamma}J^\mu
$$

**Where:** GA §42.4 p.463 (pdf 480)

### Maxwell's equations in differential-form language

*law · core* · also: dF = 0 and d*F = *J

The complete coordinate-free statement of electromagnetism: F = dA (so dF = 0, containing div B = 0 and Faraday's law) and d*F = *J (containing Gauss's law and Ampere-Maxwell).

**How introduced:** Built step by step in 42.4 from exterior derivatives of A, F and *F, with explicit component expansions to confirm each pair.

**Prerequisites:** Faraday 2-form, Maxwell 2-form, current 3-form, dd = 0 identity

$$
d\tilde F = 0
$$

$$
d\star F = \star J
$$

**Notes:** dF = 0 needs no metric; the metric enters only through the star.

**Where:** GA §42.4 p.461 (pdf 478); GA §42.4 p.464 (pdf 481)

*mathematical-object · mention* · also: epsilon tensor, volume form components

The totally antisymmetric symbol with epsilon_{0123} = +1 in this chapter's usage, used to build Hodge duals.

**How introduced:** Used without re-definition in Examples 42.11 and 42.12; the ordered-sum notation epsilon_{|mu nu| alpha beta} absorbs the factor 1/2.

**Prerequisites:** antisymmetric tensor

\varepsilon_{0123} = +1

### Wheeler slogan for electromagnetism

*historical-idea · mention* · also: fields tell charges how to move

A mnemonic parallel to Wheeler's gravity slogan: the electromagnetic field dictates how charges accelerate, and charges and currents dictate how the field is arranged.

**How introduced:** Offered on the opening page to frame electromagnetism as a model for gravity.

### projection tensors

**How introduced:** Only in Exercise 42.10.

### scalar theory of gravitation

*solution-or-model · mention* · also: Padmanabhan scalar gravity, conformally flat gravity

A trial theory in which a scalar field multiplies the particle's proper-time action as (1 + Phi); it satisfies the equivalence principle and amounts to a conformally flat metric (1+Phi)^2 eta, with Poisson's equation as field equation, but cannot couple to energy without modification.

**How introduced:** Exercises 42.13-42.14 apply the chapter's coupling-plus-Lagrangian method to gravity.

### (42.1) Free-particle action · supporting · GA §42.1 p.454 (pdf 471)

$$
S_{\rm m} = -m\int\sqrt{-ds^2}

Starting action: minus mass times elapsed proper time.

**Symbols:** m rest mass; ds^2 = eta_{mu nu} dx^mu dx^nu

### (42.2) Charge-field interaction action · central · GA §42.1 p.454 (pdf 471)

$$

Simplest Lorentz-invariant coupling: line integral of the potential 1-form along the world line, weighted by charge.

**Symbols:** q charge; A_sigma components of the gauge 1-form

### (42.3) Total particle action · supporting · GA §42.1 p.454 (pdf 471)

$$
S = \int\left[-m\left(-\eta_{\mu\nu}\frac{dx^\mu}{d\tau}\frac{dx^\nu}{d\tau}\right)^{1/2} + qA_\sigma\frac{dx^\sigma}{d\tau}\right]d\tau

Lagrangian for a massive charge in an external field, parametrized by proper time.

**Symbols:** tau proper time

### (42.5) Free part of Euler-Lagrange · derivation-step · GA §42.1 p.454 (pdf 471)

Kinetic term contributes mass times covariant acceleration once proper-time normalization is used.

The potential changes along the path because the particle moves through a varying field.

F_{\alpha\beta;\gamma} + F_{\gamma\alpha;\beta} + F_{\beta\gamma;\alpha} = 0

Homogeneous pair in curved spacetime (margin note).

### (42.34) Electromagnetic energy-momentum tensor (flat) · central · GA §42.2 p.458 (pdf 475)

T^{\mu\nu} = F^{\mu\alpha}F^{\nu}{}_{\alpha} - \tfrac14\eta^{\mu\nu}F_{\alpha\beta}F^{\alpha\beta}

Energy, momentum and stress carried by the field.

T^{00} = \tfrac12(\vec E^2 + \vec B^2)

Energy per unit volume of the field.

$$
T^{0j} = (\vec E\times\vec B)^j

Field momentum density equals energy flux (c = 1).

### (42.37) Field stress · supporting · GA §42.2 p.458 (pdf 475)

T^{jk} = -\left[(E^jE^k + B^jB^k) - \tfrac12(\vec E^2+\vec B^2)\delta^{jk}\right]

Momentum flux: tension along field lines, pressure across them.

### (42.38) Electromagnetic energy-momentum tensor (curved) · central · GA §42.2 p.458 (pdf 475)

The (0,2) tensor that sources curvature in the field equations; repeated as (42.78).

### (42.39) d'Alembertian · supporting · GA §42.3 p.458 (pdf 475)

$$
\partial^2 \equiv -\frac{\partial^2}{\partial t^2} + \nabla^2

Wave operator used in the gauge section.

### (42.40) Maxwell equation in terms of A · derivation-step · GA §42.3 p.458 (pdf 475)

$$
\partial^\mu(\partial_\nu A^\nu) - \partial^2A^\mu = J^\mu

Sourced equation written for the potential; the first term motivates gauge fixing.

### (42.41) Condition on chi for Lorenz gauge · derivation-step · GA §42.3 p.458 (pdf 475)

$$
\partial^\mu A'_\mu = \partial^\mu A_\mu - \partial^\mu\partial_\mu\chi = 0

Choose chi solving partial^2 chi = partial^mu A_mu.

### (42.42) Lorenz-gauge field equation · central · GA §42.3 p.459 (pdf 476)

$$
\partial^2A^\mu = -J^\mu

Each component of the potential obeys a sourced wave equation.

### (42.43) Vacuum wave equation · supporting · GA §42.3 p.459 (pdf 476)

$$
-\frac{\partial^2A^\mu}{\partial t^2} + \nabla^2A^\mu = 0

Electromagnetic disturbances travel at light speed.

\partial_0\xi = A'_0\quad(\partial^2\xi = 0)

Uses leftover freedom to set A''_0 = 0, so the Lorenz condition becomes div A = 0.

**Symbols:** xi residual gauge function

\nabla^2A^0 = -\rho,\qquad \nabla^2\vec A = -\vec J\quad(\text{static})

Time-independent limit of the Lorenz-gauge equation.

A^0(\vec x) = \frac1{4\pi}\int d\mathcal V'\frac{\rho(\vec x')}{|\vec x-\vec x'|},\quad \vec A(\vec x) = \frac1{4\pi}\int d\mathcal V'\frac{\vec J(\vec x')}{|\vec x-\vec x'|}

Superpose source elements weighted by inverse distance.

### (42.48) Retarded time · supporting · GA §42.3 p.459 (pdf 476)

Source must be evaluated when its signal left.

**Symbols:** c restored temporarily

### (42.50) Retarded potential · central · GA §42.3 p.460 (pdf 477)

$$
A^\mu(t,\vec x) = \frac1{4\pi}\int d\mathcal V'\frac{J^\mu(t_{\rm r},\vec x')}{|\vec x-\vec x'|}

General solution of the sourced Lorenz-gauge wave equation; template for gravitational potentials. (42.49) gives the same in 0 and spatial parts.

### (42.51) Faraday 2-form as exterior derivative · central · GA §42.4 p.460 (pdf 477)

\tilde F = d\tilde A

Coordinate-free definition of the field strength.

### (42.52) Component check of dA · derivation-step · GA §42.4 p.460 (pdf 477)

d\tilde A = \frac{\partial A_\nu}{\partial x^\mu}dx^\mu\wedge dx^\nu = \tfrac12\left(\frac{\partial A_\nu}{\partial x^\mu} - \frac{\partial A_\mu}{\partial x^\nu}\right)dx^\mu\wedge dx^\nu

Antisymmetrizing reproduces F_{mu nu} of (42.13) = (42.53).

### (42.54) Faraday 2-form in E and B · central · GA §42.4 p.460 (pdf 477)

\tilde F = E^x dx\wedge dt + E^y dy\wedge dt + E^z dz\wedge dt + B^x dy\wedge dz + B^y dz\wedge dx + B^z dx\wedge dy

Electric parts pair space with time, magnetic parts pair two spatial directions.

### (42.56) Slot-filling for uniform B^x · derivation-step · GA §42.4 p.461 (pdf 478)

$$
\frac{d\tilde p}{d\tau} = qB^x\,dy\wedge dz(\,,u) = qB^x(dy\,u^z - dz\,u^y)
$$

Inserting u into the magnetic 2-form yields the force 1-form.

### (42.57) Component equations for helix · supporting · GA §42.4 p.461 (pdf 478)

$$
\frac{dp_x}{d\tau} = 0,\quad \frac{dp_y}{d\tau} = qB^x\frac{dz}{d\tau},\quad \frac{dp_z}{d\tau} = -qB^x\frac{dy}{d\tau}

Momentum along B is conserved; transverse components rotate.

### (42.59) Gauge transformation as forms · central · GA §42.4 p.461 (pdf 478)

$$
\tilde A\to\tilde A - d\chi

Gauge freedom is adding an exact 1-form.

### (42.60) Gauge invariance of F · supporting · GA §42.4 p.461 (pdf 478)

$$
\tilde F = d\tilde A\to d\tilde A - dd\chi = \tilde F
$$

dd = 0 makes F, hence E and B, gauge invariant.

### (42.61) Homogeneous Maxwell equations as forms · central · GA §42.4 p.461 (pdf 478)

$$
d\tilde F = 0
$$

Automatic since F = dA; contains div B = 0 and Faraday's law.

### (42.62) dF in components · derivation-step · GA §42.4 p.461 (pdf 478)

$$
d\tilde F = \tfrac12\frac{\partial F_{\alpha\beta}}{\partial x^\sigma}\,dx^\sigma\wedge dx^\alpha\wedge dx^\beta = 0
$$

Starting point for extracting the homogeneous Maxwell equations; wedge antisymmetry kills repeated indices, leaving only the cyclic sum.

### (42.63) Expansion of dF · supporting · GA §42.4 p.462 (pdf 479)

$$
d\tilde F = (\nabla\cdot\vec B)\,dx\wedge dy\wedge dz + (\partial_tB^x + \partial_yE^z - \partial_zE^y)\,dt\wedge dy\wedge dz + (\partial_tB^y + \partial_zE^x - \partial_xE^z)\,dt\wedge dz\wedge dx + (\partial_tB^z + \partial_xE^y - \partial_yE^x)\,dt\wedge dx\wedge dy = 0

Setting each coefficient to zero gives (42.64): div B = 0 and curl E = -dB/dt.

### (42.64) Homogeneous Maxwell equations recovered · supporting · GA §42.4 p.462 (pdf 479)

$$
\nabla\cdot\vec B = 0,\qquad \nabla\times\vec E = -\frac{\partial\vec B}{\partial t}
$$

The four coefficients of dF = 0 are exactly the no-monopole law and Faraday's law.

### (42.65) Bianchi identity from dF = 0 · supporting · GA §42.4 p.462 (pdf 479)

\frac{\partial F_{\alpha\beta}}{\partial x^\gamma} + \frac{\partial F_{\gamma\alpha}}{\partial x^\beta} + \frac{\partial F_{\beta\gamma}}{\partial x^\alpha} = 0

Only all-different index triples survive the wedge, reproducing (42.24).

The source equation must use the dual of the (2,0) Faraday tensor, not F itself.

Hodge dual of the (2,0) Faraday tensor.

## Worked examples

### Example 42.1 · standard · GA §42.1 p.454 (pdf 471)

**Problem:** Find the equation of motion of a massive charge from the action combining the free-particle term and the displacement coupling to A.

**Method:** Split L into L_m and L_em; differentiate each with respect to the 4-velocity and the position; use proper-time normalization so the free term gives m times acceleration; collect the two derivative-of-A terms and relabel dummy indices.

**Key insight:** The piece of dA/dtau along the path cancels against part of the gradient term, leaving only an antisymmetric combination of derivatives; the force is therefore built from an antisymmetric tensor.

**Result:** m eta_{mu alpha} d^2x^mu/dtau^2 = q(partial_alpha A_beta - partial_beta A_alpha) u^beta, i.e. m a_alpha = q F_{alpha beta} u^beta.

**Concepts:** minimal coupling of a particle to a 1-form field, Faraday tensor, covariant Lorentz force law

### Example 42.2 · intro · GA §42.2 p.457 (pdf 474)

**Problem:** Write the action for the electromagnetic field itself together with its coupling to a current distribution.

**Method:** Take the Chapter 40 field Lagrangian -1/4 F F, generalize the point-charge coupling to the density J^mu A_mu, and expand F in terms of derivatives of A.

**Key insight:** The same A that couples to charges also carries its own dynamics; varying A in this action returns the sourced Maxwell equations.

**Result:** S = integral d^4x [ -1/4 (partial_mu A_nu - partial_nu A_mu)(partial^mu A^nu - partial^nu A^mu) + J^mu A_mu ].

**Concepts:** Maxwell Lagrangian density, 4-current

### Example 42.3 · standard · GA §42.2 p.457 (pdf 474)

**Problem:** Obtain source-free Maxwell equations in curved spacetime from a Lagrangian.

**Method:** Replace commas by semicolons in F, write the Lagrangian with explicit inverse metrics, compute the derivative with respect to A_{alpha;beta}, take its covariant divergence and set to zero, then strip an overall inverse metric.

**Key insight:** The comma-to-semicolon rule plus careful index raising gives the covariant divergence of F vanishing; the homogeneous pair follows by the same rule applied to the cyclic identity.

**Result:** F_{gamma delta;beta} g^{beta delta} = 0 and F_{alpha beta;gamma} + cyclic = 0.

**Concepts:** Maxwell equations in curved spacetime, comma-goes-to-semicolon rule, Maxwell Lagrangian density

### Example 42.4 · standard · GA §42.2 p.458 (pdf 475)

**Problem:** Express the components of the electromagnetic energy-momentum tensor in terms of E and B.

**Method:** Insert the F^{mu nu} matrix into T^{mu nu} = F^{mu alpha}F^nu_alpha - (1/4) eta^{mu nu} F^2 using F_{alpha beta}F^{alpha beta} = 2(B^2 - E^2) (details left to Exercise 42.6).

**Key insight:** Energy density, Poynting flux and Maxwell stress are all blocks of a single tensor, which is what gravity responds to.

**Result:** T^{00} = (E^2 + B^2)/2; T^{0j} = (E x B)^j; T^{jk} = -[E^jE^k + B^jB^k - (1/2)(E^2+B^2) delta^{jk}].

**Concepts:** electromagnetic energy-momentum tensor, Poynting vector, Maxwell stress tensor

### Example 42.5 · standard · GA §42.3 p.458 (pdf 475)

**Problem:** Simplify the sourced Maxwell equation written in terms of A by a choice of gauge.

**Method:** Write partial_nu F^{mu nu} in terms of A; perform A -> A - d chi and demand the divergence of the new potential vanishes, which requires partial^2 chi = partial^mu A_mu.

**Key insight:** Gauge freedom can remove the coupling between components, turning Maxwell's equations into four independent wave equations.

**Result:** partial^2 A^mu = -J^mu; in vacuum a wave equation with propagation at c.

**Concepts:** Lorenz gauge, gauge transformation, electromagnetic wave equation, d'Alembertian operator

### Example 42.6 · standard · GA §42.3 p.459 (pdf 476)

**Problem:** Show the Lorenz condition leaves freedom and use it to fix A further.

**Method:** Shift again by a function xi obeying the wave equation so the Lorenz condition is preserved; pick its time derivative equal to A'_0 so the time component vanishes.

**Key insight:** Gauge conditions often come with residual freedom that can be spent on a second condition; the same pattern returns for gravitational waves.

**Result:** A''_0 = 0 and div A'' = 0 (called Coulomb gauge in the book).

**Concepts:** residual gauge freedom, Coulomb gauge, Lorenz gauge

### Example 42.7 · standard · GA §42.3 p.459 (pdf 476)

**Problem:** Solve partial^2 A^mu = -J^mu for a localized distribution of charges and currents, first statically, then with time dependence.

**Method:** In the static case reduce to Poisson equations and superpose 1/(4 pi |x - x'|) kernels over the source volume (Fig. 42.1); for time dependence evaluate the source at retarded time t - |x - x'|/c.

**Key insight:** Because influences travel at light speed, the potential here and now is the sum of what each source element was doing when its light left it.

**Result:** A^mu(t,x) = (1/4 pi) integral dV' J^mu(t_r, x')/|x - x'|.

**Concepts:** Green's function solution of Poisson's equation, retarded time, retarded potentials

### Example 42.8 · intro · GA §42.4 p.460 (pdf 477)

**Problem:** Verify that the exterior derivative of the 1-form A is the Faraday 2-form.

**Method:** Apply d to A_nu dx^nu, split the result into two halves, swap the dummy indices in one and use antisymmetry of the wedge to collect an antisymmetric coefficient.

**Key insight:** The antisymmetric combination found by brute-force Euler-Lagrange in Example 42.1 is exactly what the exterior derivative produces automatically.

**Result:** F_{mu nu} = partial_mu A_nu - partial_nu A_mu, so F = dA.

**Concepts:** exterior derivative, Faraday 2-form

### Example 42.9 · intro · GA §42.4 p.461 (pdf 478)

**Problem:** Find the equations of motion of a charge in a uniform magnetic field along x using the 2-form F = B^x dy wedge dz.

**Method:** Expand the wedge as dy (x) dz - dz (x) dy, feed u into the second slot, and read off the coefficients of dx, dy, dz.

**Key insight:** Slot filling turns the geometric force law into ordinary component ODEs; momentum along B is conserved and the transverse components rotate into each other.

**Result:** dp_x/dtau = 0, dp_y/dtau = qB^x u^z, dp_z/dtau = -qB^x u^y; solutions are helices about x (Fig. 42.3, Exercise 42.9).

**Concepts:** covariant Lorentz force law, helical motion in a magnetic field, Faraday 2-form

### Example 42.10 · standard · GA §42.4 p.462 (pdf 479)

**Problem:** Expand dF = 0 in terms of E and B.

**Method:** Differentiate each term of the E-B decomposition of F, reorder wedge products into four basis 3-forms, and set every coefficient to zero.

**Key insight:** The purely spatial 3-form coefficient is div B; the three that contain dt are the components of Faraday's law. Terms with repeated indices vanish, which is why only cyclic index combinations appear.

**Result:** div B = 0 and curl E = -dB/dt; equivalently the cyclic identity (42.65).

**Concepts:** Maxwell's equations in differential-form language, electromagnetic Bianchi identity, exterior derivative

### Example 42.11 · standard · GA §42.4 p.462 (pdf 479)

**Problem:** Compute the components of the Hodge dual of the (2,0) Faraday tensor.

**Method:** Use (*F)_{alpha beta} = (1/2) epsilon_{mu nu alpha beta} F^{mu nu}, keep only the single ordered pair (mu, nu) complementary to (alpha, beta), and evaluate the sign of each Levi-Civita permutation.

**Key insight:** Duality exchanges time-space and space-space slots, so electric and magnetic roles swap: E -> -B, B -> E, and dualizing twice gives -F.

**Result:** (*F)_{01} = B^x, (*F)_{02} = B^y, (*F)_{03} = B^z, (*F)_{23} = E^x, (*F)_{13} = -E^y, (*F)_{12} = E^z.

**Concepts:** Hodge dual, Maxwell 2-form, electric-magnetic duality rotation, Levi-Civita symbol

### Example 42.12 · standard · GA §42.4 p.463 (pdf 480)

**Problem:** Compute the dual of the 4-current as a 3-form.

**Method:** Apply (*J)_{alpha beta gamma} = epsilon_{mu alpha beta gamma} J^mu for each complementary index set and track permutation signs.

**Key insight:** Charge density sits on the purely spatial 3-volume element dx dy dz, while each current component sits on a 3-surface made of time and two spatial directions: it counts charge crossing a spatial area over an interval of time. Integrating *J over a region therefore gives either the charge inside or the charge that flowed through a boundary.

**Result:** *J = rho dx^dy^dz - J^x dt^dy^dz - J^y dt^dz^dx - J^z dt^dx^dy.

**Concepts:** current 3-form, Hodge dual

### Example 42.13 · standard · GA §42.4 p.463 (pdf 480)

**Problem:** Take the exterior derivative of the Maxwell 2-form and match it to the current 3-form.

**Method:** Differentiate each term of *F, reorder into the same four basis 3-forms as *J, and equate coefficients.

**Key insight:** Since dF is identically zero, the only way to bring sources in is through the dual; d*F = *J then yields Gauss and Ampere-Maxwell.

**Result:** div E = rho and curl B = J + dE/dt, i.e. d*F = *J. (The printed expansion flips the signs of the B-derivative terms; with correct signs the matching works.)

**Concepts:** Maxwell's equations in differential-form language, Maxwell 2-form, current 3-form

### Wheeler's slogan retold for electromagnetism: fields steer charges, charges arrange fields → electromagnetism as a field theory · useful · GA p.453 (pdf 470)

Places electromagnetism and gravity side by side so the chapter reads as a rehearsal for gravity; a margin note later says the current J is what directs the field lines.

**Where it breaks down:** Electromagnetism is linear and the field is uncharged, whereas gravity sources itself; charges come in two signs and different q/m ratios, so there is no electromagnetic equivalence principle and no geometrization of the force.

**App idea:** Split-screen animation: left, a charge's world line bent by F; right, a mass's world line straight in curved spacetime; captions swap between the two slogans.

### Choosing a gauge is like choosing which language to speak → gauge freedom · useful · GA §42.3 p.458 (pdf 475)

Margin note: the physical message is the same whichever gauge (language) is used; some languages make a given message easier to express.

**Where it breaks down:** Translation between gauges is exact and mechanical, unlike human languages; and gauge choices can be globally obstructed or leave residual freedom.

**App idea:** Show the same E and B field while the learner scrolls through different A fields related by chi; a 'translation' panel shows chi.

### Setting zero volts in two different cities → gauge transformation · strong · GA p.464 (pdf 481)

Exercise 42.3 asks why electronics lets us pick the zero of potential and whether London and Manchester may choose differently, leading to the time- and space-dependent chi.

**Where it breaks down:** A constant offset is the trivial case; a position- and time-dependent chi also requires shifting the vector potential to keep E unchanged.

**App idea:** Map of two cities with voltmeters; learner sets local zero differences and sees that a compensating vector potential must appear between them to keep measured fields fixed.

### A 2-form as intersecting sheets forming tubes, with the field circulating in the tubes → 2-form picture of the magnetic field · useful · GA §42.4 p.460 (pdf 477)

### Electromagnetic potentials as the template for gravitational potentials → retarded potentials · strong · GA §42.3 p.460 (pdf 477)

**Where it breaks down:** Only linearized gravity inherits these formulas; the full theory is nonlinear and the gauge group is diffeomorphisms, not scalar shifts.

## Misconceptions addressed

### The potentials V and A are physical quantities with unique values. · GA §42.3 p.458 (pdf 475)

**Correction:** They are defined only up to a gauge transformation; measurements depend only on F (E and B).

**Why tempting:** Voltages are read on meters and potentials appear in formulas as if they were fields in their own right.

### Imposing the Lorenz condition fixes the potential completely. · GA §42.3 p.459 (pdf 476)

**Correction:** Any further shift by a function obeying the wave equation preserves the condition; this residual freedom can be used to set A_0 = 0.

**Why tempting:** A single condition on one scalar seems to match the single scalar chi.

### The divergence-free gauge is named after Lorentz. · GA §42.3 p.459 (pdf 476)

**Correction:** It was first used by Ludvig Lorenz; the common name misattributes it to Hendrik Lorentz.

**Why tempting:** Lorentz is far better known and his name is everywhere in relativity.

### Index position on E and B components carries meaning as it does for 4-vectors. · GA §42.4 p.460 (pdf 477)

**Correction:** E and B are 3-vector components written with raised indices by choice; up and down are the same for them.

**Why tempting:** Everything else in the chapter distinguishes up and down indices carefully.

### Fields respond instantly to changes in their sources. · GA §42.3 p.459 (pdf 476)

**Correction:** Sources must be evaluated at the retarded time; information travels at light speed.

**Why tempting:** Static Coulomb and Biot-Savart formulas contain no time delay.

### A magnetic field is fundamentally an arrow. · GA §42.4 p.460 (pdf 477)

**Correction:** In spacetime B lives in the space-space components of a 2-form, naturally pictured as circulation in oriented area elements; the arrow is its 3D dual.

**Why tempting:** Field-line and arrow diagrams dominate introductory electromagnetism.

### E and B are separate vectors that each transform like a 4-vector's spatial part. · GA p.464 (pdf 481)

**Correction:** They are the six components of one antisymmetric tensor and mix into each other under boosts.

**Why tempting:** Separate 3-vector Maxwell equations suggest independent objects.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### A charge moving perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field spirals in a helix. · GA §42.4 p.461 (pdf 478)

**Correction:** Purely perpendicular motion gives a circle; a helix needs a velocity component along B. Example 42.9's wording (motion along z with B along x) would give circular motion in the y-z plane.

**Why tempting:** The example and Fig. 42.3 are presented together.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### Magnetic forces or any electromagnetic force can change a particle's rest mass. · GA §42.1 p.455 (pdf 472)

**Correction:** Antisymmetry of F makes the 4-force orthogonal to the 4-velocity, so the norm of u and the rest mass are preserved.

**Why tempting:** Electric fields do change kinetic energy, which is easy to confuse with rest mass.

### All of Maxwell's equations require a metric. · GA §42.4 p.462 (pdf 479)

**Correction:** dF = 0 and F = dA are metric-free; the metric enters only through the Hodge star in d*F = *J and in T.

**Why tempting:** The index-form equations are written with raised indices and eta throughout.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### Tensor equations derived in flat spacetime tell us nothing about curved spacetime. · GA p.453 (pdf 470)

**Correction:** A valid tensor equation carries over, with covariant derivatives replacing partials.

**Why tempting:** Curvature seems to add new physics everywhere.


## Thought experiments

### What is the simplest way a field could push a particle? · GA §42.1 p.454 (pdf 471)

**Setup:** Start with a free relativistic particle and ask which scalar you can build linearly from its displacement and a field, respecting Lorentz symmetry.

**Lesson:** Contracting dx^mu with a 1-form is the natural minimal choice, and it yields electromagnetism with an antisymmetric force tensor.

**App idea:** Coupling menu: scalar (multiplies proper time), vector (dotted with displacement), tensor (dotted with displacement twice); learner picks one and sees the resulting force law and its physical traits.

### Zero volts in London versus Manchester · GA p.464 (pdf 481)

**Setup:** Two labs choose different zeros of electric potential, possibly changing with time and place.

**Lesson:** Only differences producing E and B matter; allowing a varying zero requires a compensating vector potential, which is gauge freedom.

**App idea:** See analogy app idea: two-city voltmeter map.

### Backward light cone from a field point · GA p.465 (pdf 482)

**Setup:** The potential at event P is built from currents at events Q on the past light cone of P; a boosted observer asks whether the construction is frame independent.

**Lesson:** The retarded integral is a 4-vector because both the distance and the volume element transform by the same Doppler-like factor (Exercise 42.8).

**App idea:** 3D spacetime diagram with a past light cone intersecting a moving current tube; a boost slider shows the intersection region reshaping while the integral stays covariant.

### Can gravity be a scalar field like the electromagnetic potential? · GA p.466 (pdf 483)

**Setup:** Couple a scalar field to the particle's proper time, demand the equivalence principle, then find a field Lagrangian giving Poisson's equation.

**Lesson:** The equivalence principle forces the coupling into a conformally rescaled metric, and a scalar sourced by mass density cannot couple to energy without extra ingredients, pointing toward a tensor theory.

## Notation and conventions

- **Metric signature:** (-,+,+,+) with eta_00 = -1; hence A_0 = -A^0 = -V and the free action carries sqrt(-ds^2). (GA §42.1 p.454 (pdf 471))
- **Units:** Natural Heaviside-Lorentz-type units: c = 1, no epsilon_0 or mu_0 or 4 pi in Maxwell's equations; 1/(4 pi) appears in potentials. c is temporarily restored in the retarded time. — Poynting margin note remarks mu_0 disappears in these units. (GA §42.2 p.456 (pdf 473))
- **Faraday tensor index order:** F_{alpha beta} = partial_alpha A_beta - partial_beta A_alpha; F_{0i} = -E^i, F^{0i} = E^i, F_{12} = B^z; force law contracts u into the second index. — Same as MTW; some texts flip the overall sign. (GA §42.1 p.455 (pdf 472))
- **Tilde and bold:** Tilde marks forms (A tilde, F tilde, p tilde); bold marks tensors and vectors; empty slots are written F( , ). — In the reading copy the empty slot sometimes OCRs as a theta. (GA §42.1 p.455 (pdf 472))
- **Wedge product normalization:** dx^a wedge dx^b = dx^a (x) dx^b - dx^b (x) dx^a without 1/2; hence a 2-form is (1/2) F_{ab} dx^a wedge dx^b and a 3-form sum over all index orders carries 1/2 in dF. (GA §42.1 p.455 (pdf 472))
- **Comma and semicolon:** Comma = partial derivative, semicolon = covariant derivative; brackets [ ] denote antisymmetrization with 1/2. (GA §42.2 p.457 (pdf 474))
- **d'Alembertian:** partial^2 = -partial_t^2 + nabla^2, so the Lorenz-gauge equation reads partial^2 A = -J. — Opposite sign to the box operator common in particle physics. (GA §42.3 p.458 (pdf 475))
- **Gauge transformation sign:** A -> A - d chi in the text; Exercise 42.3 uses V -> V - partial_t chi, A -> A + grad chi (equivalent to chi -> -chi). (GA §42.2 p.456 (pdf 473))
- **Levi-Civita and dual:** epsilon_{0123} = +1 (implied by the computed components); (*F)_{alpha beta} = (1/2) epsilon_{mu nu alpha beta} F^{mu nu}; vertical bars |mu nu| restrict to mu < nu; **F = -F for 2-forms. (GA §42.4 p.462 (pdf 479))
- **Names for F and *F:** F is the Faraday tensor or 2-form; its dual *F is called the Maxwell tensor or Maxwell 2-form. — Follows MTW terminology. (GA §42.4 p.462 (pdf 479))
- **3-vector field indices:** E^i and B^i are written with raised indices; there is no distinction between up and down for them. — Example 42.13 switches to lower indices E_x without meaning a change. (GA §42.4 p.460 (pdf 477))
- **4-current:** J^mu = (rho, J^x, J^y, J^z) with upper index. (GA §42.2 p.456 (pdf 473))

## Gaps and pitfalls

- **Printed eqn 42.73 expands d*F with the magnetic-derivative terms carrying the wrong sign (it mirrors 42.63 with E and B swapped but omits the minus sign from E -> -B). The correct dt-containing coefficients are dE^i/dt minus the i-th component of curl B.** — A learner matching the printed coefficients to the -J^i components of *J obtains curl B = -J - dE/dt and may lose confidence in the whole geometric derivation. *Suggestion:* Present the corrected expansion and have learners generate it from 42.63 via the substitution E -> -B, B -> E.
- **Eqn 42.63 has 2dF on the left, but expanding F term by term gives dF itself with those coefficients; the factor is harmless only because the right side is zero. 42.73 has no such factor, so the two expansions look inconsistent.** — Confusion about wedge normalization when trying to reproduce the calculation. *Suggestion:* Derive dF directly from the E-B decomposition (42.54), where no factor of 2 appears.
- **In Example 42.1 the prefactor in 42.4 is written m/L_m, which evaluates to -1 when L_m = -m; the correct prefactor is m divided by the square root of -eta u u, which equals m for proper-time parametrization. Result 42.5 is right.** — Sign trouble for anyone checking the intermediate step. *Suggestion:* Differentiate the square root explicitly and only then set -eta u u = 1.
- **Example 42.3: eqn 42.29 is printed as a derivative with respect to A_{alpha;beta} set to zero, but it should be the derivative with respect to A_alpha (the Lagrangian has no undifferentiated A). Labels switch between L_em and L_m. The action also omits the sqrt(-g) volume factor.** — Appears to contradict 42.30 on the next line; the missing sqrt(-g) hides where the metric determinant enters the curved-space variation. *Suggestion:* State the curved-space action as integral of sqrt(-g) times the Lagrangian and note that for this Lagrangian the covariant Euler-Lagrange equation gives nabla_beta F^{alpha beta} = 0 either way.
- **Example 42.9 says the particle moves along the z-axis, but helices require a velocity component along B (x); with only u^z initially the orbit is a circle in the y-z plane.** — Learners may believe perpendicular motion alone produces a helix. *Suggestion:* Specify initial velocity with components both along and across B, and show the circle as the special case.
- **Example 42.6 calls A_0 = 0 plus div A = 0 'Coulomb gauge'. Standard Coulomb gauge imposes only div A = 0 (with A_0 then fixed by an instantaneous Poisson equation); setting A_0 = 0 in addition is the radiation gauge and is consistent only where there are no charges.** — Confusion when meeting Coulomb gauge elsewhere with a nonzero scalar potential for static charges. *Suggestion:* Name both gauges and note the source-free restriction; connect to the transverse-traceless gauge of Chapter 46 which has the same source-free caveat.
- **The Hodge star and Levi-Civita sign convention (epsilon_{0123} = +1 with lower indices) are used without restating, and the relation between the (2,0) and (0,2) routes to *F is only sketched in a margin note.** — Sign errors in *F and *J, which propagate to the source equation. *Suggestion:* Give a one-line convention box and a check: *F computed either way must satisfy **F = -F.
- **The book does not point out that Christoffel symbols cancel in F_{mu nu} = A_{nu;mu} - A_{mu;nu} and in the cyclic identity, so dF = 0 and F = dA are independent of the metric and connection.** — Missed insight that half of Maxwell's equations are pre-metric and that curvature enters only through the dual and the stress tensor. *Suggestion:* Add a short remark or exercise showing the cancellation using the symmetry of the connection.
- **Charge conservation (d*J = dd*F = 0) is the natural corollary of d*F = *J but is deferred to Chapter 43.** — The motivation for why a 3-form current is the right object is left dangling. *Suggestion:* Preview in one line: taking d of both sides forces d*J = 0, which integrates to conservation of charge.
- **Minor typos: 'charges' printed as 'changes' in the chapter summary; slogan grammar; a reference to contracting 'in eqn 42.17' that should point to 42.12; a volume element written d^3V' in one retarded-potential integral; Exercise 42.8 gives gamma = (1 - v)^(-1/2) instead of (1 - v^2)^(-1/2); in 42.52 the first middle term shows dx^mu wedge dx^mu where dx^mu wedge dx^nu is meant.** — Small but distracting, especially the gamma factor in an already hard exercise. *Suggestion:* Correct silently in app content; flag the gamma typo when assigning Exercise 42.8.
- **The claim that the displacement coupling is the 'simplest' is not argued (e.g. reparametrization invariance, linearity, why not a scalar or tensor).** — Advanced learners may find the starting point arbitrary. *Suggestion:* Compare scalar (Exercise 42.13), vector and tensor couplings side by side and note the vector one is the only linear, parametrization-independent coupling to displacement itself.

## Tutor notes

- Open with: 'If you were designing a universe, what is the simplest way to let a field push a particle without breaking relativity?' Let the learner try scalar and vector options before revealing the q A.dx coupling.
- Check prerequisites early: can the learner lower an index with eta in signature (-+++), state dd = 0, and compute a Hodge dual? If not, route to GA ch4, ch33, ch37 before section 42.4.
- Suggested order: coupling action -> force law with F -> F matrix of E and B -> Lorentz force check -> Maxwell in index form -> gauge freedom and Lorenz gauge -> retarded potentials -> stress-energy -> forms retelling (dA, dF = 0, *F, d*F = *J).
- Always verify one component: ask the learner to extract the x-component of q(E + v x B) from q F_{alpha beta} u^beta; it catches index-order and sign mistakes immediately.
- When teaching gauge freedom, start with the London/Manchester voltage question, then generalize to a function of space and time, then show the dd = 0 proof.
- For Example 42.9 make sure learners include a velocity component along B to get a helix; ask them what happens if that component is zero.
- Warn learners about the sign slip in printed eqn 42.73 and the stray factor 2 in 42.63 before they try to reproduce the forms derivation; have them derive d*F by the E -> -B, B -> E substitution.
- Check for understanding: 'Why can't the current couple to dF?' (Because dF vanishes identically.) 'Which Maxwell equations need the metric?' (Only the sourced pair, via the star.)
- Common learner question: 'Is the vector potential real?' Answer classically: only F is measurable; mention that quantum mechanics (Aharonov-Bohm) makes loop integrals of A observable, which is still gauge invariant.
- Common learner question: 'Why does E change sign when raising indices but B does not?' Walk through eta^{00} eta^{ii} = -1 for time-space entries versus +1 for space-space entries.
- Bridge to gravity at the end: map A -> metric perturbation, gauge chi -> coordinate change, Lorenz -> harmonic gauge, T_EM -> source of curvature, and preview charge conservation versus energy-momentum conservation (GA ch43, ch45-46).
