# GA ch13 · The gravitational field equations

> Builds Einstein's field equation by pairing a recap of curvature (Riemann, Ricci tensor, Ricci scalar) with a field-theory rewrite of Newtonian gravity whose Poisson equation serves as template, refutes the tempting guess Ricci = kappa T using the contracted Bianchi identity and energy-momentum conservation, repairs it with the divergence-free Einstein tensor, fixes kappa = 8 pi G / c^4 from the Newtonian limit, and then derives the trace-reversed form and the cosmological-constant form.

**Pages:** printed 141–150 · pdf 158–167 · **Difficulty:** math 3/5, conceptual 3/5, novice-friendliness 3/5

The Newtonian half uses standard vector calculus and delta functions; the relativistic half is short but index-dense (Bianchi contractions, divergence of G) and quotes several results without proof (Bianchi identity, weak-field Riemann components). Conceptually the propose-and-refute structure is clear, but the physical meaning of the constant-trace contradiction, the nonlinearity loop, and the sign story for Lambda need unpacking. The margin physics recap helps novices. Minor printed issues: the geodesic deviation margin equation lacks the minus sign (as does GA eqn 11.38, though 11.6 and 11.39 have it), the letter G is overloaded three ways, and the Lambda sign explanation is loose.

## Role in the book

This is the payoff chapter of Part II. Chapter 6 argued that gravity must be a field theory of the metric obeying some differential equation; Chapters 7-10 supplied the covariant derivative, geodesics, connection coefficients and measurement frames; Chapter 11 built the Riemann and Ricci tensors; Chapter 12 built the energy-momentum tensor and its conservation law. Chapter 13 joins the geometric and physical threads into G = 8 pi G T - Lambda g, closes Part II with a checklist of working heuristics, and hands the equation to Chapter 14 (survey of triumphs), Part III (cosmology, where Lambda returns) and Part IV (Schwarzschild solutions). The plausibility-style derivation is later deepened in Part V: the Bianchi identity is treated in Chapter 43, an action-based motivation appears in Chapter 40, and the Newtonian limit is redone in Chapter 45.

## Learning objectives

- Reader can describe the schematic statement (curvature of spacetime) = (energy density of matter) and name which tensor fills each side and why.
- Reader can trace the chain metric -> connection coefficients -> Riemann tensor -> Ricci tensor -> Ricci scalar and say that curvature needs up to second derivatives of the metric.
- Reader can derive the gravitational Poisson equation from Newton's integral expression for the field using the delta-function identity for the Laplacian of 1/|x - x'|.
- Reader can identify -1/|x - x'| as the Green's function of the Laplacian (with the 4 pi normalization) and use it to write the potential of an arbitrary mass distribution.
- Reader can explain the Newtonian template 'two derivatives of the potential proportional to the source' and why it suggests 'two derivatives of g proportional to T'.
- Reader can list three ways Einstein's gravity must differ from Newton's: no instantaneous action, light deflection by gravity, and nonlinearity because energy both sources and responds to geometry.
- Reader can state local causality, local energy-momentum conservation, the dominant energy condition and the weaker energy condition as requirements on the source.
- Reader can contract the Bianchi identity to 2 R^alpha_{gamma;alpha} = R_{;gamma} and use it with metric compatibility to prove the Einstein tensor is divergence-free.
- Reader can show that the candidate equation R^{mu nu} = kappa T^{mu nu} plus div T = 0 forces the trace of T to be the same everywhere, and explain why that rules the candidate out.
- Reader can reduce the 00 component of G = kappa T in the weak-field, low-pressure limit to Poisson's equation and deduce kappa = 8 pi G / c^4 (about 2.08e-43 N^-1).
- Reader can trace the field equation to get R = -8 pi G T and rewrite it in trace-reversed form R_{mu nu} = 8 pi G (T_{mu nu} - g_{mu nu} T / 2).
- Reader can justify why a term -Lambda g may be added without spoiling conservation, and recast it as a vacuum energy-momentum tensor T^vac = -Lambda g / (8 pi G).
- Reader can explain why, unlike electromagnetism, general relativity does not let one fix the source first and then solve for the field.

## Teaching approach

A short synthesis chapter that runs two tracks in parallel, geometry and physics, and makes them meet (Fig. 13.1). The physics track rewrites Newton's gravity as a field theory so that Poisson's equation becomes the pattern to imitate; the geometry track is a compressed recap of Chapter 11. The decisive pedagogical choice is to put a plausible but wrong field equation on the table and let a conservation-law consistency check destroy it, so the Einstein tensor enters as the repair rather than as an unexplained formula. The constant is fixed by demanding the Newtonian limit, the equation is displayed in three boxed 'versions', and the chapter closes with a bulleted toolkit of habits for the application chapters. Index manipulations are fenced into Example boxes, with a margin note restating the physics for readers who find them distracting.

**Style:** physical-motivation-first, derivation-heavy, example-driven, conversational-informal, spiral-revisit

**Narrative arc**

1. Goal stated as a word equation: curvature on the left, energy density of matter on the right; a two-column route map (Fig. 13.1) shows geometry and physics converging on G = 8 pi G T.
2. Geometry recap: the metric is a classical field; coordinate choices can fake acceleration, so the Riemann tensor is needed to isolate genuine curvature; Riemann in terms of Gamma, then Ricci tensor and Ricci scalar.
3. Correspondence principle: any relativistic theory must reduce to Newton, so Newton is rewritten as a field theory: force law, potential, integral field law.
4. Example 13.1 turns the integral law into Poisson's equation via the delta-function identity; Example 13.2 recasts the same result with a Green's function.
5. Template extracted: replace potential by metric and mass density by energy density to get the schematic boxed equation (two derivatives of g) = kappa T.
6. Caveats: Newton is instantaneous, light bends, and in GR the source itself depends on the metric, so the theory must be nonlinear (Fig. 13.2).
7. Requirements on the source: local causality, local conservation of energy-momentum, dominant energy condition; all packaged in T with div T = 0.
8. Tempting guess R^{mu nu} = kappa T^{mu nu} because both sides are rank-2; take its divergence and ask what happens on the geometric side.
9. Example 13.3 introduces the Bianchi identity (boundary of a boundary) and contracts it; the Einstein tensor is defined and shown to be divergence-free.
10. Collision: the guess plus the contracted Bianchi identity forces the Ricci scalar, hence the trace of T, to be constant everywhere; a lumpy universe contradicts this, so the guess is dropped.
11. Repair: adopt G_{mu nu} = kappa T_{mu nu}, whose divergence vanishes identically on both sides.
12. Example 13.4 fixes kappa by the weak-field, dust-dominated limit, temporarily restoring c: kappa = 8 pi G / c^4, with its SI value.
13. Boxed version 1 (G = 8 pi G T); Example 13.5 traces it to get version 2 (trace-reversed Ricci form); adding -Lambda g gives version 3, reinterpreted as vacuum energy-momentum, with a pointer to Part III.
14. Closing toolkit of heuristics (light cones, two kinds of frame, index rules, general covariance, perfect-fluid components) and a three-bullet chapter summary.

**Signature moves**

- Propose-and-refute: a natural candidate equation is written down explicitly (labelled 'incorrect guess') and killed by a consistency test, so the correct equation arrives as the fix.
- Conservation as the selection principle: the physics requirement div T = 0 must be matched by a geometric identity, which singles out the trace-reversed Ricci tensor.
- Newtonian field theory recast as a template with a boxed schematic equation (box of second derivatives of g equals box of kappa T) before any tensor detail.
- Constant of proportionality left undetermined until the end and then fixed by correspondence with Poisson's equation, with factors of c reinstated just for that calculation.
- Three numbered, boxed 'versions' of the same field equation, signalling equivalent forms and the one permissible extra term.
- Margin 'physics recap' for readers who find the index algebra distracting, restating the logic of the Bianchi argument in words.
- Geometry-versus-physics two-column route diagram that organizes the whole chapter.
- End-of-part checklist of practical heuristics that consolidates Part II habits before applications.

### §13.1 Geometry: a recap of the key ingredients — p.141 (pdf 158)

Recalls that the metric is a classical tensor field expressed through the line element. Because a single particle's acceleration could be a coordinate artefact, genuine gravity must be detected by removing coordinate effects, which is what the Riemann tensor does; it is obtained from the relative acceleration of initially parallel free particles and written in terms of connection coefficients (first derivatives of g) and their derivatives. Two contractions, the symmetric Ricci tensor and the Ricci scalar, are flagged as the ingredients of the left-hand side. A margin note suggests that the (1,3) Riemann tensor is the best candidate for 'the gravitational field', but its valence does not match the (2,0) energy-momentum tensor.

**Concepts:** metric tensor field, coordinate acceleration versus curvature, connection coefficients, geodesic deviation, Riemann curvature tensor, Ricci tensor, Ricci scalar, tensor valence matching

**Key moves**
- Line element ds^2 = g_{mu nu} dx^mu dx^nu (13.2)
- A lone accelerating particle cannot distinguish gravity from a perverse coordinate choice; real fields are inhomogeneous and curve spacetime
- Gamma from first derivatives of g (13.3); Riemann from geodesic deviation (13.4) and in component form (13.5)
- Contract to Ricci tensor (13.6, symmetric) and Ricci scalar (13.7)
- Riemann as the physical gravitational field, but valence (1,3) cannot equal the (2,0) T

### §13.2 Physics: the key ingredients — p.142 (pdf 159)

Imposes the principle that a relativistic theory must reproduce Newton in the non-relativistic limit and therefore rewrites Newtonian gravity as a field theory: force law, potential energy, potential per unit mass, then integral expressions for the field and potential of a continuous density. Example 13.1 shows the field is minus the gradient of the potential and, using the delta-function divergence identity, derives Poisson's equation; Example 13.2 re-expresses the solution with the Green's function -1/|x - x'|. The pattern (two derivatives of a potential proportional to a source) is transplanted into the schematic relativistic guess of second derivatives of g proportional to kappa T. The section then lists the differences from Newton (instantaneous action, bending of light, nonlinearity because T depends on g, Fig. 13.2) and the physical requirements on the source: local causality, local conservation of energy-momentum, and the dominant energy condition, all carried by an energy-momentum tensor obeying div T = 0.

**Concepts:** correspondence with Newtonian gravity, Newtonian gravitational field, Newtonian gravitational potential, Laplacian of 1/r identity, Poisson's equation for gravity, Green's function, nonlinearity of general relativity, local causality, local conservation of energy-momentum, dominant energy condition, weak energy condition, energy-momentum tensor

**Key moves**
- Newton's force law (13.8) and potential energy (13.9); potential per unit mass removes the test mass
- Integral laws for field (13.10) and potential (13.11) from a density rho(x')
- Gradient of 1/|x - x'| (13.12) gives g = -grad Phi (13.13)
- Divergence identity (13.15), equivalently Laplacian of 1/|x - x'| = -4 pi delta (13.14), yields div g = -4 pi G rho (13.16) and Poisson (13.17-13.19)
- Green's function construction (13.20-13.23)
- Template: Phi -> g, rho -> T, giving the schematic boxed equation (13.24); Newton's law expected to be one component of the tensor equation
- Differences: Newton instantaneous; light bends; source depends on metric -> nonlinear (Fig. 13.2)
- Source requirements: causality, local conservation (continuity equation), dominant energy condition (weak version in margin)
- All encoded in T(x) with div T = 0; T vanishes only when all matter fields vanish

### §13.3 An incorrect guess — p.145 (pdf 162)

Because T has two indices, the natural guess is to equate it to the Ricci tensor, R^{mu nu} = kappa T^{mu nu}, which was Einstein's own first attempt in autumn 1915. Taking the divergence kills the right side by conservation. Example 13.3 introduces the Bianchi identity (motivated by 'the boundary of a boundary is zero', like div curl = 0), contracts it twice to obtain 2 R^alpha_{gamma;alpha} = R_{;gamma}, and the Einstein tensor G^{mu nu} = R^{mu nu} - g^{mu nu} R / 2 is defined and shown divergence-free using metric compatibility. Combining this with the guess forces the gradient of the Ricci scalar to vanish; the guess then makes the trace of T a universal constant, which is incompatible with a clumpy universe, so the candidate is abandoned.

**Concepts:** Ricci-tensor field equation (rejected candidate), Bianchi identity, contracted Bianchi identity, Einstein tensor, metric compatibility of the connection, trace of the energy-momentum tensor, covariant derivative of a scalar field

**Key moves**
- Rank matching suggests R^{mu nu} = kappa T^{mu nu} (13.25)
- Divergence of both sides (13.26); right side vanishes by div T = 0
- Bianchi identity (13.27); contract alpha with lambda (13.28) using Riemann antisymmetries
- Raise beta, contract with delta: contracted Bianchi identity (13.29)
- Define Einstein tensor (13.30); its divergence (13.31) vanishes (13.32-13.33)
- Guess plus div G = 0 gives grad R = 0 (13.34)
- Then g^{mu nu} T_{mu nu} is constant everywhere (13.35): uniform matter, contradicted by experience

### §13.4 Einstein's field equation — p.147 (pdf 164)

The object uncovered while refuting the guess becomes the solution: G_{mu nu} = kappa T_{mu nu} has zero divergence on both sides. Example 13.4 fixes kappa in the weak-field limit: with nearly Minkowski metric and T dominated by T^00 = rho c^2, the spatial Einstein components vanish, giving R = 2 R^00 and G^00 = 2 R_00 = (2/c^2) Laplacian Phi, so matching Poisson yields kappa = 8 pi G / c^4. The field equation is boxed as version 1; Example 13.5 traces it (R = -8 pi G T) to give the trace-reversed version 2. Admitting that the route was a plausibility argument, the section adds -Lambda g (allowed because the metric is covariantly constant) to get version 3, interprets Lambda as an extra energy source that repels when positive, and rewrites it as a vacuum energy-momentum tensor, deferring its history to Part III.

**Concepts:** Einstein field equation, Einstein gravitational coupling constant, Newtonian limit of the field equation, trace-reversed form of the Einstein equation, trace of the energy-momentum tensor, cosmological constant, vacuum energy-momentum tensor, metric compatibility of the connection

**Key moves**
- Adopt G_{mu nu} = kappa T_{mu nu} (13.36); divergence-consistent by construction
- Weak field: g_00 ~ -1, g_ii ~ 1, T^00 = rho c^2 dominant, so G^{ij} = 0 (13.37) and sum of R^{ii} = 3R/2 (13.38)
- R = 2 R^00, so G^00 = 2 R_00 (13.39); R_00 = sum_i R^i_{0i0} = Laplacian Phi / c^2 gives (13.40)
- Compare with Poisson: kappa c^4 / 2 = 4 pi G (13.41-13.42); SI value about 2.08e-43 N^-1
- Version 1 in components (13.43) and index-free (13.44)
- Trace with g^{mu nu}, using g^{mu nu} g_{mu nu} = 4 (13.45-13.46) -> version 2 (13.47)
- Add -Lambda g, harmless for divergence since g_{mu nu;alpha} = 0 -> version 3 (13.48-13.49)
- Move Lambda to the source side as T^vac (13.50-13.51)

## Concepts

### Einstein field equation

*law · core* · also: Einstein equation, gravitational field equation, version 1 of the Einstein field equation, G = 8 pi G T

The tensor field equation of general relativity relating geometry to matter: the Einstein tensor built from the metric and its first two derivatives equals 8 pi G / c^4 times the energy-momentum tensor (optionally minus Lambda times the metric). It is ten coupled nonlinear second-order partial differential equations for the metric components.

**How introduced:** Reached as the repair of a failed candidate: after R^{mu nu} = kappa T^{mu nu} is shown to force a uniform trace of T, the divergence-free Einstein tensor replaces the Ricci tensor, and the constant is fixed by matching Poisson's equation. Displayed in three boxed versions (plain, trace-reversed, with Lambda).

**Prerequisites:** Einstein tensor, energy-momentum tensor, local conservation of energy-momentum, Poisson's equation for gravity

$$
R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12 g_{\mu\nu} R = 8\pi G T_{\mu\nu}
$$

$$
\boldsymbol{G} = 8\pi G\boldsymbol{T} - \Lambda\boldsymbol{g}
$$

**Notes:** Book uses c = 1 but keeps G explicit in this chapter; later chapters set G = 1.

**Where:** GA §13.4 p.147 (pdf 164); GA §13.4 p.148 (pdf 165); GA §13.4 p.149 (pdf 166); GA p.150 (pdf 167)

### Einstein tensor

*mathematical-object · core* · also: trace-reversed Ricci tensor, Einstein tensor field G

The symmetric rank-2 tensor G_{mu nu} = R_{mu nu} - (1/2) g_{mu nu} R formed from the Ricci tensor and scalar; by the contracted Bianchi identity its covariant divergence vanishes identically for any metric. Its trace is -R, so it is the Ricci tensor with its trace reversed.

**How introduced:** Defined inside the refutation of the Ricci guess, as the combination whose divergence is forced to zero by the contracted Bianchi identity; then promoted to the left-hand side of the correct equation.

**Prerequisites:** Ricci tensor, Ricci scalar, contracted Bianchi identity, metric compatibility of the connection

$$
G^{\mu\nu} = R^{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12 g^{\mu\nu}R
$$

$$
G^{\mu\nu}{}_{;\mu} = 0
$$

$$
g_{\mu\nu}G^{\mu\nu} = -R
$$

**Notes:** Bold G for the tensor collides with Newton's constant G (margin note 17) and with the Green's function G(x, x') of Example 13.2.

**Where:** GA §13.3 p.146 (pdf 163); GA §13.4 p.147 (pdf 164); GA p.150 (pdf 167)

### Ricci-tensor field equation (rejected candidate)

*historical-idea · developed* · also: incorrect guess, R^{mu nu} = kappa T^{mu nu}

The proposal that the Ricci tensor itself is proportional to the energy-momentum tensor. Combined with energy-momentum conservation and the contracted Bianchi identity it forces the Ricci scalar, and hence the trace of T, to be constant throughout spacetime, which contradicts the observed non-uniform distribution of matter.

**How introduced:** Offered as the obvious rank-2 match for T, credited in a margin note to Einstein's first attempt in 1915, then refuted by taking the divergence of both sides.

**Prerequisites:** Ricci tensor, local conservation of energy-momentum, contracted Bianchi identity

$$
R^{\mu\nu} = \kappa T^{\mu\nu}
$$

$$
R^{;\nu} = 0 \Rightarrow g^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu} = \text{const}
$$

**Where:** GA §13.3 p.145 (pdf 162); GA §13.3 p.146 (pdf 163); GA §13.3 p.147 (pdf 164)

### Bianchi identity

*identity · introduced* · also: second Bianchi identity, differential Bianchi identity

The differential identity satisfied by the Riemann tensor of any metric-compatible, torsion-free connection: the cyclic sum of its covariant derivatives over the last three lower-index positions vanishes, R^a_{b[cd;e]} = 0.

**How introduced:** Quoted without proof in Example 13.3 and motivated by the slogan that the boundary of a boundary is zero, the same idea behind div curl = 0; full treatment deferred to Chapter 43.

**Prerequisites:** Riemann curvature tensor, covariant derivative

$$
R^{\alpha}{}_{\beta\gamma\delta;\lambda} + R^{\alpha}{}_{\beta\lambda\gamma;\delta} + R^{\alpha}{}_{\beta\delta\lambda;\gamma} = 0
$$

**Where:** GA §13.3 p.146 (pdf 163)

### contracted Bianchi identity

*identity · developed* · also: twice-contracted Bianchi identity

The result of contracting the Bianchi identity twice: twice the divergence of the mixed Ricci tensor equals the gradient of the Ricci scalar, 2 R^alpha_{gamma;alpha} = R_{;gamma}, equivalently the Einstein tensor is divergence-free.

**How introduced:** Worked out step by step in Example 13.3 by setting alpha = lambda, using Riemann antisymmetries, then raising beta and contracting with delta.

**Prerequisites:** Bianchi identity, Ricci tensor, Ricci scalar, symmetries of the Riemann tensor

$$
2R^{\alpha}{}_{\gamma;\alpha} = R_{;\gamma}
$$

$$
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{G} = 0
$$

**Notes:** Geometric counterpart of energy-momentum conservation; this match is the logical heart of the chapter.

**Where:** GA §13.3 p.146 (pdf 163)

### local conservation of energy-momentum

*principle · core* · also: div T = 0, covariant conservation of T, continuity equation for energy-momentum

The requirement that energy and momentum of matter fields obey a local continuity equation, expressed covariantly as T^{mu nu}_{;mu} = 0, so that nothing is created or destroyed at a point.

**How introduced:** Listed among the physical principles the source must satisfy, identified with the constraint div T = 0 from Chapter 12, and then used as the test that eliminates the Ricci guess and selects the Einstein tensor.

**Prerequisites:** energy-momentum tensor, covariant derivative

$$
T^{\mu\nu}{}_{;\mu} = 0
$$

**Notes:** The text says T encodes conservation 'at least approximately'; in curved spacetime the covariant law is exact but does not give a globally conserved total energy.

**Where:** GA §13.2 p.145 (pdf 162); GA §13.3 p.145 (pdf 162); GA §13.4 p.147 (pdf 164)

### Einstein gravitational coupling constant

*physical-quantity · core* · also: kappa, 8 pi G / c^4

The proportionality constant between the Einstein tensor and the energy-momentum tensor, kappa = 8 pi G / c^4, about 2.08e-43 N^-1 in SI units, reducing to 8 pi G when c = 1.

**How introduced:** Left as an unknown kappa through the argument and fixed in Example 13.4 by requiring the 00 component to reproduce Poisson's equation.

**Prerequisites:** Newtonian limit of the field equation, Poisson's equation for gravity

$$
\kappa = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4} \approx 2.08\times10^{-43}\ \mathrm{N^{-1}}
$$

**Notes:** Its tiny size is why enormous energy densities are needed for appreciable curvature.

**Where:** GA §13.4 p.148 (pdf 165)

### Newtonian limit of the field equation

*technique · developed* · also: weak-field limit, weak-field, slow-motion limit

The approximation of nearly flat spacetime (metric close to Minkowski), static fields and non-relativistic matter with pressure negligible compared with rho c^2, in which the 00 component of the Einstein equation reduces to Poisson's equation for the Newtonian potential.

**How introduced:** Carried out in Example 13.4 with c restored: vanishing spatial Einstein components give R = 2 R^00, and R_00 is identified with the Laplacian of Phi over c^2.

**Prerequisites:** Einstein tensor, Ricci tensor, Poisson's equation for gravity, connection coefficients

$$
G^{00} \approx 2R_{00} = \frac{2}{c^2}\nabla^2\Phi
$$

$$
\Gamma^i_{00} = \partial_i(\Phi/c^2)
$$

$$
R^i{}_{0j0} = \partial_i\partial_j(\Phi/c^2)
$$

**Notes:** Treated again with linearized theory in Chapter 45 and surveyed in Chapter 14.

**Where:** GA §13.4 p.147 (pdf 164); GA §13.4 p.148 (pdf 165)

### correspondence with Newtonian gravity

*principle · developed* · also: correspondence principle, Newtonian compatibility requirement

The requirement that a relativistic, geometric theory of gravity reproduce Newton's law of gravitation in the regime of weak fields and slow motion.

**How introduced:** Opens the physics section as the guiding principle, motivating a field-theory rewrite of Newton and, later, the determination of kappa.

**Prerequisites:** Newtonian gravitational potential

**Where:** GA §13.2 p.142 (pdf 159); GA §13.4 p.147 (pdf 164)

### Riemann curvature tensor

*mathematical-object · revisited* · also: Riemann tensor field R(x), (1,3) curvature tensor

The (1,3) tensor built from connection coefficients and their first derivatives (hence from first and second derivatives of the metric) that vanishes everywhere if and only if spacetime is flat; it governs the relative acceleration of nearby free-falling particles.

**How introduced:** Recapped as the tool that strips out coordinate artefacts to reveal genuine curvature, extracted from the geodesic deviation equation; a margin note calls it the best candidate for the gravitational field while noting its valence does not fit the field equation directly.

**Prerequisites:** connection coefficients, geodesic deviation

$$
R^{\alpha}{}_{\beta\gamma\delta} = \partial_\gamma\Gamma^\alpha_{\delta\beta} - \partial_\delta\Gamma^\alpha_{\gamma\beta} + \Gamma^\alpha_{\gamma\mu}\Gamma^\mu_{\delta\beta} - \Gamma^\alpha_{\delta\mu}\Gamma^\mu_{\gamma\beta}
$$

**Where:** GA §13.1 p.142 (pdf 159)

### Ricci tensor

*mathematical-object · revisited* · also: R_{mu nu}

The symmetric rank-2 tensor obtained by contracting the upper index of the Riemann tensor with its third index, R_{mu nu} = R^alpha_{mu alpha nu}; physically it captures the part of curvature that changes the volume of a small ball of free particles.

**How introduced:** Recapped as the first contraction of Riemann; its symmetry is flagged for later use; margin notes describe it as an average of Riemann that makes volumes shrink.

**Prerequisites:** Riemann curvature tensor

$$
R_{\mu\nu} = R^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\alpha\nu}
$$

$$
R_{\mu\nu} = R_{\nu\mu}
$$

**Where:** GA §13.1 p.142 (pdf 159); GA §13.3 p.145 (pdf 162); GA §13.4 p.147 (pdf 164)

### Ricci scalar

*mathematical-object · revisited* · also: curvature scalar, R(x)

The full trace of the Ricci tensor with the metric, R = g_{mu nu} R^{mu nu}, a coordinate-invariant scalar field.

**How introduced:** Recapped as the second contraction; later shown to be constant under the rejected guess and equal to -8 pi G T under the correct equation.

### Newtonian gravitational field

*physical-quantity · developed* · also: gravitational field vector g, force per unit mass

The vector field giving gravitational force per unit test mass at each point, g(x) = -G integral of rho(x') (x - x') / |x - x'|^3 over sources, equal to minus the gradient of the potential.

**How introduced:** Generalized from the two-body force law to a continuous density as the first step of recasting Newton as a field theory.

**Notes:** Arrowed g is the Newtonian field, not the bold metric g.

### Newtonian gravitational potential

### Green's function

*technique · introduced* · also: point-source response

For a linear differential operator L, a function G(x, y) satisfying L G = delta(x - y); the solution for a general source is the superposition A(x) = integral of G(x, y) f(y) dy.

**How introduced:** Margin note 11 gives the general idea; Example 13.2 identifies -1/|x - x'| (with 4 pi normalization) as the Green's function for gravitational Poisson.

### nonlinearity of general relativity

*phenomenon · introduced* · also: gravity gravitates, self-coupling of gravity

The property that the field equation is not linear in the metric: the source T depends on the metric being solved for, and gravitational energy itself acts back on the geometry, so solutions cannot simply be superposed.

**How introduced:** Argued from the loop in Fig. 13.2 (source fixes geometry, geometry shapes source) and contrasted in margin note 13 with electromagnetism and Newtonian gravity, where sources can be fixed first.

**Prerequisites:** energy-momentum tensor, metric tensor field

**Where:** GA §13.2 p.144 (pdf 161)

### local causality

*principle · introduced* · also: no faster-than-light signalling

A signal can pass between two events only if their separation is timelike or null, never spacelike; fields and sources must respect the light-cone structure.

**How introduced:** First of the physical principles constraining the right-hand side, also used to fault Newton's instantaneous action.

**Where:** GA §13.2 p.145 (pdf 162)

### dominant energy condition

*principle · introduced* · also: DEC

The requirement that every observer measures a non-negative local energy density and a causal (timelike or null) energy flux, so positive energy never flows faster than light.

**How introduced:** Listed as the third principle on the source; later invoked (loosely) to explain why a positive Lambda acts with opposite sign to ordinary matter.

**Prerequisites:** energy-momentum tensor, local causality

$$
T_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu \ge 0,\quad -T^{\mu}{}_{\nu}u^\nu\ \text{causal for all timelike } u

**Notes:** Formula is the standard statement, not printed in the book.

**Where:** GA §13.2 p.145 (pdf 162); GA §13.4 p.149 (pdf 166)

### weak energy condition

*principle · mention* · also: WEC

The less restrictive requirement that the energy density measured by every observer be non-negative, with no condition on the energy flux.

**How introduced:** Mentioned in margin note 14 as a looser alternative to the dominant energy condition.

The equivalent form R_{mu nu} = 8 pi G (T_{mu nu} - (1/2) g_{mu nu} T), obtained by tracing the field equation to find R = -8 pi G T and substituting back; in vacuum it reduces to R_{mu nu} = 0.

### cosmological constant

*physical-quantity · introduced* · also: Lambda, version 3 of the Einstein field equation

A constant Lambda multiplying the metric that may be added to the field equation, G_{mu nu} + Lambda g_{mu nu} = 8 pi G T_{mu nu}, without spoiling conservation because the metric has zero covariant derivative; a positive value produces cosmic repulsion.

**How introduced:** Introduced after admitting the derivation was haphazard and asking which extra terms are permitted; interpreted as an additional energy source and its Einstein history deferred to Part III.

**Prerequisites:** Einstein field equation, metric compatibility of the connection

**Notes:** The repulsion comes from the negative pressure p = -rho of the vacuum term, not from a negative energy density; see gaps.

**Where:** GA §13.4 p.148 (pdf 165); GA §13.4 p.149 (pdf 166)

### vacuum energy-momentum tensor

*mathematical-object · introduced* · also: T^vac, dark energy tensor

The effective source T^vac = -Lambda g / (8 pi G) that reproduces the cosmological-constant term when moved to the right-hand side; it describes a medium with energy density Lambda / (8 pi G) and pressure equal to minus that density.

**How introduced:** Offered as a bookkeeping device to keep the compact form G = 8 pi G (T^mat + T^vac).

**Notes:** Density and pressure expressions are standard consequences with signature (-,+,+,+), not printed here.

### (13.32) Applying the contracted Bianchi identity · derivation-step · GA §13.3 p.146 (pdf 163)

$$
G^{\mu\nu}{}_{;\mu} = R^{\mu\nu}{}_{;\mu} - g^{\mu\nu}R^{\alpha}{}_{\mu;\alpha} = 0
$$

Raising and renaming dummy indices shows the two terms cancel.

### (13.33) Einstein tensor is divergence-free · central · GA §13.3 p.146 (pdf 163)

$$
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol G = \left(R^{\mu\nu} - \frac12 g^{\mu\nu}R\right)_{;\mu} = 0
$$

An identity true for every metric, matching the conservation law of T.

### (13.34) Consequence of the incorrect guess · derivation-step · GA §13.3 p.146 (pdf 163)

$$
-\frac12 g^{\mu\nu}R_{;\mu} = -\frac12 R^{;\nu} = 0
$$

If Ricci is divergence-free (guess plus conservation) then div G = 0 forces the Ricci scalar to be constant.

### (13.35) Constant trace contradiction · supporting · GA §13.3 p.147 (pdf 164)

$$
R = g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu} = \kappa g^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu} = \text{const}
$$

The guess would require the trace of T to be the same everywhere, i.e. a uniform universe, so it is rejected.

### (13.36) New candidate field equation · central · GA §13.4 p.147 (pdf 164)

$$
G_{\mu\nu} = \kappa T_{\mu\nu}
$$

Both sides are divergence-free identically, so consistency with conservation is automatic.

### (13.37) Spatial Einstein components in the Newtonian limit · derivation-step · GA §13.4 p.147 (pdf 164)

$$
G^{ij} = R^{ij} - \frac12 g^{ij}R = 0
$$

With pressure and momentum flux negligible, the spatial source components vanish.

**Symbols:** i, j spatial; g^{ii} ~ 1

### (13.38) Sum of spatial Ricci components · derivation-step · GA §13.4 p.147 (pdf 164)

$$
R^{11} + R^{22} + R^{33} = \frac32 R
$$

Each diagonal spatial Ricci component equals R/2.

### Ricci scalar in the Newtonian limit · derivation-step · GA §13.4 p.147 (pdf 164)

$$
R = g_{\mu\nu}R^{\mu\nu} = -R^{00} + \frac32 R\ \Rightarrow\ R = 2R^{00}
$$

Intermediate step of Example 13.4.

### (13.39) 00 Einstein component in the Newtonian limit · derivation-step · GA §13.4 p.147 (pdf 164)

$$
G^{00} = 2R^{00} \approx 2R_{00}
$$

Uses g^{00} ~ -1 so that G^00 = R^00 + R/2.

### Weak-field connection and curvature · derivation-step · GA §13.4 p.147 (pdf 164)

$$
\Gamma^i_{00} = \frac{\partial(\Phi/c^2)}{\partial x^i},\qquad R^i{}_{0j0} = \frac{\partial^2(\Phi/c^2)}{\partial x^i\partial x^j},\qquad R_{00} = \sum_i R^i{}_{0i0}
$$

Recalled weak-field results: curvature components are the Newtonian tidal tensor; tracing gives the Laplacian.

### (13.40) Einstein 00 component as Laplacian of the potential · derivation-step · GA §13.4 p.147 (pdf 164)

$$
G^{00} = \frac{2}{c^2}\nabla^2\Phi
$$

Links the geometric side to Newtonian gravity.

### (13.41) Predicted Newtonian field equation · derivation-step · GA §13.4 p.148 (pdf 165)

$$
\nabla^2\Phi = \frac{\kappa c^4}{2}\rho
$$

What G = kappa T gives with T^00 = rho c^2.

### (13.42) Coupling constant · central · GA §13.4 p.148 (pdf 165)

$$
\kappa = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4} \approx 2.08\times10^{-43}\ \mathrm{N^{-1}}
$$

Matching 13.41 to Poisson's equation.

### (13.43) Einstein field equation, version 1 (components) · central · GA §13.4 p.148 (pdf 165)

$$
R_{\mu\nu} - \frac12 g_{\mu\nu}R = 8\pi G T_{\mu\nu}
$$

The field equation of general relativity (c = 1).

### (13.44) Einstein field equation, version 1 (index-free) · central · GA §13.4 p.148 (pdf 165)

$$
\boldsymbol G(x) = 8\pi G\,\boldsymbol T(x)
$$

Coordinate-free statement at each event.

### (13.45) Trace of the field equation · derivation-step · GA §13.4 p.148 (pdf 165)

$$
g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu} - \frac12 g^{\mu\nu}g_{\mu\nu}R = 8\pi G\,g^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu}
$$

Contract with the inverse metric.

### Ricci scalar from the trace of T · supporting · GA §13.4 p.148 (pdf 165)

$$
R - 2R = -R = 8\pi G T,\qquad g^{\mu\nu}g_{\mu\nu} = 4
$$

Curvature scalar fixed algebraically by the local trace of energy-momentum.

**Symbols:** T = g^{mu nu} T_{mu nu}

### (13.46) Substituting the trace back · derivation-step · GA §13.4 p.148 (pdf 165)

$$
R_{\mu\nu} + \frac12 g_{\mu\nu}(8\pi G T) = 8\pi G T_{\mu\nu}
$$

Eliminate R from version 1.

### (13.47) Einstein field equation, version 2 (trace-reversed) · central · GA §13.4 p.148 (pdf 165)

$$
R_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G\left(T_{\mu\nu} - \frac12 g_{\mu\nu}T\right)
$$

Equivalent form giving Ricci directly; in vacuum it reduces to R_{mu nu} = 0.

### (13.48) Einstein field equation, version 3 (components) · central · GA §13.4 p.149 (pdf 166)

$$
R_{\mu\nu} - \frac12 g_{\mu\nu}R = 8\pi G T_{\mu\nu} - \Lambda g_{\mu\nu}
$$

Most general form allowed by the divergence argument, with cosmological constant Lambda.

**Symbols:** Lambda: cosmological constant

### (13.49) Einstein field equation, version 3 (index-free) · supporting · GA §13.4 p.149 (pdf 166)

$$
\boldsymbol G(x) = 8\pi G\,\boldsymbol T(x) - \Lambda\boldsymbol g(x)
$$

Coordinate-free version with Lambda.

### (13.50) Lambda absorbed into the source · supporting · GA §13.4 p.149 (pdf 166)

$$
\boldsymbol G = 8\pi G\left(\boldsymbol T^{\mathrm{mat}} + \boldsymbol T^{\mathrm{vac}}\right)
$$

Keep the compact form by treating Lambda as an energy-momentum contribution.

### (13.51) Vacuum energy-momentum tensor · supporting · GA §13.4 p.149 (pdf 166)

$$
\boldsymbol T^{\mathrm{vac}} = -\frac{\Lambda}{8\pi G}\boldsymbol g
$$

Effective source equivalent to the cosmological constant; with signature (-,+,+,+) it has rho = Lambda/(8 pi G) and p = -rho.

### Perfect fluid in an orthonormal spherical frame · supporting · GA §13.4 p.150 (pdf 167)

$$
T_{\hat t\hat t} = \rho,\quad T_{\hat r\hat r} = T_{\hat\theta\hat\theta} = T_{\hat\phi\hat\phi} = p
$$

Heuristic reminder for the application chapters.

### (13.53) Summary form of the Einstein equation · supporting · GA p.150 (pdf 167)

$$
R_{\mu\nu} - \frac12 g_{\mu\nu}R = 8\pi G T_{\mu\nu} - \Lambda g_{\mu\nu},\qquad G_{\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu} - \frac12 g_{\mu\nu}R\ (13.52)
$$

Chapter summary restates the Einstein tensor (13.52) and the full equation (13.53).

### (13.54) Point-source Laplacian (exercise) · supporting · GA p.150 (pdf 167)

\nabla^2\left(\frac1r\right) = -4\pi\delta(\vec x)

Statement to be proved in Exercise 13.2.

### (13.55) Second derivatives of 1/r (exercise) · derivation-step · GA p.150 (pdf 167)

$$
\frac{\partial^2}{\partial x^i\partial x^j}\left(\frac1r\right) = \frac{3n^in^j - \delta^{ij}}{r^3},\qquad n^i = x^i/r

Traceless tidal form away from the origin; its trace gives the vanishing Laplacian for r != 0.

## Worked examples

### Example 13.1 · standard · GA §13.2 p.143 (pdf 160)

**Problem:** Starting from the integral expressions for the Newtonian field and potential of a continuous mass density, show that the field is minus the gradient of the potential and derive the differential field equation.

**Method:** Differentiate 1/|x - x'| under the integral to reproduce the inverse-square kernel, giving g = -grad Phi; then take the divergence of the field integral using the identity that the divergence of the kernel is 4 pi times a 3D delta function, collapse the integral, and combine with g = -grad Phi.

**Key insight:** Action-at-a-distance integrals are equivalent to a local differential equation in which second derivatives of the potential at a point are set by the density at that point, the structural idea carried into GR.

**Result:** div g = -4 pi G rho and Laplacian Phi = 4 pi G rho (eqns 13.16-13.17).

**Concepts:** Newtonian gravitational field, Newtonian gravitational potential, Laplacian of 1/r identity, Poisson's equation for gravity

### Example 13.2 · intro · GA §13.2 p.144 (pdf 161)

**Problem:** Solve Poisson's equation for gravity by the Green's function method.

**Method:** Define the Green's function as the solution of the Laplacian with a 4 pi delta source, write the potential as G times the integral of the Green's function against the density, and compare with the integral for Phi from Example 13.1 to identify the Green's function.

**Key insight:** Because the Newtonian equation is linear, the potential of any mass distribution is a superposition of point-source responses; the loss of this superposition property is what makes GR harder.

**Result:** G(x, x') = -1/|x - x'| (eqn 13.23).

**Concepts:** Green's function, Poisson's equation for gravity

### Example 13.3 · standard · GA §13.3 p.146 (pdf 163)

**Problem:** From the Bianchi identity derive a relation between the divergence of the Ricci tensor and the gradient of the Ricci scalar.

**Method:** Quote the Bianchi identity; contract the upper index with the derivative index, using antisymmetry of Riemann in its index pairs to turn two terms into Ricci tensors; raise the remaining free lower index and contract it with another to obtain Ricci-scalar and Ricci-divergence terms.

**Key insight:** A purely geometric identity delivers a built-in conservation law for a particular combination of curvature, which is what a field equation sourced by a conserved T needs.

**Result:** 2 R^alpha_{gamma;alpha} = R_{;gamma} (eqn 13.29), the contracted Bianchi identity.

**Concepts:** Bianchi identity, contracted Bianchi identity, symmetries of the Riemann tensor

### Divergence of the Einstein tensor and refutation of the Ricci guess (in-text) · standard · GA §13.3 p.146 (pdf 163)

**Problem:** Show that the Einstein tensor is divergence-free and use this to test the candidate equation R^{mu nu} = kappa T^{mu nu}.

**Method:** Take the covariant divergence of R^{mu nu} - g^{mu nu} R/2, pull g through by metric compatibility, substitute the contracted Bianchi identity and rename dummy indices so the terms cancel. Then note the guess plus div T = 0 makes the Ricci divergence zero, so the remaining term forces grad R = 0; tracing the guess converts this into a constant trace of T.

**Key insight:** The wrong equation fails not through an algebraic error but because it would impose an absurd global restriction on matter; conservation acts as a filter on possible field equations.

**Result:** div G = 0 identically (13.33); the guess would demand g^{mu nu} T_{mu nu} = const everywhere (13.35), so it is rejected.

**Concepts:** Einstein tensor, Ricci-tensor field equation (rejected candidate), metric compatibility of the connection, trace of the energy-momentum tensor

### Example 13.4 · standard · GA §13.4 p.147 (pdf 164)

**Problem:** Find the constant kappa in G_{mu nu} = kappa T_{mu nu} by demanding agreement with Newtonian gravity for weak fields.

**Method:** Take the metric nearly Minkowski so index positions only flip signs on time components; keep only T^00 = rho c^2 since pressure is negligible. Setting spatial Einstein components to zero gives each spatial Ricci diagonal component equal to R/2, hence R = 2R^00 and G^00 = 2R_00. Use the weak-field connection Gamma^i_00 = partial_i Phi / c^2 and the resulting R^i_{0j0} to get R_00 = Laplacian Phi / c^2, then compare G^00 = kappa rho c^2 with Poisson.

**Key insight:** Only one component of the tensor equation survives in the Newtonian regime, and it is Poisson's equation in disguise; the rest of the tensor structure is invisible to Newton.

**Result:** kappa = 8 pi G / c^4, about 2.08e-43 N^-1; with c = 1, kappa = 8 pi G.

**Concepts:** Newtonian limit of the field equation, Einstein gravitational coupling constant, Einstein tensor, Poisson's equation for gravity

### Example 13.5 · intro · GA §13.4 p.148 (pdf 165)

**Problem:** Rewrite the Einstein equation so that the Ricci tensor stands alone on the left.

**Method:** Contract the field equation with the inverse metric, use g^{mu nu} g_{mu nu} = 4 to get R - 2R = 8 pi G T, so R = -8 pi G T; substitute into the original equation and rearrange.

**Key insight:** In four dimensions the curvature scalar is fixed locally by the trace of the source, so the equation can be trace-reversed; the form makes vacuum (Ricci-flat) problems especially transparent.

**Result:** R_{mu nu} = 8 pi G (T_{mu nu} - g_{mu nu} T / 2) (eqn 13.47).

**Concepts:** trace-reversed form of the Einstein equation, trace of the energy-momentum tensor, Ricci scalar

### Cosmological-constant term (in-text) · intro · GA §13.4 p.148 (pdf 165)

**Problem:** Determine whether extra terms can be added to G = 8 pi G T without breaking consistency with energy-momentum conservation.

**Method:** Observe that a constant multiple of the metric has zero covariant divergence because g_{mu nu;alpha} = 0, so adding -Lambda g to the right leaves the divergence argument intact; then move the term into an effective source tensor.

**Key insight:** The consistency argument fixes the equation only up to a term proportional to the metric; that leftover freedom is the cosmological constant.

**Result:** G = 8 pi G T - Lambda g (13.49), equivalently T^vac = -Lambda g / (8 pi G) (13.51).

**Concepts:** cosmological constant, vacuum energy-momentum tensor, metric compatibility of the connection


## Analogies and intuitions

### Poisson's equation as a template: Newtonian potential stands in for the metric, mass density for energy-momentum, and the Laplacian for 'two derivatives'. → Einstein field equation · strong · GA §13.2 p.144 (pdf 161)

Newtonian gravity is rewritten as a local field equation so its shape (second derivatives of a field proportional to a source) can be copied into the schematic relativistic equation with an unknown constant kappa, which is later fixed by the same analogy.

**Where it breaks down:** The copy is only structural: GR is nonlinear, the source depends on the metric, and the metric has ten components rather than one potential; the naive rank-2 copy (Ricci = kappa T) actually fails.

**App idea:** Two-row dictionary animation: Phi, rho, Laplacian and 4 pi G morph into g_{mu nu}, T_{mu nu}, second-derivative curvature operator and 8 pi G, with each pair highlighted when the learner hovers over it.

### Electromagnetism (and Newtonian gravity) as linear theories where you place the charges first and then solve for the fields. → nonlinearity of general relativity · strong · GA §13.2 p.144 (pdf 161)

Margin note 13 contrasts this workflow with GR, where the source tensor is itself expressed with the unknown metric, so metric and matter must be found together.

**Where it breaks down:** Maxwell theory in curved spacetime also couples to g; the contrast is about gravity's self-coupling rather than about EM being simpler in every respect.

**App idea:** Paint-a-source sandbox with a 'linear' toggle: in linear mode the field appears immediately by superposition; in GR mode a loop animation shows repeated metric/matter updates.

### The Ricci tensor as an average of the Riemann tensor that keeps only the volume-shrinking part of curvature. → Ricci tensor · useful · GA §13.3 p.145 (pdf 162)

Offered in margin notes 15 and 21 to explain why Ricci looked like the natural object to equate to matter and what physical content it carries.

**Where it breaks down:** Ricci omits the shape-distorting (Weyl) part, which is exactly what survives in vacuum, so 'average' should not suggest Ricci contains all gravitational effects.

**App idea:** Ball of free test particles falling through a star: display volume change (Ricci) inside the matter and pure shape distortion with constant volume (Weyl) outside.

### The boundary of a boundary is zero, as in the vector identity div curl = 0. → Bianchi identity · useful · GA §13.3 p.146 (pdf 163)

One-sentence motivation that makes an intimidating index identity feel like a familiar vector-calculus fact, deferring the proof to Chapter 43.

**Where it breaks down:** Only suggestive at this stage; the precise link needs differential forms and the curvature two-form.

**App idea:** Show a cube's faces (boundary) and then the edges of those faces, each edge traversed twice in opposite directions so contributions cancel; link to the analogous cancellation in the Bianchi identity.

### A Green's function as the response to a single point source, from which any source's response is built by adding up point responses. → Green's function · useful · GA §13.2 p.144 (pdf 161)

Margin note 11 and Example 13.2 present the solution of Poisson's equation as superposition of -1/|x - x'| kernels.

**Where it breaks down:** Superposition is unavailable in full GR; it returns only in linearized theory.

**App idea:** Click to drop point masses on a 2D grid and watch the potential surface add up as a sum of kernels, with a toggle to paint a smooth density.

### The Riemann tensor as 'the gravitational field' itself. → Riemann curvature tensor · useful · GA §13.1 p.142 (pdf 159)

Margin note 5 identifies what cannot be transformed away at a point with the physical field of gravitation, while explaining why its valence keeps it off the left side of the field equation.

**Where it breaks down:** Riemann corresponds to tidal field (second derivatives of Phi), not to the Newtonian g-vector (first derivative), which is frame-dependent in GR.

### Treating the cosmological constant as one more ingredient in the Universe's energy budget. → vacuum energy-momentum tensor · useful · GA §13.4 p.149 (pdf 166)

The Lambda term is moved to the source side as T^vac so the equation keeps its G = 8 pi G T form.

**Where it breaks down:** This 'ingredient' has negative pressure equal in magnitude to its density and does not dilute as space expands, unlike any ordinary matter.

**App idea:** Energy-budget pie with a Lambda slice; toggling Lambda shows T^vac components and the sign of rho + 3p that controls attraction or repulsion.

### Two columns, geometry and physics, growing from common principles and meeting at an equals sign. → Einstein field equation · useful · GA p.141 (pdf 158)

Fig. 13.1 structures the chapter as building two sides separately before joining them.

**Where it breaks down:** The picture hides the feedback of Fig. 13.2: the physics column depends on the metric too.


## Misconceptions addressed

### Because the energy-momentum tensor has two indices, the Ricci tensor (also two indices) should simply be set proportional to it. · GA §13.3 p.145 (pdf 162)

**Correction:** Rank matching is necessary but not sufficient: the equation must also respect div T = 0. The Ricci tensor is not identically divergence-free, and imposing R^{mu nu} = kappa T^{mu nu} forces a constant trace of T everywhere. The divergence-free Einstein tensor is required.

**Why tempting:** It is the simplest curvature object of the right type, and Einstein himself tried it.

### The full Riemann tensor, being the real measure of gravity, should itself appear on the left of the field equation. · GA §13.1 p.142 (pdf 159)

**Correction:** Riemann has valence (1,3) while T has valence (2,0); only contractions of Riemann can be equated to T, and the part of Riemann not fixed by matter (the Weyl part) is determined by the field equations plus boundary conditions rather than locally by the source.

**Why tempting:** Riemann is presented as the definitive, coordinate-independent signature of gravitation.

### Since Einstein's equation is modelled on Poisson's equation, one can specify the matter distribution first and then solve for the metric, adding up contributions as in Newtonian gravity. · GA §13.2 p.144 (pdf 161)

**Correction:** The energy-momentum tensor is built with the metric, and matter moves according to geometry, so source and metric must be solved together; the equations are nonlinear and superposition fails except in the weak-field approximation.

**Why tempting:** The template analogy and experience with electrostatics and Newtonian potentials.

### If a single particle accelerates in some coordinates, gravity (curvature) must be present. · GA §13.1 p.141 (pdf 158)

**Correction:** An accelerating coordinate system or a homogeneous field can mimic the effect; only curvature, revealed by relative acceleration of neighbouring free particles, is an unambiguous signature.

**Why tempting:** In Newtonian physics acceleration relative to coordinates is taken as evidence of a force.

### A constant Ricci scalar is by itself what rules out the Ricci guess. · GA §13.3 p.146 (pdf 163)

**Correction:** A constant R is not unphysical on its own; the problem arises because the guess ties R to the trace of T, so matter would have to be uniformly spread through the Universe.

**Why tempting:** The derivation reaches R = const first, which looks like the punchline.

### The bold G of the Einstein tensor is the same thing as Newton's gravitational constant G (or the Green's function G). · GA §13.3 p.146 (pdf 163)

**Correction:** Three different objects share the letter in this chapter: the Einstein tensor (rank-2 tensor field), Newton's constant (a number), and the Green's function G(x, x') of Example 13.2.

**Why tempting:** Identical symbol, often in the same equation (G = 8 pi G T).

### A positive cosmological constant repels because it corresponds to negative energy density. · GA §13.4 p.149 (pdf 166)

**Correction:** With signature (-,+,+,+), T^vac = -Lambda g / (8 pi G) has positive energy density Lambda / (8 pi G) and pressure equal to minus that density; gravitational attraction in GR is sourced by rho + 3p, which is negative here, producing repulsion.

**Why tempting:** The book attributes the repulsion to the opposite sign of the Lambda g term relative to positive T, which readers easily translate into 'negative energy'.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### Where there is no matter (T = 0), spacetime must be flat. · GA §13.4 p.148 (pdf 165)

**Correction:** The trace-reversed form gives R_{mu nu} = 0 in vacuum, which constrains only the Ricci part of curvature; the Riemann tensor can be non-zero (tidal fields around a star, gravitational waves).

**Why tempting:** Reading 'curvature = energy' literally suggests no energy means no curvature.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### The chapter's argument proves Einstein's equation uniquely from first principles. · GA §13.4 p.148 (pdf 165)

**Correction:** The authors call their route haphazard: it is a plausibility argument that fixes the equation only once one assumes a linear relation between T and a curvature tensor built from g and its second derivatives, and even then a Lambda g term remains free.

**Why tempting:** The step-by-step elimination of alternatives feels like a derivation.

### Covariant conservation div T = 0 means the total energy of matter in the Universe is conserved, just as in flat spacetime. · GA §13.2 p.145 (pdf 162)

**Correction:** The covariant law is local; in curved spacetime it cannot generally be integrated into a conserved global total, because matter exchanges energy with the gravitational field, which has no local energy-momentum tensor.

**Why tempting:** In special relativity the same-looking equation does give global conservation via Gauss's theorem.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*


## Thought experiments

### Gravity or perverse coordinates? · GA §13.1 p.141 (pdf 158)

**Setup:** Watch a single free particle appear to accelerate in some coordinate system and ask whether a gravitational field is responsible or merely the coordinates.

**Lesson:** One particle cannot decide; one needs the coordinate-independent residue, curvature, revealed by the changing separation of neighbouring free particles, which motivates the Riemann tensor as the left-side ingredient.

**App idea:** Toggle between a rotating/accelerating grid in flat space and a real tidal field: a lone particle looks the same in both, but a pair of particles reveals the difference.

### A universe obeying Ricci = kappa T · GA §13.3 p.147 (pdf 164)

**Setup:** Suppose the Ricci guess were true and apply energy-momentum conservation together with the contracted Bianchi identity, then look at a universe with lumps of matter (e.g. a star surrounded by empty space).

**Lesson:** The trace of T would have to take the same value inside the star and in the vacuum around it, which is impossible; the guess contradicts the clumpy Universe we see. (The star illustration is the dossier's concrete version of the book's argument.)

**App idea:** Map of trace(T) across a region containing a star; under the Ricci guess a warning overlay shows it must be flat-coloured, while the real equation allows the lump.

### Solving for sources and fields in sequence · GA §13.2 p.144 (pdf 161)

**Setup:** Try the electrostatics workflow in GR: write down a star's energy-momentum tensor and then solve for the metric.

**Lesson:** Writing T already requires the metric (e.g. normalizing four-velocities, lowering indices), so the attempt is circular; one must search for consistent pairs of g and T, which is why GR is hard.

**App idea:** Fig. 13.2 redesign: iterative star solver that lights up each arrow of the loop per iteration.

- **Size of the Einstein coupling constant** (numerical-estimate, GA §13.4 p.148 (pdf 165)): In SI units kappa = 8 pi G / c^4 is extraordinarily small, so curvature produced by everyday energy densities is tiny; strong curvature needs astrophysical densities. - **Newton's gravitational constant** (numerical-estimate, GA §13.2 p.142 (pdf 159)): Quoted alongside Newton's force law as the constant in the field theory; the value printed is an older determination. Key numbers: G = 6.672(4)e-11 m^3 kg^-1 s^-2 (book); CODATA 2018 value 6.67430(15)e-11 (dossier note).
- **Gravitational bending of starlight** (observation, GA §13.2 p.144 (pdf 161)): Cited as experimental evidence that gravity cannot simply act within flat space: light, which travels straight in flat spacetime, is deflected, so gravity must warp spacetime.
- **Non-uniform distribution of matter** (observation, GA §13.3 p.147 (pdf 164)): The everyday fact that matter is clumped (stars, planets, empty space) is the observation that kills the Ricci-tensor guess, which would require a uniform trace of T.
- **Pressure versus energy density in ordinary matter** (numerical-estimate, GA §13.4 p.147 (pdf 164)): The Newtonian limit keeps only T^00 because p is tiny compared with rho c^2. Dossier estimate (not in the book): at the Sun's centre p ~ 2.5e16 Pa and rho c^2 ~ 1.4e22 Pa, a ratio of about 2e-6; in the Earth's core the ratio is of order 1e-10. Key numbers: p / (rho c^2) ~ 2e-6 (solar core), ~1e-10 (Earth's core) (own estimates).
- **Curvature scale inside the Earth** (numerical-estimate, GA §13.4 p.148 (pdf 165)): Dossier estimate (not in the book): with R = -8 pi G T / c^4 and T ~ -rho c^2 for dust, the Ricci scalar inside rock of mean density 5500 kg/m^3 is about 8 pi G rho / c^2, showing how weakly ordinary matter curves spacetime. Key numbers: R ~ 1e-22 m^-2, curvature radius ~ 1e11 m (about 0.7 AU) (own estimate).

## Notation and conventions

- **Metric signature:** (-,+,+,+): in the weak-field limit g_00 ~ g^00 ~ -1 and g_ii ~ g^ii ~ 1. — Hence A^00 ~ A_00 and A^ii ~ A_ii for nearly flat metrics; mixed 0i components flip sign. (GA §13.4 p.147 (pdf 164))
- **Units:** c = 1 for most of the chapter with Newton's G kept explicit; factors of c are temporarily restored in Example 13.4; later chapters also set G = 1. — kappa = 8 pi G / c^4 in SI becomes 8 pi G with c = 1. (GA §13.4 p.147 (pdf 164))
- **Letter G:** Bold G is the Einstein tensor, plain G is Newton's constant, and G(x, x') in Example 13.2 is a Green's function. — Margin note 17 flags the first clash; the Green's function clash is not flagged. (GA §13.3 p.146 (pdf 163))
- **Covariant and partial derivatives:** Semicolon subscripts denote covariant derivatives, commas partial derivatives; div T is written T^{mu nu}_{;mu}. For scalars the two coincide. (GA §13.3 p.146 (pdf 163))
- **Riemann and Ricci index conventions:** R^alpha_{beta gamma delta} with the derivative of Gamma^alpha_{delta beta} along x^gamma carrying a plus sign (eqn 13.5); Ricci contracts the upper index with the third slot, R_{mu nu} = R^alpha_{mu alpha nu}. — Same as Chapter 11 (MTW-style); with signature (-,+,+,+) this gives G = +8 pi G T. (GA §13.1 p.142 (pdf 159))
- **Tensor valence:** (m,n) counts upper and lower slots: Riemann is (1,3), the energy-momentum tensor is written as (2,0) with components T^{mu nu}; the Einstein tensor is (2,0) or (0,2). (GA §13.1 p.142 (pdf 159))
- **Cosmological constant sign:** Lambda appears as -Lambda g on the right-hand side, equivalent to +Lambda g_{mu nu} on the left; positive Lambda is repulsive. (GA §13.4 p.149 (pdf 166))
- **Schematic boxes:** Boxed symbols such as the box of second derivatives of g and the box of kappa T denote the schematic structure of an equation, not precise tensor expressions. (GA §13.2 p.144 (pdf 161))
- **Newtonian versus spacetime objects:** Arrowed symbols (g-vector, F, x) are Euclidean 3-vectors; bold symbols (g, T, R, G) are spacetime tensor fields. delta^(3) is the three-dimensional Dirac delta. — The Newtonian field g-vector and the metric g share a letter. (GA §13.2 p.143 (pdf 160))
- **Source superscripts and hats:** T^mat and T^vac separate matter and vacuum (Lambda) contributions; hatted indices denote orthonormal-frame components. (GA §13.4 p.149 (pdf 166))

## Teaching gems

### Put the natural wrong equation R^{mu nu} = kappa T^{mu nu} on the board, take its divergence, and let conservation plus the contracted Bianchi identity expose that it would force a uniform Universe; the Einstein tensor then appears as the fix. · GA §13.3 p.145 (pdf 162)

**Why it works:** Learners see why the odd-looking -g R/2 term is necessary instead of memorizing it, and they learn that conservation laws constrain which field equations are possible.

**App idea:** Field-equation workbench: learner picks coefficients in R_{mu nu} + a g_{mu nu} R + b g_{mu nu} = kappa T_{mu nu}; the app shows the divergence of the left side symbolically and, for a star-in-vacuum density map, the constraint it imposes on matter. Only a = -1/2 (any b) passes, and b is labelled as Lambda.

### Rewrite Newton as a field theory until Poisson's equation reads 'two derivatives of a field proportional to its source', then transplant that shape into relativity with an unknown constant. · GA §13.2 p.144 (pdf 161)

**Why it works:** It gives a structural expectation (second-order equation for g sourced by T) before any tensor detail and reuses what learners already trust.

**App idea:** Morphing equation card: Laplacian Phi = 4 pi G rho animates term by term into G_{mu nu} = 8 pi G T_{mu nu}, with tooltips explaining what changed and why each change is forced.

### Leave the constant kappa unknown until the end and determine it by requiring the 00 component to reproduce Poisson's equation. · GA §13.4 p.147 (pdf 164)

**Why it works:** Shows correspondence as a working tool that pins down numbers, and reveals that Newton sees only one component of a ten-component equation.

**App idea:** Kappa slider: learner varies kappa and the app computes the weak-field potential of the Earth or the Sun and the resulting orbital period of a test satellite, comparing with the Newtonian/observed value; the match occurs only at 8 pi G / c^4.

### Contrast the one-way 'place sources, solve fields' workflow of electromagnetism with the source-geometry loop of GR (Fig. 13.2). · GA §13.2 p.144 (pdf 161)

**Why it works:** Makes nonlinearity concrete as a workflow difference rather than an abstract property, and explains why exact GR solutions are rare.

**App idea:** Iterative self-consistent star solver with a linear-mode toggle, animating each pass around the loop (see Fig. 13.2 redesign).

### Trace the field equation to discover that the curvature scalar is set locally by the trace of the source, then trace-reverse to isolate Ricci. · GA §13.4 p.148 (pdf 165)

**Why it works:** A two-line manipulation yields a new, often more useful form and immediately shows that vacuum means Ricci-flat, not flat.

**App idea:** Source picker (dust, radiation, stiff fluid, vacuum energy): the app displays T_{mu nu}, its trace, R, and R_{mu nu} from version 2; radiation shows R = 0, vacuum energy shows R_{mu nu} proportional to g_{mu nu}.

### Treat the cosmological constant as the leftover freedom of the consistency argument (anything with zero divergence can be added) and then relabel it as a vacuum energy-momentum tensor. · GA §13.4 p.149 (pdf 166)

**Why it works:** Lambda emerges from logic rather than from history or fudge, and moving it across the equals sign demonstrates that geometry and source terms are interchangeable bookkeeping.

**App idea:** Toggle Lambda on the left or right of the equation: the same term is displayed as geometry or as T^vac with rho and p = -rho, and a ball of test particles contracts or expands depending on the sign of rho + 3p.

### Offer a words-only margin recap of the Bianchi argument for readers overwhelmed by indices. · GA §13.4 p.147 (pdf 164)

**Why it works:** Separates the logical skeleton (conservation must be built into geometry) from the algebra, letting learners of different levels follow the same argument.

**App idea:** Dual-track explanation toggle in the tutor: 'story mode' narrates the logic with icons; 'index mode' shows each contraction step, with synchronized highlighting.

### Derive Poisson's equation from the integral law and recover the potential with a Green's function, then point out that superposition is what GR loses. · GA §13.2 p.143 (pdf 160)

**Why it works:** Sets up the contrast between linear and nonlinear field theories using a technique learners can actually compute.

**App idea:** 2D density painter: the potential is computed as a sum of -1/r kernels and the numerically evaluated Laplacian heat-map is shown to reproduce 4 pi G rho point by point.


## Gaps and pitfalls

- **Margin eqn 13.4 writes the geodesic deviation equation as D^2 xi / dtau^2 = +R(., u, xi, u). The minus sign is present in GA eqns 11.6 and 11.39 but missing from 11.38 as well, so the book is internally inconsistent on this sign.** — Sign confusion about whether positive curvature focuses or defocuses neighbouring geodesics, and apparent inconsistency with Chapter 11. *Suggestion:* Use D^2 xi^a / dtau^2 = -R^a_{bcd} u^b xi^c u^d throughout and verify the sign with the Newtonian tidal tensor.
- **The explanation that positive Lambda repels 'owing to the difference in sign compared to positive T' is misleading: T^vac = -Lambda g / (8 pi G) has positive energy density and negative pressure p = -rho, and it satisfies the dominant energy condition; the repulsion comes from rho + 3p < 0.** — Learners may conclude that dark energy has negative energy density or violates the energy conditions just discussed. *Suggestion:* Compute T^vac_{tt} and T^vac_{ii} explicitly with signature (-,+,+,+), then show that the acceleration of a small ball of test particles is governed by rho + 3p.
- **The letter G stands for the Einstein tensor, Newton's constant and, in Example 13.2, a Green's function (eqn 13.22 even contains both G's in one expression). Only the first clash is flagged.** — Misreading of equations such as Phi = G integral of G(x, x') rho, and of G = 8 pi G T. *Suggestion:* In the app use distinct glyphs: G_{mu nu} for Einstein, G_N for Newton's constant, and a script or subscripted symbol for the Green's function.
- **Margin note 11 defines the Green's function with L G = delta, but eqn 13.21 uses a 4 pi delta source, and the resulting kernel carries a minus sign and an external factor G.** — Normalization and sign errors when learners try to apply the general recipe. *Suggestion:* State one convention and show explicitly how the 4 pi and the minus sign are absorbed.
- **Uniqueness is not addressed: the authors admit the route is haphazard but do not mention that, in four dimensions, G_{mu nu} + Lambda g_{mu nu} is the only divergence-free symmetric tensor built from the metric and its first two derivatives (Lovelock's theorem).** — Learners may wonder whether other curvature combinations would work, or believe the plausibility argument is a proof. *Suggestion:* Add a short note on the assumptions (locality, second order, divergence-free, four dimensions) and name Lovelock's theorem; point to the action-principle route in Chapter 40.
- **Energy-momentum conservation is described as encoded 'at least approximately' by div T = 0, and the chapter does not explain that the covariant law is exact but only local, with no local energy density for gravity.** — Confusion about whether energy is conserved in GR, and why gravitational energy is not part of T despite the nonlinearity discussion. *Suggestion:* Clarify local versus global conservation, and note that the gravitational field's energy cannot be localized in a tensor.
- **Example 13.4 is terse: it recalls weak-field Gamma and Riemann components without derivation, silently drops Gamma-Gamma products and time derivatives, and uses the field equation itself (with vanishing spatial T) to set G^{ij} = 0.** — The key identification R_00 = Laplacian Phi / c^2 may feel like magic, and the logic of using the equation to calibrate itself can seem circular. *Suggestion:* Show explicitly that R^i_{0j0} is the Newtonian tidal tensor to first order in Phi, and explain that the procedure is a consistency calibration valid to leading order.
- **Margin note 15 dates Einstein's Ricci-tensor proposal to October 1915; the proposal is usually associated with his 4 and 11 November 1915 Prussian Academy communications (and earlier notebook explorations of 1912-13).** — Minor historical inaccuracy that a tutor might repeat. *Suggestion:* Say 'autumn 1915, in the weeks before the final November 25 paper' unless a precise source is checked.
- **The printed value of Newton's constant, 6.672(4)e-11, is an old determination.** — Numerical answers differ slightly from modern references. *Suggestion:* Use the CODATA value 6.67430(15)e-11 m^3 kg^-1 s^-2 in the app.
- **Margin note 10 says eqn 13.18 matches eqn 0.12, but the Overture's continuous-density Gauss law appears to be eqn 0.14, with 0.12 its point-mass form.** — A learner following the pointer may find a slightly different equation. *Suggestion:* Cross-link both Overture equations when citing.
- **The chapter never states the vacuum consequence R_{mu nu} = 0 of version 2, nor that vacuum spacetime can still be curved.** — The literal reading 'curvature = energy' suggests that empty space is flat, which would make Schwarzschild and gravitational waves puzzling. *Suggestion:* Immediately after version 2, set T = 0, get Ricci-flatness, and contrast Ricci with the full Riemann tensor using the tidal field outside a star.
- **The contraction steps in Example 13.3 (which indices to raise, which symmetries turn Riemann into Ricci with a sign) are compressed into two lines.** — Index-novices get lost and lose the thread of the conservation argument. *Suggestion:* Show each contraction with colour-coded indices and state the symmetry used at each step; offer the margin physics recap as a parallel track.
- **In listing how Einstein's theory must differ from Newton's, the book cites Fermat's principle as saying light goes straight in flat spacetime, then cites starlight bending as proof that gravity warps spacetime. The inference is fast: Newtonian corpuscular light also bends (by half the GR amount), so bending alone does not force a geometric theory. The decisive evidence is the measured size of the deflection.** — Learners may think any observed deflection of light rules out Newtonian gravity, or may mix up Fermat's least-time principle with a statement about straight lines. *Suggestion:* Say that Fermat's principle picks out paths of stationary travel time, which are straight only in flat geometry, and quote the 0.87 versus 1.75 arcsecond solar-limb values so the factor of 2 is what discriminates. Point ahead to the light-bending treatment in the Schwarzschild chapters.

## Tutor notes

- Open with: 'Newton says mass tells the potential how to curve via Poisson's equation. If gravity is geometry, what should play the role of the potential, and what the mass density?' Let the learner propose metric and energy-momentum before showing the template.
- Recommended order: (1) Poisson as template, (2) recap Riemann -> Ricci -> R, (3) conservation of T, (4) the Ricci guess and why it fails, (5) Einstein tensor, (6) fixing kappa, (7) trace-reversed form and vacuum, (8) Lambda.
- For learners weak on indices, run the Bianchi step in 'story mode' first: geometry has a built-in conservation law, matter has one too, and the field equation must make them agree. Show the index version only on request.
- Check for understanding: ask why R^{mu nu} = kappa T^{mu nu} is not ruled out by dimensional analysis or rank but by conservation; a good answer mentions div T = 0 and the contracted Bianchi identity.
- Check for understanding: ask the learner to compute the trace of G_{mu nu} (Exercise 13.3) and to explain why G is called trace-reversed.
- Make the constant-trace contradiction concrete: inside a star the trace of T is roughly -rho, outside it is zero, so it cannot be constant everywhere.
- When fixing kappa, emphasize that only the 00 component survives in the Newtonian regime and that pressure is negligible because p is roughly a few hundred thousand times smaller than rho c^2 even in the Sun's core (ratio about 2e-6).
- Common learner question: 'If there is no matter, is spacetime flat?' Answer with version 2: Ricci vanishes, but Riemann (tidal curvature) need not; point ahead to Schwarzschild.
- Common learner question: 'Why does a positive cosmological constant push things apart if it is positive energy?' Explain negative pressure and the rho + 3p rule rather than repeating the book's sign argument.
- Common learner question: 'Is this derivation unique?' Say it is a plausibility argument; mention that under reasonable assumptions (four dimensions, second-order, divergence-free, built from the metric) only G + Lambda g works, and point to the action route in Chapter 40.
- Watch the symbol G: always say 'Einstein tensor' or 'Newton's constant' aloud, and warn about the Green's function G in Example 13.2.
- Use the heuristics list at the end of the chapter as a checklist before moving to Schwarzschild or cosmology problems: light cones, frame choice, index rules, general covariance, u . u = -1, perfect-fluid components.
