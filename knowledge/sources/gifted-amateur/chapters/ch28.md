# GA ch28 · Hawking radiation

> A short, heuristic tour of semiclassical black-hole physics: a vacuum pair split by the horizon explains why black holes glow, an uncertainty-plus-redshift estimate gives an emitted energy of order hbar/M, and the result is promoted to a black-body temperature, evaporation law, surface gravity, Bekenstein-Hawking entropy, the four laws of black-hole mechanics, the generalized second law, the information puzzle and the Unruh effect.

**Pages:** printed 289–296 · pdf 306–313 · **Difficulty:** math 3/5, conceptual 4/5, novice-friendliness 3/5

Algebra in Examples 28.1-28.4 is short, but the conceptual content (virtual particles, Killing energy sign flips, horizon thermodynamics, information loss, observer-dependent particles) is subtle and presented heuristically with the rigorous calculation only quoted. Surface gravity and Exercise 28.4 need comfort with covariant derivatives, Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates and Wick rotation. Several printed slips (lifetime formula, eqn 28.12 reference, M_CMB power of ten, eqn 28.23, work-term signs, Unruh units, d theta squared) can confuse careful readers.

## Role in the book

The only quantum-flavoured chapter of Part IV and its second-to-last chapter (Chapter 29 on charged and rotating holes closes the part). Chapters 21-27 built the Schwarzschild geometry, geodesic conserved quantities, Eddington-Finkelstein and Kruskal coordinates and Penrose diagrams as purely classical tools; this chapter cashes them in to show that horizons have thermodynamic properties once quantum fields are allowed. It relies on the Killing-vector energy -p.xi, the swap of timelike and spacelike character across r = 2M, ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates for the surface-gravity calculation, and the Rindler picture from Chapter 26. It sets up Chapter 29 (Omega and Phi in the first law belong to rotating and charged holes) and points ahead to quantum gravity (Chapter 49) as the missing ingredient for the end state of evaporation. It is deliberately qualitative: no quantum field theory in curved spacetime is done, and the rigorous numbers are quoted.

## Learning objectives

- Reader can describe the pair-creation picture of Hawking radiation: a virtual pair produced just outside the horizon, one member crossing inside, the other escaping as real radiation.
- Reader can explain why the conserved Killing energy -p.xi may be negative for a particle inside the horizon without any locally measured negative energy, using the sign change of xi.xi = g_tt at r = 2M.
- Reader can show that a photon on a radial plunge with u^t = 0 inside the horizon has positive locally measured energy exactly when p^r < 0.
- Reader can reproduce the order-of-magnitude estimate E_infinity ~ hbar/4M from the proper time to fall from 2M + epsilon to the horizon, the uncertainty relation and the redshift factor g^tt, and explain why epsilon cancels.
- Reader can quote the Hawking temperature k_B T = hbar/(8 pi M) (and its SI form), evaluate it for a solar-mass hole, and compare it with the CMB temperature.
- Reader can derive the scalings L proportional to M^-2 and lifetime proportional to M^3 from Stefan-Boltzmann with horizon area 16 pi M^2, and explain the negative heat capacity.
- Reader can define surface gravity through xi^mu xi^nu_{;mu} = kappa xi^nu on a Killing horizon and compute kappa = 1/4M for Schwarzschild in ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates.
- Reader can obtain S = k_B A/(4 hbar) by matching dM = T dS with dA = 32 pi M dM, and state the four laws of black-hole mechanics with their thermodynamic counterparts.
- Reader can explain why Hawking evaporation violates the classical area theorem and how the generalized second law restores entropy bookkeeping.
- Reader can state the black-hole information problem, the candidate end states mentioned (complete evaporation, remnant, final burst), and read the Penrose diagram of an evaporating hole.
- Reader can connect Hawking radiation to the Unruh effect via the near-horizon Rindler form of Schwarzschild, and quote T_Unruh = hbar a/(2 pi c k_B).
- Reader can carry out the Euclidean near-horizon argument: rewrite the metric as flat polar coordinates after Wick rotation and read off the temperature from the required periodicity of imaginary time.

## Teaching approach

An openly heuristic, example-led chapter. Instead of quantum field theory in curved spacetime, the authors run a chain of short classical-GR calculations dressed with one quantum ingredient (the energy-time uncertainty relation) to make Hawking radiation plausible and to get its scale, then quote the exact result and spend the second half on thermodynamic consequences stated as analogies and laws. Heavy lifting is pushed into margin notes (Killing horizons, CMB feeding, thermodynamic dictionary) and exercises (horizon area, heat capacity, surface gravity via Killing's equation, Euclidean temperature). The tone is informal, with jokes about BBC polls, a spoof newspaper and cooking steak by acceleration.

**Style:** physical-motivation-first, example-driven, analogy-driven, thought-experiment, survey-overview, conversational-informal

**Narrative arc**

1. Motivating claim: black holes are not purely absorbers; they glow like black bodies because the quantum vacuum fluctuates.
2. Quantum ingredient: virtual pairs are off the mass shell and live for a time set by the uncertainty relation.
3. Mechanism picture (Fig. 28.1): the horizon can split a pair, freeing both members.
4. Physical constraint: locally measured energy -p.u must be positive to protect causality.
5. Check inside the horizon (Example 28.1): a plunging observer finds infalling photons have positive local energy with no constraint on p^t, so the partner is freed.
6. Energy at infinity: -p.xi can be negative inside because xi turns spacelike, so the infalling member carries negative Killing energy and the escaping one positive.
7. Estimate (Example 28.2): uncertainty-limited energy near 2M + epsilon redshifted to infinity gives hbar/4M.
8. Quote the rigorous answer: thermal spectrum with k_B T = hbar/8 pi M.
9. Consequence (Example 28.3): Stefan-Boltzmann gives dM/dt proportional to -M^-2 and lifetime proportional to M^3; margin note on CMB feeding.
10. Geometry of temperature: define surface gravity via Killing horizon and compute kappa = 1/4M (Example 28.4), so k_B T = hbar kappa/2 pi.
11. Entropy from the first law: area plays the role of entropy, S = k_B A/4 hbar.
12. Generalize: the four laws of black-hole mechanics and a thermodynamic dictionary.
13. Puzzles: evaporation shrinks area, requiring the generalized second law; information loss threatens unitarity; the end state needs quantum gravity (Example 28.5, Fig. 28.2).
14. Echo in flat spacetime: near-horizon Rindler form and the Unruh effect (Example 28.6, Fig. 28.3).
15. Exercises close the loop: horizon area, negative heat capacity, the full surface-gravity computation, and the Euclidean derivation of the exact Hawking temperature.

**Signature moves**

- Replace a quantum field theory calculation by a semiclassical estimate whose free parameter (the distance epsilon above the horizon) cancels, so the answer is forced to scale as hbar/M.
- Distinguish the locally measured energy -p.u (always positive) from the Killing energy -p.xi (sign-indefinite inside the horizon) to defuse the negative-energy worry.
- Choose the simplest possible interior observer (u^t = 0 radial plunge) so that a single component survives the inner product.
- Promote an estimate to a law by quoting the exact result and immediately extracting scaling consequences (T proportional to 1/M, lifetime proportional to M^3).
- Build entropy by reading the first law of thermodynamics backwards from dM expressed in terms of dA.
- Side-by-side lists: laws of black-hole mechanics in the text, ordinary thermodynamic laws in the margin, then an explicit dictionary.
- Use the Rindler wedge to rerun the pair-splitting story in flat spacetime, turning Hawking into Unruh.
- Offload the most satisfying rigorous argument (Euclidean periodicity) to a scaffolded exercise.

## Section by section

### Chapter opening: vacuum fluctuations and virtual pairs — p.289 (pdf 306)

Announces Hawking's 1974 result that black holes emit thermal radiation and names quantum field theory as its proper home. Introduces the quantum vacuum as a ground state that still fluctuates, producing short-lived particle-antiparticle pairs whose borrowed energy and lifetime obey an uncertainty relation, and explains that such virtual particles violate the mass-shell condition p.p = -m^2.

**Concepts:** vacuum fluctuations, virtual particles, energy-time uncertainty relation, mass shell

**Key moves**
- Frames Hawking radiation as a quantum effect that classical GR alone cannot produce.
- Links the finite pair lifetime to the energy borrowed from the vacuum via Delta E Delta t ~ hbar.
- Characterizes virtual particles as off-shell, so their energy is not fixed by the usual dispersion relation.

### §28.1 Hawking radiation — p.289 (pdf 306)

Pictures Hawking emission as a vacuum pair born just outside r = 2M in which one member crosses the horizon and the other escapes. Imposes positivity of locally measured energy -p.u, then (Example 28.1) shows that for an observer plunging inside the horizon with u^t = 0 an infalling photon has positive local energy whatever its p^t, so it can propagate freely and release its partner. Next it considers the Killing energy -p.xi measured by an observer at infinity: since xi = e_t becomes spacelike inside the horizon, the infalling member may carry negative Killing energy while the escaping one carries an equal positive amount, conserving energy at infinity. Example 28.2 estimates the escaping energy from the proper time of fall from 2M + epsilon, the uncertainty relation and the static redshift factor, obtaining hbar/4M. The section ends by noting that the rigorous calculation gives a similar answer.

**Concepts:** Hawking radiation, horizon pair-splitting picture, positivity of locally measured energy, Killing energy, timelike Killing vector turning spacelike inside the horizon, negative Killing energy inside the horizon, radial plunge with zero Killing energy inside the horizon, gravitational redshift near the horizon, energy-time uncertainty relation

**Key moves**
- Require E_obs = -p.u > 0 for any physical propagation, justified by causality.
- Inside r < 2M pick u = u^r e_r with u^r = -(2M/r - 1)^{1/2} from u.u = -1 (eqn 28.1).
- Evaluate E_obs = -(2M/r - 1)^{-1/2} p^r (eqn 28.2): positive for any infalling photon, with p^t unconstrained, so the uncertainty-imposed lifetime no longer applies.
- Note xi.xi = g_tt = -(1 - 2M/r) flips sign at the horizon, so -p.xi can be negative inside without being unphysical.
- Energy conservation at infinity then forces the negative-Killing-energy member to fall in and the positive one to escape.
- Use a frame momentarily at rest at 2M + epsilon: E-tilde ~ (epsilon/2M)^{1/2} (28.4), proper fall time ~ 2(2M epsilon)^{1/2} (28.5), local energy ~ hbar over that time (28.6).
- Relate local and infinity energies through E_obs = -g^{tt} E_inf E-tilde with g^{tt} ~ -2M/epsilon (28.7-28.8); epsilon cancels, leaving E_inf = hbar/4M (28.10).

### §28.2 Black-hole thermodynamics — p.292 (pdf 309)

Quotes the exact result that the emitted spectrum is thermal with k_B T = hbar/8 pi M. Example 28.3 uses the black-body law with area 16 pi M^2 to get dM/dt proportional to -1/M^2 and a lifetime scaling as M^3; a long margin note explains that the CMB keeps feeding real holes, so only holes below about 2.3 x 10^-8 solar masses (roughly 4.5 x 10^22 kg; the book's kg figure is misprinted as 10^23) could currently shrink. Surface gravity is defined geometrically on a Killing horizon (eqn 28.13) and computed in ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates as kappa = 1/4M (Example 28.4), giving k_B T = hbar kappa/2 pi. Matching dM written in terms of dA against dU = T dS identifies entropy S = k_B A/4 hbar.

**Concepts:** Hawking temperature, Stefan-Boltzmann law, black-hole evaporation, negative heat capacity of black holes, black holes in the cosmic microwave background, surface gravity, Killing horizon, ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates, Bekenstein-Hawking entropy

**Key moves**
- Replace the estimate hbar/4M by the exact thermal result k_B T = hbar/8 pi M.
- Luminosity proportional to A T^4 with A = 16 pi M^2 and T proportional to 1/M gives L proportional to M^-2; integrating dM/dt gives lifetime proportional to M^3.
- Compare T for one solar mass (62 nK) with T_CMB = 2.73 K to show astrophysical holes grow.
- Define kappa by xi^mu xi^nu_{;mu} = kappa xi^nu on the horizon, lower the index of xi = partial_V to get xi_nu = (-1 + 2M/r, 1, 0, 0), and evaluate to kappa = 1/4M.
- Conclude k_B T = hbar kappa/2 pi.
- Write dM = dA/(32 pi M) = [hbar/(8 pi k_B M)] d(k_B A/4 hbar) and read off S.

### Within 28.2: laws of black-hole mechanics, generalized second law and information — p.293 (pdf 310)

States four laws for a hole with mass M, angular momentum L, charge q, area A and surface gravity kappa (kappa uniform on the horizon; dM = kappa dA/8 pi + Omega dL + Phi dq; area never decreases; kappa cannot be driven to zero in finitely many steps), with the ordinary laws of thermodynamics and a variable dictionary in the margin. Notes that merging holes cannot lose total area and that swallowing matter increases the hole's entropy. Because evaporation shrinks the area, it introduces the generalized second law for hole plus environment. It then raises the information problem (apparent loss of unitarity), mentions remnant and explosion proposals and Hawking's view that information is lost, and (Example 28.5) shows the Penrose diagram of a completely evaporating hole while stressing that the final stage needs quantum gravity.

**Concepts:** laws of black-hole mechanics, zeroth law of black-hole mechanics, first law of black-hole mechanics, Hawking area theorem, third law of black-hole mechanics, generalized second law, black-hole information problem, unitarity of quantum evolution, Penrose diagram of an evaporating black hole, quantum gravity at the end of evaporation

**Key moves**
- Pair each black-hole law with its thermodynamic twin (margin notes 16-17).
- Apply the area law to black-hole mergers: final area at least the sum of initial areas.
- Recognize that Hawking emission violates dA >= 0 and repair the second law by counting radiation entropy.
- Frame information loss as a clash with unitary evolution and list proposed escapes.
- Accept complete evaporation provisionally to draw the corresponding Penrose diagram.

### Within 28.2: Unruh effect and chapter summary — p.295 (pdf 312)

Recalls that near the horizon Schwarzschild coordinates behave like accelerated coordinates in Minkowski space, and introduces the Unruh effect: a uniformly accelerated observer in vacuum detects thermal radiation at k_B T = hbar a/(2 pi c) while an inertial observer detects none. Example 28.6 writes the near-horizon metric in Rindler form, identifies T = +-X as the accelerated observer's horizon (Fig. 28.3), reruns the pair-splitting story across that horizon, and estimates the temperature by dimensional analysis. The chapter summary recaps emission, T proportional to 1/M and the thermodynamic analogy.

**Concepts:** Unruh effect, Unruh temperature, Rindler coordinates, Rindler horizon, near-horizon Rindler approximation

**Key moves**
- Map near-horizon Schwarzschild to the Rindler line element ds^2 = -x^2 dt^2 + dx^2.
- Observers at fixed Rindler x are hyperbolae in the wedge X > |T|; nothing from X < |T| reaches them, so T = +-X acts as a horizon.
- Split a vacuum pair across T = X: the partner left in X > |T| is Unruh radiation.
- Dimensional analysis gives k_B T ~ hbar a/c, consistent with the exact hbar a/(2 pi c).

### Exercises: area, heat capacity, surface gravity and the Euclidean temperature — p.296 (pdf 313)

Four exercises finish the chapter's sketches: integrate the horizon area, find the (negative) heat capacity, complete the Eddington-Finkelstein surface-gravity computation using Killing's equation and a covariant-derivative rule, and derive the exact Hawking temperature by expanding Schwarzschild near r = 2M, Wick-rotating, and demanding that the resulting flat polar geometry be free of a conical defect.

**Concepts:** Killing's equation, negative heat capacity of black holes, Wick rotation and Euclidean periodicity, near-horizon Rindler approximation

**Key moves**
- Use Killing's equation to rewrite the surface-gravity definition as -xi^mu xi_mu^{;sigma} = kappa xi^sigma and reduce it to xi_{V;r} = -kappa.
- Use Gamma^V_{rV} = 0 and Gamma^r_{rV} = -M/r^2 to recover eqn 28.15.
- Substitute rho^2 = 8M(r - 2M) to obtain Rindler-like form, set t = -i t_E and t_E = 4M psi to get d rho^2 + rho^2 d psi^2.
- Require psi to have period 2 pi so that t_E has period 8 pi M, identifying hbar/k_B T = 8 pi M.

## Concepts

### vacuum fluctuations

*phenomenon · introduced* · also: quantum vacuum fluctuations, zero-point fluctuations

In quantum field theory the lowest-energy state of a field (the vacuum, containing no real particles) is not static: it continually and briefly produces particle-antiparticle pairs that recombine.

**How introduced:** Opening paragraph, as the physical source of Hawking radiation, described in words with no field theory formalism.

**Prerequisites:** energy-time uncertainty relation

**Where:** GA p.289 (pdf 306)

### virtual particles

*definition · introduced* · also: virtual pair, off-shell particles

Transient particles in intermediate states whose four-momentum does not satisfy p.p = -m^2; the energy excess they borrow limits their lifetime to roughly hbar divided by that energy.

**How introduced:** Introduced right after vacuum fluctuations to explain why pairs must annihilate quickly; the off-shell property explains why their energy is not fixed by the dispersion relation.

**Prerequisites:** mass shell, energy-time uncertainty relation

$$
p\cdot p \neq -m^2
$$

$$
\Delta t \approx \hbar/\Delta E
$$

**Notes:** The printed opening writes the lifetime as Delta E/hbar; it should be hbar/Delta E (correctly given later on the same page).

**Where:** GA p.289 (pdf 306); GA §28.1 p.289 (pdf 306)

### mass shell

*identity · revisited* · also: on-shell condition, invariant dispersion relation

A free particle of rest mass m has four-momentum obeying p.p = -m^2 (signature -+++), i.e. E^2 = |p|^2 + m^2 in an inertial frame.

**How introduced:** Recalled to define what it means for virtual particles to be off shell; margin note 2 uses the two roots E = +-(p^2 + m^2)^{1/2} to discuss antiparticles.

**Prerequisites:** four-momentum

$$
p\cdot p=-m^2
$$

$$
E=\pm(\vec p^{\,2}+m^2)^{1/2}
$$

**Where:** GA p.289 (pdf 306); GA §28.1 p.290 (pdf 307)

### energy-time uncertainty relation

*principle · introduced* · also: Heisenberg energy-time relation

Heuristic quantum relation Delta E Delta t ~ hbar linking the energy uncertainty of a transient state to its duration; used to bound how long a vacuum pair can exist.

**How introduced:** Invoked in the opening and again in Example 28.2 to assign an energy hbar/Delta tau to a photon that exists for the proper time a frame takes to fall to the horizon.

$$
\Delta E\,\Delta t\approx\hbar
$$

**Notes:** Time is not an operator, so this is an order-of-magnitude statement rather than a strict inequality.

**Where:** GA p.289 (pdf 306); GA §28.1 p.291 (pdf 308)

### antiparticle positive energy

*principle · mention* · also: reinterpretation of negative-energy solutions, Feynman-Stueckelberg interpretation

Every observed particle and antiparticle has positive energy; the negative root of the dispersion relation is reinterpreted as a positive-energy antiparticle with reversed three-momentum, equivalently a negative-energy state running backward in time.

**How introduced:** Margin notes 2 and 3 correct the common claim that antiparticles carry negative energy, using the four-momentum flip (-E, p) to (E, -p) and the wavefunction phase exp(-iEt/hbar).

**Prerequisites:** mass shell

$$
p^\mu=(-E,\vec p)\ \to\ p^\mu=(E,-\vec p)
$$

$$
\psi\propto e^{-iEt/\hbar}
$$

**Where:** GA §28.1 p.290 (pdf 307)

### positivity of locally measured energy

*principle · core* · also: local energy positivity

Any real particle or photon must have positive energy E_obs = -p.u as measured by a local observer with four-velocity u; otherwise it would effectively travel backward in time and threaten causality.

**How introduced:** Stated as the key physical requirement in section 28.1 and used as the test for whether a photon can propagate inside the horizon (Example 28.1).

**Prerequisites:** observer-measured energy, antiparticle positive energy

$$
E_{\rm obs}=-\boldsymbol p\cdot\boldsymbol u>0
$$

**Where:** GA §28.1 p.289 (pdf 306); GA §28.1 p.290 (pdf 307); GA §28.1 p.291 (pdf 308)

### observer-measured energy

*physical-quantity · revisited* · also: E_obs = -p.u

The energy of a particle with four-momentum p as measured by an observer with four-velocity u is E_obs = -p.u, the time component of p in that observer's local inertial frame.

**How introduced:** Recalled from earlier chapters and used twice: for the plunging interior observer (Example 28.1) and for a frame momentarily at rest just outside the horizon (Example 28.2).

**Prerequisites:** four-momentum, metric tensor

$$
E_{\rm obs}=-\boldsymbol p\cdot\boldsymbol u
$$

**Where:** GA §28.1 p.289 (pdf 306); GA §28.1 p.290 (pdf 307); GA §28.1 p.291 (pdf 308)

### Killing energy

*physical-quantity · developed* · also: conserved energy at infinity, energy measured at infinity, -p.xi

For a stationary spacetime with timelike Killing vector xi = partial_t, the quantity -p.xi = -p_t is conserved along a geodesic and equals the energy that a static observer at infinity (whose four-velocity is xi there) would measure.

**How introduced:** Margin note 6 identifies the static observer at infinity with xi^mu = (1,0,0,0) and relates E-tilde = -xi.u to E = -xi.p; the text then argues about its sign across the horizon.

**Prerequisites:** Killing vector, observer-measured energy

$$
E^\infty_{\rm obs}=-\boldsymbol p\cdot\boldsymbol\xi=-p_t
$$

$$
\tilde E=-\boldsymbol\xi\cdot\boldsymbol u=-u_t
$$

**Where:** GA §28.1 p.290 (pdf 307); GA §28.1 p.291 (pdf 308)

### timelike Killing vector turning spacelike inside the horizon

*phenomenon · core* · also: sign flip of g_tt at r = 2M

The Schwarzschild Killing vector xi = e_t has norm xi.xi = g_tt = -(1 - 2M/r), negative (timelike) for r > 2M, null at r = 2M and positive (spacelike) for r < 2M.

**How introduced:** Margin note 7, used to explain why a negative value of -p.xi is not forbidden inside the horizon: there, -p.xi is a momentum-like component rather than an energy anyone measures.

**Prerequisites:** Killing vector, Schwarzschild metric, event horizon

$$
\boldsymbol\xi\cdot\boldsymbol\xi=g_{tt}=-\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)
$$

**Where:** GA §28.1 p.290 (pdf 307)

### negative Killing energy inside the horizon

*phenomenon · core* · also: negative energy at infinity for infalling partner

Inside a black hole a particle can have -p.xi < 0 while every local observer still measures positive energy; the infalling member of a Hawking pair carries such negative Killing energy, so the hole's mass decreases by the energy the escaping partner takes to infinity.

**How introduced:** Argued in section 28.1 from the norm flip of xi and energy conservation for the observer at infinity, then reused in 28.2 to explain the loss of mass.

**Prerequisites:** Killing energy, timelike Killing vector turning spacelike inside the horizon, positivity of locally measured energy

$$
-\boldsymbol p_{\rm in}\cdot\boldsymbol\xi=-(-\boldsymbol p_{\rm out}\cdot\boldsymbol\xi)<0
$$

**Notes:** Same geometric mechanism as the Penrose process in the ergoregion of a Kerr hole (not mentioned in this chapter).

**Where:** GA §28.1 p.290 (pdf 307); GA §28.1 p.291 (pdf 308); GA §28.2 p.292 (pdf 309)

### radial plunge with zero Killing energy inside the horizon

*technique · introduced* · also: u^t = 0 interior observer

Inside r < 2M, r is a time coordinate, so an observer can move with only u^r non-zero; normalization u.u = -1 then gives u^r = -(2M/r - 1)^{1/2}, a timelike path with E-tilde = 0.

**How introduced:** Chosen in Example 28.1 as the simplest interior observer so that the inner product p.u reduces to a single g_rr p^r u^r term.

**Prerequisites:** Schwarzschild metric, four-velocity normalization

$$
u^r=-\left(\frac{2M}{r}-1\right)^{1/2}
$$

**Where:** GA §28.1 p.290 (pdf 307)

### Hawking radiation

*phenomenon · core* · also: black-hole radiation, Hawking emission

Quantum emission of particles by a black-hole horizon with a thermal (black-body) spectrum at temperature k_B T = hbar c^3/(8 pi G M); the energy carried off reduces the hole's mass.

**How introduced:** Introduced as a surprising 1974 result, made plausible by the pair-splitting picture and a redshift estimate, then quoted exactly at the start of section 28.2.

**Prerequisites:** vacuum fluctuations, event horizon, negative Killing energy inside the horizon, gravitational redshift near the horizon

$$
E^\infty_{\rm obs}\sim\hbar/4M\ (\text{estimate})
$$

$$
k_BT=\hbar/8\pi M
$$

**Notes:** Not yet observed, as the book notes. The heuristic picture gets the scale but not the spectrum or the 2 pi.

**Where:** GA p.289 (pdf 306); GA §28.1 p.289 (pdf 306); GA §28.1 p.291 (pdf 308); GA §28.2 p.292 (pdf 309); GA §28.2 p.295 (pdf 312)

### horizon pair-splitting picture

*solution-or-model · developed* · also: pair-creation mechanism, heuristic Hawking mechanism

Cartoon model in which a vacuum pair forms just outside the horizon; one member crosses r = 2M and is no longer forced to recombine, so the other escapes as real radiation.

**How introduced:** Fig. 28.1 and the opening of 28.1, justified by Examples 28.1-28.2 and later rerun across the Rindler horizon for the Unruh effect.

**Prerequisites:** virtual particles, event horizon, positivity of locally measured energy

**Notes:** A heuristic: the actual derivation compares vacuum states at past and future infinity (Bogoliubov coefficients) and the emission is not localized at the horizon.

**Where:** GA §28.1 p.289 (pdf 306); GA §28.1 p.290 (pdf 307); GA §28.2 p.295 (pdf 312)

### gravitational redshift near the horizon

*phenomenon · revisited* · also: static-observer redshift factor, (1 - 2M/r)^{-1/2} blueshift

A quantum of energy E_inf at infinity is measured by a static observer at radius r to have energy E_inf (1 - 2M/r)^{-1/2}; near r = 2M + epsilon this factor is about (2M/epsilon)^{1/2}.

**How introduced:** Implicit in Example 28.2: the combination -g^{tt} E-tilde with g^{tt} ~ -2M/epsilon and E-tilde ~ (epsilon/2M)^{1/2} is exactly this factor, which lets the near-horizon energy be carried to infinity.

### radial energy equation for geodesics

For radial Schwarzschild motion, (dr/dtau)^2 = E-tilde^2 - 1 + 2M/r for massive particles, and for photons 1/b^2 = (1/L^2)(dr/dlambda)^2 + (1/r^2)(1 - 2M/r), which for zero angular momentum gives E-tilde = +-dr/dlambda.

**How introduced:** Recalled in margin notes 5 and 9 to fix E-tilde for a frame at rest at 2M + epsilon and to compute the proper time to fall to the horizon.

### Hawking temperature

*physical-quantity · core* · also: black-hole temperature

Temperature of the thermal spectrum emitted by a Schwarzschild hole, k_B T = hbar/(8 pi M) in G = c = 1 units, i.e. T = hbar c^3/(8 pi G M k_B), about 62 nK for one solar mass; it rises as the mass falls.

**How introduced:** Quoted as the rigorous result after the hbar/4M estimate, then re-expressed through surface gravity (k_B T = hbar kappa/2 pi) and derived in Exercise 28.4 from Euclidean periodicity.

**Prerequisites:** Hawking radiation, black-body radiation

$$
k_BT=\frac{\hbar}{8\pi M}
$$

$$
k_BT=\frac{\hbar\kappa}{2\pi}
$$

$$
T=\frac{\hbar c^3}{8\pi GMk_B}
$$

**Notes:** The book writes approximately equal in eqn 28.11, but for Schwarzschild the relation is exact.

**Where:** GA §28.2 p.292 (pdf 309); GA §28.2 p.293 (pdf 310); GA p.296 (pdf 313)

### Stefan-Boltzmann law

*law · mention* · also: black-body luminosity law

A black body of area A at temperature T radiates power proportional to A T^4.

**How introduced:** Used in Example 28.3 with A = 16 pi M^2 to find how quickly a hole loses mass.

**Notes:** Real Hawking emission is modified by greybody factors and the number of particle species; the book ignores both.

### black-hole evaporation

*phenomenon · developed* · also: evaporation of black holes, mass loss by Hawking radiation

An isolated black hole loses mass through Hawking emission at a rate proportional to 1/M^2, growing hotter as it shrinks, so its total lifetime scales as M^3.

**How introduced:** Example 28.3 combines T proportional to 1/M with the Stefan-Boltzmann law; the end state is discussed with Fig. 28.2 and Example 28.5.

**Prerequisites:** Hawking temperature, Stefan-Boltzmann law

\frac{dM}{dt}\propto-\frac{1}{M^2}

**Where:** GA §28.2 p.292 (pdf 309); GA §28.2 p.294 (pdf 311)

### negative heat capacity of black holes

*physical-quantity · introduced* · also: black-hole heat capacity

Because T is proportional to 1/M, adding energy cools a Schwarzschild hole and removing energy heats it, so C = dM/dT = -hbar/(8 pi k_B T^2) (G = c = 1) is negative, which makes evaporation a runaway and prevents stable equilibrium with an infinite bath.

**How introduced:** Only in Exercise 28.2, where the reader must find C and explain the minus sign.

**Prerequisites:** Hawking temperature, Bekenstein-Hawking entropy

$$
C=T\frac{dS}{dT}=\frac{dM}{dT}=-\frac{\hbar}{8\pi k_BT^2}

**Notes:** Printed eqn 28.23 gives -hbar^2/(16 pi k_B T^2); direct differentiation gives -hbar/(8 pi k_B T^2) = -2S (see gaps).

**Where:** GA p.296 (pdf 313)

A black hole colder than the 2.73 K CMB absorbs more radiation than it emits and so grows; only holes lighter than about 4.5 x 10^22 kg are currently hotter than the CMB.

**How introduced:** Margin note 12 attached to the evaporation estimate, using the solar-mass temperature and the mass at which T equals T_CMB.

**Where:** GA §28.2 p.292 (pdf 309)

### surface gravity

*physical-quantity · core* · also: kappa

For a Killing horizon generated by a Killing vector xi normalized at infinity, kappa is defined by xi^mu nabla_mu xi^nu = kappa xi^nu on the horizon; physically it is the force per unit mass that must be applied from infinity to hold a test body at the horizon (the redshifted acceleration). For Schwarzschild kappa = 1/4M.

**How introduced:** Presented as the geometric counterpart of temperature, defined by eqn 28.13, explained in margin note 13 and computed in Example 28.4 and Exercise 28.3.

**Prerequisites:** Killing horizon, covariant derivative, Killing vector

$$
\xi^\mu\xi^\nu{}_{;\mu}=\kappa\xi^\nu
$$

$$
\kappa=\frac{1}{4M}
$$

$$
-\xi^\mu\xi_\mu{}^{;\sigma}=\kappa\xi^\sigma
$$

**Notes:** kappa depends on the normalization of xi, which is why the Killing vector is fixed to have unit norm at infinity; the local proper acceleration of a hovering observer diverges at the horizon.

**Where:** GA §28.2 p.292 (pdf 309); GA §28.2 p.293 (pdf 310); GA p.296 (pdf 313)

### Bekenstein-Hawking entropy

*physical-quantity · core* · also: black-hole entropy, horizon-area entropy

Entropy of a black hole proportional to its horizon area, S = k_B A/(4 hbar) in G = c = 1 units, i.e. S = k_B c^3 A/(4 G hbar) = k_B A/(4 l_P^2).

S=\frac{Ak_B}{4\hbar}

### first law of thermodynamics

*law · mention* · also: dU = T dS - p dV

Change in internal energy equals heat absorbed T dS plus work done on the system (dU = T dS - p dV).

**How introduced:** Used as the template for identifying black-hole entropy and listed in margin note 16 with the other thermodynamic laws.

### laws of black-hole mechanics

*law · developed* · also: four laws of black-hole thermodynamics

Four statements for stationary black holes that mirror thermodynamics: kappa is uniform on the horizon (zeroth), dM = kappa dA/8 pi + Omega dL + Phi dq (first), horizon area does not decrease classically (second), and kappa cannot be reduced to zero in finitely many steps (third).

**How introduced:** Listed after the entropy identification, with the ordinary thermodynamic laws in margin note 16 and a dictionary (M-U, kappa-T, A-S, work terms) in margin note 17.

**Prerequisites:** surface gravity, Bekenstein-Hawking entropy, Hawking temperature

**Where:** GA §28.2 p.293 (pdf 310); GA §28.2 p.294 (pdf 311)

### zeroth law of black-hole mechanics

*law · introduced*

The surface gravity of a stationary black hole takes the same value everywhere on its horizon, analogous to uniform temperature in equilibrium.

**How introduced:** First entry in the list of four laws.

**Prerequisites:** surface gravity

**Where:** GA §28.2 p.293 (pdf 310)

### first law of black-hole mechanics

*law · introduced*

Neighbouring stationary black holes differing by dM, dL, dq satisfy dM = (kappa/8 pi) dA + Omega dL + Phi dq, where Omega is the horizon angular velocity and Phi the horizon electric potential.

**How introduced:** Second entry in the list; generalizes the Schwarzschild entropy calculation to rotating and charged holes (developed in Chapter 29).

**Prerequisites:** surface gravity, horizon area of Schwarzschild black hole

$$
dM=\frac{\kappa}{8\pi}dA+\Omega\,dL+\Phi\,dq
$$

**Where:** GA §28.2 p.293 (pdf 310)

### Hawking area theorem

*theorem · introduced* · also: second law of black-hole mechanics, area increase law

Classically (with matter obeying the null energy condition) the total area of black-hole horizons never decreases, so merging holes produce a final area at least equal to the sum of the initial areas.

**How introduced:** Third entry in the list, applied to colliding holes and to matter falling in; then shown to fail for evaporation.

**Prerequisites:** horizon area of Schwarzschild black hole, event horizon

$$
dA\ge 0
$$

**Notes:** The book does not state the energy-condition hypothesis, which is exactly what Hawking radiation violates.

**Where:** GA §28.2 p.293 (pdf 310); GA §28.2 p.294 (pdf 311)

### third law of black-hole mechanics

*law · mention*

No finite sequence of operations can reduce a black hole's surface gravity to zero (reach an extremal hole), analogous to the unattainability of absolute zero.

**How introduced:** Fourth entry in the list.

**Prerequisites:** surface gravity

**Where:** GA §28.2 p.293 (pdf 310)

### generalized second law

*law · introduced* · also: GSL

The sum of black-hole entropy (k_B A/4 hbar) and the ordinary entropy outside the hole never decreases.

**How introduced:** Introduced because Hawking evaporation shrinks the horizon area, violating dA >= 0; the radiation's entropy is argued to compensate once thermalized with the environment.

**Prerequisites:** Bekenstein-Hawking entropy, Hawking area theorem, black-hole evaporation

$$
\frac{d}{dt}\left(S_{\rm BH}+S_{\rm outside}\right)\ge 0
$$

**Where:** GA §28.2 p.294 (pdf 311)

### black-hole information problem

*phenomenon · introduced* · also: information loss paradox, information paradox

If a black hole evaporates completely, the quantum information carried by what fell in appears to be destroyed, which would break the unitary (reversible) evolution of quantum mechanics.

**How introduced:** Raised after the GSL as an open worry; the book lists remnants and a final explosion as proposed escapes and records Hawking's view that information is lost.

**Prerequisites:** black-hole evaporation, unitarity of quantum evolution, event horizon

**Notes:** Treatment is brief and pre-dates later developments (complementarity, firewalls, Page curve and island calculations).

**Where:** GA §28.2 p.294 (pdf 311)

### unitarity of quantum evolution

*principle · mention* · also: unitary time evolution

Quantum states evolve by unitary operators, so a later state determines the earlier one and vice versa; information is never destroyed.

**How introduced:** Invoked to explain why information loss would be troubling.

**Where:** GA §28.2 p.294 (pdf 311)

### Wick rotation and Euclidean periodicity

*technique · introduced* · also: Euclidean method for black-hole temperature, imaginary-time periodicity

Continuing t = -i t_E turns the near-horizon metric into flat 2D polar form d rho^2 + rho^2 d psi^2 with psi = t_E/4M; smoothness at rho = 0 requires psi to have period 2 pi, so t_E has period 8 pi M, and in thermal field theory an imaginary-time period hbar/k_B T means k_B T = hbar/8 pi M.

**How introduced:** Exercise 28.4 with a short italic explanation and a pointer to Zee.

$$

$$
t_E\sim t_E+8\pi M\ \Rightarrow\ k_BT=\hbar/8\pi M
$$

**Notes:** The book never names the conical-defect argument; it just asks the reader to interpret psi as an angle.

**Where:** GA p.296 (pdf 313)

Conserved along the geodesic; for the escaping member it is what the distant observer measures.

## Worked examples

### Example 28.1 · standard · GA §28.1 p.290 (pdf 307)

**Problem:** Show that one member of a virtual photon pair created just outside r = 2M can propagate freely once it is inside the horizon.

**Method:** Adopt an interior observer on a radial plunge with u^t = 0, normalize to get u^r, take a radially infalling photon with E-tilde = +-p^r, and compute E_obs = -g_rr p^r u^r.

**Key insight:** Inside the horizon the positivity condition constrains only p^r, never p^t, so the uncertainty-imposed lifetime is lifted and the outside partner is released.

**Result:** E_obs = -(2M/r - 1)^{-1/2} p^r, positive for p^r < 0.

**Concepts:** positivity of locally measured energy, radial plunge with zero Killing energy inside the horizon, horizon pair-splitting picture

### Example 28.2 · standard · GA §28.1 p.291 (pdf 308)

**Problem:** Estimate the energy delivered to infinity by the escaping photon of a pair created near the horizon.

**Method:** Use a freely falling frame momentarily at rest at 2M + epsilon; find E-tilde from the radial energy equation, the proper time to fall to r = 2M, and the uncertainty-limited energy; connect to the conserved p_t via E_obs = -g^{tt} E_inf E-tilde with g^{tt} ~ -2M/epsilon.

**Key insight:** The near-horizon blueshift (2M/epsilon)^{1/2} exactly compensates the epsilon dependence of the lifetime, so the result depends only on M.

**Result:** E_inf = hbar/4M (about 2 pi times the exact k_B T).

**Concepts:** energy-time uncertainty relation, gravitational redshift near the horizon, Killing energy, radial energy equation for geodesics

### Example 28.3 · intro · GA §28.2 p.292 (pdf 309)

**Problem:** Find how the mass and lifetime of an isolated hole scale when it radiates as a black body.

**Method:** Combine power proportional to A T^4, horizon area 16 pi M^2 and T proportional to 1/M; equate power to -dM/dt and integrate.

**Key insight:** Smaller holes are hotter and radiate faster, so evaporation accelerates; lifetime grows as the cube of the initial mass.

**Result:** dM/dt proportional to -M^-2; tau ~ M^3.

**Concepts:** black-hole evaporation, Stefan-Boltzmann law, Hawking temperature

### Example 28.4 · challenging · GA §28.2 p.293 (pdf 310)

**Problem:** Compute the surface gravity of a Schwarzschild black hole and relate it to its temperature.

**Method:** Work in ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates where xi = partial_V is regular at r = 2M, lower its index to get xi_nu = (-1 + 2M/r, 1, 0, 0), evaluate the definition xi^mu xi^nu_{;mu} = kappa xi^nu including the connection term, and set r = 2M; details in Exercise 28.3.

**Key insight:** Temperature is geometric: k_B T is hbar/2 pi times kappa, the redshifted acceleration needed to hover at the horizon.

**Result:** kappa = 1/4M and k_B T = hbar kappa/2 pi.

**Concepts:** surface gravity, Killing horizon, ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates, Hawking temperature

### Example 28.5 · intro · GA §28.2 p.294 (pdf 311)

**Problem:** Discuss whether evaporation can run to completion and what spacetime would then look like.

**Method:** Qualitative: note that GR should fail when the hole becomes very small, then provisionally assume it vanishes and present the conformal diagram.

**Key insight:** The endpoint is beyond classical GR; if the hole disappears, the horizon and singularity are finite in extent and r = 0 becomes an ordinary centre again.

**Result:** Fig. 28.2.

**Concepts:** Penrose diagram of an evaporating black hole, quantum gravity at the end of evaporation, black-hole information problem

### Example 28.6 · standard · GA §28.2 p.295 (pdf 312)

**Problem:** Explain the Unruh effect by transplanting the Hawking pair argument to accelerated observers in flat spacetime.

**Method:** Write the near-horizon metric in Rindler form, identify accelerated world lines with constant x (hyperbolae in X > |T|), treat T = +-X as a horizon, split a pair across T = X, and estimate the temperature by dimensional analysis.

**Key insight:** Any horizon, even one that exists only because of an observer's acceleration, can split vacuum pairs and produce thermal radiation.

**Result:** k_B T ~ hbar a/c (exact: hbar a/2 pi c).

**Concepts:** Unruh effect, Rindler horizon, near-horizon Rindler approximation, horizon pair-splitting picture

### Example · intro · GA §28.2 p.292 (pdf 309)

**Problem:** Margin note 12: which black holes can actually be shrinking today, given that the CMB at 2.73 K heats them?

**Method:** Evaluate the Hawking temperature for one solar mass and solve T(M) = T_CMB for the critical mass.

**Key insight:** Stellar and supermassive holes are nanokelvin-cold and must keep growing; only holes below roughly 10^-8 solar masses (an asteroid's mass) are hot enough to lose mass now.

**Result:** T(M_sun) ~ 62 nK; M_CMB ~ 2.3 x 10^-8 M_sun ~ 4.5 x 10^22 kg (printed as 10^23 kg).

**Concepts:** black holes in the cosmic microwave background, Hawking temperature

### Example · intro · GA §28.2 p.293 (pdf 310)

**Problem:** Identify the entropy of a Schwarzschild hole from its temperature.

**Method:** Treat M as internal energy, differentiate A = 16 pi M^2 to get dM = dA/32 pi M, factor out the Hawking temperature, and compare with dU = T dS (no work).

**Key insight:** Area is the variable conjugate to temperature, so entropy scales with area, not volume.

**Result:** S = k_B A/(4 hbar).

**Concepts:** Bekenstein-Hawking entropy, first law of thermodynamics, Hawking temperature


## Analogies and intuitions

### A black hole as a thermodynamic black body → Hawking temperature · strong · GA §28.2 p.292 (pdf 309)

The opening and section 28.2 insist the emission spectrum is exactly that of an ideal thermal radiator, which licenses using Stefan-Boltzmann and thermodynamic laws.

**Where it breaks down:** Greybody factors make the spectrum only approximately Planckian far from the hole; heat capacity is negative unlike ordinary bodies.

**App idea:** Overlay a Planck curve at k_B T = hbar/8 pi M with the hole's spectrum as the learner changes M, showing the peak wavelength compared with the horizon size.

### Borrowing energy from the vacuum on a short-term loan → virtual particles · useful · GA p.289 (pdf 306)

Pair creation is described as borrowing Delta E that must be repaid within a time set by hbar; the horizon lets the loan go unpaid.

**Where it breaks down:** Heuristic; in field theory no energy is literally borrowed and the Hawking flux is not a local process at the horizon.

**App idea:** Loan meter: a timer bar for each virtual pair drains; if the partner crosses the horizon before the bar empties, the debt is written off and a photon leaves.

### Dictionary between black-hole mechanics and thermodynamics (M as U, kappa as T, A as S, Omega dL and Phi dq as work) → laws of black-hole mechanics · strong · GA §28.2 p.294 (pdf 311)

Four laws are listed in parallel with the ordinary laws and an explicit variable-by-variable table.

**Where it breaks down:** kappa and A correspond to T and S only up to the constant factors hbar/2 pi k_B and k_B/4 hbar; the printed sign of the work terms is inconsistent; the classical area law fails quantum mechanically.

**App idea:** Two-column card flip: tapping a thermodynamic law reveals its black-hole twin with the conversion factors and a physical example.

### Hovering near a black hole is like accelerating in empty space → Unruh effect · strong · GA §28.2 p.295 (pdf 312)

Near-horizon Schwarzschild is written as Rindler space, so the Hawking argument is rerun across the acceleration horizon.

**Where it breaks down:** Unruh radiation is observer-dependent and not a flux reaching inertial detectors; Hawking radiation reaches distant inertial observers. Global structure differs.

**App idea:** Split screen: static observer at height rho above a horizon versus accelerated rocket in flat space with matching acceleration; both thermometers read the same local temperature.

### Cooking a steak by accelerating it → Unruh temperature · useful · GA §28.2 p.295 (pdf 312)

Unruh's quip in margin note 20 conveys that acceleration equals temperature, while the huge acceleration needed shows how tiny the effect is.

**Where it breaks down:** Humorous; the printed acceleration has wrong units (should be cm s^-2) and is about an order of magnitude low for 300 C.

**App idea:** Acceleration dial with everyday benchmarks (car, fighter jet, electron in a laser field) and the temperature each would give.

### Euclidean near-horizon geometry as a flat plane in polar coordinates with time as the angle → Wick rotation and Euclidean periodicity · strong · GA p.296 (pdf 313)

Exercise 28.4 turns the near-horizon metric into d rho^2 + rho^2 d psi^2 so that the need for a smooth centre forces a period on imaginary time, hence a temperature.

**Where it breaks down:** Requires accepting that thermal field theory lives on periodic imaginary time; the Lorentzian meaning of the construction is indirect.

**App idea:** 3D paper cone: learner chooses the period of psi; unless it is 2 pi the surface shows a cone tip at rho = 0, and the corresponding temperature readout snaps to hbar/8 pi M only when the tip disappears.

### Inside the horizon, the time Killing direction behaves like a space direction → negative Killing energy inside the horizon · useful · GA §28.1 p.290 (pdf 307)

Since xi is spacelike inside, -p.xi is like a momentum component that can have either sign, so negative values are no more alarming than negative momentum.

**Where it breaks down:** Only the Killing energy flips sign; locally measured energies remain positive.

**App idea:** Light-cone tilt animation showing xi as an arrow that passes from inside the cone (outside the hole) to outside the cone (inside the hole).


## Misconceptions addressed

### Black holes can only absorb; nothing ever comes out. · GA p.289 (pdf 306)

**Correction:** Quantum effects make every black hole radiate thermally, although the radiation is far too weak to observe for astrophysical holes.

**Why tempting:** Classical GR and popular descriptions define black holes by the fact that light cannot escape.

### Antiparticles carry negative energy. · GA §28.1 p.290 (pdf 307)

**Correction:** All observed particles and antiparticles have positive energy; the negative root of the dispersion relation is reinterpreted as a positive-energy antiparticle with reversed momentum.

**Why tempting:** Dirac-sea stories and the plus-or-minus square root in E^2 = p^2 + m^2.

### Hawking radiation requires particles with negative energy as measured locally. · GA §28.1 p.291 (pdf 308)

**Correction:** Only the Killing energy -p.xi of the infalling member is negative, which is allowed because xi is spacelike inside the horizon; local observers always measure positive energy.

**Why tempting:** The hole loses mass, so something with negative energy must fall in, and energy is usually thought of as a single observer-independent number.

### Every black hole in the universe is currently shrinking by Hawking radiation. · GA §28.2 p.292 (pdf 309)

**Correction:** Holes colder than the 2.73 K CMB absorb more than they emit; any hole above about 10^-8 solar masses is still growing, even ignoring accretion of gas.

**Why tempting:** Evaporation is presented for an isolated hole, and the environment is easy to forget.

### A more massive black hole should be hotter, the way more massive main-sequence stars are hotter. · GA §28.2 p.292 (pdf 309)

**Correction:** Hawking temperature is inversely proportional to mass; large holes are extraordinarily cold and tiny ones are hot.

**Why tempting:** Intuition from ordinary lab bodies, which warm up when energy is added, and from the stellar mass-temperature trend; the negative heat capacity of self-gravitating systems is unfamiliar.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### Shrinking horizon area during evaporation means the second law of thermodynamics is violated. · GA §28.2 p.294 (pdf 311)

**Correction:** The area theorem assumes classical matter; the generalized second law counts the radiation's entropy, which at least compensates the loss of horizon entropy.

**Why tempting:** The chapter first presents dA >= 0 as the second law of black-hole mechanics.

### Hawking radiation has been detected. · GA §28.1 p.289 (pdf 306)

**Correction:** The book states it has not been measured; for astrophysical holes it is swamped by the CMB and by accretion.

**Why tempting:** It is widely discussed as established physics and supported by analogue experiments.

### The heuristic estimate hbar/4M is the actual temperature or photon energy. · GA §28.2 p.292 (pdf 309)

**Correction:** It sets the scale only; the exact result is k_B T = hbar/8 pi M, smaller by 2 pi, and the spectrum is thermal with mean photon energy of a few k_B T.

**Why tempting:** The derivation looks clean and produces a definite number.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### An accelerated detector and an inertial one agree on whether particles are present. · GA §28.2 p.295 (pdf 312)

**Correction:** In the Unruh effect the accelerated observer sees a thermal bath in the same state that the inertial observer calls empty vacuum; particle number depends on the observer.

**Why tempting:** In classical physics the presence of a particle is observer-independent.

### Surface gravity is the acceleration an astronaut hovering at the horizon would feel. · GA §28.2 p.292 (pdf 309)

**Correction:** The locally felt acceleration diverges at the horizon; kappa is that acceleration multiplied by the redshift factor, i.e. the force per unit mass applied from infinity.

**Why tempting:** The name suggests the Newtonian surface gravity GM/R^2 (which happens to give 1/4M at R = 2M).

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### Hawking particles are emitted from the singularity or escape from inside the horizon. · GA §28.1 p.289 (pdf 306)

**Correction:** In the heuristic picture the escaping quantum is created outside the horizon; nothing crosses r = 2M outward.

**Why tempting:** Radiation from a black hole sounds like something leaking out of it.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### Plunging interior observer checks an infalling photon · GA §28.1 p.290 (pdf 307)

## Applications and observations

- **Hawking radiation not yet detected** (observation, GA §28.1 p.289 (pdf 306)): The book notes that no measurement of Hawking radiation exists.

## Notation and conventions

- **Units:** G = c = 1, with hbar and k_B written explicitly (e.g. k_B T = hbar/8 pi M, S = A k_B/4 hbar); c is restored in the Unruh temperature hbar a/(2 pi c k_B). — Mixed practice; learners should restore constants when quoting numbers. (GA §28.2 p.292 (pdf 309))
- **Metric signature:** (-,+,+,+): p.p = -m^2, u.u = -1, g_tt = -(1 - 2M/r). (GA p.289 (pdf 306))
- **E-tilde and L-tilde:** Tilde marks conserved energy and angular momentum per unit rest mass; E without tilde is total energy -xi.p. — Margin note 6 spells out the distinction. (GA §28.1 p.290 (pdf 307))
- **E_obs and E_obs^infinity:** E_obs = -p.u is energy measured by a local observer; superscript infinity denotes energy measured by the static observer at infinity, -p.xi. (GA §28.1 p.290 (pdf 307))
- **Killing vector xi:** Bold xi = e_t with components (1,0,0,0) in Schwarzschild coordinates and also (1,0,0,0) as partial_V in ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates (V, r, theta, phi). (GA §28.2 p.293 (pdf 310))
- **Derivative notation:** Semicolon for covariant derivative, comma for partial; X_{alpha;mu} = X_{alpha,mu} - Gamma^sigma_{alpha mu} X_sigma. (GA p.296 (pdf 313))
- **Black-hole variables:** M mass, L angular momentum (not J), q charge, A horizon area, kappa surface gravity, Omega horizon angular velocity, Phi horizon electric potential. — Many references use J and Q. (GA §28.2 p.293 (pdf 310))
- **Rindler versus Minkowski coordinates:** Lower-case (t, x) are Rindler coordinates with ds^2 = -x^2 dt^2 + dx^2; upper-case (T, X) are Minkowski with ds^2 = -dT^2 + dX^2. — Acceleration parameter set to 1 in the Rindler form. (GA §28.2 p.295 (pdf 312))
- **Work sign convention:** Margin note 17 states dW = -p dV and maps Omega dL and Phi dq to -dW. — Internally inconsistent with dU = T dS - p dV; the work terms should correspond to +dW (work done on the hole). (GA §28.2 p.294 (pdf 311))
- **Euclidean time:** t = -i t_E, with t_E = 4M psi in Exercise 28.4. — The italic text writes i t_E = hbar/k_B T; what is meant is that the period of t_E equals hbar/k_B T. (GA p.296 (pdf 313))

### Estimate the Hawking energy by combining the uncertainty-limited energy of a pair near 2M + epsilon with the redshift to infinity, and watch epsilon cancel. · GA §28.1 p.291 (pdf 308)

**Why it works:** Resolves the negative-energy puzzle geometrically and reinforces what conserved quantities do and do not mean.

**App idea:** Plot g_tt(r) crossing zero at 2M with light cones overlaid; drag a momentum vector and see -p.u and -p.xi update with signs.

**Why it works:** A two-line calculation turns an analogy into a formula and makes 'entropy proportional to area' concrete.

**App idea:** Step-through derivation card where learner fills the missing factor at each line with instant feedback.

**Why it works:** Pure proportional reasoning from a black-body law gives evaporation dynamics and highlights negative heat capacity.

**App idea:** Evaporation simulator plotting M(t) and T(t) with log axes, an optional CMB bath whose temperature falls with cosmic time, and markers for primordial-mass holes.

## Gaps and pitfalls

- **The opening paragraph writes the pair lifetime as Delta t ~ Delta E/hbar, which is dimensionally wrong; the correct hbar/Delta E appears later.** — Confusion about whether larger borrowed energy means longer or shorter lifetimes. *Suggestion:* State Delta t ~ hbar/Delta E and emphasize that energetic fluctuations are briefer.
- **The pair-splitting argument (Examples 28.1-28.2) is heuristic: it treats a single virtual photon classically, claims p^t is unconstrained, calls p^t a flat-spacetime component, and does not explain the thermal spectrum. The real derivation uses mode mixing between past and future vacua.** — Learners may think Hawking radiation is derived here or that emission is localized at the horizon. *Suggestion:* Label the argument as a plausibility estimate, show that its output is the redshift factor at work, and give a qualitative account of the Bogoliubov picture for advanced learners.
- **Eqn 28.11 is written with an approximately-equal sign and the text equates the typical photon energy with k_B T; the relation is exact and the mean photon energy is about 2.7 k_B T.** — Uncertainty about what is exact and misreading of the spectrum. *Suggestion:* Write k_B T = hbar c^3/8 pi G M exactly and show a Planck curve.
- **Margin note 12 cites eqn 28.12 for the Hawking temperature (it should be 28.11) and gives M_CMB = 4.5 x 10^23 kg, which is ten times the value corresponding to 2.3 x 10^-8 solar masses.** — Learners reproducing the numbers get mismatched answers. *Suggestion:* Use M_CMB ~ 4.5 x 10^22 kg and reference eqn 28.11.
- **Printed eqn 28.23 for the heat capacity, -hbar^2/(16 pi k_B T^2), does not follow from S = k_B A/4 hbar and k_B T = hbar/8 pi M, which give C = -hbar/(8 pi k_B T^2) = -2S.** — Students cannot reach the stated answer and may doubt a correct calculation. *Suggestion:* Provide the corrected result, and discuss the sign physically.
- **Margin note 17 claims dW = -p dV yet maps Omega dL and Phi dq to -dW, which conflicts with dU = T dS - p dV; also kappa and A correspond to T and S only up to constants.** — Sign confusion when extending the first law to rotating and charged holes. *Suggestion:* Write dM = T dS + Omega dJ + Phi dQ with T = hbar kappa/2 pi k_B and S = k_B A/4 hbar, and treat the last two terms as work done on the hole.
- **The area theorem is stated without its energy-condition hypothesis ('as a function of cosmic time' is vague), so its violation by evaporation looks arbitrary.** — Learners may think the area law is broken by fiat. *Suggestion:* Explain that Hawking radiation's negative energy flux into the hole violates the null energy condition the theorem assumes, which is why the generalized second law is needed.
- **Surface gravity's normalization dependence and its meaning as a redshifted acceleration are left to a margin note; the phrase 'suitably chosen linear combination of Killing vectors' is only relevant for rotating holes.** — Learners may equate kappa with the local acceleration at the horizon, which is infinite. *Suggestion:* Show that local proper acceleration times the redshift factor tends to 1/4M as r approaches 2M.
- **Unruh margin note gives acceleration in cm s^-1 (should be cm s^-2) and the number is roughly an order of magnitude below what T_U = hbar a/2 pi c k_B gives for 300 C.** — Unit errors undermine numerical intuition. *Suggestion:* Quote a ~ 10^23 m s^-2 for a few hundred kelvin and compare with a = g giving 4 x 10^-20 K.
- **Exercise 28.4 has a missing square in d Omega^2 and writes i t_E = hbar/k_B T instead of stating that the period of t_E equals hbar/k_B T; the no-conical-singularity reasoning is left implicit.** — Readers without QFT background do not know why psi must have period 2 pi. *Suggestion:* Show a cone versus a flat disc and state the smoothness condition explicitly.
- **Bekenstein (area-entropy, 1973) and Bardeen, Carter and Hawking (four laws, 1973) are not credited, and there is no mention of greybody factors, particle species, or the statistical origin of entropy.** — Incomplete historical and physical picture. *Suggestion:* Add a short timeline and a note that entropy counting is a test for quantum gravity candidates.
- **Information-loss discussion is dated: no mention of complementarity, firewalls, the Page curve or recent island calculations, and Hawking's later views are not noted.** — Learners may think the debate stalled with remnants and explosions. *Suggestion:* Add a brief modern update framed as ongoing research, clearly marked beyond the book.
- **Fig. 28.1 draws horizons as vertical timelike lines with a timelike singularity between them, unlike the causal structure of Schwarzschild.** — May reinforce a picture in which one could hover inside the horizon or the singularity is a place in space. *Suggestion:* Redraw on Eddington-Finkelstein or Penrose diagrams with light cones.
- **The chapter summary says the pair is created 'at' the event horizon, while section 28.1 and Example 28.2 place it just outside (at 2M + epsilon); the Unruh example likewise creates it on T = X.** — Learners may picture particles materializing on the horizon itself, where no static observer exists, and lose the role of the small but finite epsilon in the estimate. *Suggestion:* Say 'straddling' or 'just outside' the horizon and point out that the final answer is independent of how close.
