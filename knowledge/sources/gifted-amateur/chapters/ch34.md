# GA ch34 · Geometry of the connection

> Rebuilds the connection and covariant derivative from the coordinate-free machinery of Part V: axioms for nabla, torsion and the loop-closure picture, the connection coefficients as the components of nabla acting on basis vectors, the non-tensorial transformation of Gamma, the +Gamma/-Gamma rule for arbitrary tensors, and finally metric compatibility (nabla g = 0) as the link that makes parallel transport preserve lengths and single out affinely parametrized geodesics.

**Pages:** printed 351–362 · pdf 368–379 · **Difficulty:** math 4/5, conceptual 4/5, novice-friendliness 2/5

Dense index-free manipulation mixed with components, several printed slips (wrong free index in 34.30 and 34.31, t for u in 34.56, a dropped metric-derivative term in 34.58), inconsistent slot ordering and non-standard vocabulary (affine versus non-affine connection, d acting on vectors). The physical payoff (nabla g = 0 preserves lengths; torsion-free picture) is clear, but the route relies on comfort with Chapters 31-33. The authors themselves mark it skippable.

## Role in the book

Part V revisits, with forms and slot machines, the geometry that Part II used on trust. Chapter 33 supplied the exterior and Lie derivatives; this chapter adds the third, physically essential derivative and proves results quoted earlier (eqns 7.11, 7.34, 7.40 and 12.37). It closes the loop between the abstract manifold and the metric field of GR by showing that the connection must be tied to g, and it prepares the ground for Riemann curvature revisited (Chapter 35), Cartan's connection 1-forms (Chapter 36), the Palatini remark in Chapter 40 and the forms identity used for the Bianchi identity in Chapter 43. The authors flag it as skippable on a first reading.

## Learning objectives

- Reader can state the three defining rules of a connection (linear in the direction slot, linear in the operand, Leibniz on tensor products) and explain why linearity in the direction makes nabla_u depend only on u at a point.
- Reader can define the torsion tensor tau(u,v) and explain why torsion-free connections have Gamma symmetric in its lower indices in a coordinate basis.
- Reader can relate the Lie bracket to covariant derivatives for a torsion-free connection and interpret the result as the gap in a transported quadrilateral.
- Reader can expand nabla v = dv^mu (x) e_mu + v^mu nabla e_mu and identify the connection coefficients as the components of nabla acting on basis vectors, Gamma^sigma_{beta mu} = <omega^sigma, nabla_beta e_mu>.
- Reader can derive the inhomogeneous transformation law of the connection coefficients and point to the second-derivative term that stops Gamma being a tensor.
- Reader can derive the minus sign in the covariant derivative of a 1-form by differentiating the duality relation <omega^nu, e_mu> = delta^nu_mu.
- Reader can write the covariant derivative of any (m,n) tensor using one +Gamma term per upper index and one -Gamma term per lower index.
- Reader can show that for a torsion-free connection the antisymmetrized covariant derivative of a 1-form equals the exterior derivative, with all Gammas cancelling.
- Reader can state the mathematical definition of spacetime as a manifold with a Lorentz metric obeying the Einstein equation, and place connection and curvature in the hierarchy built on the metric.
- Reader can distinguish geodesics with nabla_u u proportional to u from affinely parametrized ones with nabla_u u = 0, and show that metric compatibility plus fixed normalization forces the latter.
- Reader can prove that parallel transport preserves all inner products if and only if nabla g = 0.
- Reader can raise and lower indices inside covariant derivatives correctly, without treating Gamma as a tensor.

## Teaching approach

A formal, derivation-led spiral revisit. The authors motivate a third derivative by the shortcomings of d and the Lie derivative, lay down axioms in a margin box, then keep translating between three languages (index-free nabla_u, slot-machine objects, and components with semicolons). Almost every result is packaged as a short numbered Example that ends by pointing back to an earlier chapter where the formula was used without proof. Pictures are sparse but strategic: a failing quadrilateral for torsion, a four-level stack for the logical structure of GR, and tick marks along curves for affine versus non-affine parametrization. The chapter ends by welding the connection to the metric.

**Style:** spiral-revisit, derivation-heavy, index-free-abstract, example-driven, conversational-informal

**Narrative arc**

1. Motivating problem: d only acts on forms and the Lie derivative needs a neighbourhood of the flow field, so neither is the derivative physics needs; parallelism must be added.
2. Axioms of the connection (linearity in direction and operand, Leibniz rule) plus the notion of torsion.
3. Picture: torsion-free transport makes the u-v quadrilateral close up to the Lie bracket, linking nabla to the Lie derivative of Chapter 33.
4. Formalism: nabla acting on scalars equals d; on vectors it produces a vector-valued 1-form whose basis-vector part defines Gamma.
5. Components: extract Gamma with dual basis 1-forms, build nabla v and nabla_u v, and interpret numbers from slot filling.
6. Check against Part II: derive the non-tensorial transformation law of Gamma (proof of eqn 7.11).
7. Extension: explain why d and nabla part company on p-forms, derive the -Gamma rule for 1-forms from the duality relation, relate dA to antisymmetrized semicolon derivatives.
8. Generalization: the +Gamma per upper index, -Gamma per lower index recipe for arbitrary tensors (proof of eqns 7.34, 7.40, 12.37).
9. Payoff: the metric as foundation (M, g), affine versus non-affine parallelism, and nabla g = 0 as the condition that preserves lengths and inner products.
10. Practical coda: raising and lowering indices through semicolon derivatives.

**Signature moves**

- Rules of the game placed in a shaded margin box before the main text so the body can proceed to pictures immediately.
- Grey signpost boxes next to Example 34.6, Example 34.7 and eqn 34.45 name the Part II equations (7.11, 7.34/7.40, 12.37) that were used on trust and are now proved.
- Reading valency (1,0) as 'vector-valued 0-form' so that d can be stretched to act on vectors once a connection exists.
- Obtaining the 1-form rule by differentiating the constant contraction <omega^nu, e_mu> = delta^nu_mu, so the minus sign is forced rather than memorized.
- Counting basis elements to build the general tensor rule: each basis vector contributes +Gamma, each basis 1-form -Gamma.
- Tick-mark cartoon on a patch of manifold to distinguish evenly and unevenly parametrized geodesics.
- Stating a one-sentence mathematical definition of general relativity as the destination of the geometry part.

## Section by section

### Chapter opening: why a third derivative — p.351 (pdf 368)

Recalls the two derivatives of Part V so far and why neither suits gravitation; introduces parallelism, the connection nabla as a non-tensorial operator that agrees with d on scalars, and the directional covariant derivative nabla_u = u.nabla. A margin box on the next page lists the linearity and Leibniz rules; margin notes credit Koszul and Nomizu and say the chapter may be skipped on first reading.

**Concepts:** affine connection, covariant derivative, parallel transport, defining properties of a connection, exterior derivative, Lie derivative

**Key moves**
- Shortcomings of d (forms only) and the Lie derivative (not pointwise in the flow field)
- Parallelism as the extra structure that connects bases at different points
- nabla agrees with d on scalars and on (n,0) tensors but not on forms
- Axioms 34.1-34.3 in the margin

### §34.1 Covariant derivative in pictures — p.352 (pdf 369)

Defines torsion as the part of nabla_u v - nabla_v u not accounted for by the Lie bracket, notes that GR assumes zero torsion (so Gamma is symmetric in a coordinate basis) while alternative theories use it, and then shows via the transported quadrilateral of Fig. 34.1 that the closing vector is the difference of covariant derivatives, giving L_u v in semicolon form. For commuting fields the covariant derivative becomes symmetric.

**Concepts:** torsion tensor, torsion-free connection, Lie bracket as loop-closure failure, Lie derivative in terms of covariant derivatives, symmetry of the covariant derivative for commuting fields, Riemann curvature tensor, torsion-based theories of gravity

**Key moves**
- tau(u,v) = nabla_u v - nabla_v u - [u,v]
- tau = 0 gives Gamma^mu_{alpha beta} = Gamma^mu_{beta alpha} in coordinates
- Lie derivative components rewritten using semicolon derivatives (eqn 34.6)
- Changes of v along u and of u along v are the edges' mismatches; their difference is the bracket
- [u,v] = 0 implies nabla_u v = nabla_v u

### §34.2 Connection and exterior derivative — p.353 (pdf 370)

Gives meaning to nabla with no direction attached. On a function it is df; on a vector v = v^mu e_mu it yields a vector-valued 1-form whose second piece depends on how the basis vectors change, and that change defines Gamma. Examples extract Gamma as a component, assemble nabla v and its semicolon components, interpret nabla v as a two-slot gradient, give nabla_u v and the number <sigma, nabla_u v>, and derive the inhomogeneous transformation law for Gamma in general frames and in coordinates.

**Concepts:** covariant derivative of a scalar, gradient of a vector field, vector-valued 1-form, connection coefficients as components of the connection, component extraction with dual basis 1-forms, directional covariant derivative, non-tensorial transformation of connection coefficients, linear slot machine picture of tensors

**Key moves**
- nabla f = df and u.nabla f = u^mu partial_mu f = u[f]
- Product rule on v^mu e_mu isolates nabla e_mu
- Definition nabla e_mu = Gamma^alpha_{nu mu} omega^nu (x) e_alpha
- <omega^sigma, nabla_beta e_mu> = Gamma^sigma_{beta mu}
- nabla v = v^mu_{;beta} omega^beta (x) e_mu
- Leibniz rule applied to Lambda^gamma_{gamma'} e_gamma produces the extra e_beta'[Lambda] term
- Coordinate frame version shows the second-derivative term

### §34.3 Covariant derivative of tensors — p.355 (pdf 372)

Extends d = nabla to all (n,0) tensors but explains why the identification fails on p-forms: d builds antisymmetric wedge products, while nabla of a tensor is an ordinary tensor product with one extra lower slot. The 1-form rule follows from differentiating the duality contraction, giving -Gamma. A worked example shows that the components of dA are antisymmetrized semicolon derivatives because the torsion-free Gammas cancel. Counting basis elements then yields +Gamma for every upper index and -Gamma for every lower index, boxed as the general recipe.

**Concepts:** exterior versus covariant derivative on forms, covariant derivative of basis 1-forms, covariant derivative of a 1-form, exterior derivative of a 1-form from covariant derivatives, covariant derivative of tensors, antisymmetrization bracket notation, comma and semicolon notation

**Key moves**
- dS = nabla S only for (n,0) tensors
- 0 = nabla<omega^nu, e_mu> gives nabla omega^nu(e_mu, ) = -Gamma^nu_{beta mu} omega^beta
- 1-form rule carries a minus-Gamma term (eqn 34.35)
- Filling one slot of dA and comparing with A_{gamma;beta} - A_{beta;gamma}
- (dA)_{beta gamma} = -2 A_{[beta;gamma]}
- (2,0) rule by adding a Gamma for the second basis vector; (0,2) rule by subtracting for each basis 1-form
- General boxed recipe 34.47

### §34.4 The metric revisited — p.358 (pdf 375)

Places the metric at the base of the structure (manifold, then metric, then connection and curvature, then GR) and states a compact definition of GR. Contrasts a weak notion of parallelism in which the tangent to a geodesic stays proportional to itself (non-affine) with the strict one in which nabla_u u = 0 and the tangent keeps its length (affine). Metric compatibility nabla g = 0 is introduced as what ties the connection to the metric; Example 34.9 shows that preserving inner products under parallel transport is equivalent to g_{beta gamma;alpha} = 0, and that with u.u fixed it forces affine geodesics. Example 34.10 raises and lowers indices through semicolon derivatives.

**Concepts:** spacetime as a Lorentzian manifold, hierarchy of geometric structure, metric tensor, line element tensor, non-affine parametrization of a geodesic, affine parameter, geodesic equation (coordinate-free), metric compatibility of the connection, preservation of inner products under parallel transport, Levi-Civita connection, raising and lowering indices inside covariant derivatives, Palatini formulation

**Key moves**
- GR stated as (M, g) with Lorentz g related to matter by the Einstein equation
- nabla_u u proportional to u versus nabla_u u = 0
- Physical requirement u.u = -1 selects constant-length tangents
- nabla g = 0 as the link between connection and metric
- Leibniz expansion of u^alpha (g_{beta gamma} v^beta w^gamma)_{;alpha} with the transport conditions
- g_{beta gamma;alpha} = 0 plus zero torsion fixes nabla
- Lowering an index through a semicolon derivative (eqn 34.59)

Three bullet points: nabla gives a derivative adequate for tensor fields in curved spacetime, its components are the connection coefficients, and nabla g = 0 guarantees the book's affine connection.

## Concepts

### affine connection

*mathematical-object · core* · also: connection, connection operator, nabla, linear connection

An operator nabla that assigns to tensor fields a covariant derivative: it is linear over functions in the direction it is pointed, additive in its operand and obeys the Leibniz rule; it is not itself a tensor and is equivalent to a choice of how basis vectors at neighbouring points are related (parallelism).

**How introduced:** Motivated as the missing structure that lets vectors at different points be compared; defined abstractly with symbol nabla, described as an upgrade of d, and characterized through its axioms and its action on basis vectors.

**Prerequisites:** tangent vector, exterior derivative, parallel transport

$$
\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}=\boldsymbol u\cdot\nabla
$$

$$
\nabla\boldsymbol e_\mu=\Gamma^\alpha{}_{\nu\mu}\,\boldsymbol\omega^\nu\otimes\boldsymbol e_\alpha
$$

**Notes:** The book uses 'affine connection' in a non-standard, narrower sense tied to affine parametrization and to nabla g = 0; in standard usage any linear connection on the tangent bundle is affine.

**Where:** GA p.351 (pdf 368); GA p.352 (pdf 369); GA §34.4 p.360 (pdf 377)

### covariant derivative

*operation · core* · also: nabla_u, directional covariant derivative

The derivative of a tensor field along a vector u that compares the field at a displaced point with its parallel-transported value, producing a tensor of the same type; in components it adds connection-coefficient terms to partial derivatives.

**How introduced:** Introduced in the opening as the derivative that compares a vector with its transported counterpart, then written as nabla_u = u.nabla and computed in components through the chapter.

**Prerequisites:** affine connection, parallel transport

$$
(\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}\boldsymbol v)^\mu=u^\alpha\left(\partial_\alpha v^\mu+\Gamma^\mu{}_{\alpha\lambda}v^\lambda\right)
$$

**Where:** GA p.351 (pdf 368); GA §34.2 p.355 (pdf 372)

### defining properties of a connection

*definition · developed* · also: axioms of the connection, linearity and Leibniz rules

A connection must satisfy nabla_{au+bv} w = a nabla_u w + b nabla_v w (with a, b allowed to be functions), nabla_u(alpha c + beta d) = alpha nabla_u c + beta nabla_u d for constants, and nabla(S (x) T) = nabla S (x) T + S (x) nabla T.

**How introduced:** Listed in a shaded margin box; linearity in the direction slot is justified as what makes the operator use information at one point only.

**Prerequisites:** tensor product

$$
\nabla_{a\boldsymbol u+b\boldsymbol v}\boldsymbol w=a\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}\boldsymbol w+b\nabla_{\boldsymbol v}\boldsymbol w
$$

$$
\nabla(\boldsymbol S\otimes\boldsymbol T)=\nabla\boldsymbol S\otimes\boldsymbol T+\boldsymbol S\otimes\nabla\boldsymbol T
$$

**Notes:** The book does not state explicitly that a and b may be functions, which is the property that makes nabla_u pointwise in u.

**Where:** GA p.352 (pdf 369)

### parallel transport

*operation · revisited* · also: parallelism

Moving a vector (or tensor) along a curve so that its covariant derivative along the tangent vanishes; this is how vectors at different points are compared.

**How introduced:** Named in the opening as the extra structure to add to manifolds, used implicitly in the quadrilateral picture, and made precise in §34.4 via nabla_u v = 0 and the length-preservation question.

**Prerequisites:** covariant derivative, tangent vector

$$
\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}\boldsymbol v=0,\quad u^\alpha v^\beta{}_{;\alpha}=0
$$

**Where:** GA p.351 (pdf 368); GA §34.4 p.359 (pdf 376); GA §34.4 p.360 (pdf 377)

### torsion tensor

*mathematical-object · introduced* · also: torsion, tau

The (1,2) tensor tau(u,v) = nabla_u v - nabla_v u - [u,v], measuring how much the covariant derivatives of two fields fail to reproduce their Lie bracket; in coordinates its components are Gamma^mu_{alpha beta} - Gamma^mu_{beta alpha}.

**How introduced:** Introduced at the start of §34.1 as the geometric quantity GR sidelines in favour of curvature; a margin note pairs it with the Riemann tensor as the two independent invariants of a connection.

**Prerequisites:** affine connection, Lie bracket of vector fields

$$
\boldsymbol\tau(\boldsymbol u,\boldsymbol v)=\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}\boldsymbol v-\nabla_{\boldsymbol v}\boldsymbol u-[\boldsymbol u,\boldsymbol v]
$$

**Where:** GA §34.1 p.352 (pdf 369)

### torsion-free connection

*definition · developed* · also: symmetric connection, symmetry of connection coefficients

A connection with tau = 0, equivalently nabla_u v - nabla_v u = [u,v] for all fields, equivalently Gamma^mu_{alpha beta} = Gamma^mu_{beta alpha} in any coordinate basis.

**How introduced:** Stated as the assumption made in conventional GR immediately after defining torsion; the coordinate symmetry of Gamma appears in margin note 4.

**Prerequisites:** torsion tensor, coordinate basis

$$
\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}\boldsymbol v-\nabla_{\boldsymbol v}\boldsymbol u=[\boldsymbol u,\boldsymbol v]
$$

$$
\Gamma^\mu{}_{\alpha\beta}=\Gamma^\mu{}_{\beta\alpha}
$$

**Notes:** In a non-coordinate basis the Gammas need not be symmetric even when torsion vanishes, because the basis vectors have non-zero brackets.

**Where:** GA §34.1 p.352 (pdf 369); GA §34.4 p.360 (pdf 377)

### Lie bracket as loop-closure failure

*identity · revisited* · also: commutator of vector fields, failure of the quadrilateral to close

Following u then v versus v then u from a point generally ends at different places; to leading order the gap is the commutator [u,v]. With a torsion-free connection that gap equals the difference of the two covariant derivatives.

**How introduced:** Recalled from Chapter 33 and redrawn in Fig. 34.1 with the edge mismatches labelled by covariant derivatives.

**Prerequisites:** Lie derivative, integral curve

$$
[\boldsymbol u,\boldsymbol v]=\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}\boldsymbol v-\nabla_{\boldsymbol v}\boldsymbol u
$$

**Where:** GA §34.1 p.352 (pdf 369)

### Lie derivative in terms of covariant derivatives

*identity · developed* · also: comma-to-semicolon rule for the Lie derivative

For a torsion-free connection the Lie derivative of a vector field can be written with covariant derivatives in place of partial derivatives because the Gamma terms cancel in the antisymmetric combination (see the formula field).

**How introduced:** Example 34.1, obtained directly from the vanishing torsion condition.

**Prerequisites:** torsion-free connection, Lie derivative

$$
(\mathcal L_{\boldsymbol u}\boldsymbol v)^\alpha=v^\alpha{}_{;\beta}u^\beta-u^\alpha{}_{;\beta}v^\beta
$$

**Where:** GA §34.1 p.352 (pdf 369)

### Lie derivative

*operation · mention* · also: Lie dragging derivative

The derivative of a tensor field along the flow of a vector field u, defined by dragging with the flow; it needs no connection but depends on derivatives of u, not only on u at a point.

**How introduced:** Recalled in the opening as one of two derivatives already available and judged unsuitable as the derivative of physics; reconnected to nabla in Example 34.1.

**Prerequisites:** integral curve

$$
\mathcal L_{\boldsymbol u}\boldsymbol v=[\boldsymbol u,\boldsymbol v]
$$

**Where:** GA p.351 (pdf 368)

### symmetry of the covariant derivative for commuting fields

*identity · introduced* · also: symmetry of the covariant derivative

If two vector fields commute, [u,v] = 0 (for example two coordinate basis fields), a torsion-free connection gives nabla_u v = nabla_v u.

**How introduced:** Read off from the closed quadrilateral: when the bracket vanishes the figure closes and the two covariant derivatives agree.

**Prerequisites:** torsion-free connection, Lie bracket as loop-closure failure

$$
\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}\boldsymbol v=\nabla_{\boldsymbol v}\boldsymbol u\quad([\boldsymbol u,\boldsymbol v]=0)
$$

**Where:** GA §34.1 p.353 (pdf 370)

### exterior derivative

*operation · revisited* · also: d

The operator d taking p-forms to (p+1)-forms by antisymmetrized differentiation; on functions it gives the gradient 1-form df with <df,u> = u[f].

**How introduced:** Recalled from Chapter 33 as the first derivative of Part V and used as the template for nabla; the chapter stretches the symbol d to act on vectors when a connection exists.

**Prerequisites:** p-form, wedge product

$$
\boldsymbol d\tilde{\boldsymbol A}=\partial_\alpha A_\mu\,\boldsymbol dx^\alpha\wedge\boldsymbol dx^\mu
$$

**Where:** GA p.351 (pdf 368); GA §34.2 p.353 (pdf 370); GA §34.3 p.357 (pdf 374)

### covariant derivative of a scalar

*identity · developed* · also: nabla f = df

On a scalar function the connection adds nothing: nabla f = df, and in the direction u it is the ordinary directional derivative u^mu partial_mu f.

**How introduced:** Opening statement of §34.2, with a margin note listing equivalent notations <df,u> and u[f].

**Prerequisites:** exterior derivative, tangent vector

$$
\nabla f=\boldsymbol df
$$

$$
\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}f=u^\mu\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^\mu}=\langle\boldsymbol df,\boldsymbol u\rangle=\boldsymbol u[f]
$$

**Where:** GA §34.2 p.353 (pdf 370)

### gradient of a vector field

*mathematical-object · developed* · also: nabla v, dv

The (1,1) object nabla v = v^mu_{;beta} omega^beta (x) e_mu obtained by applying the connection to a vector field without choosing a direction; inserting a direction u gives the vector nabla_u v, and further inserting a 1-form gives a number.

**How introduced:** Built in Examples 34.4-34.5 by the product rule on v^mu e_mu, then described as a two-slot machine.

**Prerequisites:** affine connection, linear slot machine picture of tensors, connection coefficients as components of the connection

$$
\nabla\boldsymbol v=\left(\partial_\beta v^\mu+v^\alpha\Gamma^\mu{}_{\beta\alpha}\right)\boldsymbol\omega^\beta\otimes\boldsymbol e_\mu
$$

$$
\nabla\boldsymbol v(\boldsymbol u,\tilde{\boldsymbol\sigma})=\langle\tilde{\boldsymbol\sigma},\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}\boldsymbol v\rangle
$$

**Where:** GA §34.2 p.353 (pdf 370); GA §34.2 p.354 (pdf 371); GA §34.2 p.355 (pdf 372)

### vector-valued 1-form

*mathematical-object · developed* · also: tensor-valued form

An object that eats a vector (like a 1-form) and returns a vector rather than a number; nabla v and nabla e_mu are examples, and their values depend on the connection.

**How introduced:** Named when nabla v is expanded; a margin note contrasts it with a general (1,1) tensor, stressing that its construction needs the connection.

**Prerequisites:** gradient of a vector field

$$
\boldsymbol d\boldsymbol v=\boldsymbol dv^\mu\otimes\boldsymbol e_\mu+v^\mu\boldsymbol d\boldsymbol e_\mu
$$

**Notes:** The valency reading of a (1,0) vector as a 'vector-valued 0-form' is the book's device for extending d to vectors.

**Where:** GA §34.2 p.353 (pdf 370); GA §34.2 p.354 (pdf 371)

### connection coefficients as components of the connection

*definition · core* · also: Christoffel symbols, Gamma, connection coefficients

Numbers Gamma^alpha_{nu mu} defined by expanding the covariant derivative of each basis vector in the basis: nabla e_mu = Gamma^alpha_{nu mu} omega^nu (x) e_alpha, so Gamma^sigma_{beta mu} = <omega^sigma, nabla_{e_beta} e_mu>; the first lower index is the direction, the second labels the differentiated basis vector.

**How introduced:** Defined geometrically in §34.2 as the content of d e_mu (how basis vectors change), then matched to the component notion of eqn 7.6 in Example 34.3 and to nabla e_gamma in Example 34.5.

**Prerequisites:** affine connection, component extraction with dual basis 1-forms

$$
\langle\boldsymbol\omega^\sigma,\nabla_\beta\boldsymbol e_\mu\rangle=\Gamma^\sigma{}_{\beta\mu}
$$

$$
(\nabla\boldsymbol e_\gamma)^\mu{}_\alpha=\Gamma^\mu{}_{\alpha\gamma}
$$

**Where:** GA §34.2 p.353 (pdf 370); GA §34.2 p.354 (pdf 371); GA §34.2 p.355 (pdf 372); GA §34.4 p.360 (pdf 377)

### component extraction with dual basis 1-forms

*technique · developed* · also: contracting with omega^sigma

Any component of a tensor-valued expression is obtained by feeding basis vectors and dual basis 1-forms into its slots, using omega^nu(e_beta) = delta^nu_beta.

**How introduced:** Used repeatedly in Examples 34.3, 34.5 and 34.8 to turn index-free expressions into Gammas and semicolon components.

**Prerequisites:** dual basis, linear slot machine picture of tensors

$$
\boldsymbol\omega^\nu(\boldsymbol e_\beta)=\delta^\nu{}_\beta
$$

**Where:** GA §34.2 p.354 (pdf 371); GA §34.3 p.357 (pdf 374)

### directional covariant derivative

*operation · developed* · also: nabla_u v as a vector

Filling the direction slot of nabla v with u gives the vector nabla_u v whose components are u^alpha v^mu_{;alpha}; pairing with a 1-form gives the count of how many sheets of the 1-form that vector pierces.

**How introduced:** Stated after Example 34.5 as the payoff of the gradient construction.

**Prerequisites:** gradient of a vector field

$$
(\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}\boldsymbol v)^\mu=u^\alpha\left(\partial_\alpha v^\mu+v^\lambda\Gamma^\mu{}_{\alpha\lambda}\right)
$$

**Where:** GA §34.2 p.355 (pdf 372)

### linear slot machine picture of tensors

*convention · revisited* · also: slot machine

### non-tensorial transformation of connection coefficients

*theorem · developed* · also: transformation law of Gamma, Gamma is not a tensor

Under a change of frame the connection coefficients pick up, besides the homogeneous tensor term, an inhomogeneous term built from derivatives of the transformation matrix; in coordinates this is a second derivative of the old coordinates with respect to the new ones, so Gamma is not a tensor.

**How introduced:** Example 34.6 derives it index-free using linearity in the direction slot and the Leibniz rule on the transformed basis vectors, giving the proof of eqn 7.11.

**Where:** GA §34.2 p.355 (pdf 372)

### exterior versus covariant derivative on forms

*principle · introduced* · also: why d is not nabla on p-forms

d and nabla coincide on (n,0) tensors, but on a p-form with p >= 1 d produces an antisymmetric (p+1)-form using wedge products, whereas nabla produces a general tensor with one extra lower slot using ordinary tensor products; they are different objects.

**How introduced:** Opens §34.3: after extending dS = nabla S to all (n,0) tensors the authors warn that the identification cannot go further and define nabla S for a general (n,m) tensor.

**Prerequisites:** exterior derivative, wedge product, covariant derivative of tensors

$$
\boldsymbol d\boldsymbol S=\nabla\boldsymbol S\quad(\boldsymbol S\ \text{of type}\ (n,0))
$$

**Where:** GA §34.3 p.355 (pdf 372); GA §34.3 p.356 (pdf 373)

### covariant derivative of basis 1-forms

*identity · developed* · also: nabla omega^nu

Because <omega^nu, e_mu> is constant, the covariant derivative of a basis 1-form carries minus the connection coefficients: nabla omega^nu = -Gamma^nu_{beta mu} omega^beta (x) omega^mu (with the derivative slot first).

**How introduced:** Derived in Example 34.7 by applying the Leibniz rule to the duality contraction.

**Prerequisites:** defining properties of a connection, dual basis, connection coefficients as components of the connection

$$
\nabla\boldsymbol\omega^\nu(\boldsymbol e_\mu,\,)=-\Gamma^\nu{}_{\beta\mu}\boldsymbol\omega^\beta

**Notes:** Printed margin eqn 34.30 repeats omega^nu where omega^mu is meant.

**Where:** GA §34.3 p.356 (pdf 373)

### covariant derivative of a 1-form

*identity · developed* · also: semicolon derivative of covectors

For a 1-form sigma, sigma_{nu;mu} = partial_mu sigma_nu - Gamma^lambda_{mu nu} sigma_lambda; the sign of the Gamma term is opposite to that for vectors.

**How introduced:** Assembled from the basis 1-form result by the product rule on sigma_nu omega^nu, then given in operator, semicolon and directional forms (proof of eqns 7.34 and 7.40).

**Prerequisites:** covariant derivative of basis 1-forms

$$
\sigma_{\nu;\mu}=\sigma_{\nu,\mu}-\sigma_\lambda\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}
$$

$$
(\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}\tilde{\boldsymbol\sigma})_\alpha=u^\mu\left(\partial_\mu\sigma_\alpha-\sigma_\lambda\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\alpha}\right)
$$

**Where:** GA §34.3 p.356 (pdf 373)

### exterior derivative of a 1-form from covariant derivatives

*identity · developed* · also: dA from antisymmetrized semicolon derivatives

For a torsion-free connection, the components of the 2-form dA equal A_{gamma;beta} - A_{beta;gamma}, since the symmetric Gamma terms cancel; index-free, dA(u,v) = (nabla_u A)(v) - (nabla_v A)(u) = u[A(v)] - v[A(u)] - A([u,v]).

**How introduced:** Example 34.8 fills one slot of dA, then the other, and compares with the coordinate semicolon expression; a margin note and Exercise 34.6 give index-free versions used in Chapter 43.

**Prerequisites:** exterior derivative, covariant derivative of a 1-form, torsion-free connection

$$
(\boldsymbol d\tilde{\boldsymbol A})_{\beta\gamma}=A_{\gamma;\beta}-A_{\beta;\gamma}=-2A_{[\beta;\gamma]}
$$

$$
\boldsymbol d\tilde{\boldsymbol A}(\boldsymbol u,\boldsymbol v)=\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}\langle\tilde{\boldsymbol A},\boldsymbol v\rangle-\nabla_{\boldsymbol v}\langle\tilde{\boldsymbol A},\boldsymbol u\rangle-\tilde{\boldsymbol A}([\boldsymbol u,\boldsymbol v])
$$

**Notes:** This is why the Faraday tensor F = dA can be computed with partial derivatives in curved spacetime.

**Where:** GA §34.3 p.357 (pdf 374); GA p.361 (pdf 378)

### antisymmetrization bracket notation

*convention · mention* · also: square brackets on indices

T_{[ab]} = (T_{ab} - T_{ba})/2; vertical bars inside brackets exclude indices from the antisymmetrization.

**How introduced:** Used without fuss in eqn 34.41 and margin eqn 34.43.

A_{[\beta;\gamma]}=\tfrac12\left(A_{\beta;\gamma}-A_{\gamma;\beta}\right)

**Where:** GA §34.3 p.357 (pdf 374)

### covariant derivative of tensors

*identity · core* · also: general +Gamma/-Gamma rule

The covariant derivative of an (m,n) tensor is the partial derivative of its components plus one Gamma term contracted into each upper index and minus one Gamma term contracted into each lower index, with the derivative index always in the first lower slot of Gamma.

**How introduced:** Built by analogy: a (2,0) tensor has two basis vectors so gets two +Gamma terms; a (0,2) tensor has two basis 1-forms so gets two -Gamma terms; then boxed in general (proof of eqn 12.37). A margin note says the reader may skip straight to the box.

**Prerequisites:** covariant derivative of a 1-form, gradient of a vector field, defining properties of a connection

$$
(\nabla_\alpha\boldsymbol T)^{\mu\nu}=\partial_\alpha T^{\mu\nu}+\Gamma^\mu{}_{\alpha\beta}T^{\beta\nu}+\Gamma^\nu{}_{\alpha\beta}T^{\mu\beta}
$$

$$
(\nabla_\alpha\boldsymbol\xi)_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\alpha\xi_{\mu\nu}-\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\alpha\mu}\xi_{\lambda\nu}-\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\alpha\nu}\xi_{\mu\lambda}
$$

**Where:** GA §34.3 p.357 (pdf 374); GA §34.3 p.358 (pdf 375)

### geodesic equation (coordinate-free)

### metric compatibility of the connection

*principle · core* · also: nabla g = 0, compatibility condition

The requirement that the covariant derivative of the metric vanishes, g_{beta gamma;alpha} = 0; equivalently parallel transport preserves all inner products (lengths and angles).

**How introduced:** Introduced as the condition that permanently couples connection to metric, motivated by the physical need for a constant-norm four-velocity, then proved equivalent to inner-product preservation in Example 34.9.

\nabla\boldsymbol g=0

### preservation of inner products under parallel transport

*theorem · developed* · also: length preservation

If v and w are parallel transported along any curve then g(v,w) is constant for all such v, w, u exactly when g_{beta gamma;alpha} = 0.

**How introduced:** Example 34.9 expands u^alpha(g_{beta gamma} v^beta w^gamma)_{;alpha} with the Leibniz rule and kills the transported terms.

### Levi-Civita connection

*theorem · introduced* · also: metric connection, Christoffel connection

The unique connection that is both torsion-free and metric compatible; in a coordinate basis its coefficients are half a signed sum of three first derivatives of the metric (the Christoffel formula).

**How introduced:** Not named; the text asserts that nabla g = 0 together with zero torsion fixes nabla, and Exercise 34.2 derives the Christoffel formula from compatibility.

**Prerequisites:** metric compatibility of the connection, torsion-free connection

\Gamma_{\alpha\beta\sigma}=\tfrac12\left(g_{\alpha\beta,\sigma}+g_{\alpha\sigma,\beta}-g_{\beta\sigma,\alpha}\right)

**Notes:** Named here with its standard label for retrieval; the book never uses the term in this chapter.

**Where:** GA §34.4 p.360 (pdf 377); GA p.361 (pdf 378)

## Key equations

### (34.1) Linearity in the direction · central · GA p.352 (pdf 369)

$$
\nabla_{a\boldsymbol u+b\boldsymbol v}\boldsymbol w=a\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}\boldsymbol w+b\nabla_{\boldsymbol v}\boldsymbol w
$$

The derivative along a combination of directions is the same combination of derivatives; this is what makes nabla local in the direction field.

**Symbols:** a, b scalars (functions); u, v directions; w operand

### (34.2) Linearity in the operand · central · GA p.352 (pdf 369)

$$
\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}(\alpha\boldsymbol c+\beta\boldsymbol d)=\alpha\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}\boldsymbol c+\beta\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}\boldsymbol d
$$

Additivity of the connection in the object being differentiated.

**Symbols:** alpha, beta constants

### (34.3) Leibniz rule · central · GA p.352 (pdf 369)

$$
\nabla(\boldsymbol S\otimes\boldsymbol T)=(\nabla\boldsymbol S\otimes\boldsymbol T)+(\boldsymbol S\otimes\nabla\boldsymbol T)
$$

Product rule for tensor products; together with 34.1-34.2 it defines a connection and later yields the 1-form and tensor rules.

**Symbols:** S, T arbitrary tensors

### Torsion-free symmetry of Gamma · supporting · GA §34.1 p.352 (pdf 369)

$$
\Gamma^\mu{}_{\alpha\beta}=\Gamma^\mu{}_{\beta\alpha}
$$

In a coordinate basis, vanishing torsion means the connection coefficients are symmetric in their lower indices (margin note 4).

### (34.4) Torsion tensor · central · GA §34.1 p.352 (pdf 369)

$$
\boldsymbol\tau(\boldsymbol u,\boldsymbol v)=\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}\boldsymbol v-\nabla_{\boldsymbol v}\boldsymbol u-[\boldsymbol u,\boldsymbol v]
$$

Defines torsion as the mismatch between antisymmetrized covariant derivatives and the Lie bracket.

**Symbols:** tau (1,2) torsion tensor; [u,v] Lie bracket

### (34.5) Torsion-free condition · central · GA §34.1 p.352 (pdf 369)

$$
\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}\boldsymbol v-\nabla_{\boldsymbol v}\boldsymbol u=[\boldsymbol u,\boldsymbol v]
$$

The assumption of GR: antisymmetrized covariant derivatives reproduce the commutator.

### (34.6) Lie derivative via semicolons · supporting · GA §34.1 p.352 (pdf 369)

$$
(\mathcal L_{\boldsymbol u}\boldsymbol v)^\alpha=v^\alpha{}_{;\beta}u^\beta-u^\alpha{}_{;\beta}v^\beta
$$

For zero torsion the Lie derivative can use covariant instead of partial derivatives.

### (34.7) Edge mismatches of the quadrilateral · derivation-step · GA §34.1 p.352 (pdf 369)

$$
\boldsymbol v(\lambda_0+1)-\boldsymbol v(\lambda_0)\approx\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}\boldsymbol v,\qquad \boldsymbol u(\sigma_0+1)-\boldsymbol u(\sigma_0)\approx\nabla_{\boldsymbol v}\boldsymbol u
$$

Differences of each field across the figure, compared after transport, approximate the covariant derivatives; the unit step stands for a small displacement.

**Symbols:** lambda, sigma parameters labelling the field evaluations

### (34.8) Symmetry of the covariant derivative · supporting · GA §34.1 p.353 (pdf 370)

$$
\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}\boldsymbol v=\nabla_{\boldsymbol v}\boldsymbol u\quad\text{when }[\boldsymbol u,\boldsymbol v]=0
$$

For commuting fields and zero torsion, the order of differentiation does not matter.

### (34.9) Connection on a scalar · central · GA §34.2 p.353 (pdf 370)

$$
\nabla f=\boldsymbol df
$$

On functions the connection is the exterior derivative, producing a 1-form.

### (34.10) Directional derivative of a scalar · supporting · GA §34.2 p.353 (pdf 370)

$$
\boldsymbol u\cdot\nabla f=\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}f\equiv u^\mu\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^\mu}\equiv\partial_{\boldsymbol u}f
$$

Pointing nabla f along u gives the familiar directional derivative.

### (34.11) Further notations · supporting · GA §34.2 p.353 (pdf 370)

$$
\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}f\equiv\langle\boldsymbol df,\boldsymbol u\rangle\equiv\boldsymbol u[f]
$$

Equivalent geometric notations (margin note 6).

**Symbols:** u[f] vector acting as derivation

### (34.12) Covariant derivative of a vector as vector-valued 1-form · central · GA §34.2 p.353 (pdf 370)

$$
\nabla\boldsymbol v\equiv\boldsymbol d\boldsymbol v=\boldsymbol dv^\mu\otimes\boldsymbol e_\mu+v^\mu\,\boldsymbol d\boldsymbol e_\mu
$$

Product rule splits the change of a vector into the change of its components and the change of the basis vectors.

**Symbols:** d here is the book's extension of d to vector-valued 0-forms (standard name: exterior covariant derivative)

### (34.13) General (1,1) tensor · supporting · GA §34.2 p.353 (pdf 370)

$$
\boldsymbol S=S^\mu{}_\nu\,\boldsymbol\omega^\nu\otimes\boldsymbol e_\mu
$$

Reference form of a (1,1) tensor contrasted with the vector-valued 1-form (margin note 7).

### (34.14) Definition of connection coefficients · central · GA §34.2 p.353 (pdf 370)

$$
\nabla\boldsymbol e_\mu\equiv\boldsymbol d\boldsymbol e_\mu=\Gamma^\alpha{}_{\nu\mu}\left(\boldsymbol\omega^\nu\otimes\boldsymbol e_\alpha\right)
$$

The whole content of the connection is how each basis vector changes; its expansion coefficients are Gamma.

**Symbols:** Gamma^alpha_{nu mu}: nu = direction, mu = differentiated basis vector, alpha = output component

### (34.15) Extracting Gamma · central · GA §34.2 p.354 (pdf 371)

$$
\langle\boldsymbol\omega^\sigma,\nabla_\beta\boldsymbol e_\mu\rangle=\Gamma^\sigma{}_{\beta\mu}
$$

Components of the derivative of a basis vector along e_beta, obtained by contracting with the dual basis.

### (34.16) · derivation-step · GA §34.2 p.354 (pdf 371)

$$
\nabla\boldsymbol v=\boldsymbol dv^\mu\otimes\boldsymbol e_\mu+v^\alpha\Gamma^\mu{}_{\beta\alpha}(\boldsymbol\omega^\beta\otimes\boldsymbol e_\mu)=\frac{\partial v^\mu}{\partial x^\beta}(\boldsymbol dx^\beta\otimes\boldsymbol e_\mu)+v^\alpha\Gamma^\mu{}_{\beta\alpha}(\boldsymbol\omega^\beta\otimes\boldsymbol e_\mu)
$$

Chain rule plus the definition of Gamma, before collecting terms.

### (34.17) Gradient of a vector · central · GA §34.2 p.354 (pdf 371)

$$
\nabla\boldsymbol v=\left(\frac{\partial v^\mu}{\partial x^\beta}+v^\alpha\Gamma^\mu{}_{\beta\alpha}\right)(\boldsymbol\omega^\beta\otimes\boldsymbol e_\mu)
$$

The components of nabla v are exactly the semicolon derivatives v^mu_{;beta}.

### (34.18) Gradient as a two-slot machine · supporting · GA §34.2 p.354 (pdf 371)

$$
\nabla\boldsymbol v(\,,\,)=v^\mu{}_{;\nu}\,\boldsymbol\omega^\nu(\,)\otimes\boldsymbol e_\mu(\,)
$$

Slot-machine reading: one slot for a direction vector, one for a 1-form.

### (34.19) Components of the gradient · supporting · GA §34.2 p.354 (pdf 371)

$$
\nabla\boldsymbol v(\boldsymbol e_\alpha,\boldsymbol\omega^\mu)=(\nabla\boldsymbol v)^\mu{}_\alpha=\frac{\partial v^\mu}{\partial x^\alpha}+v^\lambda\Gamma^\mu{}_{\alpha\lambda}
$$

Filling both slots with basis elements yields the covariant derivative components.

### (34.20) · supporting · GA §34.2 p.354 (pdf 371)

$$
(\nabla\boldsymbol e_\gamma)^\mu{}_\alpha=\delta^\lambda{}_\gamma\Gamma^\mu{}_{\alpha\lambda}=\Gamma^\mu{}_{\alpha\gamma}
$$

Inserting a basis vector as v shows that the components of nabla are the connection coefficients.

### (34.21) Directional covariant derivative of a vector · central · GA §34.2 p.355 (pdf 372)

$$
\langle\boldsymbol\omega^\mu,\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}\boldsymbol v\rangle=(\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}\boldsymbol v)^\mu=u^\alpha\left(\frac{\partial v^\mu}{\partial x^\alpha}+v^\lambda\Gamma^\mu{}_{\alpha\lambda}\right)
$$

Components of the vector nabla_u v.

### (34.22) · supporting · GA §34.2 p.355 (pdf 372)

$$
\nabla\boldsymbol v(\boldsymbol u,\tilde{\boldsymbol\sigma})=\langle\tilde{\boldsymbol\sigma},\nabla_{\boldsymbol u}\boldsymbol v\rangle
$$

Filling both slots produces a number: how many sheets of sigma the vector nabla_u v crosses.

### (34.25) Frame transformation of Gamma · derivation-step · GA §34.2 p.355 (pdf 372)

$$
\Gamma^{\alpha'}{}_{\beta'\gamma'}=\Lambda^{\alpha'}{}_\alpha\,\boldsymbol e_{\beta'}\!\left[\Lambda^\alpha{}_{\gamma'}\right]+\Lambda^{\alpha'}{}_\alpha\Lambda^\beta{}_{\beta'}\Lambda^\gamma{}_{\gamma'}\Gamma^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma}
$$

General-frame law: a homogeneous tensor term plus an inhomogeneous term from the derivative of the transformation matrix along the new basis vector.

**Symbols:** Lambda transformation matrices; e[f] derivative of f along e

### (34.26) Coordinate transformation of Gamma · central · GA §34.2 p.355 (pdf 372)

$$
\Gamma^{\alpha'}{}_{\beta'\gamma'}=\frac{\partial x^{\alpha'}}{\partial x^\alpha}\frac{\partial^2x^\alpha}{\partial x^{\beta'}\partial x^{\gamma'}}+\frac{\partial x^{\alpha'}}{\partial x^\alpha}\frac{\partial x^\beta}{\partial x^{\beta'}}\frac{\partial x^\gamma}{\partial x^{\gamma'}}\Gamma^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma}
$$

The second-derivative term shows Gamma is not a tensor; this proves eqn 7.11.

### (34.27) · supporting · GA §34.3 p.355 (pdf 372)

$$
\boldsymbol d\boldsymbol S=\nabla\boldsymbol S\quad(\boldsymbol S\text{ an }(n,0)\text{ tensor})
$$

d and nabla agree on tensor-valued 0-forms only.

### (34.28) Covariant derivative of a general tensor · supporting · GA §34.3 p.356 (pdf 373)

$$

## Worked examples

### Example 34.1 · intro · GA §34.1 p.352 (pdf 369)

**Problem:** Express the Lie derivative of a vector field using covariant derivatives when torsion vanishes.

**Method:** Rewrite the torsion-free condition as the bracket and take components with semicolon notation.

**Key insight:** Zero torsion lets partial derivatives in the Lie derivative be upgraded to covariant ones at no cost.

**Result:** (\mathcal L_u v)^\alpha = v^\alpha_{;\beta}u^\beta - u^\alpha_{;\beta}v^\beta

**Concepts:** Lie derivative in terms of covariant derivatives, torsion-free connection

### Example 34.2 · intro · GA §34.1 p.352 (pdf 369)

**Problem:** Interpret nabla_u v - nabla_v u geometrically by building a quadrilateral from two vector fields evaluated at shifted points.

**Method:** Evaluate v at two points separated along u and u at two points separated along v; identify each edge difference with a covariant derivative; the leftover closing vector is their difference, which must be the commutator.

**Key insight:** The covariant derivative inherits the failure-to-close picture of the Lie bracket once torsion is zero.

**Result:** \nabla_u v - \nabla_v u = [u,v]

**Concepts:** Lie bracket as loop-closure failure, torsion-free connection, covariant derivative

### Example 34.3 · intro · GA §34.2 p.354 (pdf 371)

**Problem:** Relate the geometric definition nabla e_mu = Gamma^alpha_{nu mu} omega^nu (x) e_alpha to the component definition of Gamma from Chapter 7.

**Method:** Insert e_beta in the direction slot, use omega^nu(e_beta) = delta^nu_beta, then contract the resulting vector with omega^sigma.

**Key insight:** The first lower index of Gamma is the direction of differentiation; the second labels the basis vector being differentiated.

**Result:** \langle\omega^\sigma, \nabla_\beta e_\mu\rangle = \Gamma^\sigma_{\beta\mu}

**Concepts:** connection coefficients as components of the connection, component extraction with dual basis 1-forms

### Example 34.4 · standard · GA §34.2 p.354 (pdf 371)

**Problem:** Compute nabla v for v = v^mu e_mu without specifying a direction.

**Method:** Product rule on components times basis vectors; d acting on the component functions gives partial derivatives times dx^beta; nabla e_mu supplies Gamma; relabel dummy indices and collect.

**Key insight:** Covariant derivative components are the derivative of the components plus the derivative of the basis, which is why Gamma appears.

**Result:** \nabla v = (\partial_\beta v^\mu + v^\alpha\Gamma^\mu_{\beta\alpha})\,\omega^\beta\otimes e_\mu = v^\mu_{;\beta}\,\omega^\beta\otimes e_\mu

**Concepts:** gradient of a vector field, vector-valued 1-form, comma and semicolon notation

### Example 34.5 · intro · GA §34.2 p.354 (pdf 371)

**Problem:** Summarize what kind of object the gradient nabla v is and extract its components, including the case v = e_gamma.

**Method:** Treat nabla v as a two-slot machine and fill with e_alpha and omega^mu; substitute a basis vector for v.

**Key insight:** The components of nabla itself, acting on basis vectors, are just the connection coefficients.

**Result:** (\nabla e_\gamma)^\mu{}_\alpha = \Gamma^\mu_{\alpha\gamma}

**Concepts:** gradient of a vector field, vector-valued 1-form, directional covariant derivative

### Example 34.6 · challenging · GA §34.2 p.355 (pdf 372)

**Problem:** Derive how connection coefficients transform between frames.

**Method:** Write primed Gamma as <omega^alpha', nabla_{e_beta'} e_gamma'>, expand primed basis elements with Lambda matrices, pull Lambda^beta_beta' out of the direction slot by linearity, then use the Leibniz rule on Lambda^gamma_gamma' e_gamma so that a derivative of Lambda appears; specialize to a coordinate frame.

**Key insight:** Linearity in the direction slot gives tensorial behaviour in that index, but the Leibniz rule in the operand slot generates an inhomogeneous derivative-of-Lambda term; that term is the obstruction to Gamma being a tensor.

**Result:** \Gamma^{\alpha'}_{\beta'\gamma'} = \frac{\partial x^{\alpha'}}{\partial x^\alpha}\frac{\partial^2 x^\alpha}{\partial x^{\beta'}\partial x^{\gamma'}} + \frac{\partial x^{\alpha'}}{\partial x^\alpha}\frac{\partial x^\beta}{\partial x^{\beta'}}\frac{\partial x^\gamma}{\partial x^{\gamma'}}\Gamma^\alpha_{\beta\gamma}

**Concepts:** non-tensorial transformation of connection coefficients, defining properties of a connection

### Example 34.7 · standard · GA §34.3 p.356 (pdf 373)

**Problem:** Find the covariant derivative of the basis 1-forms, and hence of any 1-form.

**Method:** Differentiate the constant contraction <omega^nu, e_mu> = delta^nu_mu with the Leibniz rule (treating the contraction as a tensor product followed by a contraction), substitute nabla e_mu, and solve; then apply the product rule to sigma_nu omega^nu.

**Key insight:** Because the pairing of dual bases never changes, whatever Gamma does to basis vectors must be undone on basis 1-forms, forcing the minus sign.

**Result:** \sigma_{\nu;\mu} = \sigma_{\nu,\mu} - \sigma_\lambda\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}

**Concepts:** covariant derivative of basis 1-forms, covariant derivative of a 1-form

### Example 34.8 · standard · GA §34.3 p.357 (pdf 374)

**Problem:** Express the exterior derivative of a 1-form A in a coordinate frame using covariant derivatives.

**Method:** Write dA with wedge products, fill the first slot with e_beta by expanding the wedge into two tensor products, fill the second slot with e_gamma, and compare with A_{gamma;beta} - A_{beta;gamma} where the symmetric Gammas cancel.

**Key insight:** Antisymmetrization kills the connection terms, so the exterior derivative is secretly an antisymmetrized covariant derivative and needs no connection.

**Result:** (dA)_{\beta\gamma} = A_{\gamma;\beta} - A_{\beta;\gamma} = -2A_{[\beta;\gamma]}

**Concepts:** exterior derivative of a 1-form from covariant derivatives, torsion-free connection, antisymmetrization bracket notation

### Example 34.9 · standard · GA §34.4 p.359 (pdf 376)

**Problem:** Show that metric compatibility is exactly the condition for parallel transport to preserve inner products, and deduce the affine geodesic condition for unit-normalized tangents.

**Method:** Transport v and w along u, demand that the covariant derivative of g(v,w) along u vanish, expand with the Leibniz rule, drop terms using the transport conditions, and use the arbitrariness of u, v, w; then combine nabla_u u proportional to u with g(u,u) = -1.

**Key insight:** Constant inner products for all transported pairs is equivalent to g_{beta gamma;alpha} = 0; given that, a fixed-length tangent cannot have a component of nabla_u u along u, so a geodesic of the weak kind is automatically affinely parametrized.

**Result:** g_{\beta\gamma;\alpha} = 0

**Concepts:** metric compatibility of the connection, preservation of inner products under parallel transport, affine parameter, non-affine parametrization of a geodesic

### Example 34.10 · standard · GA §34.4 p.360 (pdf 377)

**Problem:** Lower the free index of a covariant derivative v^mu_{;alpha} with the metric.

**Method:** First attempt: multiply the component expansion by g_{mu nu} and try to raise an index on Gamma; the authors pause because Gamma is not a tensor and instead use the 1-form rule directly.

**Key insight:** Since nabla g = 0 the metric commutes with the semicolon, so lowering then differentiating equals differentiating then lowering; the price is that the Gamma term changes sign and index positions.

**Result:** g_{\mu\nu}v^\mu_{;\alpha} = v_{\nu;\alpha} = v_{\nu,\alpha} - \Gamma^\gamma_{\alpha\nu}v_\gamma

**Concepts:** raising and lowering indices inside covariant derivatives, metric compatibility of the connection

### Example · intro · GA §34.3 p.357 (pdf 374)

**Problem:** Extend the covariant derivative from vectors and 1-forms to (2,0), (0,2) and general tensors.

**Method:** Write T = T^{mu nu} e_mu (x) e_nu; the product rule differentiates components and each basis vector, each giving a +Gamma; for xi_{mu nu} omega^mu (x) omega^nu each basis 1-form gives -Gamma; generalize by counting.

**Key insight:** The recipe is not a new axiom but a bookkeeping consequence of the Leibniz rule applied to a basis expansion.

**Result:** (\nabla_\alpha T)^{\mu\nu} = \partial_\alpha T^{\mu\nu} + \Gamma^\mu_{\alpha\beta}T^{\beta\nu} + \Gamma^\nu_{\alpha\beta}T^{\mu\beta}

**Concepts:** covariant derivative of tensors, defining properties of a connection


## Analogies and intuitions

### Walking around a block: go along u then v, or v then u, and see whether you end up at the same corner. → torsion-free connection · strong · GA §34.1 p.352 (pdf 369)

Fig. 34.1 redraws the Lie-bracket quadrilateral with edges corrected by covariant derivatives; the unclosed corner is the bracket, so zero torsion means the connection accounts for nothing extra.

**Where it breaks down:** It is a leading-order sketch drawn on a flat page with unit parameter steps. For a step epsilon the gap is epsilon^2 [u,v]. Curvature does not change that gap at order epsilon^2, but at the same order it does rotate a vector carried around the loop, and the picture cannot show that. The book also uses the figure to equate the two expressions, which quietly assumes zero torsion.

**App idea:** Step-size slider showing the gap scaling as epsilon^2 and matching [u,v].

### nabla as an upgraded d: same symbol-level behaviour on functions, but able to act on vectors and tensors once parallelism is supplied. → affine connection · useful · GA p.351 (pdf 368)

Positions the new operator as a natural extension of a tool the reader already has, and explains where the two diverge (on forms).

**Where it breaks down:** Can mislead readers into writing d v for nabla v in standard texts, where d on a vector is undefined without calling it an exterior covariant derivative.

### Tensors as slot machines: nabla v has a slot for a direction and a slot for a 1-form, and pays out a number when both are filled. → gradient of a vector field · useful · GA §34.2 p.354 (pdf 371)

Clarifies what nabla v is before a direction is chosen and why nabla_u v is a vector.

**Where it breaks down:** Slot order conventions differ between texts and even between equations in the chapter.

**App idea:** Drag a vector into one slot and a 1-form into the other; the output number and the intermediate vector nabla_u v appear.

### A 1-form as stacked sheets: the number <sigma, nabla_u v> counts how many sheets the arrow nabla_u v pierces. → directional covariant derivative · useful · GA §34.2 p.355 (pdf 372)

Gives a picture for the fully contracted gradient.

**Where it breaks down:** Only a local, linearized picture; sheets are not globally level sets unless the form is exact.

### Change of a vector = change of its components + change of its basis arrows (like describing a moving arrow in rotating polar axes). → connection coefficients as components of the connection · strong · GA §34.2 p.353 (pdf 370)

The product rule on v^mu e_mu isolates d e_mu as the content of the connection.

**Where it breaks down:** In genuinely curved space there is no global set of fixed arrows to compare with; the basis change is defined only through the connection.

**App idea:** Polar-coordinate plane where a constant Cartesian vector field is shown with components v^r, v^theta changing and basis arrows rotating so that the Gamma terms cancel the component changes.

### Building a pyramid: each layer (manifold, metric, connection and curvature, GR) rests on the one below. → hierarchy of geometric structure · useful · GA §34.4 p.358 (pdf 375)

Fig. 34.2 orients the reader at the start of §34.4.

**Where it breaks down:** Suggests a connection cannot exist without a metric, which is false; the metric picks out one special connection.

### Tick marks along a road: evenly spaced marks versus marks that bunch up describe the same road with different speedometers. → affine parameter · strong · GA §34.4 p.359 (pdf 376)

Fig. 34.3 separates the path from its parametrization, explaining why nabla_u u can be proportional to u on a geodesic.

**Where it breaks down:** The book attaches the label to the connection rather than to the parametrization.

**App idea:** Draggable tick spacing on a geodesic with a live |u| readout.

### Each index of a tensor is like a basis element that turns with the connection: vectors turn one way (+Gamma), 1-forms must turn the opposite way (-Gamma) to keep their mutual pairing fixed. → covariant derivative of tensors · strong · GA §34.3 p.356 (pdf 373)

Justifies the sign rule by the constancy of the duality contraction.

**Where it breaks down:** Gamma is not literally a rotation unless the connection is compatible and the basis orthonormal.


## Misconceptions addressed

### The connection nabla (or its coefficients Gamma) is a tensor. · GA §34.2 p.355 (pdf 372)

**Correction:** nabla is an operator, and Gamma transforms with an extra second-derivative term; only the difference of two connections, or torsion, is tensorial.

**Why tempting:** Gamma carries indices in tensor positions and appears inside tensor equations.

### Since d and nabla agree on scalars and vectors, they can be used interchangeably on every object. · GA §34.3 p.355 (pdf 372)

**Correction:** On p-forms d yields an antisymmetric (p+1)-form, whereas nabla yields a general tensor with an extra slot; they differ, though for torsion-free connections antisymmetrizing nabla reproduces d.

**Why tempting:** The book itself writes dv = nabla v, inviting over-generalization.

### The covariant derivative of a 1-form has the same +Gamma term as that of a vector. · GA §34.3 p.356 (pdf 373)

**Correction:** It carries -Gamma, forced by the constancy of <omega^nu, e_mu>.

**Why tempting:** Memorizing one formula and lowering indices seems enough.

### You can freely raise or lower indices on Gamma like on a tensor to move the metric through a covariant derivative. · GA §34.4 p.360 (pdf 377)

**Correction:** Moving the metric through a semicolon works because nabla g = 0; the resulting formula is the 1-form rule, not an index manipulation on Gamma.

**Why tempting:** Index gymnastics usually commute with everything.

### Parallel transport automatically keeps a vector's length fixed. · GA §34.4 p.359 (pdf 376)

**Correction:** Only a metric-compatible connection guarantees this; a general connection may stretch vectors while keeping them parallel.

**Why tempting:** In flat Euclidean space transported arrows obviously keep their length.

### Zero torsion means nabla_u v = nabla_v u for any two vector fields. · GA §34.1 p.353 (pdf 370)

**Correction:** Zero torsion gives nabla_u v - nabla_v u = [u,v]; the symmetry holds only when the fields commute.

**Why tempting:** Torsion-free is often summarized as 'Gamma is symmetric'.

### A curve with nabla_u u proportional to u is not a geodesic. · GA §34.4 p.359 (pdf 376)

**Correction:** It is the same geodesic path traced with a non-affine parameter; reparametrizing makes nabla_u u = 0.

**Why tempting:** The geodesic equation is usually quoted only in the affine form.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### 'Affine' versus 'non-affine' is a property of the connection. · GA §34.4 p.359 (pdf 376)

**Correction:** In standard usage every linear connection is affine; affinity refers to the parametrization of the curve. The book's wording conflates the two.

**Why tempting:** The chapter explicitly names 'affine connection' and 'non-affine connection' this way.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### A connection cannot exist without a metric. · GA p.351 (pdf 368)

**Correction:** Connections can be defined on any smooth manifold; a metric singles out the unique torsion-free compatible (Levi-Civita) connection used in GR.

**Why tempting:** Margin notes say that parallelism relies on a metric, and Fig. 34.2 stacks nabla above g.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### Metric compatibility forces every constant-speed curve to satisfy nabla_u u = 0. · GA §34.4 p.360 (pdf 377)

**Correction:** It only forces the acceleration to be orthogonal to the velocity; the curve can still accelerate sideways.

**Why tempting:** The chapter shows compatibility plus normalization gives affine geodesics, which is easy to over-read.

### The covariant derivative nabla_u v uses only information at one point about both u and v. · GA p.351 (pdf 368)

**Correction:** It is pointwise in the direction u, but needs v along a curve through the point (a derivative of v).

**Why tempting:** The text says the connection, like an ordinary derivative, uses the properties of space at a single point.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*


## Thought experiments

### Two-field quadrilateral · GA §34.1 p.352 (pdf 369)

**Setup:** Starting at a point, follow field v a small step then u, and separately u then v, using parallel transport to compare the edge vectors.

**Lesson:** The mismatch is the Lie bracket; for zero torsion it equals nabla_u v - nabla_v u, giving the covariant derivative a closure interpretation.

**App idea:** Animated two-path race on a grid with the gap vector highlighted.

### Stretching arrow along a geodesic · GA §34.4 p.359 (pdf 376)

**Setup:** Carry the tangent to a geodesic along itself while allowing the parameter to speed up or slow down.

**Lesson:** The tangent stays parallel but changes length unless the parameter is affine; the physical four-velocity with fixed norm demands the affine choice, and a compatible metric enforces it.

**App idea:** Great-circle bead with adjustable speed profile and a length meter for u.

### Transporting a pair of vectors · GA §34.4 p.359 (pdf 376)

**Setup:** Parallel transport two vectors v and w along the same curve and watch their dot product.

**Lesson:** Their inner product stays constant for every choice exactly when nabla g = 0.

**App idea:** On a sphere, transport two arrows around a latitude loop; show that angle and lengths stay fixed while both rotate relative to the starting pair; then switch to a deliberately non-compatible connection and see the angle drift.


## Applications and observations

- **Conserved quantities on geodesics from ignorable coordinates** (astrophysical-system, GA p.361 (pdf 378)): Exercise 34.5 shows that if the metric does not depend on a coordinate, the matching lowered component of the four-velocity is conserved along geodesics. Own illustration: in Schwarzschild spacetime independence of t and phi gives conserved energy per unit mass u_t and angular momentum per unit mass u_phi, the basis of orbit calculations in Part IV.
- **Divergence in curvilinear coordinates via sqrt|g|** (numerical-estimate, GA p.361 (pdf 378)): Exercise 34.3 derives a divergence formula needing only the metric determinant. Own illustration: in flat spherical polars sqrt|g| = r^2 sin(theta), which reproduces the familiar vector-calculus divergence; the same formula is used for conservation laws in curved spacetime. Key numbers: sqrt|g| = r^2 sin(theta) for flat spherical polars (own example)
- **Electromagnetism in curved spacetime** (data-analysis, GA §34.3 p.357 (pdf 374)): Example 34.8 shows that antisymmetrized covariant derivatives of a 1-form reduce to partial derivatives. Own illustration: the Faraday tensor F = dA can be computed from the four-potential with ordinary partial derivatives in any coordinates; the identity is used again in Chapter 43 for charge conservation.

## Notation and conventions

- **Connection and covariant derivative symbols:** Bold nabla for the connection; nabla_u = u . nabla for the derivative along u; nabla_beta for the derivative along e_beta; D/dlambda for the derivative along a parametrized curve (as in Part II). (GA p.351 (pdf 368))
- **Index order of connection coefficients:** nabla e_mu = Gamma^alpha_{nu mu} omega^nu (x) e_alpha, so Gamma^sigma_{beta mu} = <omega^sigma, nabla_beta e_mu>: first lower index is the derivative direction, second is the differentiated basis vector. — Only immaterial in a coordinate basis with zero torsion; matters in non-coordinate frames (Chapter 36). (GA §34.2 p.353 (pdf 370))
- **Slot order of derivative tensors:** In 34.14, 34.18 and 34.19 the derivative (1-form) slot comes first, e.g. nabla v(u, sigma) = <sigma, nabla_u v>; in 34.28 the extra omega^k is appended last. — Internal inconsistency; state a convention explicitly when teaching. (GA §34.3 p.356 (pdf 373))
- **d applied to vectors:** The book writes dv = nabla v and d e_mu = nabla e_mu, treating a vector as a vector-valued 0-form; this requires a connection. — Standard texts would call this the exterior covariant derivative, often written D. (GA §34.2 p.353 (pdf 370))
- **Derivative notations for scalars:** nabla_u f = u^mu partial f / partial x^mu = partial_u f = <df, u> = u[f]; square brackets denote a vector acting on a function. (GA §34.2 p.353 (pdf 370))
- **Comma and semicolon:** Comma: partial derivative; semicolon: covariant derivative component, e.g. v^mu_{;beta}, sigma_{nu;mu}. (GA §34.3 p.356 (pdf 373))
- **1-forms and basis:** 1-forms carry a tilde (sigma-tilde, A-tilde); basis vectors e_mu and dual basis 1-forms omega^mu, with dx^mu identified with omega^mu in a coordinate basis. (GA §34.2 p.354 (pdf 371))
- **Wedge product and 2-form components:** dx^alpha wedge dx^mu = dx^alpha (x) dx^mu - dx^mu (x) dx^alpha, and a 2-form is written (1/2) F_{beta gamma} dx^beta wedge dx^gamma; antisymmetrization brackets include a factor 1/2 and vertical bars exclude indices. (GA §34.3 p.357 (pdf 374))
- **Metric signature and four-velocity:** Timelike four-velocity normalized to u.u = -1, consistent with a mostly-plus signature; metric written g = g_{mu nu} omega^mu (x) omega^nu or ds^2 = g_{mu nu} dx^mu (x) dx^nu. (GA §34.4 p.359 (pdf 376))
- **Lowered connection coefficients:** Gamma_{nu alpha lambda} = g_{nu mu} Gamma^mu_{alpha lambda}, lowering onto the first (upper) index. — Used in Example 34.10 and Exercise 34.2. (GA §34.4 p.360 (pdf 377))
- **Transformation matrices:** Lambda^{alpha'}_alpha and Lambda^beta_{beta'} relate primed and unprimed frames; in coordinates Lambda^alpha_beta' = partial x^alpha / partial x^beta'. (GA §34.2 p.355 (pdf 372))
- **Torsion symbol:** tau denotes the (1,2) torsion tensor; R the (1,3) Riemann tensor. (GA §34.1 p.352 (pdf 369))

## Gaps and pitfalls

- **The book calls a connection 'affine' or 'non-affine' according to whether geodesics satisfy nabla_u u = 0 or nabla_u u proportional to u, and says the metric guarantees affinity. Standard usage: every linear connection on the tangent bundle is affine; affinity is a property of the curve's parameter, and nabla g = 0 makes the connection metric.** — Learners moving to Carroll, Wald or MTW will find the vocabulary clashing and may think reparametrization changes the connection. *Suggestion:* Teach 'affine parameter' and 'metric (Levi-Civita) connection' as the standard terms and mention the book's wording as a local idiosyncrasy.
- **Margin notes and Fig. 34.2 suggest parallelism requires a metric.** — Hides that connections are independent structure (relevant to Palatini, torsion theories and gauge theory in Chapter 44). *Suggestion:* State that any manifold admits connections; a metric plus zero torsion selects a unique one.
- **Example 34.2 labels v by lambda and u by sigma although v is compared across a step along u; eqn 34.7 uses 'approximately equal' with a unit step; it also switches between [v,u] and [u,v] and effectively assumes the result it proves.** — Sign and labelling confusion; the picture seems to prove torsion vanishes rather than illustrate what zero torsion means. *Suggestion:* Redraw with an explicit small step epsilon, state that the closing gap is epsilon^2 [u,v] and that equating it to epsilon^2 (nabla_u v - nabla_v u) is the torsion-free assumption.
- **Margin eqns 34.30 and 34.31 repeat omega^nu where omega^mu should appear, leaving an index both free and summed.** — Index-checking fails; learners may doubt the derivation. *Suggestion:* Use nabla omega^nu = -Gamma^nu_{beta mu} omega^beta (x) omega^mu (derivative slot first) and d omega^nu = -Gamma^nu_{beta mu} omega^beta wedge omega^mu.
- **Eqn 34.56 prints t^alpha instead of u^alpha.** — Minor confusion over a mysterious new vector. *Suggestion:* Read t as u (the Fig. 34.3 labels also use t for the tangent).
- **In Example 34.10, eqn 34.58 pulls g_{mu nu} inside a partial derivative as if it were constant, silently dropping the -g_{mu nu,alpha} v^mu term. The authors then pause over raising an index on Gamma, which is not actually the problem: contracting Gamma with the metric is harmless algebra.** — Learners may conclude index lowering commutes with partial derivatives, or that Gamma is partially tensorial. *Suggestion:* Show the full calculation: include the metric-derivative term, rewrite it with the compatibility relation 34.61, and watch it combine into -Gamma^gamma_{alpha nu} v_gamma.
- **Slot ordering for nabla S is inconsistent: derivative slot first in 34.14/34.18/34.19 but appended last in 34.28 and the 34.30 margin formula.** — Errors when contracting nabla T with vectors, especially for non-symmetric tensors. *Suggestion:* Fix one convention (for example derivative index last, T^mu_{nu;alpha}) and apply it everywhere in the app.
- **Writing dv = nabla v and calling nabla v a vector-valued 1-form that is 'more than' a (1,1) tensor.** — Once the connection is chosen, nabla v is an ordinary (1,1) tensor field; the extra structure lies in the operation, not in the object's type. *Suggestion:* Say: nabla v is a (1,1) tensor, but you need a connection to build it from v; reserve d for forms.
- **Example 34.9 is announced as showing that nabla g = 0 preserves lengths, but the derivation assumes preserved inner products and derives nabla g = 0.** — The logical direction is blurred. *Suggestion:* Present both directions: the same Leibniz expansion shows equivalence for arbitrary u, v, w.
- **Exercise 34.1 asks to prove additivity in the direction slot 'using symmetry', but additivity is already axiom 34.1.** — Puzzlement about what is being proved. *Suggestion:* Reframe as checking that the torsion-free symmetry is consistent with linearity, or skip.
- **The Levi-Civita connection and the fundamental theorem (unique torsion-free metric connection) are never named; uniqueness is asserted and left to an exercise.** — Learners lack the standard name to connect with other texts. *Suggestion:* Name the theorem and walk through the cyclic-permutation proof explicitly.
- **The opening contrasts the Lie derivative (two points) with nabla (single point) without explaining that nabla_u is pointwise in u but not in the differentiated field.** — Learners may think a covariant derivative needs no neighbourhood information. *Suggestion:* Show that nabla_{fu} v = f nabla_u v while L_{fu} v involves derivatives of f.

## Tutor notes

- Opening question: 'You have two arrows at two different points on a sphere. What would it even mean to say they are the same?' Let the learner discover that a rule for transport is needed before naming the connection.

- Recommended order: axioms -> scalar case (nabla f = df) -> product rule on v^mu e_mu defines Gamma -> semicolon formula -> 1-form sign from the duality pairing -> general tensor rule -> torsion and the quadrilateral -> metric compatibility -> affine parameters.

- Check for understanding: ask the learner to write nabla_alpha of T^mu_nu without looking, then ask why the lower index gets a minus sign.
- Check: 'Is Gamma a tensor? What breaks?' Expect the second-derivative term from eqn 34.26; follow up with 'Give a flat-space coordinate system where Gamma is non-zero' (polar coordinates).
- Check: 'Why can F_{mu nu} = partial_mu A_nu - partial_nu A_mu be used in curved spacetime?' Target answer: Gamma symmetric, cancels in the antisymmetric combination.
- When discussing Fig. 34.3, immediately translate the book's 'affine/non-affine connection' into 'affine/non-affine parameter' to avoid vocabulary clashes with other sources.
