# GA ch29 · Charged and rotating black holes

> Moves beyond Schwarzschild to the two other stationary black holes: after using the no-hair idea to argue that mass, charge and spin are all that matter, it quotes the Reissner-Nordstrom metric and reads its two horizons, three regions, avoidable timelike singularity and endless Penrose tower, then quotes the Kerr metric in Boyer-Lindquist coordinates and probes it with test particles and light to reveal the ring singularity, frame dragging, the stationary limit and ergosphere, and finally the Penrose process that mines a spinning hole for energy and angular momentum.

**Pages:** printed 297–306 · pdf 314–323 · **Difficulty:** math 3/5, conceptual 4/5, novice-friendliness 3/5

Mathematically the chapter mostly quotes metrics and delegates heavy algebra, so the main-text calculations are short (index raising, quadratic for light, dot products with Killing vectors). Conceptually it is dense: two new global structures, timelike and ring singularities, negative r, closed timelike curves, frame dragging, the distinction between stationary limit and horizon, and negative Killing energy all arrive in ten pages, largely relying on familiarity with Penrose diagrams and the Hawking-radiation argument. Several printed slips (missing z in 29.3, Δ² in 29.6, missing square root in 29.9, ρ+ in 29.34, and the summary calling r0 a horizon) can derail a careful novice.

## Role in the book

Final chapter of Part IV. Chapters 21-27 built the Schwarzschild toolkit (Killing-vector constants of motion, effective potentials for particles and photons, horizons, closed trapped surfaces, Kruskal and Penrose diagrams) and Chapter 28 used the sign change of the timelike Killing vector's norm to explain Hawking radiation. This chapter re-deploys every one of those tools on the charged and rotating solutions, so it functions as a capstone survey rather than a new-formalism chapter: the metrics are quoted, not derived, and most algebra is pushed into exercises. It also supplies the astrophysically essential message that real holes spin. Part V then leaves black holes and rebuilds geometry rigorously; Killing vectors, electromagnetic stress-energy and curvature computations that would justify these metrics return in Chapters 33, 36 and 42.

## Learning objectives

- Reader can state the no-hair idea and explain why it means mass, charge and angular momentum fully characterize a stationary black hole seen from outside.
- Reader can write the Reissner-Nordstrom line element, recover Schwarzschild as q -> 0, and find the horizon radii r± = M ± (M² - q²)^{1/2}.
- Reader can classify the regions I, II, III of a two-horizon black hole by whether surfaces of constant r are timelike or spacelike, and say which regions are static.
- Reader can read the maximally extended Reissner-Nordstrom Penrose diagram to explain why its r = 0 singularity is timelike and avoidable and why travel to another asymptotically flat region is one-way.
- Reader can explain what makes a metric stationary and axisymmetric and why such a metric generically carries a g_tφ cross term.
- Reader can write the Kerr metric in Boyer-Lindquist coordinates, identify Δ and ρ², relate a to J and M, and check the a -> 0 and large-r limits.
- Reader can locate the ring singularity (ρ = 0) and the horizons (Δ = 0), and state the extremal bound a ≤ M (J ≤ M²).
- Reader can compute the Kerr horizon area A = 8πMr+ from the induced 2-metric on the horizon.
- Reader can use the conserved u_t and u_φ together with the inverse t-φ metric block to show that a zero-angular-momentum particle acquires dφ/dt = g^{tφ}/g^{tt} (frame dragging).
- Reader can derive the coordinate angular velocities of tangential light rays from the null condition and show that where g_tt = 0 one of them vanishes.
- Reader can prove that no stationary observer can exist where g_tt ≥ 0 and derive the stationary limit r0 = M + (M² - a² cos²θ)^{1/2}, sketching the ergosphere between r+ and r0.
- Reader can explain the Penrose process: why a spacelike Killing vector in the ergosphere permits negative Killing energy, how that lets the escaping fragment carry out more energy than came in, and why the captured fragment must have negative angular momentum.
- Reader can interpret the prograde/retrograde asymmetry in the Kerr equatorial effective potentials for particles and photons.

## Teaching approach

A brisk survey that treats both new metrics as given and learns about them by interrogation rather than derivation. The authors justify the scope with the no-hair slogan, use the charged hole (admitted to be astrophysically implausible) as a gentler rehearsal of the two-horizon causal structure, and then spend most of the chapter on Kerr. The Kerr geometry is explored in the book's familiar Part IV style: find the Killing vectors, write the conserved quantities, drop in test particles and light rays, and read physics off effective potentials and null conditions. Penrose diagrams and cartoons carry the global structure; formal Example boxes carry the short calculations; the heavier algebra (inverse metric, effective potentials, horizon area) is delegated to exercises. The chapter ends with an application, the Penrose process, deliberately built on the same spacelike-Killing-vector argument used for Hawking radiation in the previous chapter, and with a humble margin essay on how hard the Kerr solution is to derive.

**Style:** survey-overview, example-driven, diagram-driven, conversational-informal, thought-experiment, physical-motivation-first

**Narrative arc**

1. Motivating claim: Schwarzschild is only the simplest member of a family; the no-hair slogan says external observers can only ever learn M, q and J, so studying charge and spin covers every stationary hole.
2. Charge first, with a caveat: real holes would neutralize, but allowing q reveals rich structure. Quote the Reissner-Nordstrom metric and check q -> 0.
3. Find the horizons from the zeros of the metric function; divide spacetime into regions I, II, III and say which are static.
4. Read the causal story off the infinite Penrose tower: region II traps, region III has a timelike repulsive singularity, and a traveller can pass one-way into a fresh asymptotic region.
5. Ask what observers would see: infinite redshift at r+ for distant watchers, and a blueshifted fast-forward of region I for someone crossing r-.
6. Pivot to rotation as the realistic case; Example 29.1 shows the general stationary axisymmetric form and flags the dφ dt cross term as the source of new physics.
7. Introduce Boyer-Lindquist coordinates, quote the Kerr metric, and run sanity checks (t, φ reversal symmetry, flat at large r, Schwarzschild at a = 0).
8. Catalogue singular surfaces: ρ = 0 is a physical ring, Δ = 0 gives two horizons, and a ≤ M caps the spin; compute the horizon area (Example 29.2).
9. Show the Kerr Penrose diagram with its negative-r extension, closed timelike curves and wormhole passages.
10. Probe the geometry with test bodies: a particle dropped with zero angular momentum still circulates (frame dragging, Example 29.3); equatorial orbits obey a spin-modified effective potential (Example 29.4).
11. Probe with light: tangential photons satisfy a quadratic null condition (Example 29.5); where g_tt vanishes, counter-rotating light stalls, so no observer can hover, which defines the stationary limit and the ergosphere.
12. Complete the light picture with the photon effective potential depending on the sense of orbit (Example 29.6) and a light-cone map of the regions.
13. Application: the Penrose process. Local momentum conservation plus a spacelike Killing vector inside the ergosphere allows a negative-energy fragment to fall in, so energy is extracted; Example 29.7 shows this also spins the hole down.
14. Close with a summary and a margin essay on the notorious difficulty of deriving and perturbing the Kerr solution.

**Signature moves**

- Scope justified by a theorem slogan: the no-hair metaphor licenses a three-parameter survey instead of an open-ended one.
- Rehearsal on a toy: the unrealistic charged hole is used to teach the two-horizon, three-region causal layout that Kerr then repeats.
- Global structure read from Penrose diagrams (with lines of constant r and t drawn in) instead of from integrated geodesics.
- The Part IV recipe applied to a non-diagonal metric: Killing vectors -> conserved u_t, u_φ -> invert the t-φ block -> read off coordinate velocities.
- Photons as the universal speed limit: if the null condition forbids counter-rotating light, it forbids every massive body, so a property of light rays defines a region of spacetime.
- One-line impossibility proof: a stationary 4-velocity would need g_tt (u^t)² = -1, which fails once g_tt ≥ 0.
- Explicit reuse of the previous chapter's geometric trick (sign flip of ξ·ξ) to power a new phenomenon, the Penrose process, with a footnote that plants the fact early.
- Energy bounds obtained by dotting local conservation laws with Killing vectors and with an observer's 4-velocity built from Killing vectors.
- Heavy algebra quoted in footnotes and pushed to exercises, keeping the main text a narrative of physical consequences.
- Candid admission (margin essay) that the metric is being quoted because its derivation is famously arduous.

## Section by section

### Chapter opening: more general black holes and no hair — p.297 (pdf 314)

Frames Schwarzschild as the simplest case and announces the two generalizations, charge and rotation, assuming collapse has exposed the central region. Because an event horizon hides the interior, outside observers can only access mass, charge and angular momentum; the no-hair slogan and theorem say these fix the exterior geometry independent of how the hole formed, so the analysis applies broadly.

**Concepts:** no-hair theorem, event horizon

**Key moves**
- Horizon hides everything except globally measurable charges (M, q, J).
- Independence from formation history turns a special-case study into a general one.
- Margin notes credit Bekenstein with the phrase and Hawking, Carter and Israel with the theorem.

### §29.1 Charged black holes — p.297 (pdf 314)

Concedes that charged holes would neutralize in astrophysical settings, then studies them anyway. The Reissner-Nordstrom line element adds a q²/r² term reflecting electromagnetic field energy; it reduces to Schwarzschild as q -> 0. For q² > M² only r = 0 is singular; for q² ≤ M² two coordinate singularities r± split spacetime into static region I, non-static region II (full of closed trapped surfaces) and static region III, which contains a timelike, repulsive physical singularity. The maximally extended Penrose diagram is an infinite vertical tower of region-I pairs linked through II and III, so a timelike traveller can dodge the singularity and emerge, irreversibly, in a new asymptotically flat universe. Distant observers see infallers infinitely redshifted at r+, while an observer entering region III could see all of a region I's history, strongly blueshifted.

**Concepts:** Reissner-Nordstrom metric, inner and outer horizons, regions I, II and III of a two-horizon black hole, timelike singularity, repulsive singularity, maximally extended Reissner-Nordstrom Penrose diagram, passage to another asymptotically flat region, naked singularity, extremal black hole, closed trapped surface, blueshift at the inner horizon

**Key moves**
- Justify the q²/r² term as the gravitational effect of electromagnetic energy density on the right-hand side of the field equation.
- Solve 1 - 2M/r + q²/r² = 0 to get r± and note three cases: q² > M², q² = M² (no region II), q² < M².
- Classify regions by the causal character of constant-r and constant-t surfaces.
- Contrast the timelike r = 0 of region III with the spacelike Schwarzschild and Robertson-Walker singularities.
- Trace a timelike curve I -> II -> III -> II' -> I' and argue it cannot return.
- Discuss what infalling and distant observers see (redshift at r+, blueshifted history near r-).

### §29.2 Kerr black holes: metric, coordinates and singular surfaces — p.299 (pdf 316)

Rotation breaks spherical symmetry, so Schwarzschild cannot describe most astrophysical objects; Kerr is introduced as the stationary, axisymmetric, asymptotically flat rotating solution. Example 29.1 gives the general stationary axisymmetric line element with an angular velocity ω producing a dt dφ cross term. Boyer-Lindquist coordinates are defined via a modified spherical-to-Cartesian map, so constant-r surfaces are ellipsoids and r = 0 is not a point. The Kerr metric is quoted with Δ = r² - 2Mr + a² and ρ² = r² + a² cos²θ, component forms, Killing vectors ξ and η, and a rewritten ω-form; a = J/M is the Kerr parameter. Symmetry and limit checks follow. Singular surfaces: ρ = 0 is a physical ring of radius a in the equatorial plane; Δ = 0 gives inner and outer horizons r±, which exist only for a ≤ M, so spin is capped at J = M² (extremal Kerr). Example 29.2 finds the outer horizon area 8πMr+. The Kerr Penrose diagram extends through the ring to negative r; region III contains timelike, avoidable singularities, closed timelike curves and passages to further universes.

**Concepts:** stationary spacetime, axisymmetric spacetime, general stationary axisymmetric metric, off-diagonal metric cross term g_tφ, Kerr metric, Boyer-Lindquist coordinates, Kerr parameter, ring singularity, inner and outer horizons, extremal black hole, Kerr horizon area, Kerr Penrose diagram with negative r, closed timelike curves, Killing vectors and conserved quantities in Kerr, Kerr-Newman metric, Cotton-Darboux theorem

**Key moves**
- Argue from stationarity and axisymmetry that components depend only on r and θ and that a cross term is expected.
- Map Boyer-Lindquist (r, θ, φ) to Cartesian coordinates to show constant-r surfaces are oblate ellipsoids.
- Quote the Kerr metric and verify: invariance under t -> -t with φ -> -φ, Minkowski at large r, Schwarzschild at a = 0.
- Separate ρ = 0 (curvature singularity, a ring x² + y² = a², z = 0) from Δ = 0 (coordinate singularities at r±).
- Use the square root in r± to derive the extremal bound a ≤ M.
- Set dt = dr = 0 at r = r+ and use r+² + a² = 2Mr+ to simplify the horizon 2-metric and integrate its area.
- Explain why the Penrose diagram can continue past r = 0 into negative r, unlike Schwarzschild or Reissner-Nordstrom.

### §29.2 Kerr black holes: probing with particles and light — p.302 (pdf 319)

Uses test bodies to map the Kerr regions. Conserved u_t = -Ẽ and u_φ = L̃ are written with the cross terms. Example 29.3 raises indices with the inverse t-φ block and shows a particle with u_φ = 0 has dφ/dt = g^{φt}/g^{tt} ≠ 0: inertial frame dragging. Example 29.4 gives equatorial u^t, u^φ in terms of Ẽ, L̃ and a Kerr effective potential with a spin-dependent 1/r² term and a (L̃ - aẼ)² term. Example 29.5 solves the null condition for tangential photons; where g_tt = 0 (ρ² = 2Mr), the co-rotating ray moves at -2g_tφ/g_φφ while the counter-rotating one has zero coordinate angular velocity, so nothing can counter-rotate or hover there. A stationary observer would need g_tt(u^t)² = -1, impossible once g_tt ≥ 0. The surface g_tt = 0 is the stationary limit, r0 = M + (M² - a² cos²θ)^{1/2}, lying outside r+ except at the poles; the region between them is the ergosphere, part of region I. Example 29.6 gives the equatorial photon potential, whose dependence on σ = sign(L̃) reflects frame dragging, and a light-cone map shows how cones tip in each region.

**Concepts:** Killing vectors and conserved quantities in Kerr, inverse metric of the t-φ block, frame dragging, Kerr equatorial effective potential for massive particles, coordinate angular velocity of tangential light rays, stationary observer, stationary limit surface, ergosphere, Kerr photon effective potential, light-cone tilting in the Kerr geometry, prograde and retrograde motion

**Key moves**
- Express covariant conserved components through contravariant velocities, then invert to find u^t, u^φ.
- Set u_φ = 0 and take the ratio u^φ/u^t, which survives only because g^{tφ} ≠ 0.
- Solve the quadratic g_tt + 2g_tφ Ω + g_φφ Ω² = 0 for the angular velocity of light.
- Specialize to g_tt = 0 to find one root equal to zero and argue from light-cone causality that all bodies must co-rotate.
- Give the independent normalization argument against stationary observers.
- Solve g_tt = 0 for r to find the stationary limit and compare it with r+ at poles and equator.
- Footnote 11 notes g_tt changes sign across the stationary limit, just as it does at r = 2M in Schwarzschild, seeding Section 29.3.

### §29.3 Interacting with the Kerr geometry — p.304 (pdf 321)

Describes Penrose's energy-extraction scheme: a particle from infinity enters the ergosphere and splits; one piece crosses the horizon and the other escapes. Local 4-momentum conservation dotted with the timelike-at-infinity Killing vector ξ gives E_out = E_in - E_bh for energies at infinity. Normally E_bh > 0, but inside the ergosphere ξ·ξ = g_tt > 0 so ξ is spacelike and -p_bh·ξ can be negative, allowing E_out > E_in with the hole's mass decreasing. Example 29.7 takes an observer circulating at fixed r and θ with 4-velocity proportional to ξ + Ωη, demands positive locally measured energy for the infalling piece, and obtains E_bh ≥ Ω L_bh; since Ω > 0 in the ergosphere, negative E_bh requires negative L_bh, so the hole also loses angular momentum.

**Concepts:** Penrose process, Killing energy and negative-energy orbits, spacelike Killing vector inside the ergosphere, positivity of locally measured energy, circularly rotating observer, spin-down by energy extraction, conservation of 4-momentum in decays

**Key moves**
- Write p_in = p_out + p_bh at the decay event.
- Dot with ξ to turn local conservation into a statement about energies at infinity.
- Evaluate ξ·ξ = g_tt and note it is positive inside the ergosphere, so Killing energy need not be positive.
- Build a physically allowed observer from Killing vectors, u = u^t(ξ + Ωη), and impose -u·p_bh ≥ 0 for all allowed Ω.
- Conclude E_bh ≥ Ω L_bh, hence negative energy implies negative angular momentum for the captured fragment.

### Chapter summary and the difficulty of deriving Kerr — p.305 (pdf 322)

Four bullet points recap charged holes (unlikely in nature, three regions, repulsive singularity, wormhole travel) and Kerr holes (Boyer-Lindquist form, ring singularity, ergosphere where nothing stays still). One bullet mislabels r0 as an ellipsoidal event horizon; r0 is the stationary limit. A margin essay recounts that Kerr's 1963 announcement gave no derivation, that Kerr and Schild (1965) and Landau and Lifshitz stressed how laborious the calculations are, that Chandrasekhar later presented a better-motivated derivation, and that his perturbation calculations filled hundreds of pages deposited in a Chicago library.

**Concepts:** Kerr metric, ergosphere, no-hair theorem

**Key moves**
- Recap the qualitative anatomy of both solutions.
- Normalize the fact that the metric was quoted: even experts found it hard to derive.

## Concepts

### no-hair theorem

*theorem · introduced* · also: black holes have no hair, black-hole uniqueness

The result that a stationary black hole in general relativity (with electromagnetism) is completely specified by its mass, electric charge and angular momentum; every other detail of the collapsed matter is radiated away or hidden, so the exterior metric belongs to a three-parameter family.

**How introduced:** Motivated by the observation that a horizon hides the interior, so only globally measurable quantities survive; presented through Wheeler's hair metaphor and used to justify why studying charge and spin covers all black holes.

**Prerequisites:** event horizon, Schwarzschild metric

**Notes:** The book's justification ('owing to the singularity') is loose; the actual theorems (Israel, Carter, Hawking, Robinson) are uniqueness theorems for stationary vacuum or electrovac black holes.

**Where:** GA p.297 (pdf 314)

### event horizon

*definition · revisited* · also: horizon

The boundary of the region from which no future-directed causal curve can reach distant (future null) infinity; for stationary holes it coincides with a null surface where the relevant metric function vanishes.

**How introduced:** Taken from earlier chapters and used as the reason outside observers cannot learn internal details of the hole.

**Prerequisites:** null surface, Penrose diagram

**Where:** GA p.297 (pdf 314); GA §29.2 p.300 (pdf 317)

### Reissner-Nordstrom metric

*solution-or-model · core* · also: Reissner-Nordström line element, charged black hole metric

The static, spherically symmetric solution of the coupled Einstein-Maxwell equations outside a mass M carrying charge q; in geometrized Gaussian units ds² = -f dt² + f⁻¹ dr² + r² dΩ² with f = 1 - 2M/r + q²/r².

**How introduced:** Quoted after arguing that the stress-energy side of the field equation must now include electromagnetic field energy; checked by the q -> 0 Schwarzschild limit.

**Prerequisites:** Schwarzschild metric, energy-momentum tensor, electromagnetic field energy density

$$
ds^2=-\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{q^2}{r^2}\right)dt^2+\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{q^2}{r^2}\right)^{-1}dr^2+r^2d\Omega^2
$$

**Where:** GA §29.1 p.298 (pdf 315)

### inner and outer horizons

*definition · developed* · also: r_+ and r_-, outer event horizon, inner event horizon, Cauchy horizon

The two radii at which the radial metric function of a charged or rotating hole vanishes: r± = M ± (M² - q²)^{1/2} for Reissner-Nordstrom and r± = M ± (M² - a²)^{1/2} for Kerr. The outer one is the event horizon; inside the inner one, surfaces of constant r become timelike again, and in the extended spacetime it acts as a Cauchy horizon.

**How introduced:** Found as coordinate singularities of the quoted metrics; for Kerr they are the zeros of Δ and are displayed as nested surfaces in a cartoon.

**Prerequisites:** event horizon, coordinate singularity

$$
r_\pm = M \pm (M^2-q^2)^{1/2}
$$

$$
r_\pm = M \pm (M^2-a^2)^{1/2}
$$

**Notes:** The book calls both 'event horizons'; strictly only r+ is the event horizon. The Cauchy-horizon instability of r- is not discussed.

**Where:** GA §29.1 p.298 (pdf 315); GA §29.2 p.300 (pdf 317); GA §29.2 p.301 (pdf 318)

### regions I, II and III of a two-horizon black hole

*definition · core* · also: three regions

The division of a Reissner-Nordstrom or Kerr spacetime into the exterior r > r+ (region I), the between-horizons zone r- < r < r+ (region II) where r is a time-like coordinate and every point lies on a closed trapped surface, and the interior r < r- (region III) where constant-r surfaces are again timelike and the singularity lives.

**How introduced:** Introduced by cutting spacetime at the two coordinate singularities and checking which regions are static, then labelled on the Penrose diagrams.

**Prerequisites:** inner and outer horizons, closed trapped surface, static spacetime

**Notes:** For Kerr the ergosphere belongs to region I, and region III extends to negative r.

**Where:** GA §29.1 p.298 (pdf 315); GA §29.2 p.301 (pdf 318)

### closed trapped surface

*definition · revisited* · also: trapped surface

A closed spacelike 2-surface such that both the outgoing and ingoing families of orthogonal light rays converge, so its area decreases in every future null direction.

**How introduced:** Recalled from the Schwarzschild interior to characterize region II of the charged and rotating holes.

**Prerequisites:** null geodesic, event horizon

**Where:** GA §29.1 p.298 (pdf 315); GA §29.2 p.301 (pdf 318)

### timelike singularity

*definition · developed*

A curvature singularity whose locus in spacetime is a timelike curve or surface, so it sits beside observers in space rather than lying unavoidably in their future; drawn as a vertical line on a Penrose diagram.

**How introduced:** Contrasted with the spacelike singularities of Schwarzschild and the Big Bang; appears because inside r- the roles of r and t revert to their exterior character.

**Prerequisites:** spacelike singularity, Penrose diagram, regions I, II and III of a two-horizon black hole

**Where:** GA §29.1 p.298 (pdf 315); GA §29.2 p.302 (pdf 319)

### repulsive singularity

*phenomenon · introduced*

A singularity that freely falling massive particles never reach, because timelike geodesics turn around before hitting it; in Reissner-Nordstrom the q²/r² term dominates near r = 0 and acts repulsively.

**How introduced:** Stated for the region III singularity of the charged hole, as the reason a traveller can avoid it and continue through the wormhole.

**Prerequisites:** timelike singularity, geodesic

**Notes:** Holds for geodesics of uncharged particles; accelerated observers or charged particles could reach it.

**Where:** GA §29.1 p.298 (pdf 315)

### maximally extended Reissner-Nordstrom Penrose diagram

*mathematical-object · developed* · also: Penrose diagram of a charged hole, Penrose tower

The conformal diagram of the analytically extended charged-hole spacetime: an infinite vertical chain in which pairs of asymptotically flat regions I are joined upward through region II to two region-III patches bounded by timelike r = 0 lines, then through another region II to a new pair of region I, and so on.

**How introduced:** Shown in two versions, one with region labels and conformal infinities and one with lines of constant r and t, and used to tell the traveller's story.

**Prerequisites:** Penrose diagram, Kruskal extension, regions I, II and III of a two-horizon black hole

**Where:** GA §29.1 p.298 (pdf 315); GA §29.1 p.299 (pdf 316)

### passage to another asymptotically flat region

*phenomenon · introduced* · also: wormhole to a new universe, tunnelling into a new universe

In the maximally extended charged or rotating black-hole spacetime, a future-directed timelike curve can enter through r+, cross r-, avoid the timelike singularity and exit through another pair of horizons into a different asymptotically flat region; no future-directed curve leads back.

**How introduced:** Told as a traveller's journey on the Penrose tower right after establishing that the singularity is avoidable.

**Prerequisites:** maximally extended Reissner-Nordstrom Penrose diagram, timelike singularity, wormhole

**Notes:** Relies on the idealized eternal solution; perturbations make the inner horizon singular, so this is not expected physically.

**Where:** GA §29.1 p.298 (pdf 315); GA §29.2 p.302 (pdf 319)

### naked singularity

*phenomenon · mention* · also: super-extremal case

A curvature singularity not hidden behind any event horizon, as in Reissner-Nordstrom with q² > M² or Kerr with a² > M².

**How introduced:** Only implied: the book notes that for q² > M² the metric has no singular radius except r = 0 and that Kerr horizons exist only for a ≤ M.

**Prerequisites:** event horizon, inner and outer horizons

**Where:** GA §29.1 p.298 (pdf 315); GA §29.2 p.300 (pdf 317)

### extremal black hole

*definition · introduced* · also: extremal Kerr black hole, extremal Reissner-Nordstrom

A black hole whose charge or spin sits at the maximum compatible with a horizon, q² = M² or a = M (J = M²), so the two horizons merge and region II disappears.

**How introduced:** Arises from the square root in r±: for Kerr the maximum angular momentum is read off directly; for Reissner-Nordstrom the vanishing of region II is noted in parentheses.

**Prerequisites:** inner and outer horizons, Kerr parameter

$$
J_{\max}=M^2
$$

$$
r_+=r_-=M
$$

**Where:** GA §29.1 p.298 (pdf 315); GA §29.2 p.300 (pdf 317)

### blueshift at the inner horizon

*phenomenon · mention* · also: infinite blueshift

An observer crossing the inner horizon of a charged or rotating hole receives light from the whole future history of an exterior region in a finite proper time, so that incoming radiation is blueshifted without bound.

**How introduced:** Read off the Penrose diagram when asking what observers in the charged spacetime would see, paired with the familiar infinite redshift at r+.

**Prerequisites:** maximally extended Reissner-Nordstrom Penrose diagram, gravitational redshift

**Notes:** This is the physical seed of the inner-horizon instability, which the book does not mention.

**Where:** GA §29.1 p.298 (pdf 315); GA §29.1 p.299 (pdf 316)

### stationary spacetime

*definition · introduced*

A spacetime admitting a timelike (at infinity) Killing vector, so in adapted coordinates no metric component depends on t; unlike a static spacetime the metric may contain time-space cross terms.

**How introduced:** Defined inside Example 29.1 as the property that the metric components carry no t dependence, in preparation for Kerr.

**Prerequisites:** Killing vector, static spacetime

**Where:** GA §29.2 p.299 (pdf 316)

### axisymmetric spacetime

*definition · introduced* · also: axial symmetry

A spacetime with a spacelike Killing vector whose orbits are closed circles about an axis, so the metric components are independent of the azimuthal angle φ.

**How introduced:** Presented as the symmetry that survives when rotation breaks spherical symmetry.

**Prerequisites:** Killing vector

**Where:** GA §29.2 p.299 (pdf 316)

### general stationary axisymmetric metric

*mathematical-object · introduced* · also: ω-form of the line element

The line element ds² = -e^{2ν}dt² + e^{2ψ}(dφ - ω dt)² + e^{2μ₂}(dx²)² + e^{2μ₃}(dx³)² with all functions depending only on the two non-symmetry coordinates; ω is an angular velocity that produces the dt dφ cross term.

**How introduced:** Given in Example 29.1 as the most general form compatible with stationarity and axisymmetry, and later matched by rewriting Kerr in eqn 29.6.

**Prerequisites:** stationary spacetime, axisymmetric spacetime, off-diagonal metric cross term g_tφ

$$
ds^2=-e^{2\nu}dt^2+e^{2\psi}(d\phi-\omega\,dt)^2+e^{2\mu_2}(dx^2)^2+e^{2\mu_3}(dx^3)^2
$$

**Where:** GA §29.2 p.299 (pdf 316); GA §29.2 p.300 (pdf 317)

### off-diagonal metric cross term g_tφ

*mathematical-object · core* · also: cross term, gravitomagnetic term

A non-zero mixed time-azimuth metric component, which means the coordinate basis vectors e_t and e_φ are not orthogonal; in Kerr g_tφ = -2Mar sin²θ/ρ² and it encodes the rotation of the source.

**How introduced:** Flagged in Example 29.1 as the origin of most new Kerr physics, then shown to cause frame dragging (via g^{tφ}) and the stationary-limit behaviour of light (via g_tφ).

**Prerequisites:** metric tensor, inverse metric

$$
g_{t\phi}=-\frac{2Mar\sin^2\theta}{\rho^2}
$$

**Where:** GA §29.2 p.299 (pdf 316); GA §29.2 p.302 (pdf 319); GA §29.2 p.303 (pdf 320)

### Cotton-Darboux theorem

*theorem · mention*

The statement that any metric on a three-dimensional manifold can be brought to diagonal form locally by a coordinate change; in four dimensions this is not guaranteed.

**How introduced:** A footnote explaining why the book's earlier metrics could be diagonal, contrasting with the unavoidable cross term in Kerr.

**Prerequisites:** metric tensor, coordinate transformation

**Where:** GA §29.2 p.299 (pdf 316)

### Kerr metric

*solution-or-model · core* · also: Kerr geometry, Kerr solution, rotating black hole metric

The two-parameter (M, a) stationary, axisymmetric, asymptotically flat vacuum solution of the Einstein equation describing a rotating black hole; in Boyer-Lindquist coordinates it has components g_tt = -(1 - 2Mr/ρ²), g_tφ = -2Mar sin²θ/ρ², g_φφ = [(r² + a²)² - a²Δ sin²θ] sin²θ/ρ², g_rr = ρ²/Δ, g_θθ = ρ².

**How introduced:** Quoted after motivating rotation as realistic; checked by reversal symmetry, flat large-r behaviour and the a -> 0 limit; then interrogated by singularities, Killing vectors, test particles and light.

**Prerequisites:** Schwarzschild metric, Boyer-Lindquist coordinates, stationary spacetime, axisymmetric spacetime, off-diagonal metric cross term g_tφ

$$
ds^2=-\left(1-\frac{2Mr}{\rho^2}\right)dt^2-\frac{4Mar\sin^2\theta}{\rho^2}dt\,d\phi+\frac{(r^2+a^2)^2-a^2\Delta\sin^2\theta}{\rho^2}\sin^2\theta\,d\phi^2+\frac{\rho^2}{\Delta}dr^2+\rho^2d\theta^2
$$

$$
\Delta=r^2-2Mr+a^2,\quad \rho^2=r^2+a^2\cos^2\theta
$$

**Notes:** The book says it is the only exact solution for the exterior of a rotating mass; it is the unique rotating black-hole solution, but the exterior of a rotating star is generally not Kerr.

**Where:** GA §29.2 p.300 (pdf 317)

### Boyer-Lindquist coordinates

*convention · developed*

Coordinates (t, r, θ, φ) for Kerr in which t and φ are adapted to the two Killing vectors and r, θ are spheroidal: x = (r² + a²)^{1/2} sinθ cosφ, y = (r² + a²)^{1/2} sinθ sinφ, z = r cosθ, so constant-r surfaces are oblate ellipsoids and r = 0 is a disc of radius a.

**How introduced:** Presented as spherical polars with an adjusted radius, with the consequence that the Kerr singularity cannot be a point.

**Prerequisites:** spherical polar coordinates, Kerr metric

$$
x=(r^2+a^2)^{1/2}\sin\theta\cos\phi,\; y=(r^2+a^2)^{1/2}\sin\theta\sin\phi,\; z=r\cos\theta
$$

**Notes:** The printed eqn 29.3 omits the z relation.

**Where:** GA §29.2 p.299 (pdf 316)

### Kerr parameter

*physical-quantity · developed* · also: spin parameter, a = J/M

The angular momentum per unit mass of the hole, a = J/M, with dimensions of length in geometrized units; a = 0 is Schwarzschild and a = M is extremal.

**How introduced:** Introduced alongside the quoted metric as the measure of rotation, with J identified as the angular momentum seen from infinity.

**Prerequisites:** Kerr metric, angular momentum

$$
J=Ma
$$

$$
0\le a\le M
$$

**Where:** GA §29.2 p.300 (pdf 317)

### ring singularity

*phenomenon · developed* · also: physical ring singularity

The curvature singularity of Kerr at ρ² = r² + a² cos²θ = 0, i.e. r = 0 and θ = π/2, which in the Boyer-Lindquist Cartesian picture is a ring x² + y² = a² in the equatorial plane; it is timelike and can be passed through into negative r.

**How introduced:** Found by listing where the metric blows up (ρ = 0 versus Δ = 0) and substituting into the coordinate map.

**Prerequisites:** Boyer-Lindquist coordinates, curvature singularity, timelike singularity

$$
\rho=0\iff r=0,\ \theta=\pi/2\iff x^2+y^2=a^2,\ z=0
$$

**Where:** GA §29.2 p.300 (pdf 317); GA §29.2 p.301 (pdf 318); GA §29.2 p.302 (pdf 319)

### Kerr horizon area

*physical-quantity · introduced* · also: area of the r+ horizon

The proper area of the outer Kerr horizon on a constant-t slice, A = 8πMr+ = 4π(r+² + a²), which decreases from 16πM² at a = 0 to 8πM² at a = M.

**How introduced:** Example 29.2 restricts the metric to dt = dr = 0 at r = r+, simplifies using r+² + a² = 2Mr+ and integrates the area element.

**Prerequisites:** Kerr metric, induced metric, inner and outer horizons

$$
A=\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^{\pi}\sqrt{g^{(2)}}\,d\theta\,d\phi=8\pi M r_+
$$

**Notes:** Relevant to black-hole thermodynamics (area as entropy) of Chapter 28, which the chapter does not connect explicitly.

**Where:** GA §29.2 p.301 (pdf 318); GA p.306 (pdf 323)

### Kerr Penrose diagram with negative r

*mathematical-object · introduced* · also: maximally extended Kerr spacetime

The conformal diagram of the analytically extended Kerr solution along the symmetry axis: a vertical chain of asymptotically flat regions I, trapped regions II and regions III, where region III continues through the disc r = 0 into a second asymptotically flat region with negative r.

**How introduced:** Shown with lines of constant r; the text explains that because the singularity is a ring, spacetime can be continued past r = 0.

**Prerequisites:** maximally extended Reissner-Nordstrom Penrose diagram, ring singularity

**Where:** GA §29.2 p.301 (pdf 318); GA §29.2 p.302 (pdf 319)

### closed timelike curves

*phenomenon · mention* · also: CTCs, causality violation

Future-directed timelike curves that return to their starting event; in Kerr they exist near the ring singularity in the negative-r part of region III, where g_φφ < 0 makes circles of constant t, r, θ timelike.

**How introduced:** Mentioned in one sentence while reading the Kerr Penrose diagram.

**Prerequisites:** Kerr Penrose diagram with negative r, ring singularity

**Where:** GA §29.2 p.302 (pdf 319)

### Killing vectors and conserved quantities in Kerr

*principle · revisited* · also: Ẽ and L̃ in Kerr

Because the Kerr metric is independent of t and φ, ξ = ∂_t and η = ∂_φ are Killing vectors and geodesics conserve Ẽ = -ξ·u = -u_t and L̃ = η·u = u_φ; with the cross term these are mixtures u_t = g_tt u^t + g_tφ u^φ and u_φ = g_φt u^t + g_φφ u^φ.

**How introduced:** Carried over from the Schwarzschild recipe, with a warning that the cross terms complicate converting to u^t and u^φ.

**Prerequisites:** Killing vector, geodesic, Kerr metric

$$
\tilde{E}=-\boldsymbol{\xi}\cdot\boldsymbol{u}=-u_t
$$

$$
\tilde{L}=\boldsymbol{\eta}\cdot\boldsymbol{u}=u_\phi
$$

**Where:** GA §29.2 p.300 (pdf 317); GA §29.2 p.302 (pdf 319)

### inverse metric of the t-φ block

*technique · introduced*

Inverting the 2x2 matrix of g_tt, g_tφ, g_φφ (determinant -Δ sin²θ for Kerr) to obtain g^{tt}, g^{tφ}, g^{φφ}, which convert conserved covariant components into coordinate velocities.

**How introduced:** Quoted in a footnote with the derivation assigned as an exercise; used immediately for frame dragging.

**Prerequisites:** inverse metric, off-diagonal metric cross term g_tφ

$$
g^{tt}=-\frac{(r^2+a^2)^2-a^2\Delta\sin^2\theta}{\rho^2\Delta}
$$

$$
g^{t\phi}=-\frac{2Mra}{\rho^2\Delta}
$$

$$
g^{\phi\phi}=\frac{\Delta-a^2\sin^2\theta}{\rho^2\Delta\sin^2\theta}
$$

**Where:** GA §29.2 p.302 (pdf 319); GA p.306 (pdf 323)

### frame dragging

*phenomenon · core* · also: inertial frame dragging, Lense-Thirring effect (weak-field version)

The effect by which a rotating mass forces nearby freely falling bodies and light to acquire angular velocity in the sense of rotation; a particle with zero conserved angular momentum still has dφ/dt = g^{tφ}/g^{tt} = 2Mar/Σ², which equals the function ω in the stationary-axisymmetric form.

**How introduced:** Example 29.3 drops a particle with u_φ = 0 and shows its coordinate angular velocity is non-zero purely because of the off-diagonal inverse metric.

**Prerequisites:** Killing vectors and conserved quantities in Kerr, inverse metric of the t-φ block, off-diagonal metric cross term g_tφ

$$
\frac{d\phi}{dt}\Big|_{u_\phi=0}=\frac{g^{\phi t}}{g^{tt}}=\frac{2Mar}{(r^2+a^2)^2-a^2\Delta\sin^2\theta}
$$

**Notes:** The book says the particle 'gains' angular momentum; its conserved L̃ stays zero, what appears is angular velocity relative to distant stars.

**Where:** GA §29.2 p.302 (pdf 319)

### Kerr equatorial effective potential for massive particles

*mathematical-object · developed* · also: V_eff in Kerr

For equatorial geodesics, (Ẽ² - 1)/2 = ½(dr/dτ)² + V_eff with V_eff = -M/r + [L̃² - a²(Ẽ² - 1)]/(2r²) - M(L̃ - aẼ)²/r³, which reduces to the Schwarzschild potential at a = 0 and treats co- and counter-rotating orbits differently.

**How introduced:** Example 29.4 finds u^t and u^φ from the conserved quantities, imposes u·u = -1 and solves for u^r, stressing the resemblance to the Schwarzschild case.

**Prerequisites:** Killing vectors and conserved quantities in Kerr, effective potential

$$
V_{\rm eff}=-\frac{M}{r}+\frac{\tilde{L}^2-a^2(\tilde{E}^2-1)}{2r^2}-\frac{M(\tilde{L}-a\tilde{E})^2}{r^3}
$$

**Where:** GA §29.2 p.302 (pdf 319); GA §29.2 p.303 (pdf 320)

### coordinate angular velocity of tangential light rays

*technique · developed* · also: null condition for azimuthal photons

For a photon moving only in t and φ, the null condition g_tt dt² + 2g_tφ dt dφ + g_φφ dφ² = 0 gives two angular velocities Ω± = -g_tφ/g_φφ ± [(g_tφ/g_φφ)² - g_tt/g_φφ]^{1/2}, bracketing the angular velocities any body at that point can have.

**How introduced:** Example 29.5 sets up photons launched in ±φ and solves the quadratic; the special case g_tt = 0 is then examined.

**Prerequisites:** null curve, light cone, Kerr metric

$$
\frac{d\phi}{dt}=-\frac{g_{t\phi}}{g_{\phi\phi}}\pm\left[\left(\frac{g_{t\phi}}{g_{\phi\phi}}\right)^2-\frac{g_{tt}}{g_{\phi\phi}}\right]^{1/2}
$$

**Where:** GA §29.2 p.303 (pdf 320)

### stationary observer

*definition · developed* · also: static observer, hovering observer

An observer at fixed r, θ, φ with 4-velocity u^μ = (u^t, 0, 0, 0); normalization requires g_tt (u^t)² = -1, so such observers exist only where g_tt < 0.

**How introduced:** Used as a second, one-line proof that nothing can remain at rest where g_tt vanishes.

**Prerequisites:** 4-velocity normalization, Kerr metric

$$
g_{tt}(u^t)^2=-1
$$

**Where:** GA §29.2 p.303 (pdf 320)

### stationary limit surface

*definition · core* · also: static limit, stationary limit, ergosurface, r_0

The surface g_tt = 0 (equivalently ρ² = 2Mr), at r0 = M + (M² - a² cos²θ)^{1/2}; inside it no observer can remain at rest relative to infinity. It touches the outer horizon at the poles and reaches r = 2M at the equator.

**How introduced:** Obtained by noticing that at g_tt = 0 a counter-rotating photon has zero angular velocity, then solving g_tt = 0 for r.

**Prerequisites:** stationary observer, coordinate angular velocity of tangential light rays, inner and outer horizons

$$
r_0=M+\left(M^2-a^2\cos^2\theta\right)^{1/2}
$$

**Notes:** It is not a horizon: signals and particles can escape from inside it. The chapter summary mistakenly calls it an event horizon.

**Where:** GA §29.2 p.303 (pdf 320); GA §29.2 p.304 (pdf 321)

### ergosphere

*definition · core* · also: ergoregion

The region r+ < r < r0 between the outer horizon and the stationary limit, part of the exterior region I, in which every observer must co-rotate with the hole and the Killing vector ∂_t is spacelike, permitting negative Killing energy.

**How introduced:** Named once the stationary limit is found; drawn in side and top views for a near-extremal hole and in a 3D cartoon; exploited in Section 29.3.

**Prerequisites:** stationary limit surface, inner and outer horizons, frame dragging

$$
r_+\le r\le r_0
$$

**Where:** GA §29.2 p.303 (pdf 320); GA §29.2 p.301 (pdf 318); GA §29.3 p.304 (pdf 321)

### Kerr photon effective potential

*mathematical-object · introduced* · also: W_eff in Kerr

For equatorial photons, (1/L̃²)(dr/dλ)² = 1/b² - W_eff with W_eff = (1/r²)[1 - (a/b)² - (2M/r)(1 - σa/b)²], where b = L̃/Ẽ in magnitude and σ = sign(L̃) distinguishes prograde from retrograde light.

**How introduced:** Example 29.6 quotes it (derivation left as an exercise) and interprets the σ dependence as frame dragging.

**Prerequisites:** impact parameter, photon effective potential, frame dragging

$$
W_{\rm eff}=\frac{1}{r^2}\left[1-\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^2-\frac{2M}{r}\left(1-\sigma\frac{a}{b}\right)^2\right]
$$

**Where:** GA §29.2 p.304 (pdf 321)

### prograde and retrograde motion

*definition · developed* · also: co-rotating and counter-rotating orbits

Orbits or rays whose angular momentum is aligned (prograde) or anti-aligned (retrograde) with the hole's spin; in Kerr their potentials, orbital radii and allowed angular velocities differ.

**How introduced:** Appears through the (L̃ - aẼ) and σ factors in the effective potentials and the two roots of the tangential null condition.

**Prerequisites:** Kerr equatorial effective potential for massive particles, Kerr photon effective potential

**Where:** GA §29.2 p.303 (pdf 320); GA §29.2 p.304 (pdf 321)

### light-cone tilting in the Kerr geometry

*phenomenon · introduced* · also: light-cone structure of Kerr

The progressive tipping of local future light cones: far away they are upright, at the stationary limit they touch the direction of rest, inside the ergosphere they point entirely in the rotation direction, between the horizons they point entirely inward, and inside r- outward motion becomes possible again.

**How introduced:** Summarized by a top-down diagram of small cone sections drawn across the regions.

**Prerequisites:** light cone, ergosphere, regions I, II and III of a two-horizon black hole

**Where:** GA §29.2 p.304 (pdf 321)

### Penrose process

*phenomenon · core* · also: energy extraction from a rotating black hole

A mechanism in which a body entering the ergosphere splits into two, one fragment falling through the horizon on a negative-Killing-energy trajectory and the other escaping with more energy at infinity than the original body had, the excess being taken from the hole's mass and spin.

**How introduced:** Presented as Penrose's suggestion and justified by local momentum conservation, a dot product with ξ, and the spacelike character of ξ in the ergosphere.

**Prerequisites:** ergosphere, Killing energy and negative-energy orbits, conservation of 4-momentum in decays

$$
E^\infty_{\rm out}=E^\infty_{\rm in}-E^\infty_{\rm bh}
$$

**Notes:** The maximum extractable energy (down to the irreducible mass) and the link with the area theorem are not discussed.

**Where:** GA §29.3 p.304 (pdf 321); GA §29.3 p.305 (pdf 322)

### Killing energy and negative-energy orbits

*physical-quantity · developed* · also: energy at infinity, E^∞ = -ξ·p

The conserved quantity E = -ξ·p built from the stationary Killing vector; it equals energy measured by an observer at rest at infinity, but where ξ is spacelike it is not a locally measured energy and can take negative values on physically allowed trajectories.

**How introduced:** Recalled from the Hawking radiation chapter and used to convert the decay's momentum balance into energies at infinity.

**Prerequisites:** Killing vector, energy measured by an observer

$$
E^\infty=-\boldsymbol{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}
$$

**Where:** GA §29.3 p.304 (pdf 321); GA §29.3 p.305 (pdf 322)

### spacelike Killing vector inside the ergosphere

*phenomenon · developed* · also: sign change of ξ·ξ

The norm of the stationary Killing vector is ξ·ξ = g_tt, which is negative outside the stationary limit and positive inside, so ξ becomes spacelike in the ergosphere, just as ∂_t does inside r = 2M in Schwarzschild.

**How introduced:** Planted in footnote 11 and cashed in when explaining why Killing energy is no longer bounded below.

**Prerequisites:** stationary limit surface, Killing vector

$$
\boldsymbol{\xi}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}=g_{tt}=-\left(1-\frac{2Mr}{\rho^2}\right)>0
$$

**Where:** GA §29.2 p.303 (pdf 320); GA §29.3 p.304 (pdf 321); GA §29.3 p.305 (pdf 322)

### positivity of locally measured energy

*principle · revisited*

Any physical observer with 4-velocity u measures a particle energy E = -u·p that is non-negative for future-directed timelike or null momentum.

**How introduced:** Recalled from the Hawking radiation argument and imposed for a circulating observer in Example 29.7.

**Prerequisites:** 4-momentum, energy measured by an observer

$$
-\boldsymbol{u}_{\rm obs}\cdot\boldsymbol{p}\ge 0
$$

**Where:** GA §29.3 p.305 (pdf 322)

### circularly rotating observer

*definition · introduced* · also: observer at fixed r and θ

An observer who keeps r and θ fixed but circulates with angular velocity Ω = dφ/dt, so u = u^t(ξ + Ωη); inside the ergosphere Ω must be positive (co-rotating).

**How introduced:** Constructed in Example 29.7 from the two Killing vectors to obtain an energy bound valid everywhere in the ergosphere.

**Prerequisites:** Killing vectors and conserved quantities in Kerr, stationary observer

$$
u^\mu=u^t(1,0,0,\Omega)
$$

**Where:** GA §29.3 p.305 (pdf 322)

### spin-down by energy extraction

*phenomenon · developed*

Because locally positive energy requires E_bh ≥ Ω L_bh with Ω > 0 in the ergosphere, a fragment with negative Killing energy must carry negative angular momentum, so the Penrose process lowers both the mass and angular momentum of the hole.

**How introduced:** Derived in Example 29.7 and stated as the conclusion of the chapter's application.

**Prerequisites:** Penrose process, circularly rotating observer, positivity of locally measured energy

$$
E_{\rm bh}\ge \Omega L_{\rm bh}
$$

**Where:** GA §29.3 p.305 (pdf 322)

### conservation of 4-momentum in decays

*law · revisited*

At a local event where one particle splits into two, the 4-momenta satisfy p_in = p_out + p_bh, whatever the curvature of spacetime.

**How introduced:** Used as the starting point for the Penrose process.

**Prerequisites:** 4-momentum

$$
\boldsymbol{p}_{\rm in}=\boldsymbol{p}_{\rm out}+\boldsymbol{p}_{\rm bh}
$$

**Where:** GA §29.3 p.304 (pdf 321)

### Kerr-Newman metric

*solution-or-model · mention*

The stationary black-hole solution carrying mass, charge and angular momentum simultaneously, which contains both Reissner-Nordstrom and Kerr as special cases.

**How introduced:** Named only in a biographical footnote on Kerr.

**Prerequisites:** Kerr metric, Reissner-Nordstrom metric

**Where:** GA §29.2 p.299 (pdf 316)

### asymptotic flatness

*definition · revisited*

The property that the metric approaches Minkowski form at large distances, so that mass and angular momentum can be measured from infinity and the Penrose diagram has i0, i± and null infinities.

**How introduced:** Checked for Kerr by taking r large, and used to label region I in the Penrose diagrams.

**Prerequisites:** Minkowski metric, Penrose diagram

**Where:** GA §29.1 p.298 (pdf 315); GA §29.2 p.300 (pdf 317)

## Key equations

### (29.1) Reissner-Nordstrom line element · central · GA §29.1 p.298 (pdf 315)

$$
ds^{2}=-\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{q^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)dt^{2}+\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{q^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)^{-1}dr^{2}+r^{2}\left(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\,d\phi^{2}\right)
$$

Geometry outside a spherically symmetric charged mass; the q² term is the gravitational effect of the electric field's energy. Reduces to Schwarzschild when q = 0.

**Symbols:** M mass, q charge (both lengths in geometrized Gaussian units), (t, r, θ, φ) Schwarzschild-like coordinates

### Reissner-Nordstrom horizon radii · central · GA §29.1 p.298 (pdf 315)

$$
r_{\pm}=M\pm\left(M^{2}-q^{2}\right)^{1/2}
$$

Zeros of the metric function; for q² < M² two horizons, for q² = M² one degenerate horizon, for q² > M² none (naked singularity).

**Symbols:** r+ outer horizon, r- inner horizon

### (29.2) General stationary axisymmetric line element · supporting · GA §29.2 p.299 (pdf 316)

$$
ds^{2}=-e^{2\nu}dt^{2}+e^{2\psi}\left(d\phi-\omega\,dt\right)^{2}+e^{2\mu_{2}}\left(dx^{2}\right)^{2}+e^{2\mu_{3}}\left(dx^{3}\right)^{2}
$$

Most general metric with time-translation and rotational symmetry; expanding the second term yields a dt dφ cross term proportional to ω.

**Symbols:** ν, ψ, ω, μ₂, μ₃ functions of x², x³ only; ω is an angular velocity

### (29.3) Boyer-Lindquist to Cartesian map · supporting · GA §29.2 p.299 (pdf 316)

$$
x=\left(r^{2}+a^{2}\right)^{1/2}\sin\theta\cos\phi,\quad y=\left(r^{2}+a^{2}\right)^{1/2}\sin\theta\sin\phi,\quad z=r\cos\theta
$$

Spheroidal coordinates: constant-r surfaces are oblate ellipsoids and r = 0 is a disc of radius a. The z relation is missing from the printed equation and has been restored here.

**Symbols:** a Kerr parameter

### (29.4) Kerr line element in Boyer-Lindquist coordinates · central · GA §29.2 p.300 (pdf 317)

$$
ds^{2}=-\left(1-\frac{2Mr}{\rho^{2}}\right)dt^{2}-\frac{4Mar\sin^{2}\theta}{\rho^{2}}dt\,d\phi+\frac{\left(r^{2}+a^{2}\right)^{2}-a^{2}\Delta\sin^{2}\theta}{\rho^{2}}\sin^{2}\theta\,d\phi^{2}+\frac{\rho^{2}}{\Delta}dr^{2}+\rho^{2}d\theta^{2}
$$

Exterior and interior geometry of a rotating black hole; the dt dφ term carries the rotation.

**Symbols:** Δ = r² - 2Mr + a², ρ² = r² + a² cos²θ, a = J/M

### Kerr auxiliary functions · central · GA §29.2 p.300 (pdf 317)

$$
\Delta=r^{2}-2Mr+a^{2},\qquad \rho^{2}=r^{2}+a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta,\qquad J=Ma
$$

Δ vanishes at the horizons, ρ vanishes on the ring singularity; a is angular momentum per unit mass.

**Symbols:** J angular momentum measured at infinity

### (29.5) Kerr metric components · central · GA §29.2 p.300 (pdf 317)

$$
g_{tt}=-\left(1-\frac{2Mr}{\rho^{2}}\right)=-\frac{\Delta-a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{\rho^{2}},\quad g_{t\phi}=-\frac{2Mar\sin^{2}\theta}{\rho^{2}},\quad g_{\phi\phi}=\frac{\left(r^{2}+a^{2}\right)^{2}-a^{2}\Delta\sin^{2}\theta}{\rho^{2}}\sin^{2}\theta,\quad g_{rr}=\frac{\rho^{2}}{\Delta},\quad g_{\theta\theta}=\rho^{2}
$$

Component list used throughout the examples; negative g_tφ for a > 0 means rotation in the +φ sense.

### Kerr Killing vectors and conserved quantities · central · GA §29.2 p.300 (pdf 317)

$$
\xi^{\mu}=(1,0,0,0),\quad \eta^{\mu}=(0,0,0,1),\quad \tilde{E}=-\boldsymbol{\xi}\cdot\boldsymbol{u}=-u_{t},\quad \tilde{L}=\boldsymbol{\eta}\cdot\boldsymbol{u}=u_{\phi}
$$

t- and φ-independence give two Killing vectors and hence conserved energy and angular momentum per unit mass along geodesics.

**Symbols:** ξ stationary Killing vector, η axial Killing vector, u 4-velocity

### (29.6) Kerr metric in ω form · supporting · GA §29.2 p.300 (pdf 317)

$$
ds^{2}=-\frac{\rho^{2}\Delta}{\Sigma^{2}}dt^{2}+\frac{\Sigma^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{\rho^{2}}\left(d\phi-\omega\,dt\right)^{2}+\frac{\rho^{2}}{\Delta}dr^{2}+\rho^{2}d\theta^{2},\qquad \Sigma^{2}=\left(r^{2}+a^{2}\right)^{2}-a^{2}\Delta\sin^{2}\theta,\quad \omega=\frac{2Mar}{\Sigma^{2}}
$$

Shows Kerr is an instance of eqn 29.2, with ω the frame-dragging angular velocity. The book prints the dr² coefficient as ρ²/Δ²; the correct coefficient, consistent with eqn 29.4, is ρ²/Δ.

**Symbols:** Σ² book's notation for (r² + a²)² - a²Δ sin²θ (called A or Σ in other texts)

### (29.7) Kerr horizon radii · central · GA §29.2 p.300 (pdf 317)

$$
r_{\pm}=M\pm\left(M^{2}-a^{2}\right)^{1/2}
$$

Where Δ = 0; horizons exist only for a ≤ M, giving the maximum spin J = M². At a = 0, r+ = 2M and r- = 0.

### Ring singularity location · central · GA §29.2 p.300 (pdf 317)

$$
\rho=0\;\Longleftrightarrow\; r=0,\ \theta=\tfrac{\pi}{2}\;\Longleftrightarrow\; x^{2}+y^{2}=a^{2},\ z=0
$$

The physical curvature singularity of Kerr is a ring of coordinate radius a in the equatorial plane.

### (29.8) Induced metric on the outer horizon · derivation-step · GA §29.2 p.301 (pdf 318)

$$
ds^{2}=\rho_{+}^{2}d\theta^{2}+\left(r_{+}^{2}+a^{2}+\frac{2Mr_{+}a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{\rho_{+}^{2}}\right)\sin^{2}\theta\,d\phi^{2}=\rho_{+}^{2}d\theta^{2}+\left(\frac{2Mr_{+}}{\rho_{+}}\right)^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\,d\phi^{2}
$$

Setting dt = dr = 0 at r = r+ and using r+² + a² = 2Mr+ gives the 2-geometry of the horizon, which is not a round sphere.

**Symbols:** ρ+² = r+² + a² cos²θ

### (29.9) Kerr horizon area · central · GA §29.2 p.301 (pdf 318)

$$
A=\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\phi\int_{0}^{\pi}d\theta\,\sqrt{g^{(2)}}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\phi\int_{0}^{\pi}2Mr_{+}\sin\theta\,d\theta=8\pi Mr_{+}
$$

Area of the event horizon; 16πM² for Schwarzschild, 8πM² for extremal Kerr. The book writes the integrand as g, the determinant; it should be the square root of the determinant of the induced 2-metric.

**Symbols:** g^{(2)} determinant of eqn 29.8's 2-metric

### (29.10) Conserved quantities with cross terms · supporting · GA §29.2 p.302 (pdf 319)

$$
-\tilde{E}=u_{t}=g_{tt}u^{t}+g_{t\phi}u^{\phi},\qquad \tilde{L}=u_{\phi}=g_{\phi t}u^{t}+g_{\phi\phi}u^{\phi}
$$

Each conserved quantity mixes time and azimuthal velocity components because the metric is not diagonal.

### (29.11) Coordinate angular velocity as a ratio · derivation-step · GA §29.2 p.302 (pdf 319)

$$
\frac{d\phi}{dt}=\frac{d\phi}{d\tau}\frac{d\tau}{dt}=\frac{u^{\phi}}{u^{t}}
$$

Angular velocity seen from infinity equals the ratio of contravariant velocity components.

### (29.12) Inverse Kerr metric (t-φ block) · supporting · GA §29.2 p.302 (pdf 319)

$$
g^{tt}=-\frac{\left(r^{2}+a^{2}\right)^{2}-a^{2}\Delta\sin^{2}\theta}{\rho^{2}\Delta},\qquad g^{t\phi}=g^{\phi t}=-\frac{2Mra}{\rho^{2}\Delta},\qquad g^{\phi\phi}=\frac{\Delta-a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{\rho^{2}\Delta\sin^{2}\theta}
$$

Needed to raise indices on the conserved components; obtained by inverting the 2x2 block whose determinant is -Δ sin²θ.

### (29.13) Ratio of inverse metric components · central · GA §29.2 p.302 (pdf 319)

$$
\frac{g^{\phi t}}{g^{tt}}=\frac{2Mra}{\left(r^{2}+a^{2}\right)^{2}-a^{2}\Delta\sin^{2}\theta}
$$

The frame-dragging angular velocity; it is identical to ω = 2Mar/Σ² in eqn 29.6, a connection the text leaves implicit.

### (29.14) Raising indices on the 4-velocity · derivation-step · GA §29.2 p.302 (pdf 319)

$$
u^{\phi}=g^{\phi\phi}u_{\phi}+g^{\phi t}u_{t},\qquad u^{t}=g^{tt}u_{t}+g^{t\phi}u_{\phi}
$$

Converts conserved covariant components into coordinate velocities.

### (29.15) Frame dragging of a zero-angular-momentum particle · central · GA §29.2 p.302 (pdf 319)

$$
\left.\frac{d\phi}{dt}\right|_{u_{\phi}=0}=\frac{g^{\phi t}}{g^{tt}}\neq 0
$$

A body with no conserved angular momentum still circulates in the sense of the hole's rotation.

### (29.16) Equatorial contravariant velocities · derivation-step · GA §29.2 p.302 (pdf 319)

$$
u^{t}=\frac{1}{\Delta}\left[\left(r^{2}+a^{2}+\frac{2Ma^{2}}{r}\right)\tilde{E}-\frac{2Ma}{r}\tilde{L}\right],\qquad u^{\phi}=\frac{1}{\Delta}\left[\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)\tilde{L}+\frac{2Ma}{r}\tilde{E}\right]
$$

Time and azimuthal velocity components for θ = π/2 geodesics in terms of conserved Ẽ and L̃.

### (29.17) Radial energy equation · supporting · GA §29.2 p.302 (pdf 319)

$$
\frac{\tilde{E}^{2}-1}{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{dr}{d\tau}\right)^{2}+V_{\rm eff}(r,\tilde{E},\tilde{L})
$$

Same structure as the Schwarzschild energy equation: a kinetic term plus an effective potential.

### (29.18) Kerr equatorial effective potential · central · GA §29.2 p.303 (pdf 320)

$$
V_{\rm eff}(r,\tilde{E},\tilde{L})=-\frac{M}{r}+\frac{\tilde{L}^{2}-a^{2}\left(\tilde{E}^{2}-1\right)}{2r^{2}}-\frac{M\left(\tilde{L}-a\tilde{E}\right)^{2}}{r^{3}}
$$

Spin modifies the centrifugal term and the relativistic 1/r³ term; the (L̃ - aẼ) combination makes prograde and retrograde orbits differ. At a = 0 it becomes the Schwarzschild potential.

### (29.19) Null condition for tangential photons · derivation-step · GA §29.2 p.303 (pdf 320)

$$
ds^{2}=g_{tt}dt^{2}+2g_{t\phi}dt\,d\phi+g_{\phi\phi}d\phi^{2}=0
$$

Light moving purely in φ at fixed r, θ.

### (29.20) Angular velocities of light · central · GA §29.2 p.303 (pdf 320)

$$
\frac{d\phi}{dt}=-\frac{g_{t\phi}}{g_{\phi\phi}}\pm\left[\left(\frac{g_{t\phi}}{g_{\phi\phi}}\right)^{2}-\frac{g_{tt}}{g_{\phi\phi}}\right]^{1/2}
$$

The two roots bound the angular velocity of any body at that location.

### (29.21) Light angular velocities where g_tt = 0 · central · GA §29.2 p.303 (pdf 320)

$$
\frac{d\phi}{dt}=-\frac{2g_{t\phi}}{g_{\phi\phi}}\quad\text{or}\quad\frac{d\phi}{dt}=0
$$

On the stationary limit the co-rotating ray moves while the counter-rotating ray has zero coordinate angular velocity; nothing can move against the rotation.

### Condition for a stationary observer · supporting · GA §29.2 p.303 (pdf 320)

$$
u^{\mu}=(u^{t},0,0,0),\qquad g_{tt}\left(u^{t}\right)^{2}=-1
$$

Hovering is possible only where g_tt < 0; fails at and inside the stationary limit.

### Where g_tt vanishes · derivation-step · GA §29.2 p.303 (pdf 320)

$$
g_{tt}=0\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\rho^{2}=2Mr
$$

Footnote condition defining the stationary limit surface.

### (29.22) Stationary limit surface · central · GA §29.2 p.303 (pdf 320)

$$
r_{0}=M+\left(M^{2}-a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta\right)^{1/2}
$$

Outer boundary of the ergosphere: equals r+ at the poles and 2M at the equator.

**Symbols:** θ polar angle from the rotation axis

### (29.23) Photon radial equation in Kerr · supporting · GA §29.2 p.304 (pdf 321)

$$
\frac{1}{\tilde{L}^{2}}\left(\frac{dr}{d\lambda}\right)^{2}=\frac{1}{b^{2}}-W_{\rm eff}(r,b,\sigma)
$$

Equatorial photon motion governed by an impact parameter and an orientation-dependent potential.

**Symbols:** λ affine parameter, b impact parameter (magnitude of L̃/Ẽ), σ = sign(L̃)

### (29.24) Kerr photon effective potential · central · GA §29.2 p.304 (pdf 321)

$$
W_{\rm eff}(r,b,\sigma)=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\left[1-\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{2}-\frac{2M}{r}\left(1-\sigma\frac{a}{b}\right)^{2}\right]
$$

The σ dependence makes prograde and retrograde light see different barriers; reduces to the Schwarzschild W_eff at a = 0.

### (29.25) Momentum conservation at the split · derivation-step · GA §29.3 p.304 (pdf 321)

$$
\boldsymbol{p}_{\rm in}=\boldsymbol{p}_{\rm out}+\boldsymbol{p}_{\rm bh}
$$

Local 4-momentum balance when the infalling body divides in the ergosphere.

### (29.26) Energy balance at infinity · central · GA §29.3 p.304 (pdf 321)

$$
E_{\rm out}^{\infty}=E_{\rm in}^{\infty}-E_{\rm bh}^{\infty}
$$

Dotting 29.25 with ξ gives conserved Killing energies; extraction occurs if the captured piece has negative Killing energy.

**Symbols:** E^∞ = -ξ·p

### (29.27) Norm of the stationary Killing vector · central · GA §29.3 p.304 (pdf 321)

$$
\boldsymbol{\xi}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}=\boldsymbol{e}_{t}\cdot\boldsymbol{e}_{t}=g_{tt}=-\left(1-\frac{2Mr}{\rho^{2}}\right)>0\quad\text{(inside the ergosphere)}
$$

ξ is spacelike in the ergosphere, so -p·ξ is no longer forced to be positive.

### (29.28) 4-velocity of a circulating observer · derivation-step · GA §29.3 p.305 (pdf 322)

$$
u^{\mu}=u^{t}\left(1,0,0,\Omega\right)
$$

Observer at fixed r and θ rotating with angular velocity Ω.

**Symbols:** Ω = dφ/dt

### (29.29) Observer velocity from Killing vectors · derivation-step · GA §29.3 p.305 (pdf 322)

$$
\boldsymbol{u}_{\rm obs}=u^{t}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}+\Omega\boldsymbol{\eta}\right)
$$

Writing the observer in terms of symmetry generators so that dot products with p become conserved quantities.

### (29.30) Positive local energy of the infalling fragment · derivation-step · GA §29.3 p.305 (pdf 322)

$$
-\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}+\Omega\boldsymbol{\eta}\right)\cdot\boldsymbol{p}_{\rm bh}\ge 0
$$

Holds for every Ω that makes u timelike (u·u = -1 achievable).

### (29.31) Energy-angular momentum bound · central · GA §29.3 p.305 (pdf 322)

$$
E_{\rm bh}\ge\Omega L_{\rm bh}
$$

With Ω > 0 in the ergosphere, E_bh < 0 forces L_bh < 0: extraction spins the hole down.

**Symbols:** E = -ξ·p, L = η·p

### (29.32) Alternative form of g_tt (exercise) · derivation-step · GA p.306 (pdf 323)

$$
g_{tt}=-\frac{\Delta-a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{\rho^{2}}
$$

Identity used to see where g_tt changes sign.

### (29.33) Alternative form of g_φφ (exercise) · derivation-step · GA p.306 (pdf 323)

$$
g_{\phi\phi}=\left(\frac{2Mra^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{\rho^{2}}+r^{2}+a^{2}\right)\sin^{2}\theta
$$

Form of g_φφ that makes the horizon simplification in 29.8 immediate.

### (29.34) Horizon 2-metric (exercise restatement of 29.8) · derivation-step · GA p.306 (pdf 323)

$$
ds^{2}=\rho_{+}^{2}d\theta^{2}+\left(r_{+}^{2}+a^{2}+\frac{2Mr_{+}a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{\rho_{+}^{2}}\right)\sin^{2}\theta\,d\phi^{2}=\rho_{+}^{2}d\theta^{2}+\left(\frac{2Mr_{+}}{\rho_{+}}\right)^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\,d\phi^{2}

## Worked examples

### Example 29.1 · intro · GA §29.2 p.299 (pdf 316)

**Problem:** What is the most general line element for a spacetime that is both stationary and axisymmetric?

**Method:** Impose independence of t and φ, let the remaining functions depend on the other two coordinates, and allow an angular-velocity function ω to couple dφ and dt.

**Key insight:** Rotation introduces a non-removable dt dφ cross term, unlike every metric used earlier in the book, and this term will be responsible for Kerr's novel effects.

**Result:** ds² = -e^{2ν}dt² + e^{2ψ}(dφ - ω dt)² + e^{2μ₂}(dx²)² + e^{2μ₃}(dx³)², with a cross term ∝ ω dφ dt.

**Concepts:** general stationary axisymmetric metric, stationary spacetime, axisymmetric spacetime, off-diagonal metric cross term g_tφ

### Example 29.2 · standard · GA §29.2 p.301 (pdf 318)

**Problem:** Find the area of the outer horizon of a Kerr black hole.

**Method:** Set dt = dr = 0 and r = r+ in the metric, use r+² + a² = 2Mr+ to collapse g_φφ to (2Mr+/ρ+)² sin²θ, then integrate the square root of the 2D metric determinant over θ and φ.

**Key insight:** The horizon is a surface of constant t and r whose 2-geometry is not a round sphere, yet its area has a compact closed form.

**Result:** A = 8πMr+ (the printed integrand omits the square root).

**Concepts:** Kerr horizon area, inner and outer horizons, Kerr metric

### Example 29.3 · standard · GA §29.2 p.302 (pdf 319)

**Problem:** A particle is released with zero angular momentum (u_φ = 0). Does it fall straight in?

**Method:** Write dφ/dt = u^φ/u^t, raise indices with the inverse metric so that u^φ = g^{φt} u_t and u^t = g^{tt} u_t, and take the ratio.

**Key insight:** Zero conserved angular momentum does not mean zero angular velocity: the off-diagonal inverse metric component forces circulation.

**Result:** dφ/dt = g^{φt}/g^{tt} = 2Mra/[(r² + a²)² - a²Δ sin²θ] ≠ 0 (frame dragging).

**Concepts:** frame dragging, inverse metric of the t-φ block, Killing vectors and conserved quantities in Kerr

### Example 29.4 · challenging · GA §29.2 p.302 (pdf 319)

**Problem:** Obtain the effective-potential description of equatorial massive-particle motion in Kerr.

**Method:** Invert the conserved-quantity relations at θ = π/2 for u^t and u^φ, set u^θ = 0, impose u·u = -1 and solve for (dr/dτ)²; details are an exercise.

**Key insight:** The Schwarzschild energy-equation format survives, but spin enters through a²(Ẽ² - 1) in the centrifugal term and through (L̃ - aẼ)² in the strong-field term.

**Result:** (Ẽ² - 1)/2 = ½(dr/dτ)² + V_eff, with V_eff = -M/r + [L̃² - a²(Ẽ² - 1)]/(2r²) - M(L̃ - aẼ)²/r³.

**Concepts:** Kerr equatorial effective potential for massive particles, prograde and retrograde motion

### Example 29.5 · standard · GA §29.2 p.303 (pdf 320)

**Problem:** How fast, in angle, can light travelling tangentially (±φ) move at a given point of the equatorial Kerr geometry?

**Method:** Impose the null condition with only dt and dφ non-zero and solve the resulting quadratic for dφ/dt.

**Key insight:** The two roots are not symmetric about zero; the offset -g_tφ/g_φφ is a dragging angular velocity, and when g_tt = 0 the retrograde root is exactly zero.

**Result:** dφ/dt = -g_tφ/g_φφ ± [(g_tφ/g_φφ)² - g_tt/g_φφ]^{1/2}; for g_tt = 0, dφ/dt = -2g_tφ/g_φφ or 0.

**Concepts:** coordinate angular velocity of tangential light rays, stationary limit surface

### Example · standard · GA §29.2 p.303 (pdf 320)

**Problem:** Embedded calculation after Example 29.5: can any observer remain at rest where g_tt = 0, and where is that surface?

**Method:** Argue first from light cones (if counter-rotating light cannot move backward, no body can) and then from normalization of a static 4-velocity; solve g_tt = 0, i.e. r² - 2Mr + a² cos²θ = 0, for the larger root.

**Key insight:** Being unable to stay still is a different condition from being unable to escape; the first happens at r0, outside the horizon r+.

**Result:** Stationary limit r0 = M + (M² - a² cos²θ)^{1/2}; ergosphere r+ ≤ r ≤ r0.

**Concepts:** stationary observer, stationary limit surface, ergosphere

### Example 29.6 · standard · GA §29.2 p.304 (pdf 321)

**Problem:** State the effective-potential description of equatorial photon orbits in Kerr.

**Method:** Quote the radial equation in terms of the impact parameter b and a potential W_eff, with derivation deferred to an exercise.

**Key insight:** The photon potential depends on σ = sign(L̃), so prograde and retrograde light face different barriers, a direct signature of frame dragging.

**Result:** (1/L̃²)(dr/dλ)² = 1/b² - W_eff with W_eff = (1/r²)[1 - (a/b)² - (2M/r)(1 - σa/b)²].

**Concepts:** Kerr photon effective potential, prograde and retrograde motion, frame dragging

### Example · standard · GA §29.3 p.304 (pdf 321)

**Problem:** Embedded calculation opening Section 29.3: how can splitting a particle in the ergosphere yield net energy at infinity?

**Method:** Write local 4-momentum conservation, dot with ξ to express energies at infinity, then evaluate ξ·ξ = g_tt and note it is positive inside the ergosphere.

**Key insight:** Conservation of Killing energy is never violated; what changes is that a spacelike ξ lets a physically allowed fragment carry negative Killing energy into the hole.

**Result:** E_out^∞ = E_in^∞ - E_bh^∞ > E_in^∞ whenever E_bh^∞ < 0, which is possible only where ξ is spacelike.

**Concepts:** Penrose process, Killing energy and negative-energy orbits, spacelike Killing vector inside the ergosphere

### Example 29.7 · standard · GA §29.3 p.305 (pdf 322)

**Problem:** Show that a negative-energy fragment captured in the Penrose process must also reduce the hole's angular momentum.

**Method:** Use an observer at fixed r, θ circulating with angular velocity Ω, write u = u^t(ξ + Ωη), require that observer to measure non-negative energy for the fragment, and translate the dot products into E and L.

**Key insight:** Constructing observers from Killing vectors turns a local positivity statement into an inequality between conserved quantities.

**Result:** E_bh ≥ Ω L_bh with Ω > 0 in the ergosphere, so E_bh < 0 requires L_bh < 0 and the hole spins down.

**Concepts:** spin-down by energy extraction, circularly rotating observer, positivity of locally measured energy, Penrose process


## Analogies and intuitions

### Hair as a metaphor for every detail of a collapsed object beyond mass, charge and spin → no-hair theorem · useful · GA p.297 (pdf 314)

Wheeler's slogan that black holes are bald compresses a uniqueness theorem into an image: all distinguishing features are shed.

**Where it breaks down:** The slogan refers to stationary end states; during formation and ringdown the hole does have 'hair' (multipole moments) that decays away, and in theories with extra fields hair can exist.

**App idea:** Collapse animation where a lumpy, magnetized, spinning star sheds its features as gravitational and electromagnetic waves until only three sliders (M, q, J) remain.

### Boyer-Lindquist coordinates as ordinary spherical polars with a stretched radius → Boyer-Lindquist coordinates · useful · GA §29.2 p.299 (pdf 316)

Replacing r by (r² + a²)^{1/2} in the x and y relations turns spheres into oblate ellipsoids and r = 0 into a disc.

**Where it breaks down:** Only a picture of the coordinate labels in a fictitious flat space; the physical geometry of Kerr is curved and the surfaces' intrinsic shapes differ.

**App idea:** Morphing grid: slide a from 0 upward and watch the constant-r spheres flatten into ellipsoids and the origin open into a disc.

### Reversing the film of a spinning object reverses its spin → Kerr metric · useful · GA §29.2 p.300 (pdf 317)

Explains why the metric is unchanged under t -> -t together with φ -> -φ, but not under either alone.

**Where it breaks down:** A symmetry check only; does not illuminate dynamics.

### Travelling through a one-way tunnel into a new universe → passage to another asymptotically flat region · strong · GA §29.1 p.298 (pdf 315)

The Penrose tower is narrated as a traveller's itinerary, making abstract causal structure into a journey with irreversible steps.

**Where it breaks down:** The eternal maximally extended solution is not what forms in realistic collapse, and inner-horizon instability likely seals the tunnel.

**App idea:** Choose-your-path game on the Penrose tower where each move must stay inside the light cone and the goal is to reach a new region I.

### The ergosphere behaves toward standing still the way the Schwarzschild interior behaves toward staying at fixed r → spacelike Killing vector inside the ergosphere · strong · GA §29.2 p.303 (pdf 320)

Footnote 11 points out that g_tt flips sign across the stationary limit exactly as it does at r = 2M, so arguments that used the sign flip (Hawking radiation) transfer to the ergosphere.

**Where it breaks down:** At r = 2M the flip coincides with the horizon; in Kerr the flip happens outside the horizon, so escape remains possible.

**App idea:** Split-screen: Schwarzschild on the left and Kerr on the right, shading where ∂_t is timelike or spacelike as the learner moves a probe inward.

### Light rays as a universal speed limit that fences in all motion → coordinate angular velocity of tangential light rays · strong · GA §29.2 p.303 (pdf 320)

If even a photon aimed backward cannot make retrograde progress, no massive body can, however much angular momentum it has.

**Where it breaks down:** Coordinate angular velocity is a statement about the view from infinity; locally every observer still measures light at speed 1.

**App idea:** Two photons launched in opposite directions from a probe point; as the probe moves inward the backward photon's progress slows and stops at r0.

### Swimming in a whirlpool or walking against a fast moving walkway (tutor-suggested, not in the book) → frame dragging · useful · GA §29.2 p.303 (pdf 320)

Gives novices a bodily sense of why, inside the ergosphere, no effort keeps you stationary relative to the distant shore.

**Where it breaks down:** Suggests a medium flowing through space; in GR it is the local light-cone structure itself that tilts, and nothing exerts a drag force.

**App idea:** River-model animation where space is shown flowing and rotating, with a warning overlay that the flow is a coordinate picture.


## Misconceptions addressed

### Charged black holes are an important class of astrophysical objects. · GA §29.1 p.297 (pdf 314)

**Correction:** Any appreciable charge would attract opposite charges from surrounding plasma and be neutralized; Reissner-Nordstrom is studied for its structure, not realism.

**Why tempting:** It is the next exact solution after Schwarzschild and appears prominently in textbooks.

### An electron is a tiny Reissner-Nordstrom black hole, since it is point-like and charged. · GA §29.1 p.298 (pdf 315)

**Correction:** The book notes the description ignores spin; moreover an electron's charge and spin vastly exceed its mass in geometrized units (q/M ~ 10^21, a/M ~ 10^44 by our estimate), so even Kerr-Newman would have no horizon, and quantum effects dominate.

**Why tempting:** A point charge seems like the ideal infinitely dense charged object.

### Every black-hole singularity lies unavoidably in the future of anyone who crosses the horizon, as in Schwarzschild. · GA §29.1 p.298 (pdf 315)

**Correction:** Inside the inner horizon of charged and rotating holes the singularity is timelike, so timelike curves can avoid it; for Reissner-Nordstrom free fall never reaches it.

**Why tempting:** Schwarzschild is the only interior most learners have studied.

### A Kerr black hole has a point singularity at r = 0 like Schwarzschild. · GA §29.2 p.299 (pdf 316)

**Correction:** In Boyer-Lindquist coordinates r = 0 is a disc; the curvature singularity is its rim, a ring of radius a in the equatorial plane, and the disc interior can be crossed to negative r.

**Why tempting:** The symbol r suggests radial distance and r = 0 suggests a point.

### A particle dropped with zero angular momentum falls radially without rotating. · GA §29.2 p.302 (pdf 319)

**Correction:** Its conserved L̃ stays zero but its angular velocity dφ/dt is non-zero because of the off-diagonal inverse metric; it circulates with the hole.

**Why tempting:** In Newtonian gravity and in Schwarzschild, zero angular momentum means purely radial motion.

### With a powerful enough rocket, an observer could hover at any point outside the event horizon. · GA §29.2 p.303 (pdf 320)

**Correction:** Inside the stationary limit the future light cone excludes the world line of rest; no thrust permits a stationary observer there, even though escape outward is still possible.

**Why tempting:** In Schwarzschild the static limit and the horizon coincide at r = 2M, so 'outside the horizon' and 'can hover' feel equivalent.

### The ergosphere is part of the black hole's interior, so anything entering it is lost. · GA §29.2 p.303 (pdf 320)

**Correction:** The ergosphere lies outside r+ and belongs to region I; particles and light can leave it, which is exactly what makes the Penrose process possible.

**Why tempting:** Its cartoon sits snugly around the horizon, and the chapter summary itself mislabels its boundary as an event horizon.

### The Penrose process creates energy from nothing or violates conservation of energy. · GA §29.3 p.305 (pdf 322)

**Correction:** Local 4-momentum and total Killing energy are conserved; the extra energy comes from the hole, whose mass and angular momentum decrease when it swallows a negative-energy, negative-angular-momentum fragment.

**Why tempting:** The escaping fragment has more energy than the incoming particle, which looks like a free lunch.

### Negative Killing energy means a particle has negative energy in its own or any observer's frame. · GA §29.3 p.305 (pdf 322)

**Correction:** Every physical observer still measures positive energy; only the Killing quantity -ξ·p can be negative, because ξ is spacelike in the ergosphere and is not an observer's 4-velocity there.

**Why tempting:** The same letter E is used for Killing energy and measured energy.

### The Kerr metric describes the exterior of any rotating star or planet, just as Schwarzschild describes any spherical one. · GA §29.2 p.299 (pdf 316)

**Correction:** There is no Birkhoff theorem for rotation; a rotating star's exterior has multipole moments that generally differ from Kerr's. Kerr is the unique end state for a rotating black hole.

**Why tempting:** The text calls Kerr the only known exact solution for the region outside a rotating mass.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### The surface r0 is the event horizon of a Kerr black hole. · GA p.305 (pdf 322)

**Correction:** r0 is the stationary limit where g_tt vanishes; the event horizon is at r+, where Δ vanishes, and the two coincide only on the rotation axis.

**Why tempting:** Both are places where a Schwarzschild-trained intuition expects the horizon, and the chapter summary uses this wording.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### A traveller could really use a charged or rotating black hole to reach another universe. · GA §29.1 p.298 (pdf 315)

**Correction:** The multi-universe tower belongs to the idealized eternal solution; realistic collapse does not produce the past regions, and radiation blueshifted at the inner horizon is expected to turn it into a singularity.

**Why tempting:** The book presents the trip enthusiastically as a consequence of the geometry.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*


## Thought experiments

### Journey through the charged black hole · GA §29.1 p.298 (pdf 315)

**Setup:** A traveller falls through r+ of a Reissner-Nordstrom hole, passes r-, steers clear of the timelike singularity and exits through another pair of horizons.

**Lesson:** The singularity is avoidable and the trip ends in a new asymptotically flat region with no route back.

**App idea:** Animated worldline on the Penrose tower with a split view showing the traveller's local sky.

### What the traveller sees at the inner horizon · GA §29.1 p.298 (pdf 315)

**Setup:** An observer crosses r- into region III while light from a whole exterior region streams in behind.

**Lesson:** The complete future history of that exterior can arrive within finite proper time, with unbounded blueshift; distant observers meanwhile see the traveller freeze and redden at r+.

**App idea:** Dual clocks: exterior time racing on a counter while the traveller's proper-time clock advances slowly, with a colour bar for blueshift.

### Dropping a non-rotating particle into a spinning hole · GA §29.2 p.302 (pdf 319)

**Setup:** A particle with zero conserved angular momentum is released far from a Kerr hole.

**Lesson:** It circulates in the rotation sense with angular velocity g^{φt}/g^{tt}, so rotation of the source drags local inertial frames.

**App idea:** 3D trajectory plotter with a spin slider showing the spiral of an L = 0 particle compared with radial infall in Schwarzschild.

### Firing photons forward and backward at the stationary limit · GA §29.2 p.303 (pdf 320)

**Setup:** At a point where g_tt = 0, one photon is sent with the rotation and one against it.

**Lesson:** The backward photon makes no angular progress, so nothing can counter-rotate or remain at rest there.

**App idea:** Probe slider moving inward from large r; two photon markers with speed readouts, the backward one stalling at r0.

### Trying to hover inside the ergosphere · GA §29.2 p.303 (pdf 320)

**Setup:** An observer attempts to stay at fixed r, θ, φ with rockets inside r0.

**Lesson:** The normalization g_tt(u^t)² = -1 cannot be satisfied; the observer must circulate, though escape outward is still allowed.

### Penrose's energy mine · GA §29.3 p.304 (pdf 321)

**Setup:** A body from infinity enters the ergosphere and splits; one fragment is aimed onto a negative-Killing-energy orbit into the hole, the other flies out.

**Lesson:** The escaping fragment can return with more energy than was sent in, paid for by a decrease in the hole's mass and angular momentum.

**App idea:** Interactive energy ledger: set the split direction and fragment speeds inside the ergosphere, see E_bh, L_bh and E_out computed and the hole's M and J update.

### A circulating observer judges the captured fragment · GA §29.3 p.305 (pdf 322)

**Setup:** An observer at fixed r, θ rotating with angular velocity Ω measures the energy of the fragment falling into the hole.

**Lesson:** Requiring positive measured energy for all allowed Ω forces E_bh ≥ Ω L_bh, so negative energy needs negative angular momentum.


## Applications and observations

- **Neutralization of charged astrophysical black holes** (astrophysical-system, GA §29.1 p.297 (pdf 314)): Ambient plasma would preferentially accrete opposite charge, so realistic holes should have negligible q; Reissner-Nordstrom is a theoretical laboratory.
- **Why astrophysical black holes need Kerr** (astrophysical-system, GA §29.2 p.299 (pdf 316)): Stars and collapsing cores rotate, so conservation of angular momentum makes spinning holes generic. Context outside the book: gravitational-wave observations of binary mergers typically find remnant spins a/M around 0.7. Key numbers: Merger remnant spins a/M ~ 0.7 (external context, not in the book)
- **Electron as a would-be charged spinning hole** (numerical-estimate, GA §29.1 p.298 (pdf 315)): Our estimate supporting the book's footnote: in geometrized Gaussian units the electron's charge-to-mass ratio and spin-to-mass ratio are enormous, so no horizon could exist. Key numbers: q/M ≈ 2×10^21; a/M = (ħ/2m_e c)/(Gm_e/c²) ≈ 3×10^44 (our estimates)
- **Horizon size and area for a solar-mass hole** (numerical-estimate, GA §29.2 p.301 (pdf 318)): Using r± and A = 8πMr+ with M = GM_sun/c² ≈ 1.48 km (our numbers). Key numbers: r+ from 2.95 km (a = 0) to 1.48 km (a = M); A from 16πM² ≈ 110 km² to 8πM² ≈ 55 km² (our estimates)
- **Ergosphere extent** (numerical-estimate, GA §29.2 p.303 (pdf 320)): From eqn 29.22 the stationary limit is at r = 2M on the equator for every spin, while r+ shrinks with spin, so the equatorial ergosphere thickness is 2M - r+. Key numbers: Extremal case: thickness M ≈ 1.48 km per solar mass; zero at the poles (our estimate)
- **Energy extraction via the Penrose process** (numerical-estimate, GA §29.3 p.304 (pdf 321)): The book shows extraction is possible and reduces spin. Beyond the book: repeated extraction can at most lower the mass to the irreducible mass (r+² + a²)^{1/2}/2, fixed by the horizon area. Key numbers: Up to 1 - 1/√2 ≈ 29% of the mass of an extremal hole (our estimate, not in the book)

## Historical notes

- **John Wheeler, Jacob Bekenstein:** Wheeler popularized the phrase that black holes have no hair, crediting his student Bekenstein with coining it. — Gave a memorable name to black-hole uniqueness. (GA p.297 (pdf 314))
- **Stephen Hawking, Brandon Carter, Werner Israel:** Established the uniqueness results that justify the no-hair slogan. — Mathematical basis for claiming that M, q and J characterize stationary black holes. (GA p.297 (pdf 314))
- **Hans Reissner, Gunnar Nordstrom, Hermann Weyl, George Barker Jeffery:** Independently found the charged spherically symmetric solution; Reissner was an aeronautical engineer, Nordstrom developed a rival gravity theory, and Jeffery was known for translating relativity papers into English. — Origin of the Reissner-Nordstrom metric, the earliest exact black-hole-type solution in which an electromagnetic field, rather than matter alone, acts as a source of gravity. (GA §29.1 p.297 (pdf 314))
- **Roy Kerr (1963):** Announced the rotating black-hole solution without giving a derivation; Kerr was also an international bridge player for New Zealand. — The astrophysically relevant black-hole metric. (GA p.305 (pdf 322))
- **Roy Kerr, Alfred Schild (1965):** Published a derivation of the rotating solution while cautioning that the calculations were far from simple. — Shows the metric was hard-won, which is why the book quotes it. (GA p.305 (pdf 322))
- **Ezra Ted Newman:** Generalized Kerr's solution to include electric charge (Kerr-Newman). — Completes the three-parameter family predicted by no-hair. (GA §29.2 p.299 (pdf 316))
- **Robert H. Boyer, Richard W. Lindquist (1967):** Introduced the coordinates now standard for Kerr; the paper appeared after Boyer was killed in the 1966 University of Texas tower shooting. — Boyer-Lindquist coordinates make Kerr's Schwarzschild-like structure visible. (GA §29.2 p.299 (pdf 316))
- **Lev Landau, Evgeny Lifshitz (1975):** Remarked in their field-theory text that no physically transparent constructive derivation of Kerr existed and that even checking it is laborious. — Context for the difficulty of the solution. (GA p.305 (pdf 322))
- **Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar:** Gave a physically motivated derivation of Kerr in his black-hole monograph and deposited hundreds of pages of perturbation calculations and notebooks at the University of Chicago library. — Illustrates both that Kerr can be derived with good motivation and the scale of the work involved. (GA p.305 (pdf 322))
- **Roger Penrose:** Proposed extracting energy from a rotating black hole via particle splitting in the ergosphere. — The first concrete demonstration that a spinning hole's rotational energy can in principle be tapped. (Beyond the book: later electromagnetic extraction ideas, such as the Blandford-Znajek mechanism for jets, share this spirit.) (GA §29.3 p.304 (pdf 321))

## Notation and conventions

- **Units:** Geometrized units G = c = 1; M, q and a all have dimensions of length, and J = Ma. — Charge appears as q²/r² in the metric, i.e. Gaussian-type geometrized charge; with SI constants the term is Gq²/(4πε₀c⁴r²). (GA §29.1 p.298 (pdf 315))
- **Metric signature:** (-+++), so timelike vectors have negative norm and u·u = -1. — Sign of g_tt is negative outside the stationary limit and positive inside. (GA §29.2 p.303 (pdf 320))
- **Kerr auxiliary functions:** Δ = r² - 2Mr + a², ρ² = r² + a² cos²θ, Σ² = (r² + a²)² - a²Δ sin²θ, ω = 2Mar/Σ². — Other texts use Σ for ρ² or A for Σ²; check before mixing formulas. (GA §29.2 p.300 (pdf 317))
- **Boyer-Lindquist coordinates:** (t, r, θ, φ) with t, φ adapted to the Killing vectors and r, θ spheroidal rather than spherical. (GA §29.2 p.299 (pdf 316))
- **Horizon and limit radii:** r+ and r- for outer and inner horizons; r0 for the stationary limit. — r0 is not the horizon despite a summary bullet implying it. (GA §29.2 p.303 (pdf 320))
- **Region labels:** I for r > r+, II for r- < r < r+, III for r < r- (extending to negative r in Kerr). (GA §29.1 p.298 (pdf 315))
- **Penrose diagram line styles:** Doubled lines mark r = 0 singularities (Reissner-Nordstrom); dotted lines mark r = r+; dashed lines mark r = r- (the Kerr caption says dot-dashed); i+, i-, i0 and script-I± mark the conformal infinities. — In the Kerr diagram the r = 0 locus in region III is a thin solid curve, not a doubled line, because r = 0 there is not an edge of spacetime. (GA §29.1 p.298 (pdf 315))
- **Conserved quantities:** Tilde quantities Ẽ = -u_t and L̃ = u_φ are per unit mass (from u); untilded E = -ξ·p and L = η·p are for 4-momentum; E^∞ emphasizes energy at infinity. (GA §29.2 p.300 (pdf 317))
- **Killing vector names:** ξ = e_t (stationary) and η = e_φ (axial). (GA §29.2 p.300 (pdf 317))
- **Angular velocities:** ω for the metric's dragging angular velocity; Ω for an observer's chosen dφ/dt; σ = sign(L̃) and b the photon impact parameter. — b is defined in Chapter 24 as L̃/Ẽ, not in this chapter. (GA §29.2 p.304 (pdf 321))
- **Sense of rotation:** a > 0 corresponds to rotation toward increasing φ, making g_tφ negative and the dragging angular velocity positive. (GA §29.2 p.300 (pdf 317))
- **Determinant symbol:** g denotes the metric determinant (Appendix B convention), used in the horizon area integral. — The area integrand should be the square root of the 2D determinant. (GA §29.2 p.301 (pdf 318))

- **29.4-29.5** (standard): Reduce the metric to the outer horizon and integrate to get its area 8πMr+. — *Connects to horizon area as entropy; the printed statements need ρ+² in place of ρ+ and a square root on the determinant.* Skills: induced metric, determinants, surface integrals

- **29.8** (standard): Find the proper time for one equatorial circular orbit at fixed radius with a given coordinate speed in Kerr, by analogy with a Schwarzschild exercise. — *The answer contains a term linear in v and a, so co- and counter-rotating orbiters age differently.* Skills: line element along a prescribed world line, time dilation with a cross term

## Teaching gems

### Justify the scope of black-hole physics with the no-hair slogan before writing any metric: three numbers are all an outsider can ever learn. · GA p.297 (pdf 314)

**Why it works:** Turns a list of exotic solutions into a complete classification, so learners know why charge and spin are the only generalizations worth studying.

**App idea:** Opening card with three dials (M, q, J) that live-update a thumbnail of the horizon structure, with a 'what happened to everything else?' collapse animation.

### Use the unrealistic charged hole as a rehearsal of the two-horizon, three-region causal structure, then show Kerr repeats it with a ring instead of a point. · GA §29.1 p.298 (pdf 315)

**Why it works:** Spherical symmetry keeps the Penrose-diagram reasoning clean, so the conceptual novelty (inner horizon, timelike singularity, onward universes) is learned before the algebraic novelty of rotation.

**App idea:** Toggle between Reissner-Nordstrom and Kerr on the same Penrose canvas with regions coloured identically.

### Prove frame dragging in two lines: set the conserved angular momentum to zero and show dφ/dt = g^{φt}/g^{tt} is still non-zero. · GA §29.2 p.302 (pdf 319)

**Why it works:** Locates the cause precisely in one metric component and contrasts sharply with Newtonian intuition that zero angular momentum means radial fall.

**App idea:** Equation-linked simulation where zeroing g_tφ with a switch makes the infalling particle's spiral straighten into a radial line.

**Why it works:** Leverages the learner's trust in light cones as the speed limit and converts a formula condition into a vivid physical barrier.

## Gaps and pitfalls

- **Eqn 29.3 gives only x and y; the relation z = r cosθ is missing even though the ring-singularity claim uses z = 0.** — Learners cannot verify that ρ = 0 is a ring or that constant-r surfaces are ellipsoids. *Suggestion:* Supply z = r cosθ and show the ellipsoid equation (x² + y²)/(r² + a²) + z²/r² = 1.
- **Eqn 29.6 prints the dr² coefficient as ρ²/Δ²; it should be ρ²/Δ, matching eqn 29.4.** — Exercise 29.3 ('verify eqn 29.6') cannot be completed as printed and may cause learners to doubt correct algebra. *Suggestion:* Flag the typo and have learners find it by comparing with 29.4.
- **Eqns 29.9 and 29.35 write the area integrand as g (the determinant) rather than the square root of the determinant of the horizon 2-metric; Exercise 29.4's eqn 29.34 prints ρ+ instead of ρ+².** — Integrating the determinant gives the wrong dimensions and answer; confusion about area elements. *Suggestion:* Show √g⁽²⁾ = ρ+ · (2Mr+/ρ+) sinθ = 2Mr+ sinθ explicitly, and correct 29.34.
- **The chapter summary says Kerr has an ellipsoidal event horizon at r0, conflating the stationary limit with the horizon; it also calls Reissner-Nordstrom a set of coordinates.** — Reinforces the most common Kerr misconception, that the ergosurface is the horizon. *Suggestion:* Restate: the event horizon is at r+ (Δ = 0); r0 (g_tt = 0) bounds the ergosphere outside it.
- **The text calls r± 'spherical surfaces' shortly after noting that constant-r surfaces in Boyer-Lindquist coordinates are ellipsoids; the horizon's intrinsic 2-geometry (eqn 29.8) is also not round.** — Mixed messages about the shape of a Kerr horizon. *Suggestion:* Distinguish coordinate shape, intrinsic geometry (oblate, equator longer than poles) and topology (a sphere).
- **Frame dragging is described as the particle 'gaining' angular momentum, although its conserved L̃ remains zero.** — Confuses conserved angular momentum with angular velocity and undermines the Killing-vector framework just set up. *Suggestion:* Say the particle acquires angular velocity relative to distant stars while its conserved angular momentum stays zero; connect g^{φt}/g^{tt} to ω in eqn 29.6.
- **The book states Kerr is the only exact solution able to describe the exterior of a rotating mass, with no caveat.** — Learners may assume Kerr describes rotating stars and planets as Birkhoff's theorem allows Schwarzschild to. *Suggestion:* Clarify that Kerr is the unique rotating black-hole solution; rotating stars have different higher multipoles and no simple exterior theorem.
- **Travel through wormholes to new universes and closed timelike curves are presented without noting that the maximal extensions are idealized and the inner horizon is unstable.** — Over-literal belief that black holes are portals; missing a key modern result. *Suggestion:* Add a caution box on the Cauchy-horizon (mass-inflation) instability tied to the infinite blueshift already mentioned.
- **The Penrose process is not quantified: no link to the area theorem of Chapter 28, the irreducible mass, or the maximum extractable fraction.** — Learners do not see why extraction must stop or how it connects to black-hole thermodynamics. *Suggestion:* Use E_bh ≥ Ω_H L_bh at the horizon to derive dM ≥ Ω_H dJ, show it is equivalent to non-decreasing area, and compute the 29% limit for an extremal hole.
- **The photon potential uses b and λ without redefining them; the effective potentials for particles and photons are quoted without the derivation or a plot.** — Hard to interpret prograde versus retrograde photon orbits or find their radii. *Suggestion:* Recall b = L̃/Ẽ from Chapter 24 and plot W_eff for σ = ±1 at a few spins, marking the two photon-orbit radii.
- **Statements that region III holds closed timelike curves and that the Kerr singularity is 'timelike' are given in a sentence without saying where CTCs occur (negative r near the ring, where g_φφ < 0).** — CTCs seem to appear everywhere inside the hole. *Suggestion:* Show g_φφ changes sign for small negative r near θ = π/2, making φ circles timelike.
- **The 'repulsive' singularity claim for Reissner-Nordstrom applies to geodesics of uncharged particles only.** — May conclude nothing can ever hit the singularity. *Suggestion:* Explain via the radial effective potential that the q²/r² term turns infalling geodesics around, while accelerated or charged trajectories can still reach r = 0.
- **Minor typos: 'cures' for curves on p.302, 'E_bf' for E_bh and a doubled 'have' on p.305, and 'Reissner-Nordstöm coordinates' in the summary, where a line element is meant.** — Small distractions; the E_bf typo appears at the key conclusion of Example 29.7. *Suggestion:* Silently correct in derived materials.

## Tutor notes

- Open with: 'Schwarzschild has one number, M. If a collapsing star could leave behind any other measurable property, what would it be?' Guide toward charge and angular momentum and then introduce the no-hair idea.
- Teach the charged hole first as a structural rehearsal; explicitly tell the learner it is not astrophysically realistic so they do not overinvest in it.
- Before showing the Penrose tower, have the learner sketch where g_tt changes sign as r decreases through r+ and r- and predict whether constant-r lines are timelike or spacelike in each region.
- When quoting Kerr, immediately ask the learner to take a -> 0 and r -> ∞ themselves; confidence in a quoted metric comes from limit checks.
- Insist on the three different surfaces: ring singularity (ρ = 0), horizons (Δ = 0), stationary limit (g_tt = 0). A good check question: 'At the equator of an extremal hole, where is each one?' (ring at r = 0, horizon at r = M, stationary limit at r = 2M).
- For frame dragging, ask 'If L = 0, must dφ/dt be zero?' and let the learner discover the role of g^{tφ}; then note that the result equals ω in the rewritten metric.
- Distinguish 'cannot stand still' (inside r0) from 'cannot escape' (inside r+) with the photon argument and the 4-velocity normalization argument; ask which one fails first as you move inward.
- Warn about the chapter-summary slip calling r0 an event horizon and about the printed typos in eqns 29.3, 29.6, 29.9 and 29.34 if the learner is working from the book.
- For the Penrose process, keep a two-column ledger: Killing energy (can be negative inside the ergosphere) versus locally measured energy (always positive). Most confusion comes from merging the two.
- Check understanding with: 'Why can't the Penrose process work in Schwarzschild outside r = 2M?' (ξ is timelike everywhere outside the horizon, so Killing energy is positive.)
- Common learner question: 'Can I really travel to another universe?' Answer: in the idealized eternal solution yes, but realistic collapse and inner-horizon instability make it implausible.
- Common learner question: 'Does the Kerr metric describe the Earth or the Sun?' Only approximately at large distance for the leading mass and spin terms; the exact exterior of a star is not Kerr.
- For advanced learners, extend Example 29.7 to the horizon angular velocity to derive dM ≥ Ω_H dJ and connect it to the area theorem from Chapter 28 and the irreducible mass.
