# SCH ch09 · Fundamentals of gravitational radiation

> Takes the linearized field equations of Chapter 8 into the time-dependent regime: shows that vacuum perturbations are light-speed waves with exactly two transverse-traceless polarizations, explains how they tidally distort free masses and how bars, radar ranging and laser interferometers sense that distortion, derives the slow-motion quadrupole formula for their generation, obtains their energy flux from a Feynman sticky-bead style energy-balance argument, applies the results to the Hulse-Taylor binary pulsar, and ends with chirp-mass scaling and the idea of binaries as standard sirens.

**Pages:** printed 202–266 · pdf 220–284 · **Difficulty:** math 4/5, conceptual 4/5, novice-friendliness 3/5

The propagation part (§9.2) is compact and elegant but assumes fluency with Chapter 8 gauge manipulations and leaves the key TT algebra to exercises. The detection section is long, largely qualitative and very accessible, apart from the radar-ranging calculation. Generation and energy (§§9.4-9.5) are the mathematical peak: Helmholtz integration with Gauss's theorem, tensor virial identity, TT projection, a planar sum with a convergence factor, and angular tensor integrals, with several printed sign and typo slips that can derail a careful reader. §9.6 is algebraically light and conceptually rich. Symbol overloading (xi, phi, R, M, psi) and frequent forward references add friction; the physical interpretation is excellent throughout.

## Role in the book

This is the first of the book's astrophysical chapters in the third edition's reorganized arc. It sits directly after the weak-field chapter because gravitational waves are the simplest departure from special relativity predicted by Einstein's equations: they need only the linearized equations, Lorenz gauge and geodesic deviation already built in Chapters 6-8. The chapter supplies the vocabulary (TT gauge, strain h, polarization, quadrupole luminosity, chirp mass, standard siren) that Chapters 10-11 use when discussing neutron stars and black holes as sources, that Chapter 12 uses to interpret the LIGO-Virgo detections, and that Chapter 13 uses when gravitational-wave distances and the primordial background enter cosmology. It opens with a short framing section recasting general relativity as central to modern astronomy rather than a theory of tiny corrections.

## Learning objectives

- Reader can show that a plane-wave ansatz solves the vacuum linearized equations only if the wave one-form is null, and conclude that gravitational waves travel at light speed without dispersion.
- Reader can use the Lorenz gauge condition and the residual gauge freedom to reduce the wave amplitude to transverse-traceless form, and count the two remaining physical degrees of freedom.
- Reader can explain why free particles stay at fixed TT coordinates yet their proper separation oscillates, and compute the fractional change in separation as one half of h_xx^TT.
- Reader can write the geodesic-deviation equation in a local inertial frame for a wave, express the relevant Riemann components through second time derivatives of h^TT, and add non-gravitational forces to treat bound systems such as rulers and bars.
- Reader can draw the deformation of a ring of test particles for plus and cross polarizations and explain why these are rotated by 45 degrees rather than 90 degrees.
- Reader can derive the forced damped oscillator equation for an idealized bar detector, find its resonant amplitude and energy, and estimate numbers for a Weber bar and the thermal-noise requirement.
- Reader can compute light round-trip times in a gravitational-wave metric to first order, derive the rate of change of return time, and explain why an interferometer does not care whether the light's wavelength is stretched.
- Reader can describe the main noise sources (seismic, thermal, shot) and the frequency bands of ground interferometers, LISA, pulsar timing and CMB polarization, and explain why networks are needed for sky position and polarization.
- Reader can derive the slow-motion quadrupole field of a periodic source from the wave equation using Gauss's theorem, and explain why energy-momentum conservation removes monopole and dipole wave fields.
- Reader can compute quadrupole moments and TT waveforms for a vibrating spring and an equal-mass circular binary, including the polarization seen along and perpendicular to the orbital axis and at general inclination.
- Reader can make order-of-magnitude estimates of wave amplitude (h of order M/r times v^2) and explain why a wave can be detectable when the static Newtonian field of the same source is not.
- Reader can reconstruct the energy-balance argument that fixes the gravitational-wave energy flux F = Omega^2 A^2 / (32 pi), and integrate it over a sphere to obtain the quadrupole luminosity formula L = (1/5) <dddot I_ij dddot I^ij>.
- Reader can estimate the period decay of the Hulse-Taylor binary from the circular-orbit formula and explain why eccentricity raises it by a factor of about 12.
- Reader can define the chirp mass, show that luminosity, energy, chirp time, time to merger and remaining phase all depend on the dimensionless product of chirp mass and orbital frequency, and use this to explain why a binary is a standard siren whose distance follows from amplitude, frequency and frequency drift.
- Reader can explain how cosmological redshift enters observed chirp masses and converts the inferred distance into a luminosity distance.

## Teaching approach

A long chapter that mixes compact tensor derivations with extended, up-to-date instrumentation and astrophysics. Schutz repeatedly derives the same physical fact in two gauges or two ways (coordinate positions versus proper distance; metric integration versus geodesic deviation; energy absorbed by detectors versus amplitude reduction downstream) so that the reader learns to trust only invariant, measurable statements. Detectors are used as a pedagogical device, not just as hardware: the bar oscillator is first a detector, then a radiator, then the device that fixes the energy flux before dropping out. The final section leans on dimensional analysis and scale-freedom to turn the quadrupole formula into observational tools (chirp mass, standard sirens).

**Style:** physical-motivation-first, derivation-heavy, observational-astrophysics, geometric-first, thought-experiment, analogy-driven, example-driven, historical-narrative

**Narrative arc**

1. Frame: general relativity is not just tiny corrections; gravitational waves, neutron stars and black holes are now observed, and waves are the simplest non-Newtonian prediction.
2. Solve the vacuum linearized wave equation with a plane wave; nullity of k gives light-speed propagation and no dispersion.
3. Spend the residual gauge freedom to reach TT gauge; only two amplitudes survive.
4. Paradox: a free particle stays at fixed TT coordinates. Resolve by computing the proper distance between two particles, which does oscillate.
5. Re-derive the effect gauge-invariantly with geodesic deviation in a local inertial frame; reinterpret as a tidal force that can be added to other forces, answering why rulers do not stretch.
6. Picture the two polarizations with a ring of particles; exhibit an exact nonlinear plane wave and state how short waves propagate through curved spacetime.
7. Survey detection: frequency bands and detector families; analyze an idealized bar as a forced damped oscillator with realistic Weber-bar numbers and noise limits.
8. Compute radar round-trip times in the wave metric, derive the return-time derivative and the three-term relation, and build the interferometer as two perpendicular beam detectors; discuss LIGO optics, noise, networks, future ground detectors and LISA.
9. Close detection with the cosmic-sound analogy: coherent, phase-sensitive, nearly omnidirectional reception.
10. Generation: crude amplitude bound M/R, then slow-motion solution of the sourced wave equation via Gauss's theorem, conservation laws, tensor virial identity, quadrupole field and TT projection.
11. Apply to a vibrating spring (hopeless laboratory source) and to a circular binary (circular versus linear polarization, inclination dependence, Hulse-Taylor amplitude); make order-of-magnitude arguments and note spherical motions do not radiate; state the retarded-integral generalization.
12. Energy: Feynman sticky-bead motivation, sheet of oscillators absorbs energy and re-radiates a wave that interferes destructively downstream; equate to get F; generalize and integrate to the quadrupole luminosity; upper luminosity c^5/G.
13. Check against nature: period decay of the Hulse-Taylor pulsar, including the eccentricity correction and 0.17 percent agreement.
14. Standard sirens: unequal masses, chirp mass as the only scale, scaling laws for chirp time, merger time, remaining phase and luminosity; distance from amplitude; redshifted masses and luminosity distance; eccentricity decay.

**Signature moves**

- Pose an apparent paradox created by a coordinate choice (particles at rest in TT gauge; coordinate light speed not equal to 1) and dissolve it by computing an invariant.
- Derive a result twice by independent routes and point out that the agreement is a consistency check (Eq. 9.24 versus 9.28; Eq. 9.45 via proper length versus geodesic deviation).
- Recycle one toy system through the whole chapter: the two-mass spring is detector (§9.3), emitter (§9.4) and energy-flux calibrator (§9.5).
- Insist that each metric component be treated as a scalar function when solving the wave equation, bringing tensor structure back only when conservation laws are applied.
- Use geometrized units throughout and convert back only at the end, advertising that luminosity is dimensionless and c^5/G is the natural unit.
- Quote realistic engineering and observational numbers (Weber bar mass and Q, 4-km arms, Q of 2 x 10^9 for suspensions, 2.5 million km LISA arms, measured period derivative) to calibrate intuition.
- Extract physics from dimensional analysis and scale-freedom (h ~ phi_0 phi_r; L depends only on the remaining number of orbits).
- Use sound as the governing analogy for gravitational-wave astronomy (listening, chirps, sirens, microphones).

## Section by section

### §9.1 The role of general relativity in the physical Universe — p.202 (pdf 220)

A framing essay. Einstein saw the theory mainly as a fix for the conflict between special relativity and Newtonian gravity, doubted that black holes were physical and that gravitational waves were real or detectable. Schutz contrasts this with the modern situation: black holes and neutron stars are common, waves have been detected, and two Nobel prizes (binary pulsar 1993, LIGO 2017) have gone to gravitational-wave science. The section lays out the plan for Chapters 9-13.

**Concepts:** gravitational waves, Hulse-Taylor binary pulsar

**Key moves**
- Replace the picture of GR as tiny corrections with GR as an observational science.
- Name Einstein's worry that waves might be coordinate artifacts, a worry the chapter will answer with invariant measurements.
- Motivate waves as the simplest non-Newtonian consequence of the field equations.

### §9.2 The propagation of gravitational waves — p.203 (pdf 221)

Solves the vacuum linearized equations with a complex plane wave, finds the wave vector must be null (light-speed propagation, no dispersion) and the amplitude orthogonal to it (Lorenz gauge). A second gauge transformation generated by a plane-wave vector field imposes tracelessness and orthogonality to a chosen four-velocity, the TT gauge, leaving only A_xx = -A_yy and A_xy for a wave along z. Free particles stay at fixed TT coordinates, but the proper distance between neighbours oscillates by a fraction h_xx^TT/2. The same effect is recovered from geodesic deviation in a local inertial frame, where the Riemann components are minus one half of second time derivatives of h^TT; this turns the wave into a tidal force that can be combined with other forces, explaining why rigid rulers barely respond. The two polarizations are drawn as elliptical deformations of a particle ring rotated 45 degrees apart.

**Concepts:** plane gravitational wave, null wave vector, dispersion relation, Lorenz gauge, residual gauge freedom, transverse-traceless gauge, plus and cross polarization, TT coordinates and free particles, proper-distance strain, geodesic deviation in a gravitational wave, tidal force of a gravitational wave, stretching of space and rigid rulers

**Key moves**
- Substitute exp(i k.x) into the wave equation: eta^{mu nu} k_mu k_nu = 0.
- Gauge condition becomes A^{alpha beta} k_beta = 0.
- Choose xi_alpha = B_alpha exp(i k.x) to set trace and A_{alpha beta} U^beta to zero; count remaining freedom.
- In the rest frame of U with k along z, only xx, yy = -xx, xy survive.
- Initial acceleration -Gamma^alpha_00 vanishes in TT gauge, so coordinate positions never change.
- Proper distance along x is (1 + h_xx^TT/2) epsilon: invariant effect proportional to separation.
- Geodesic deviation in local inertial frame: d^2 xi^j/dt^2 = -R^j_{0k0} xi^k with R_{x0x0} = -h_xx,00^TT/2.
- Add F/m terms to the deviation equation to treat non-free bodies.

### Exact plane wave and waves in curved spacetime (end of §9.2) — p.210 (pdf 228)

Using null coordinates u = t - z and v = t + z, the metric -du dv + f(u)^2 dx^2 + g(u)^2 dy^2 is an exact vacuum solution provided f''/f + g''/g = 0; for g close to 1 it reduces to the linear plus-polarized wave, and free particles still stay at fixed coordinates. The subsection on geometrical optics states without derivation that short-wavelength waves on a curved background follow null geodesics and parallel-transport their polarization, like light, can be lensed, but are almost unaffected by matter, making them uniquely penetrating messengers.

**Concepts:** exact plane wave (pp-wave), null coordinates, geometrical optics for gravitational waves, gravitational lensing

**Key moves**
- Guess a metric depending only on u that stretches only transverse directions.
- Compute Christoffel symbols and R^x_{uxu} = -f''/f; vacuum equation is a single ODE.
- Free function g(u) mirrors the arbitrary waveform of the linear theory.
- State the two short-wavelength conditions (short compared with curvature radius and with background timescale).

### §9.3 The detection of gravitational waves — p.213 (pdf 231)

Argues that the gravitational-wave spectrum is a new window, lists detector families and their bands (ground bars and interferometers above about 1 Hz, LISA in the millihertz band, spacecraft tracking, pulsar timing at nanohertz, CMB polarization at 1e-16 Hz). Models a bar detector as two masses on a damped spring in TT coordinates, deriving a forced damped oscillator driven by l_0 h_xx,00^TT/2, then finds the steady resonant amplitude and energy and plugs in Weber-bar numbers (amplitude of order 1e-15 m, energy of order 1e-20 J). Discusses thermal noise and cryogenics, pendulum isolation, transducers and the quantum limit. Then computes photon round-trip times in the wave metric, obtains the return-time derivative and the three-term formula, and explains interferometers as two beam detectors in which one arm acts as the clock for the other. Describes Fabry-Perot arm cavities, power recycling, suspension Q, shot noise, the 20 Hz-2 kHz band, networks for localization and polarization, third-generation plans, LISA's time-delay interferometry and drag-free proof masses, and closes with the cosmic-sound analogy.

**Concepts:** gravitational-wave spectrum and detector bands, resonant bar detector, quality factor, thermal noise, seismic isolation, quantum limit and squeezed light, radar ranging of proper distance, return-time response of a beam detector, beam detectors, laser interferometer detector, Fabry-Perot arm cavities and power recycling, shot noise, detector network localization, LISA, cosmic sound analogy, cosmological gravitational-wave background

**Key moves**
- Newton's law holds in a local inertial frame that coincides with TT coordinates to first order for slow motions.
- Spring force depends on proper length l = (x2 - x1)(1 + h_xx^TT/2).
- Eliminate coordinate separation to get xi'' + 2 gamma xi' + omega_0^2 xi = l_0 h_xx,00^TT / 2.
- Resonance: R = l_0 A Q/2 and E = m l_0^2 Omega^2 A^2 Q^2 / 16.
- Photon coordinate speed (1 + h_+)^{-1/2} along x; integrate with unperturbed path inside h_+.
- Differentiate return time with respect to emission time: only h at emission and at return matter (for normal incidence).
- Interferometer compares two return times, so laser wavelength changes are irrelevant.
- Plus wave overhead stretches one arm and squeezes the other; cross wave overhead gives no signal.

### Interferometers, networks, future detectors and cosmic sound (§9.3 continued) — p.222 (pdf 240)

Spacecraft tracking as the simplest beam detector and its limits (plasma, clock stability near 1e-19 would miss amplitudes of 1e-20); the interferometer as clock-free comparison of two arms; LIGO's first detection on 14 September 2015 and the binary neutron star of 17 August 2017; noise budget and data analysis ('LIGO is a 1e-22 detector'); networks of three or more detectors for triangulation and two for polarization when position is known; LIGO-India, KAGRA, Einstein Telescope, Cosmic Explorer, LISA (three spacecraft, 2.5e6 km arms, 60-degree opening angle, transponded beams, TDI, drag-free operation proven by LISA Pathfinder), DECIGO for the decihertz gap; analogy with acoustics.

**Concepts:** beam detectors, laser interferometer detector, detector network localization, LISA, time-delay interferometry, drag-free operation, cosmic sound analogy

**Key moves**
- Three-term formula lets a true wave be distinguished from local plasma noise because it appears three times with a sign flip.
- Arrival-time delays between two sites define a ring on the sky; a third detector cuts it to two patches.
- Inclination follows from the degree of circular polarization.
- Gravity has one sign of charge, so bulk mass motions add coherently, as pressure changes do for sound.

### §9.4 The generation of gravitational waves — p.230 (pdf 248)

Starts with the bound h of order M/R for a source of mass M at distance R, applied to a supernova black hole and to GW150914 (implying 10 percent-level metric distortion near the merger). Solves the sourced wave equation for a sinusoidal, compact, slow-moving source: outside the source the outgoing solution is A e^{i Omega r}/r; integrating over a small ball with Gauss's theorem gives A = 4 J with J the integrated source. Conservation of T^{mu nu} forces the time-dependent J^{mu 0} to vanish, and the tensor virial identity turns J_ij into second time derivatives of the mass quadrupole, giving the quadrupole approximation. Projection to TT gauge along z gives the waveform in terms of the reduced quadrupole. Examples: vibrating spring (radiates at omega and 2 omega, no radiation along its axis, laboratory amplitude negligibly small) and equal-mass circular binary (frequency 2 omega, circular polarization along the axis, linear in the plane, general inclination formula, Hulse-Taylor numbers). Order-of-magnitude reasoning gives h ~ phi_0 phi_r and explains why wave tidal forces beat static Newtonian tides by (phi_0 r/R)^2; spherically symmetric motion does not radiate; a rotating square shows the danger of trusting the leading-order formula. Closes with the retarded-integral generalization to arbitrary time dependence.

**Concepts:** slow-motion approximation, outgoing spherical wave, quadrupole moment tensor, tensor virial identity, absence of monopole and dipole gravitational radiation, quadrupole approximation, reduced quadrupole moment, TT projection of spherical waves, circular and elliptical polarization, orbital inclination, order-of-magnitude wave amplitude, no gravitational radiation from spherically symmetric motion, retarded solution

**Key moves**
- Treat each component of h-bar as an independent scalar solving the wave equation.
- Integrate (nabla^2 + Omega^2) B = -16 pi S over a ball of radius epsilon << wavelength; the Omega^2 term is negligible and the Laplacian becomes a surface flux -4 pi A.
- Use T^{mu nu},nu = 0 and Gauss's theorem to show J^{mu 0} = 0 for Omega not zero.
- Use d^2/dt^2 of integral T^00 x^l x^m = 2 integral T^lm to write h-bar_jk = -2 Omega^2 D_jk e^{...}/r.
- TT-project along the propagation direction: h_xx^TT = -Omega^2 (I_xx - I_yy) e/r, h_xy^TT = -2 Omega^2 I_xy e/r.
- For the binary, the i in I_xy produces a quarter-period lag: circular polarization along the axis.
- Virial theorem v^2 ~ phi_0 converts M v^2/r into phi_0 phi_r.

### §9.5 The energy carried away by gravitational waves — p.239 (pdf 257)

Recalls the historical confusion over whether waves carry energy and Feynman's sticky-bead resolution. A plane of damped oscillators at areal density sigma absorbs power m gamma <xi'^2> per oscillator from an incident plus wave. Each oscillator also radiates via the quadrupole formula; summing over the plane with a convergence factor gives a net plane wave that, after TT projection, reduces the downstream amplitude by 2 pi sigma m Omega l_0 R sin phi. Dividing the power lost by the amplitude change eliminates all oscillator parameters, yielding dF/dA = Omega^2 A/(16 pi) and hence F = Omega^2 A^2/(32 pi) = Omega^2 <h_mu nu^TT h^TT mu nu>/(32 pi). Schutz stresses that this assumes energy conservation and is valid only in linearized theory. Evaluating F on a sphere with the angular integrals of n^i n^j and n^i n^j n^k n^l gives L = (1/5) Omega^6 <I_ij I^ij>, or L = (1/5) <dddot I_ij dddot I^ij> in general; order-of-magnitude scaling L ~ phi_0^5 and an absolute bound L of order c^5/G.

**Concepts:** Feynman sticky-bead argument, energy flux of a gravitational wave, radiation reaction by destructive interference, quadrupole luminosity formula, maximum luminosity c^5/G, Isaacson stress-energy tensor, localization of gravitational energy

**Key moves**
- Energy absorbed per oscillator: <dE/dt> = m gamma Omega^2 R^2 / 2.
- Field of one oscillator from Eq. 9.93 with A -> R/2; integrate over the plane using varpi d varpi = r dr and a damping factor exp(-epsilon r).
- Superposing with the incident wave leaves a slightly phase-shifted downstream wave whose amplitude is lower by 2 pi sigma m Omega l_0 R times sin phi.
- Divide delta F by delta A, eliminate R and phi with Eqs. 9.48-9.49: oscillator properties cancel.
- Write the flux covariantly under background Lorentz transformations and apply to any small patch of wavefront.
- Replace the special z direction by n^j and integrate over angles using symmetric-tensor identities.
- Radiation energy within radius R gives Newtonian potential of order L, which cannot exceed about 1 without forming a black hole.

### An example: the Hulse-Taylor binary pulsar (end of §9.5) — p.245 (pdf 263)

Applies the luminosity formula to an equal-mass circular binary: L = (8/5) M^2 l_0^4 omega^6 = 4.0 (M omega)^{10/3}. With PSR B1913+16 numbers (P = 27907 s, M = 1.4 solar masses = 2.07 km) the circular-orbit luminosity is 1.71e-29 in geometrized units and the orbital energy -1.11e-3 m. Logarithmic differentiation of E(omega) gives dP/dt of about -2.0e-13; the eccentricity e = 0.617 raises the loss rate about twelvefold. The GR prediction -2.40263e-12 agrees with the observed -(2.398 +/- 0.004)e-12 to 0.17 percent.

**Concepts:** Hulse-Taylor binary pulsar, orbital decay by gravitational radiation, quadrupole luminosity formula, geometrized units

**Key moves**
- Write the Newtonian binding energy of the circular orbit first with the orbital radius, then eliminate the radius with Kepler's law so it scales as the 5/3 power of mass times the 2/3 power of orbital frequency.
- d ln E = (2/3) d ln omega = -(2/3) d ln P, and dE/dt = -L.
- dP/dt = 3PL/(2E) is dimensionless in any unit system.
- Explain the factor 12 by the steep omega^6 dependence at periastron.

### §9.6 Standard sirens — p.247 (pdf 265)

Generalizes binary radiation to unequal masses and finds that amplitudes depend on the masses only through mu M^{2/3}, which defines the chirp mass. Written with the chirp mass, waveform, luminosity (32/5)(M omega)^{10/3} and energy -(1/2) M (M omega)^{2/3} show that the chirp mass is the only scale, a manifestation of the scale-free character of gravity. Energy balance gives the chirp mass from omega and its time derivative, and scaling laws for chirp time, time to merger (3/8 of the chirp time), remaining orbital phase, and a mass-independent luminosity L = 1/(160 psi^2). Because amplitude, frequency and frequency drift are measured, the distance follows: binaries are standard sirens, with inclination supplied by polarization measurements across a network. Redshift multiplies observed masses by (1+z) and makes the distance a luminosity distance, opening a route to the Hubble-Lemaitre constant. Eccentricity is radiated away faster than the orbit shrinks, so ground-based sources are nearly circular.

**Concepts:** chirp mass, scale-free nature of gravity, chirp time, time to merger, remaining orbital phase and mass-independent luminosity, standard siren, luminosity distance, redshifted chirp mass, circularization by gravitational radiation, post-Newtonian corrections

**Key moves**
- Replace m by 2 mu and eliminate l_0 with Kepler's law to get amplitudes proportional to mu M^{2/3} omega^{2/3}.
- Double chirp mass, halve frequency, double distance and time: identical signal.
- Solve dE/dt = -L for omega-dot: omega-dot = (96/5) M^{-2} (M omega)^{11/3}.
- Integrate dt/d omega and d psi/d omega to infinity for merger time and phase.
- Combine L(M omega) with psi(M omega) to eliminate the mass.
- Distance r = alpha M (M omega)^{2/3}/h with alpha an O(1) orientation factor.
- Frequencies and their derivatives redshift, so the inferred chirp mass is (1+z) times the source-frame value.

## Concepts

### gravitational waves

*phenomenon · core* · also: gravitational radiation, ripples in spacetime

Propagating, time-dependent perturbations of spacetime curvature that travel at the speed of light, are sourced by accelerating non-spherical mass-energy distributions, and carry energy and angular momentum away from their sources. In linearized theory they are wavelike vacuum solutions for the metric perturbation.

**How introduced:** Framed historically (Einstein's 1916 prediction and later doubts) and then obtained as the non-static solutions of the vacuum weak-field equations that Chapter 8 had set aside when taking the Newtonian limit.

**Prerequisites:** linearized Einstein equations, metric perturbation, trace-reversed perturbation

$$

### plane gravitational wave

*solution-or-model · core* · also: monochromatic plane wave solution

The complex solution h-bar^{alpha beta} = A^{alpha beta} exp(i k_mu x^mu) of the vacuum linearized equations with constant amplitude tensor and constant real wave one-form; the physical field is its real part, and by Fourier analysis every vacuum solution in Lorenz gauge is a superposition of such waves.

**How introduced:** Posed as an ansatz for the three-dimensional wave equation; substituting shows what k must satisfy, and the gauge condition then constrains A.

**Prerequisites:** gravitational waves, Lorenz gauge, one-form

\bar h^{\alpha\beta}=A^{\alpha\beta}\exp(i k_\alpha x^\alpha)

**Notes:** Footnote 1 explains the complex-exponential convention; see also Exercise 9.3 on Fourier superposition.

### null wave vector

*mathematical-object · core* · also: wave four-vector, null one-form k

The wave one-form k_alpha of a plane wave, whose associated vector obeys eta^{mu nu} k_mu k_nu = 0; its time component is the angular frequency omega and its spatial part the wave vector. Nullity means a photon moving along k stays at constant wave phase, so the wave propagates at light speed in the direction of k.

**How introduced:** Derived from requiring the plane-wave ansatz to satisfy the wave equation, then interpreted by following a photon on x^mu = k^mu lambda + l^mu and noting its phase k_mu x^mu is constant.

**Prerequisites:** plane gravitational wave, null vector

k^\nu k_\nu=0

\vec k\to(\omega,\mathbf k)

*convention · revisited* · also: Lorentz gauge, harmonic gauge, de Donder gauge

The coordinate condition that the divergence of the trace-reversed perturbation vanishes, h-bar^{alpha beta}_{,beta} = 0; it turns the linearized field equations into flat-space wave equations and, for a plane wave, forces the amplitude to be orthogonal to the wave vector.

### residual gauge freedom

*principle · developed* · also: gauge freedom within the Lorenz class

Infinitesimal coordinate changes generated by a vector field xi with box xi = 0 keep a perturbation in Lorenz gauge; for plane waves choosing xi_alpha = B_alpha exp(i k.x) shifts A by terms built from B and k, and the four constants B_alpha can be used to impose further conditions.

**How introduced:** Introduced by recalling Eq. 8.38 and choosing a gauge vector with the same null k as the wave, then dividing out the common exponential to get an algebraic change of amplitude.

**Prerequisites:** Lorenz gauge, gauge transformation in linearized theory

$$
A^{(\rm NEW)}_{\alpha\beta}=A^{(\rm OLD)}_{\alpha\beta}-iB_\alpha k_\beta-iB_\beta k_\alpha+i\eta_{\alpha\beta}B^\mu k_\mu

**Notes:** Exercise 9.5 shows only three of the four conditions A_{alpha beta}U^beta = 0 are independent, so trace plus these use up all four B's, and that TT gauge cannot be reached for static (omega = 0) fields.

**Where:** SCH §9.2 p.204 (pdf 222); SCH §9.2 p.205 (pdf 223)

### transverse-traceless gauge

*convention · core* · also: TT gauge, TT coordinates

The gauge in which, besides the Lorenz condition, the wave amplitude is traceless and orthogonal to a chosen constant four-velocity U. In the rest frame of U with propagation along z the surviving entries are A_xx, its negative in the yy slot, and the symmetric off-diagonal pair A_xy; the trace-reversed and ordinary perturbations coincide, and no gauge freedom remains, so the two surviving components are physical.

**How introduced:** Built by spending the residual freedom on trace and U-orthogonality, then going to U's rest frame and aligning z with the wave; 'transverse' is explained by the vanishing of all components along the propagation direction.

**Prerequisites:** residual gauge freedom, Lorenz gauge, null wave vector

$$
A^\alpha{}_\alpha=0
$$

$$
A_{\alpha\beta}U^\beta=0
$$

$$
\bar h^{\rm TT}_{\alpha\beta}=h^{\rm TT}_{\alpha\beta}

### plus and cross polarization

*definition · core* · also: + and x polarizations, h_+ and h_x, linear polarization states of gravitational waves

The two independent linear polarization states of a gravitational wave: h_+ = h_xx^TT = -h_yy^TT stretches and squeezes a ring of free particles along the x and y axes, while h_x = h_xy^TT does the same along the diagonals. The two patterns are rotated by 45 degrees relative to each other, as expected for a symmetric rank-2 field, in contrast with the 90-degree separation of electromagnetic polarizations.

**How introduced:** Shown with Figure 9.1: a ring of particles around a central one is deformed into ellipses that alternate orientation every half cycle; Exercises 9.13-9.16 make the 45-degree rotation and orthogonality precise.

**Prerequisites:** transverse-traceless gauge, proper-distance strain

$$
h_+=h^{\rm TT}_{xx}=-h^{\rm TT}_{yy},\quad h_\times=h^{\rm TT}_{xy}
$$

**Notes:** The book uses the symbol h_+ (Eq. 9.58) and the words '+' and 'x' polarization in the Figure 9.1 caption; it does not introduce a separate h_x symbol.

### TT coordinates and free particles

*principle · developed* · also: free particles remain at rest in TT gauge, comoving TT coordinates

In TT gauge adapted to a particle's initial rest frame, Gamma^alpha_00 vanishes identically, so a free particle initially at rest keeps constant coordinates as the wave passes. The coordinates simply follow free particles; constancy of coordinate position therefore carries no invariant information about the wave.

**How introduced:** Obtained from the geodesic equation by evaluating the initial acceleration, then iterating the argument in time; immediately flagged as a coordinate statement so the reader does not conclude the wave has no effect.

**Prerequisites:** transverse-traceless gauge, geodesic equation, Christoffel symbols

$$
\left(\frac{dU^\alpha}{d\tau}\right)_0=-\Gamma^\alpha{}_{00}=-\tfrac12\eta^{\alpha\beta}(h_{\beta0,0}+h_{0\beta,0}-h_{00,\beta})=0
$$

**Where:** SCH §9.2 p.206 (pdf 224)

### proper-distance strain

*physical-quantity · core* · also: strain, fractional length change h/2, stretching of proper separation

The fractional change of the proper distance between free particles caused by a wave: for particles on the x axis at coordinate separation epsilon, Delta l = (1 + h_xx^TT/2) epsilon to first order. The change is proportional to the separation and to amplitudes of order 1e-21 or less, which is why detectors are kilometres long.

**How introduced:** Computed directly by integrating sqrt(g_xx) between the two coordinate-fixed particles; used as the first invariant evidence that the wave is physical.

**Prerequisites:** TT coordinates and free particles, metric perturbation, proper distance

$$
\Delta l\approx\left[1+\tfrac12 h^{\rm TT}_{xx}(x=0)\right]\varepsilon
$$

**Where:** SCH §9.2 p.206 (pdf 224); SCH §9.2 p.207 (pdf 225)

### geodesic deviation in a gravitational wave

*law · core* · also: Riemann tensor as measurable tidal field, tidal acceleration of a gravitational wave

In a local inertial frame attached to one freely falling particle, the proper components of the separation vector to a nearby free particle obey d^2 xi^j/dt^2 = -R^j_{0k0} xi^k, with R_{x0x0} = -h_xx,00^TT/2, R_{y0x0} = -h_xy,00^TT/2 and R_{y0y0} = -R_{x0x0} for a wave along z. The Riemann tensor is thus measurable by watching separations of nearby geodesics.

**How introduced:** Motivated by the need for coordinates tied to measurement: in the local inertial frame the second covariant derivative along the geodesic becomes an ordinary second derivative, and the four-velocity can be taken at zeroth order because R is already first order.

**Prerequisites:** geodesic deviation equation, Riemann curvature tensor, local inertial frame, gauge invariance of the linearized Riemann tensor

$$
\frac{d^2\xi^\alpha}{d\tau^2}=R^\alpha{}_{\mu\nu\beta}U^\mu U^\nu\xi^\beta

$$
R_{x0x0}=-\tfrac12 h^{\rm TT}_{xx,00}

### gauge invariance of the linearized Riemann tensor

*theorem · revisited* · also: Riemann tensor unchanged by infinitesimal coordinate changes

Under the gauge transformations of linearized theory the first-order Riemann tensor does not change, so its components computed in TT gauge equal those in a local inertial frame; statements expressed through R and proper lengths are therefore gauge-free.

**How introduced:** Invoked to justify evaluating R in the simple TT gauge while interpreting the deviation equation in the local inertial frame, and to reinterpret xi^alpha as proper lengths.

**Prerequisites:** Riemann curvature tensor, gauge transformation in linearized theory

### tidal force of a gravitational wave

*physical-quantity · core* · also: gravitational-wave force, effective tidal force

The effective force per unit mass -R^i_{0j0} xi^j that a wave exerts on one body relative to a freely falling reference body, measured in the reference body's local inertial frame. Being a force, it adds to non-gravitational forces, so relative motion obeys xi-double-dot = -R xi + F_B/m_B - F_A/m_A.

**How introduced:** Introduced as the advantage of the differential (deviation) form over the integrated proper-distance form: only the former can include springs, electric binding or friction.

**Prerequisites:** geodesic deviation in a gravitational wave, Newton's second law

$$
\frac{\partial^2\xi^i}{\partial t^2}=-R^i{}_{0j0}\xi^j+\frac{F^i_B}{m_B}-\frac{F^i_A}{m_A}
$$

**Where:** SCH §9.2 p.209 (pdf 227)

### stretching of space and rigid rulers

*principle · developed* · also: why rulers do not stretch, stretching of space

Saying a wave 'stretches space' means proper separations of free bodies change. **How introduced:** Posed as a common student question (a ruler is mostly empty space, so why would it not stretch too?) and answered with the forced deviation equation; followed by the preferable alternative of timing light between the free masses.

### circular and elliptical polarization

### exact plane wave (pp-wave)

*solution-or-model · introduced* · also: plane-fronted wave with parallel rays, pp-wave, nonlinear plane wave

### geometrical optics for gravitational waves

*principle · introduced* · also: short-wavelength approximation, eikonal limit

When a wave's wavelength is short compared with the curvature radius of the background and its period short compared with the timescale of background change, it travels along null geodesics of the background and parallel-transports its polarization tensor, exactly as light does. **How introduced:** Raised as the practical question of how waves reach us through the fields of stars and galaxies; answered qualitatively by analogy with electromagnetic waves in media, without derivation.

### resonant bar detector

*experiment-or-observation · developed* · also: Weber bar, resonant mass detector

### return-time response of a beam detector

### laser interferometer detector

A detector that splits stable laser light into two perpendicular arms ending on suspended, effectively free mirrors, and recombines the returning beams so that the output at the dark port tracks the difference of round-trip times. ### LISA

### quadrupole moment tensor

*physical-quantity · core* · also: mass quadrupole moment, second moment of the mass distribution, I^{lm}

The tensor I^{lm} = integral of T^00 x^l x^m d^3x, the second moment of the energy (for slow sources, mass) distribution about the origin, defined without removing the trace and without a factor of 3 (the MTW convention).

**How introduced:** Emerges when the tensor virial identity rewrites the integrated stresses J_ij as half the second time derivative of this integral.

**Prerequisites:** stress-energy tensor, tensor virial identity

$$
I^{lm}:=\int T^{00}x^lx^m\,d^3x
$$

**Notes:** The book warns conventions differ between texts; many use a trace-free version with a factor 3.

**Where:** SCH §9.4 p.233 (pdf 251)

### tensor virial identity

*identity · developed* · also: tensor virial theorem

For a conserved stress-energy tensor with compact support, the second time derivative of the integral of T^00 x^l x^m equals twice the integral of T^lm, linking internal stresses to the changing mass quadrupole.

**How introduced:** Quoted without proof (with a mis-numbered exercise pointer) to convert the spatial source integral J_ij into the second time derivative of a mass moment.

**Prerequisites:** conservation of stress-energy, quadrupole moment tensor

$$
\frac{d^2}{dt^2}\int T^{00}x^lx^m\,d^3x=2\int T^{lm}\,d^3x
$$

**Notes:** The text attributes this identity to 'Exercise 9.24', but Exercise 9.24 in this chapter asks for Eq. 9.53; the pointer is a cross-reference slip, and the identity itself follows from T^{mu nu}_{,nu} = 0 by two integrations by parts.

**Where:** SCH §9.4 p.233 (pdf 251)

### absence of monopole and dipole gravitational radiation

*principle · core* · also: no monopole or dipole gravitational waves, conservation laws suppress low multipoles

Because T^{mu nu}_{,nu} = 0, the total energy and momentum of an isolated source are constant, so the 1/r parts of h-bar^{mu 0} are time independent and carry no wave; the leading wave field comes from the time-varying mass quadrupole. This parallels the way charge conservation forbids electromagnetic monopole radiation.

**How introduced:** Derived for a sinusoidal source by differentiating J^{mu 0} in time, using the conservation law and Gauss's theorem on a surface outside the source; later generalized to arbitrary time dependence with the retarded integral.

**Prerequisites:** conservation of stress-energy, slow-motion approximation

$$
J^{\mu0}=0,\ \bar h^{\mu0}=0\ (\Omega\neq0)
$$

$$
\int T_{0\mu}\,d^3y={\rm const}
$$

**Where:** SCH §9.4 p.233 (pdf 251); SCH §9.4 p.238 (pdf 256)

### quadrupole approximation

*law · core* · also: quadrupole formula for the wave field, leading-order radiation field

The far-zone, slow-motion wave field h-bar_jk = (2/r) d^2 I_jk/dt^2 evaluated at retarded time t - r (for a sinusoidal source, -2 Omega^2 D_jk e^{i Omega (r - t)}/r), neglecting 1/r^2 terms and 1/r terms of higher order in source velocity.

**How introduced:** Assembled from the ball-integration result h-bar = 4J e^{i Omega (r-t)}/r, the vanishing of J^{mu 0}, and the tensor virial identity; justified for Newtonian-gravity-bound sources only by reference to the literature.

**Prerequisites:** tensor virial identity, absence of monopole and dipole gravitational radiation, outgoing spherical wave

$$
\bar h_{jk}=-2\Omega^2D_{jk}e^{i\Omega(r-t)}/r
$$

$$
\bar h_{jk}(t,x^i)=\frac{2}{r}\ddot I_{jk}(t-r)
$$

**Notes:** Signs as printed in Eqs. 9.83 and 9.106 are not mutually consistent; see gaps_and_pitfalls.

**Where:** SCH §9.4 p.232 (pdf 250); SCH §9.4 p.233 (pdf 251); SCH §9.4 p.238 (pdf 256)

### reduced quadrupole moment

*physical-quantity · core* · also: trace-free quadrupole moment, slashed I

The trace-free part of the mass quadrupole, I-slash_jk = I_jk - (1/3) delta_jk I^l_l. The TT wave field and the radiated power depend only on it, so spherically symmetric mass distributions (with I proportional to delta) do not contribute.

**How introduced:** Appears when the quadrupole field is put into TT gauge along the propagation direction.

**Prerequisites:** quadrupole moment tensor

$$
\mathcal I_{jk}=I_{jk}-\tfrac13\delta_{jk}I^l{}_l
$$

**Notes:** Printed as an I with a horizontal bar through it; rendered here as calligraphic I.

**Where:** SCH §9.4 p.234 (pdf 252); SCH §9.5 p.244 (pdf 262)

### TT projection of spherical waves

*operation · developed* · also: transverse-traceless projection operator

Converting a Lorenz-gauge radiation field into TT gauge for propagation direction n by projecting transversely with P_ij = delta_ij - n_i n_j and removing the transverse trace: h^TT_ij = P_i^k P_j^l h-bar_kl - (1/2) P_ij P^kl h-bar_kl. For n along z this gives h_xx^TT = (h-bar_xx - h-bar_yy)/2 and h_xy^TT = h-bar_xy.

**How introduced:** Stated as possible (Exercise 9.32) and applied along z; the projector form is given in Exercise 9.33 and used to obtain radiation in arbitrary directions.

**Prerequisites:** transverse-traceless gauge, quadrupole approximation

$$
\bar h^{\rm TT}_{ij}=P^k{}_iP^l{}_j\bar h_{kl}-\tfrac12P_{ij}\left(P^{kl}\bar h_{kl}\right)
$$

**Where:** SCH §9.4 p.233 (pdf 251); SCH §9.4 p.234 (pdf 252); SCH p.261 (pdf 279)

### orbital inclination

*physical-quantity · developed* · also: inclination angle iota

The angle iota between the normal to a binary's orbital plane and the line of sight. The plus amplitude scales as (1 + cos^2 iota) and the cross amplitude as 2 cos iota, so a face-on orbit emits circularly polarized waves toward us and an edge-on orbit linearly polarized waves; the ratio of polarizations measures iota, which must be known to convert amplitude into distance.

**How introduced:** Given as the general-inclination binary formula (derived in Exercise 9.38) and used in the network and standard-siren discussions.

**Prerequisites:** circular and elliptical polarization, quadrupole approximation

$$
\bar h_{xx}\propto 1+\cos^2\iota,\quad \bar h_{xy}\propto 2\cos\iota
$$

**Where:** SCH §9.3 p.227 (pdf 245); SCH §9.4 p.236 (pdf 254); SCH §9.6 p.248 (pdf 266); SCH §9.6 p.251 (pdf 269)

### order-of-magnitude wave amplitude

*law · core* · also: h ~ phi_0 phi_r, amplitude estimate M v^2 / r

A source of mass M, size R and internal speed v produces h of order M v^2/r at distance r; for self-gravitating motion v^2 ~ phi_0 (the internal potential), so h ~ phi_0 phi_r with phi_r = M/r, and the absolute ceiling for fully relativistic sources is about M/r.

**How introduced:** First as a crude ceiling at the start of §9.4 (metric perturbation of order 1 at distance M, falling as 1/r), then from D_jk ~ M R^2 in the quadrupole field combined with the virial theorem.

**Prerequisites:** quadrupole approximation, virial theorem

$$
h\sim\phi_0\phi_r
$$

**Notes:** Also shows the wave tidal force exceeds the static Newtonian tidal force by (phi_0 r/R)^2 and that the Newtonian description of a dynamic source's field fails beyond r ~ R/phi_0.

**Where:** SCH §9.4 p.230 (pdf 248); SCH §9.4 p.231 (pdf 249); SCH §9.4 p.237 (pdf 255)

### no gravitational radiation from spherically symmetric motion

*principle · developed* · also: spherical collapse does not radiate, Birkhoff consequence for radiation

Purely spherically symmetric mass motions emit no gravitational waves at any order. In the quadrupole approximation this follows because the quadrupole of a spherical distribution is proportional to delta_jk and its trace-free part vanishes; the full statement follows from Birkhoff's theorem, and in linear theory from the retarded integral.

**How introduced:** Presented as a reason the amplitude estimate is optimistic for realistic collapses; the caution that a leading-order argument is not a proof leads to the example of a rotating square of four masses, which has constant quadrupole yet radiates at higher order.

**Prerequisites:** reduced quadrupole moment, absence of monopole and dipole gravitational radiation

**Where:** SCH §9.4 p.237 (pdf 255); SCH §9.4 p.238 (pdf 256)

### retarded solution

*solution-or-model · developed* · also: retarded integral, retarded Green's function solution

The outgoing solution of the sourced wave equation, h-bar_mu nu(t, x) = 4 times the integral of T_mu nu(t - |x - y|, y)/|x - y| over the source, i.e. an integral over the past light cone; far away it reduces to (4/r) times the source integral at retarded time t - r.

**How introduced:** Quoted from standard wave theory as the exact solution that generalizes the sinusoidal treatment to arbitrary time dependence.

**Prerequisites:** outgoing spherical wave

$$
\bar h_{\mu\nu}(t,x^i)=4\int\frac{T_{\mu\nu}(t-R,y^i)}{R}\,d^3y,\quad R=|x^i-y^i|
$$

**Where:** SCH §9.4 p.238 (pdf 256)

### Feynman sticky-bead argument

*historical-idea · core* · also: sticky bead thought experiment

Feynman's 1957 argument that gravitational waves carry energy: if tidal motion of beads on a rod with friction produces heat, the energy must come from the wave. Schutz turns it into a calculation by replacing beads with damped oscillators and assuming energy conservation.

**How introduced:** Used to motivate §9.5 and credited in the bibliography to the Chapel Hill conference; the oscillators later drop out of the flux formula.

**Prerequisites:** tidal force of a gravitational wave, resonant bar detector

**Where:** SCH §9.5 p.239 (pdf 257); SCH §9.7 p.255 (pdf 273)

### energy flux of a gravitational wave

*physical-quantity · core* · also: gravitational-wave energy flux, wave intensity

The period-averaged energy crossing unit area per unit time, F = Omega^2 A^2/(32 pi) for a linearly polarized plane wave of amplitude A, or F = Omega^2 <h^TT_mu nu h^TT mu nu>/(32 pi); in general (1/32 pi) times the average of the squared time derivatives summed over TT components. It is invariant under background Lorentz transformations but not under gauge changes, and is meaningful only in linearized theory as an average.

**How introduced:** Fixed by demanding that the power absorbed by a sheet of oscillators equals the loss of flux implied by the reduced downstream amplitude; the ratio dF/dA contains no oscillator properties.

**Prerequisites:** Feynman sticky-bead argument, radiation reaction by destructive interference, transverse-traceless gauge

$$
F=\frac{1}{32\pi}\Omega^2A^2
$$

$$
F=\frac{1}{32\pi}\Omega^2\langle\bar h^{\rm TT}_{\mu\nu}\bar h^{\rm TT\,\mu\nu}\rangle
$$

**Where:** SCH §9.5 p.240 (pdf 258); SCH §9.5 p.241 (pdf 259); SCH §9.5 p.243 (pdf 261)

### radiation reaction by destructive interference

*phenomenon · developed* · also: forward-scattering extinction, downstream amplitude reduction

A detector driven by a wave also radiates; the portion emitted straight downstream has the incident frequency and a phase such that it interferes destructively with the incident wave, reducing its amplitude by exactly the amount that accounts for the energy the detector absorbs. Emission in other directions cancels among neighbouring oscillators in a plane array.

**How introduced:** Introduced by noticing the inconsistency of treating the detector as a test body, pictured in Figures 9.5-9.6, and computed by summing quadrupole fields over a plane with a convergence factor (footnote 4 explains why only the downstream direction matters).

**Prerequisites:** quadrupole approximation, resonant bar detector

$$
\delta A=-2\pi\sigma m\Omega l_0R\sin\phi
$$

**Where:** SCH §9.5 p.239 (pdf 257); SCH §9.5 p.240 (pdf 258); SCH §9.5 p.241 (pdf 259); SCH §9.5 p.242 (pdf 260); SCH §9.5 p.243 (pdf 261)

### quadrupole luminosity formula

*law · core* · also: Einstein quadrupole formula, quadrupole formula for radiated power

The total power radiated by a slow-motion source, L = (1/5) <third time derivative of I-slash_ij times that of I-slash^ij> in geometrized units (G/5c^5 in SI), reducing to L = (1/5) Omega^6 <I-slash_ij I-slash^ij> for sinusoidal motion.

**How introduced:** Obtained by writing the flux on the z axis in terms of the reduced quadrupole, replacing the z direction by the radial unit vector, and integrating over a sphere using the symmetric angular averages of n^i n^j and n^i n^j n^k n^l.

**Prerequisites:** energy flux of a gravitational wave, reduced quadrupole moment, TT projection of spherical waves

$$
L=\tfrac15\Omega^6\langle\mathcal I_{ij}\mathcal I^{ij}\rangle
$$

$$
L=\tfrac15\langle\dddot{\mathcal I}_{ij}\dddot{\mathcal I}^{ij}\rangle
$$

**Notes:** Order of magnitude L ~ phi_0^2 v^6, at most ~phi_0^5.

**Where:** SCH §9.5 p.244 (pdf 262); SCH §9.5 p.245 (pdf 263)

### maximum luminosity c^5/G

*principle · developed* · also: Planck luminosity, Dyson luminosity bound

No source can radiate much more than c^5/G, about 3.6e52 W (luminosity 1 in geometrized units): radiation of luminosity L crossing a region of size R contains energy of order LR, whose own Newtonian potential is of order L, and a potential well above 1 would form a black hole trapping the radiation.

**How introduced:** Obtained first from scaling of the quadrupole formula, then justified by the energy-of-radiation argument, and compared with quasars and gamma-ray bursts (below 1e-10) and black hole mergers (about 1e-3 to 1e-2).

**Prerequisites:** quadrupole luminosity formula, geometrized units

$$
L\lesssim1=c^5/G\approx3.6\times10^{52}\ {\rm W}
$$

**Where:** SCH §9.5 p.245 (pdf 263); SCH §9.6 p.250 (pdf 268)

### localization of gravitational energy

*principle · introduced* · also: nonlocality of gravitational energy

In general relativity the energy of the gravitational field cannot be assigned to points in a coordinate-independent way; near flat spacetime and to lowest order in h, averaged conserved quantities can be defined, and the wave flux is the unique expression consistent with energy conservation at that order.

**How introduced:** Raised as an objection a careful reader would make, recalling §7.3; Schutz turns the derivation around to argue the constructed flux is the only acceptable definition within linear theory.

**Prerequisites:** energy flux of a gravitational wave

**Where:** SCH §9.5 p.243 (pdf 261); SCH §9.5 p.244 (pdf 262)

### orbital decay by gravitational radiation

*phenomenon · core* · also: binary inspiral, period decrease of a radiating binary

A bound binary that loses energy at rate L becomes more tightly bound, speeding up and shortening its period: for a circular Newtonian orbit dP/dt = 3PL/(2E), a dimensionless number independent of units. The effect has been measured for binary pulsars in agreement with general relativity.

**How introduced:** Posed as how long radiation takes to change the orbit; obtained by logarithmic differentiation of E(omega) and dE/dt = -L.

**Prerequisites:** quadrupole luminosity formula, Kepler's third law

$$
\frac{1}{E}\frac{dE}{dt}=\frac23\frac1\omega\frac{d\omega}{dt}=-\frac23\frac1P\frac{dP}{dt}
$$

$$
\frac{dP}{dt}=\frac{3PL}{2E}
$$

**Where:** SCH §9.5 p.245 (pdf 263); SCH §9.5 p.246 (pdf 264); SCH §9.5 p.247 (pdf 265)

### Hulse-Taylor binary pulsar

*experiment-or-observation · developed* · also: PSR B1913+16, binary pulsar

A double neutron star system containing a radio pulsar, discovered in 1974, with orbital period about 7 h 45 min, eccentricity 0.617 and component masses near 1.4 solar masses. The measured decrease of its orbital period, -(2.398 +/- 0.004)e-12, matches the quadrupole-formula prediction -2.40263e-12 to 0.17 percent, the first strong (indirect) evidence for gravitational radiation.

**How introduced:** Named in the opening Nobel-prize framing, used for the amplitude estimate in §9.4, and analyzed quantitatively at the end of §9.5.

**Prerequisites:** orbital decay by gravitational radiation, pulsar

### geometrized units

*convention · revisited* · also: G = c = 1 units

Units with G = c = 1 in which masses and times are lengths (1.4 solar masses is 2.07 km; the Hulse-Taylor period is 8.37e12 m) and luminosity is dimensionless, with conversion factor c^5/G = 3.63e52 W.

### chirp mass

*physical-quantity · core* · also: script M

The combination M_chirp = mu^{3/5} M^{2/5} = (m1 m2)^{3/5}/(m1 + m2)^{1/5} of a binary's masses, the only mass parameter that enters the leading-order gravitational waveform amplitude, luminosity and frequency evolution; it can be read from the signal through omega and its time derivative.

**How introduced:** Discovered by rewriting unequal-mass amplitudes: they depend only on mu M^{2/3}, which motivates the definition; named for its role in the frequency sweep.

**Prerequisites:** reduced mass, quadrupole luminosity formula, Kepler's third law

$$
\mathcal M:=\mu^{3/5}M^{2/5}=\frac{m_1^{3/5}m_2^{3/5}}{M^{1/5}}
$$

$$
\mathcal M=\left(\frac{5}{96}\right)^{3/5}\dot\omega^{3/5}\omega^{-11/5}
$$

**Notes:** The Newtonian energy is also a function of the chirp mass alone, yet the quantity only became important through gravitational-wave physics.

**Where:** SCH §9.6 p.248 (pdf 266); SCH §9.6 p.249 (pdf 267)

### scale-free nature of gravity

*principle · core* · also: absence of intrinsic scale in general relativity, mass scaling of binary signals

Vacuum general relativity in geometrized units contains no preferred mass, length or time, so a binary with all masses multiplied by a factor produces the same waveform stretched in time by that factor and the same amplitude at a distance scaled by that factor; every dimensionless quantity of a circular inspiral depends only on the product of chirp mass and orbital frequency.

**How introduced:** Announced at the start of §9.6 and demonstrated by writing amplitudes, luminosity, energy and phase in terms of chirp mass and (M omega).

**Prerequisites:** chirp mass, geometrized units

**Where:** SCH §9.6 p.247 (pdf 265); SCH §9.6 p.248 (pdf 266); SCH §9.6 p.250 (pdf 268)

### chirp time

*physical-quantity · developed* · also: frequency evolution timescale, tau_chirp

The instantaneous timescale omega/omega-dot on which a binary's orbital frequency changes by order unity: (5/96) M_chirp (M_chirp omega)^{-8/3}, shrinking rapidly as the orbit tightens.

**How introduced:** Obtained from the chirp-mass form of energy balance; named after the rising-frequency 'chirp' of the signal.

**Prerequisites:** chirp mass, orbital decay by gravitational radiation

$$
\tau_{\rm chirp}:=\frac{\omega}{\dot\omega}=\frac{5}{96}\mathcal M(\mathcal M\omega)^{-8/3}

### remaining orbital phase and mass-independent luminosity

*law · core* · also: phase to merger, Forward-Berman luminosity relation

The orbital phase still to accumulate before merger is psi_merger = (1/32)(M_chirp omega)^{-5/3}, and eliminating (M_chirp omega) gives L = 1/(160 psi_merger^2): a circular binary's gravitational luminosity depends only on how many orbits it has left, whatever its masses.

*definition · core* · also: gravitational-wave standard candle, self-calibrating distance indicator

A source whose intrinsic gravitational-wave luminosity can be inferred from its own signal, so that the measured amplitude yields its distance. L=4\pi r^2F

r=\alpha\mathcal M(\mathcal M\omega)^{2/3}/h

### redshifted chirp mass

Because a detector measures only times, a source moving away with redshift z shows frequency and frequency drift reduced as if its chirp mass were (1 + z) times larger; the same holds for component masses, so gravitational waves alone cannot separate mass from redshift.

**How introduced:** Derived by inspection of the chirp-mass formula once one notes that all binary observables are time measurements affected by redshift.

### cosmological gravitational-wave background

*phenomenon · mention* · also: primordial gravitational waves, stochastic background from the Big Bang

A random, statistically superposed field of gravitational waves left over from the very early Universe, expected to have a roughly flat spectrum across frequencies and very small present-day amplitude. ## Key equations

### (9.1) Vacuum linearized field equation · central · SCH §9.2 p.203 (pdf 221)

$$
\left(-\frac{\partial^2}{\partial t^2}+\nabla^2\right)\bar h^{\alpha\beta}=0
$$

Far from sources and in Lorenz gauge, every component of the trace-reversed perturbation obeys the flat-space wave equation.

**Symbols:** h-bar^{alpha beta}: trace-reversed metric perturbation; c = 1.

### (9.2) Plane-wave ansatz · central · SCH §9.2 p.203 (pdf 221)

$$
\bar h^{\alpha\beta}=A^{\alpha\beta}\exp(i k_\alpha x^\alpha)
$$

Trial solution with constant complex amplitude and constant real wave one-form; physical field is the real part.

**Symbols:** A^{alpha beta}: amplitude tensor; k_alpha: wave one-form.

### (9.3)-(9.4) Wave operator on a plane wave · derivation-step · SCH §9.2 p.203 (pdf 221)

$$
\eta^{\mu\nu}\bar h^{\alpha\beta}{}_{,\mu\nu}=0,\qquad \bar h^{\alpha\beta}{}_{,\mu}=i k_\mu\bar h^{\alpha\beta}
$$

Each derivative of the exponential brings down i k, so the wave equation becomes algebraic.

### (9.5) Null wave vector · central · SCH §9.2 p.203 (pdf 221)

$$
\eta^{\mu\nu}k_\mu k_\nu=k^\nu k_\nu=0
$$

The plane wave solves the vacuum equation only if k is null: gravitational waves move at the speed of light.

### (9.6)-(9.7) Phase surfaces and frequency · supporting · SCH §9.2 p.204 (pdf 222)

$$
k_\alpha x^\alpha=k_0t+\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x={\rm const},\qquad \vec k\to(\omega,\mathbf k)
$$

Wave fronts are hypersurfaces of constant phase; the time component of k is the angular frequency.

**Symbols:** omega = k^0

### (9.8)-(9.9) Photon rides the wave · derivation-step · SCH §9.2 p.204 (pdf 222)

$$
x^\mu(\lambda)=k^\mu\lambda+l^\mu\ \Rightarrow\ k_\mu x^\mu(\lambda)=k_\mu l^\mu={\rm const}
$$

A photon moving along k keeps constant wave phase because k.k = 0.

**Symbols:** lambda: affine parameter; l^mu: initial position.

### (9.10) Dispersion relation · supporting · SCH §9.2 p.204 (pdf 222)

$$
\omega^2=|\mathbf k|^2
$$

Phase and group velocity both equal 1; no dispersion.

### (9.11)-(9.12) Lorenz gauge and transversality to k · central · SCH §9.2 p.204 (pdf 222)

$$
\bar h^{\alpha\beta}{}_{,\beta}=0\ \Rightarrow\ A^{\alpha\beta}k_\beta=0
$$

The gauge condition needed for Eq. 9.1 makes the amplitude orthogonal to the wave vector.

### (9.13)-(9.14) Residual gauge generator · derivation-step · SCH §9.2 p.204 (pdf 222)

$$
\left(-\partial_t^2+\nabla^2\right)\xi_\alpha=0,\qquad \xi_\alpha=B_\alpha\exp(ik_\mu x^\mu)

Binding energy of the equal-mass circular orbit; -1.11e-3 m for Hulse-Taylor numbers.

**Symbols:** r = l_0/2: orbital radius.

## Worked examples

### In-text calculation §9.2: plane-wave solution of the vacuum equations · intro · SCH §9.2 p.203 (pdf 221)

**Problem:** Find conditions under which a complex plane wave solves the vacuum linearized equations in Lorenz gauge.

**Method:** Differentiate the exponential to replace derivatives by i k, reduce the wave equation to an algebraic condition, then apply the gauge condition to the amplitude; interpret constant-phase surfaces using a photon world line.

**Key insight:** Derivatives on a plane wave are algebraic, so nullity of k and transversality of A drop out immediately.

**Result:** k must be null (omega^2 = |k|^2) and A^{alpha beta} k_beta = 0.

**Concepts:** plane gravitational wave, null wave vector, Lorenz gauge

### In-text calculation §9.2: reduction to TT gauge · standard · SCH §9.2 p.205 (pdf 223)

**Problem:** Use the remaining gauge freedom to simplify the plane-wave amplitude as far as possible.

**Method:** Generate a gauge change with a plane-wave vector of the same k, write the amplitude shift, impose trace-free and U-orthogonality (details in Exercise 9.5), then go to U's rest frame with k along z and read off surviving components.

**Key insight:** Four gauge parameters remove exactly the non-physical components; what survives cannot be transformed away and is physical.

**Result:** Only A_xx = -A_yy and A_xy remain (Eq. 9.21).

**Concepts:** residual gauge freedom, transverse-traceless gauge

### In-text calculation §9.2: free particles and proper distance · intro · SCH §9.2 p.206 (pdf 224)

**Problem:** Determine how a passing wave affects free particles initially at rest.

**Method:** Evaluate the geodesic equation's initial acceleration in TT gauge, find it zero, then integrate sqrt(g_xx) between two coordinate-fixed particles.

**Key insight:** Coordinate positions are fixed by the gauge choice; only the invariant proper distance reveals the wave.

**Result:** Delta l = (1 + h_xx^TT/2) epsilon (Eq. 9.24).

**Concepts:** TT coordinates and free particles, proper-distance strain

### In-text calculation §9.2: geodesic deviation and Riemann components · standard · SCH §9.2 p.208 (pdf 226)

**Problem:** Describe the relative acceleration of nearby free particles in a gauge-invariant way.

**Method:** Work in the local inertial frame of one particle so the covariant derivative becomes ordinary; keep U at zeroth order; evaluate R in TT gauge using gauge invariance; specialize to x- and y-separated pairs.

**Key insight:** Relative accelerations measure Riemann directly and match the proper-distance result, and the deviation form admits other forces.

**Result:** d^2 xi^i/dt^2 = -R^i_{0j0} xi^j with R_{x0x0} = -h_xx,00^TT/2 (Eqs. 9.26-9.29).

**Concepts:** geodesic deviation in a gravitational wave, gauge invariance of the linearized Riemann tensor, tidal force of a gravitational wave

### In-text calculation §9.2: exact nonlinear plane wave · standard · SCH §9.2 p.211 (pdf 229)

**Problem:** Find an exact vacuum solution resembling the linear plane wave.

**Method:** Adopt null coordinates, guess a metric depending only on u with transverse scale factors, compute Christoffel symbols and Riemann, impose Ricci flatness, and linearize.

**Key insight:** The nonlinear theory admits the same freedom of an arbitrary waveform; the linear wave is its weak limit.

**Result:** f''/f + g''/g = 0; for g = 1 + epsilon, f is approximately 1 - epsilon.

**Concepts:** exact plane wave (pp-wave), null coordinates

### In-text calculation §9.3: bar detector response equation · standard · SCH §9.3 p.216 (pdf 234)

**Problem:** Derive the equation of motion for the stretch of a two-mass spring hit by a wave along z.

**Method:** (1) Newton's law in a local inertial frame at one mass equals TT form to first order; (2) spring force uses proper length l = (x2 - x1)(1 + h_xx^TT/2); (3) define xi = l - l_0, invert for x2 - x1, and subtract the two equations.

**Key insight:** The wave enters only through the proper-length correction, yet the result is the tidal forcing of Eq. 9.28a, a consistency check.

**Result:** xi'' + 2 gamma xi' + omega_0^2 xi = l_0 h_xx,00^TT / 2 (Eq. 9.45).

**Concepts:** resonant bar detector, tidal force of a gravitational wave

### In-text calculation §9.3: resonant response and Weber-bar numbers · standard · SCH §9.3 p.218 (pdf 236)

**Problem:** Find the steady response and energy of a bar driven on resonance, and evaluate for a Weber bar with A = 1e-20.

**Method:** Solve the driven oscillator; time-average kinetic plus potential energy; set omega_0 = Omega; express in Q; insert m = 1.4e3 kg, l_0 = 1.5 m, omega_0 = 1e4 per second, Q = 1e5.

**Key insight:** Even an optimistic wave gives a nucleus-sized displacement and energy comparable with room-temperature thermal noise.

**Result:** R about 1e-15 m, E about 1e-20 J (Eqs. 9.54-9.57).

**Concepts:** resonant bar detector, quality factor, thermal noise

### In-text calculation §9.3: radar round trip in a wave metric · standard · SCH §9.3 p.221 (pdf 239)

**Problem:** Compute the proper time for light to go from one free mass to another at coordinate distance L and back, and its rate of change.

**Method:** Null condition gives coordinate speed; integrate dt = (1 + h_+)^{1/2} dx replacing t(x) in h_+ by its flat value; add the return leg; differentiate with respect to t_start so each integral of a derivative collapses to endpoint values.

**Key insight:** Differentiating the return time eliminates the integrals, so the response depends on the wave only at emission and reception (for perpendicular incidence).

**Result:** dt_return/dt_start = 1 + [h_+(t_start + 2L) - h_+(t_start)]/2 (Eq. 9.62).

**Concepts:** radar ranging of proper distance, return-time response of a beam detector

### In-text calculation §9.4: amplitude ceiling examples · intro · SCH §9.4 p.231 (pdf 249)

**Problem:** Estimate the largest plausible amplitude from a supernova forming a 10 solar mass black hole at 1e23 m, and from a pair of 30 solar mass black holes at 1e25 m.

**Method:** Use h of order M/R, with masses converted to metres.

**Key insight:** Comparing the ceiling (1e-20) with the observed 1e-21 for GW150914 shows the metric near the merger was distorted by roughly 10 percent.

**Result:** About 1e-17 and 1e-20 respectively.

**Concepts:** order-of-magnitude wave amplitude, geometrized units

### In-text calculation §9.4: slow-motion quadrupole field · challenging · SCH §9.4 p.231 (pdf 249)

**Problem:** Solve the sourced wave equation for a small, sinusoidally oscillating source.

**Method:** Factor out e^{-i Omega t}; outside, take the outgoing 1/r solution; integrate the Helmholtz equation over a ball of radius epsilon with Gauss's theorem; drop the Omega^2 term; use conservation to kill J^{mu 0}; apply the tensor virial identity; TT-project.

**Key insight:** Indices are spectators until conservation laws are used; then energy-momentum conservation leaves the quadrupole as the leading radiator.

**Result:** h-bar_jk = -2 Omega^2 D_jk e^{i Omega (r-t)}/r and TT components in terms of the reduced quadrupole (Eqs. 9.74-9.87).

**Concepts:** slow-motion approximation, quadrupole approximation, absence of monopole and dipole gravitational radiation, tensor virial identity

### In-text calculation §9.4: radiation from a vibrating spring · standard · SCH §9.4 p.234 (pdf 252)

**Problem:** Find the waves from two masses oscillating along x about separation l_0 and estimate a laboratory generator.

**Method:** Compute I_xx, split into omega and 2 omega parts, form the reduced quadrupole, evaluate Eqs. 9.85-9.86 along z and (after permuting axes) along x; insert m = 1e3 kg, l_0 = 1 m, A = 1e-4 m, omega = 1e4 per second.

**Key insight:** Radiation is linearly polarized along the spring and vanishes along its axis; laboratory sources are hopelessly weak.

**Result:** h_xx^TT = -[2 m omega^2 l_0 A cos omega(r-t) + 4 m omega^2 A^2 cos 2 omega(r-t)]/r; book quotes about 1e-34/r (our arithmetic gives about 2e-37/r).

**Concepts:** quadrupole approximation, reduced quadrupole moment

### In-text calculation §9.4: equal-mass circular binary · standard · SCH §9.4 p.235 (pdf 253)

**Problem:** Find the gravitational waves from two stars of mass m in a circular orbit of separation l_0.

**Method:** Kepler's law for omega; positions give I_ij oscillating at 2 omega; complex reduced quadrupole with a factor i in I_xy; evaluate along the orbital axis and in the plane; quote the general inclination form.

**Key insight:** The i in I_xy means the two polarizations are a quarter cycle apart face-on (circular), while edge-on only one survives (linear).

**Result:** Amplitude 2 m l_0^2 omega^2/r at frequency 2 omega, of order (m omega)^{2/3} m/r (Eqs. 9.98-9.100); about 1e-20 quoted for PSR B1913+16.

**Concepts:** circular and elliptical polarization, orbital inclination, quadrupole approximation

### In-text calculation §9.4: wave force versus Newtonian tidal force · standard · SCH §9.4 p.237 (pdf 255)

**Problem:** Explain why a distant source's waves can be detectable although its static gravity is not.

**Method:** Compare the wave tidal acceleration h l_0 omega^2 with the Newtonian tidal acceleration phi_r l_0/r^2, using h ~ phi_0 phi_r and omega ~ v/R.

**Key insight:** Waves fall off as 1/r while tides fall as 1/r^3, so the ratio grows as (phi_0 r/R)^2; this also marks where a Newtonian description of the field breaks down (r ~ R/phi_0).

**Result:** Factor about 1e22 for phi_0 = 0.1, R = 1e11 m, r = 1e23 m.

**Concepts:** order-of-magnitude wave amplitude, tidal force of a gravitational wave

### In-text calculation §9.5: energy flux from a sheet of oscillators · challenging · SCH §9.5 p.240 (pdf 258)

**Problem:** Determine the energy flux carried by a plane gravitational wave without field-theoretic energy definitions.

**Method:** Compute average power absorbed per oscillator; sum the quadrupole fields of a plane of oscillators with density sigma using a convergence factor; TT-project; superpose with the incident wave to find the amplitude reduction; divide flux change by amplitude change using the steady-state R and phi; integrate in A.

**Key insight:** Assuming only energy conservation, all detector parameters cancel, leaving a property of the wave itself.

**Result:** F = Omega^2 A^2/(32 pi) = Omega^2 <h^TT h^TT>/(32 pi) (Eqs. 9.121-9.123).

**Concepts:** energy flux of a gravitational wave, radiation reaction by destructive interference, Feynman sticky-bead argument

### In-text calculation §9.5: total luminosity of a quadrupole source · challenging · SCH §9.5 p.244 (pdf 262)

**Problem:** Integrate the flux of a general slow-motion source over a sphere.

**Method:** Write F on the z axis in terms of the reduced quadrupole, use trace-freeness to isolate z-dependence, replace z by n^j, integrate using symmetric angular tensor identities.

**Key insight:** Symmetry alone fixes the angular integrals, so the total power reduces to a single contraction of the quadrupole.

**Result:** L = (1/5) Omega^6 <I_ij I^ij>, generally (1/5) <dddot I_ij dddot I^ij> (Eqs. 9.131-9.133).

**Concepts:** quadrupole luminosity formula, TT projection of spherical waves

### In-text calculation §9.5: maximum luminosity argument · intro · SCH §9.5 p.245 (pdf 263)

**Problem:** Show that no source can have luminosity much above c^5/G.

**Method:** Radiation flux L/R^2 through a region of size R implies energy density at least of that order and total radiation energy at least LR; its Newtonian potential is at least L; potentials above 1 mean a black hole.

**Key insight:** The bound applies to all radiation, not just gravitational, and is a pure consequence of units and horizon formation.

**Result:** L of order 1 = c^5/G, about 3.6e52 W.

**Concepts:** maximum luminosity c^5/G

### In-text calculation §9.5: Hulse-Taylor period decay · standard · SCH §9.5 p.245 (pdf 263)

**Problem:** Estimate the rate of change of the Hulse-Taylor binary period assuming a circular orbit.

**Method:** Luminosity from the quadrupole formula (8/5) M^2 l_0^4 omega^6 with l_0 eliminated; Newtonian energy -M^2/(4r); log-differentiate E(omega); set dE/dt = -L; evaluate in metres.

**Key insight:** Working in geometrized units makes dP/dt manifestly dimensionless; eccentricity raises the loss about twelvefold via the omega^6 dependence near periastron.

**Result:** L = 1.71e-29, E = -1.11e-3 m, dP/dt about -2.0e-13 (circular) versus -2.40e-12 with eccentricity and observed -(2.398 +/- 0.004)e-12.

**Concepts:** orbital decay by gravitational radiation, Hulse-Taylor binary pulsar, geometrized units

### In-text calculation §9.6: chirp-mass scaling relations · standard · SCH §9.6 p.248 (pdf 266)

**Problem:** Express the waveform, energy, and evolution of an unequal-mass circular binary in terms of observables.

**Method:** Replace m by 2 mu and l_0 by (M/omega^2)^{1/3}; identify the chirp mass; energy balance dE/dt = -L gives omega-dot; form chirp time; integrate dt/d omega and d psi/d omega to infinity; eliminate (M omega) between L and psi.

**Key insight:** Every dimensionless observable depends only on the chirp mass times the orbital frequency, so the signal is self-calibrating.

**Result:** M_chirp = (5/96)^{3/5} omega-dot^{3/5} omega^{-11/5}; tau_merger = (3/8) tau_chirp; L = 1/(160 psi^2) (Eqs. 9.153-9.160).

**Concepts:** chirp mass, scale-free nature of gravity, chirp time, time to merger, remaining orbital phase and mass-independent luminosity

### In-text calculation §9.6: distance to a standard siren · standard · SCH §9.6 p.251 (pdf 269)

**Problem:** Infer the distance of an inspiralling binary from its gravitational-wave signal.

**Method:** From omega and omega-dot get the chirp mass; from network polarization get inclination; amplitude formula (9.152) inverted gives r; include redshift so masses become (1+z) times source values and r a luminosity distance.

**Key insight:** Gravity supplies the intrinsic luminosity through the chirp, the way a known bulb wattage calibrates a standard candle.

**Result:** r = alpha M_chirp (M_chirp omega)^{2/3}/h with M_obs = (1+z) M_true (Eqs. 9.162-9.163).

**Concepts:** standard siren, redshifted chirp mass, luminosity distance, orbital inclination


## Analogies and intuitions

### Gravitational-wave detectors as microphones; the waves as cosmic sound → cosmic sound analogy · strong · SCH §9.3 p.229 (pdf 247)

Both record the phase of long-wavelength waves, cannot focus on a direction, locate sources with arrays and phase delays, and receive coherent emission from bulk motions; hence the vocabulary of listening, chirps and sirens.

**Where it breaks down:** Sound needs a medium and travels slowly; gravitational waves are transverse quadrupolar strains moving at c, and detectors respond to strain, not pressure.

**App idea:** Play a detected-style chirp as audio while a spectrogram and a microphone-array analogy show how delays between sites localize a sound source and a gravitational-wave source alike.

### Standard candle (known luminosity) becomes a standard siren → standard siren · strong · SCH §9.6 p.251 (pdf 269)

Just as astronomers get distance from a source of known brightness, the chirp of a binary reveals its intrinsic gravitational luminosity, so its amplitude gives distance.

**Where it breaks down:** Unlike candles, sirens need no empirical calibration ladder, but they require polarization or sky information to remove inclination and they measure luminosity distance with redshift degeneracy.

**App idea:** Slider app: match a light bulb's apparent brightness to distance, then do the same with a chirp whose sweep rate sets the loudness.

### Charge conservation forbids electromagnetic monopole radiation; energy-momentum conservation forbids gravitational monopole and dipole radiation → absence of monopole and dipole gravitational radiation · strong · SCH §9.4 p.238 (pdf 256)

Explains why the leading gravitational radiation is quadrupolar and why spherically symmetric motion does not radiate.

**Where it breaks down:** Electromagnetism does allow dipole radiation, because charges of both signs can separate; gravity's dipole is the centre of mass, fixed by momentum conservation.

**App idea:** Multipole ladder: animate a pulsating sphere, a sloshing dipole and a rotating dumbbell side by side for electric charge and for mass, showing which radiate.

### Electromagnetic polarizations separated by 90 degrees versus gravitational polarizations by 45 degrees → plus and cross polarization · strong · SCH §9.2 p.210 (pdf 228)

Contrasts a vector field (spin-1) with a symmetric tensor field (spin-2) through how far a pattern must rotate to look like the other independent state.

**Where it breaks down:** The rotation angle argument does not by itself establish quantum spin; the book only gestures at the rank of the field.

**App idea:** Rotate a ring of test charges under an EM wave and a ring of test masses under a GW wave; show the angle at which each pattern first maps into its orthogonal partner.

### Radar ranging → radar ranging of proper distance · strong · SCH §9.3 p.220 (pdf 238)

Timing a reflected light pulse is the familiar way to measure distance and, because of light's privileged role, measures proper distance in curved spacetime too.

**Where it breaks down:** Valid as proper distance only when the separation is small compared with the gravitational wavelength; otherwise the return time mixes wave values at different times.

### One interferometer arm serves as the clock for the other → laser interferometer detector · strong · SCH §9.3 p.223 (pdf 241)

Explains why interferometers beat single-beam detectors: comparing two return times removes the need for an ultra-stable atomic clock.

**Where it breaks down:** The comparison only works if the arms respond differently to the wave; a cross wave overhead with arms on x and y gives no signal.

**App idea:** Race two photons down perpendicular arms while a wave passes; show their arrival-time difference with and without a noisy shared clock.

### Pendulum suspensions as mechanical low-pass filters; active isolation like noise-cancelling headphones → seismic isolation · useful · SCH §9.3 p.219 (pdf 237)

Makes the engineering of vibration isolation intuitive.

**Where it breaks down:** Real suspensions have internal modes and thermal noise; active control has its own sensor noise.

**App idea:** Shake the top of a single and a triple pendulum at variable frequency and plot transmitted motion versus frequency.

### Folding the light path many times → Fabry-Perot arm cavities and power recycling · useful · SCH §9.3 p.224 (pdf 242)

Arm cavities are presented as the equivalent of bouncing the beam up and down the arm repeatedly to lengthen its storage time.

**Where it breaks down:** A cavity is a resonant device with finite bandwidth; storage times longer than the wave period reduce high-frequency response.

### Bird calls that sweep upward → chirp time · useful · SCH §9.6 p.249 (pdf 267)

Names the rising-frequency signal of an inspiralling binary.

**Where it breaks down:** Purely a naming analogy.

### Gravity has only one sign of charge, so emitters add coherently, like pressure changes producing sound → cosmic sound analogy · strong · SCH §9.3 p.230 (pdf 248)

Explains why gravitational radiation comes from bulk motions rather than individual atomic transitions, unlike light from incoherently emitting atoms.

**Where it breaks down:** Microscopic gravitational emission exists in principle but is utterly negligible; the point is about which contribution dominates.

### Waves in curved spacetime behave like electromagnetic waves in a complicated medium → geometrical optics for gravitational waves · useful · SCH §9.2 p.212 (pdf 230)

Short waves follow null geodesics, transport polarization, and can be lensed, just like light.

**Where it breaks down:** Unlike light, gravitational waves are almost unaffected by matter along their path.

### Underwater acoustic arrays for geophysics and defence → detector network localization · useful · SCH §9.3 p.230 (pdf 248)

Direction to a source is found from phase or time delays across widely separated sensors.

**Where it breaks down:** Gravitational-wave networks have very few sensors and strongly direction-dependent antenna patterns.


## Misconceptions addressed

### Because free particles keep constant coordinates in TT gauge, a gravitational wave has no effect on them. · SCH §9.2 p.206 (pdf 224)

**Correction:** Constant TT coordinates just mean the coordinates are attached to free particles; the proper distance between particles changes, which is measurable.

**Why tempting:** A coordinate calculation seems to show the particle is 'at rest', and at-rest language suggests nothing happens.

### If a wave stretches space, rulers stretch too, so the stretching can never be measured. · SCH §9.2 p.209 (pdf 227)

**Correction:** Rulers are held together by electromagnetic forces far stronger than the wave's tidal force, so they barely change length; free masses do move relative to them. Light-travel timing is an even better measure.

**Why tempting:** A ruler is mostly empty space, and 'space stretching' sounds like everything inside it expands uniformly.

### An interferometer compares arm lengths with the laser wavelength, and since the wave also stretches the light, the effect cancels. · SCH §9.3 p.224 (pdf 242)

**Correction:** The interferometer compares round-trip times of light on null paths; what happens to the wavelength along the way is irrelevant, and wavelength is observer-dependent anyway (footnote 2).

**Why tempting:** Textbook pictures of interference count wavelengths, so it seems the ruler is the wavelength.

### The coordinate speed of light (1 + h_+)^{-1/2} being different from 1 violates relativity. · SCH §9.3 p.221 (pdf 239)

**Correction:** Only locally measured speeds must equal 1; coordinate speeds in arbitrary coordinates can differ, and the measurable return time is computed with proper time.

**Why tempting:** Special relativity is often summarized as 'light always moves at c' without the 'locally, in an inertial frame' qualifier.

### Gravitational waves might be mere wiggles of the coordinates rather than of geometry. · SCH §9.1 p.202 (pdf 220)

**Correction:** The Riemann tensor, which is gauge invariant, is nonzero and directly sets measurable relative accelerations; waves also deposit energy in detectors and drain energy from binaries.

**Why tempting:** Einstein himself worried about this, and in linearized theory gauge changes do produce wave-like metric perturbations with no curvature (Exercise 9.10).

### The two polarizations of gravitational waves are perpendicular to each other like those of light. · SCH §9.2 p.210 (pdf 228)

**Correction:** They are rotated by 45 degrees relative to each other, reflecting the rank-2 tensor nature of the field.

**Why tempting:** Light is the familiar model of polarization.

### An interferometer is sensitive to every wave that passes through it. · SCH §9.3 p.223 (pdf 241)

**Correction:** It responds to one linear combination of polarizations; a cross-polarized wave arriving perpendicular to both arms aligned with the plus axes produces no signal, and response varies with direction.

**Why tempting:** Interferometers are described as nearly omnidirectional.

### A spherically symmetric supernova collapse should be a strong gravitational-wave source because it involves huge masses moving fast. · SCH §9.4 p.237 (pdf 255)

**Correction:** Spherically symmetric motion does not radiate at all; only the non-spherical part of the motion contributes, so realistic amplitudes can be far below the h ~ phi_0 phi_r estimate.

**Why tempting:** Energy and speed seem to be what matters, as for explosions in general.

### If the leading-order quadrupole moment does not change, the system emits no gravitational waves. · SCH §9.4 p.238 (pdf 256)

**Correction:** The quadrupole formula gives only the dominant term; a rotating square of equal masses has a constant quadrupole but radiates through higher multipoles.

**Why tempting:** The formula is used so often that it is taken as exact.

### Since the wave amplitude is no larger than the Newtonian potential of the source at Earth, it should be even harder to detect than the source's static gravity. · SCH §9.4 p.237 (pdf 255)

**Correction:** What detectors sense is force; the wave tidal force h l omega^2 falls as 1/r and exceeds the static tidal force (falling as 1/r^3) by a factor (phi_0 r/R)^2, enormous at astronomical distances.

**Why tempting:** Comparing dimensionless amplitudes seems the natural test of detectability.

### Gravitational-wave energy can be localized at each point like electromagnetic energy density, in any gauge and at any order. · SCH §9.5 p.243 (pdf 261)

**Correction:** The flux formula is valid only as an average, only to lowest order in linearized theory, and is not gauge invariant; local gravitational energy has no general meaning in GR.

**Why tempting:** Electromagnetic energy density is local and gauge invariant, inviting the same expectation.

### In binary formulas, omega is the gravitational-wave frequency. · SCH §9.6 p.248 (pdf 266)

**Correction:** omega is the orbital angular frequency; the dominant gravitational-wave angular frequency is twice it.

**Why tempting:** Radiation formulas usually use the frequency of the emitted wave.

### Heavier merging black holes reach a higher peak gravitational luminosity. · SCH §9.6 p.250 (pdf 268)

**Correction:** Peak luminosity depends only on the number of orbits remaining and is essentially the same (about 0.01 in geometrized units) for any mass; heavier systems radiate longer and so emit more total energy in proportion to their mass.

**Why tempting:** Bigger usually means brighter in astronomy.

### A strain of 1e-21 means mirrors move by 1e-21 metres. · SCH §9.2 p.207 (pdf 225)

**Correction:** Strain is fractional; the length change is about h L/2 per arm (h L as an arm-length difference for a plus wave overhead), about 1e-18 m for 4-km arms.

**Why tempting:** Strain and displacement are easily conflated when numbers are quoted without units.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### Gravitational-wave observations measure a binary's true masses directly. · SCH §9.6 p.252 (pdf 270)

**Correction:** Detectors measure only time-scales, so masses are redshifted by (1 + z) and must be corrected using the distance and a cosmological model.

**Why tempting:** Masses seem intrinsic and unaffected by motion.

### The chirp mass is an intrinsically relativistic quantity. · SCH §9.6 p.248 (pdf 266)

**Correction:** It already appears in the Newtonian orbital energy; it simply went unnoticed until gravitational-wave dynamics made it the controlling parameter.

**Why tempting:** It was introduced in a gravitational-wave context.


## Thought experiments

### Feynman's sticky beads · SCH §9.5 p.239 (pdf 257)

**Setup:** Two beads slide on a rod with friction while a gravitational wave passes; the tidal force moves them relative to the rod and friction heats it.

**Lesson:** Heat must be paid for by the wave, so gravitational waves carry energy; demanding conservation fixes the flux.

**App idea:** Beads on a rod under an animated wave with a thermometer rising; toggling friction off leaves the beads oscillating with no heat.

### Ring of free particles · SCH §9.2 p.210 (pdf 228)

**Setup:** A circle of freely falling particles around a central one, initially at rest in a plane transverse to an incoming wave.

**Lesson:** The wave shears the ring into oscillating ellipses whose orientation distinguishes the two polarizations.

**App idea:** See Figure 9.1 redesign.

### A ruler in a gravitational wave · SCH §9.2 p.209 (pdf 227)

**Setup:** A solid ruler lies between two free particles while a wave passes.

**Lesson:** Internal forces dominate the tidal force, so the ruler keeps its length and can record the free particles' displacement.

**App idea:** Tune a 'stiffness' slider from zero (ruler follows free particles) to realistic (ruler rigid) while comparing lengths.

### A plane of absorbing oscillators · SCH §9.5 p.241 (pdf 259)

**Setup:** An infinite sheet of damped spring detectors, sigma per unit area, intercepts a plane wave.

**Lesson:** The re-radiated field forms a plane wave that reduces the downstream amplitude by exactly the absorbed energy, and the oscillators drop out of the flux formula.

**App idea:** See Figures 9.5-9.6 redesigns.

### Radar between two free masses · SCH §9.3 p.222 (pdf 240)

**Setup:** An observer at the centre of the particle ring sends photons to particles on the x and y axes and times their return.

**Lesson:** Return times differ by 2 h_+ L, a gauge-independent measurable that is the principle of interferometers (Exercise 9.26).

### Does a freely falling bowl of soup slosh? · SCH p.256 (pdf 274)

**Setup:** A small bowl of soup, much smaller than a wavelength, falls freely as a wave passes (Exercise 9.8).

**Lesson:** Invites distinguishing the absence of acceleration at the centre of mass from tidal forces across the bowl.

### Rotating square of equal masses · SCH §9.4 p.238 (pdf 256)

**Setup:** Four equal masses at the corners of a square spin about its centre (Exercise 9.31).

**Lesson:** The quadrupole moment stays constant, so the quadrupole formula predicts no radiation, yet higher multipoles radiate: leading-order formulas are not proofs.

**App idea:** Rotating mass configurations with a live readout of I_ij and the octupole moment.

### Rescaled binaries · SCH §9.6 p.248 (pdf 266)

**Setup:** Compare a binary with a given chirp mass and orbital frequency to one with twice the chirp mass and half the frequency, placed twice as far away.

**Lesson:** They produce identical amplitudes and phases once time is doubled: gravity has no intrinsic scale.

**App idea:** Overlay two chirps with a time-stretch slider that makes them coincide.


## Applications and observations

- **Weber-type resonant bar excited by a strong continuous wave** (numerical-estimate, SCH §9.3 p.218 (pdf 236)): Aluminium bar numbers inserted into the resonant response formulas show the displacement and deposited energy for an optimistic wave amplitude. Key numbers: m = 1.4e3 kg, l_0 = 1.5 m, omega_0 = 1e4 s^-1, Q about 1e5, A = 1e-20: E about 1e-20 J, R about 1e-15 m (nuclear size).
- **Thermal noise and cryogenic bars** (technology, SCH §9.3 p.219 (pdf 237)): k_B T at room temperature is comparable to the signal energy; detecting a ten times weaker wave needs a hundred times lower temperature, so bars moved to about 3 K, and Auriga went below 100 mK. Key numbers: k_B = 1.38e-23 J/K; T about 300 K to 3 K to below 0.1 K.
- **Why open a gravitational-wave window** (observation, SCH §9.3 p.213 (pdf 231)): The book justifies detection by analogy with the post-1960 opening of the electromagnetic spectrum, then names information only waves can carry: they leave supernova cores directly while light is scattered for days; detectable primordial waves may originate about 1e-25 s after the Big Bang; and the roughly 96 percent of mass-energy that does not interact electromagnetically still couples to gravity and might radiate. Key numbers: Primordial waves from about 1e-25 s; ordinary matter about 4 percent of the Universe's mass-energy (as of writing; modern value about 5 percent).
- **Bar readout, bandwidth and design limits** (technology, SCH §9.3 p.219 (pdf 237)): Weber's bar carried strain sensors at its waist where stretching is largest; later groups coupled the mode to a light resonant transducer to amplify motion and read it with superconducting SQUID electronics. Bars could not be made much longer (about 3 m at most) without isolation problems, Q was pushed with single sapphire crystals and spherical designs were proposed, but resonances above about 600 Hz sat above where most strong signals lie, and the needed 1e-21 burst sensitivity would have required beating the one-phonon quantum limit. Key numbers: Bar lengths up to about 3 m; resonances above about 600 Hz; target burst amplitude about 1e-21.
- **Detector families and frequency bands** (technology, SCH §9.3 p.215 (pdf 233)): Ground interferometers and bars above 1 Hz; LISA millihertz; decihertz gap for intermediate-mass black holes (DECIGO proposal); spacecraft tracking below 0.1 mHz; pulsar timing below 100 nHz; CMB polarization for primordial waves at about 1e-16 Hz today. Key numbers: Ground band about 10 Hz-few kHz (advanced detectors 20 Hz-2 kHz); LISA above about 300 microhertz; LISA masses 1e4-1e7 solar; ground up to about 100 solar masses.
- **Spacecraft Doppler tracking** (experiment, SCH §9.3 p.222 (pdf 240)): Radio transponders on interplanetary probes act as single-arm beam detectors; plasma delays and clock instability limit sensitivity; the three-term signature helps reject noise. Key numbers: Round-trip times of hours; clock stability not yet 1e-19, so amplitudes of 1e-20 or less are out of reach.
- **Pulsar timing arrays** (observation, SCH §9.3 p.215 (pdf 233)): Correlated timing residuals of many millisecond pulsars search for nanohertz waves; SKA would monitor hundreds of pulsars; the International Pulsar Timing Array coordinates efforts. Key numbers: Sensitive below about 100 nHz; averages over months.
- **Polarization of the cosmic microwave background** (observation, SCH §9.3 p.215 (pdf 233)): Primordial gravitational waves present at recombination imprint a polarization pattern; measuring it would probe early-Universe physics; details deferred to Chapter 13. Key numbers: Wave frequency today about 1e-16 Hz.
- **LIGO, Virgo, GEO600, KAGRA and LIGO-India** (technology, SCH §9.3 p.224 (pdf 242)): Kilometre-scale Fabry-Perot Michelson interferometers with suspended optics; GEO600 pioneered fused-silica monolithic suspensions; KAGRA is underground and cryogenic; LIGO-India is under construction. Key numbers: LIGO arms 4 km, 1.6 m diameter vacuum tubes, sites about 3000 km apart; Virgo 3 km; KAGRA 3 km with mirrors to 20 K; GEO600 600 m; suspension Q about 2e9; shot noise dominates above about 200 Hz.
- **First direct detections** (detection-event, SCH §9.3 p.224 (pdf 242)): LIGO detected a binary black hole merger on 14 September 2015 and further events over two years; with Virgo it observed the first binary neutron star merger on 17 August 2017. Key numbers: Observed peak amplitude about 1e-21 for the first event.
- **Upper-limit amplitudes from simple estimates** (numerical-estimate, SCH §9.4 p.231 (pdf 249)): h of order M/R gives ceilings for a nearby-galaxy supernova forming a black hole and for the first detected merger, implying order-10-percent metric distortion near the merging holes. Key numbers: 10 solar masses at 1e23 m: 1e-17; 30 solar mass pair at 1e25 m: 1e-20 (observed 1e-21).
- **Laboratory gravitational-wave generator** (numerical-estimate, SCH §9.4 p.235 (pdf 253)): A tonne-scale vibrating spring produces an utterly negligible wave. Key numbers: m = 1e3 kg, l_0 = 1 m, A = 1e-4 m, omega = 1e4 s^-1: book quotes h about 1e-34/r (r in metres); our own evaluation of 2 m omega^2 l_0 A/r gives about 2e-37/r.
- **Hulse-Taylor binary pulsar as a wave source and clock** (astrophysical-system, SCH §9.4 p.236 (pdf 254)): Two 1.4 solar mass neutron stars in a 7 h 45 min orbit emit at twice the orbital frequency, below all planned detector bands; orbital decay tests the quadrupole formula. Key numbers: P = 27907 s = 8.3721e12 m; masses 2.07 km each; e = 0.617; GW frequency 72 microhertz; book states 8 kpc and amplitude 1e-20. Our check: the 1e-20 follows from the mis-printed 2.4e17 m; at the true 2.5e20 m the face-on amplitude is about 4e-23.
- **Measured orbital decay of PSR B1913+16** (observation, SCH §9.5 p.247 (pdf 265)): Circular-orbit estimate followed by the eccentric-orbit GR prediction compared with timing data. Key numbers: Circular estimate dP/dt = -2.0e-13 (-6e-6 s/yr); GR prediction -2.40263e-12; observed -(2.398 +/- 0.004)e-12; agreement 0.17 percent.
- **Wave force versus Newtonian tidal force at astronomical distances** (numerical-estimate, SCH §9.4 p.237 (pdf 255)): Illustrates why waves are detectable far beyond where the source's static tide could be felt. Key numbers: phi_0 = 0.1, R = 1e11 m, r = 1e23 m: ratio about 1e22.
- **Maximum luminosity and the brightest sources** (numerical-estimate, SCH §9.5 p.245 (pdf 263)): c^5/G bounds any luminosity; quasars and gamma-ray bursts are far below; black hole mergers approach it in gravitational waves; merging black holes briefly outshine the electromagnetic luminosity of the observable Universe (Exercise 9.45). Key numbers: c^5/G = 3.63e52 W; quasars and GRBs below 1e-10; merger peaks about 1e-3 (numerical simulations) to 0.01 (point-mass estimate).
- **Networks: localization, polarization and confidence** (data-analysis, SCH §9.3 p.226 (pdf 244)): Time delays within +/- d/c between sites give sky rings; three detectors give two patches; polarization from non-aligned detectors gives inclination; electromagnetic counterparts can pin down position. Key numbers: LIGO sites separated by about 3000 km (light time about 10 ms, our estimate).
- **Matched filtering and detection thresholds** (data-analysis, SCH §9.3 p.226 (pdf 244)): Signals below the broadband noise are extracted by computer filtering; long waveforms like neutron star inspirals can be found below the burst threshold. Key numbers: A 200 Hz burst of 1e-22 is near the noise level of current LIGO ('a 1e-22 detector').
- **LISA and LISA Pathfinder** (technology, SCH §9.3 p.228 (pdf 246)): Three drag-free spacecraft with transponded 2-W lasers and time-delay interferometry; Pathfinder demonstrated free fall at the L1 point. Key numbers: Arms 2.5e6 km; 1 AU heliocentric orbit trailing Earth by 20 degrees; 60-degree opening angle reduces response by sin 60 = 0.866; launch mid-2030s.
- **Third-generation ground detectors** (technology, SCH §9.3 p.228 (pdf 246)): Einstein Telescope (Europe) and Cosmic Explorer (USA) would be about ten times more sensitive than Advanced LIGO, observing neutron star and stellar black hole mergers essentially throughout the Universe. Key numbers: Operation probably after 2035 (as of mid-2021 writing).
- **Standard-siren distances and the Hubble-Lemaitre constant** (data-analysis, SCH §9.6 p.251 (pdf 269)): LIGO-Virgo assign distances to all binary detections with the siren method and correct masses for redshift; sirens can calibrate the cosmic expansion rate. Key numbers: Forward-Berman era bars: binary neutron star range about 1 kpc; LIGO-Virgo by 2021: beyond 100 Mpc.
- **Circularization of the Hulse-Taylor orbit** (numerical-estimate, SCH §9.6 p.253 (pdf 271)): Because eccentricity decays faster than the separation, the system will be almost circular by the time it reaches the ground-based band, one or two minutes before merger. Key numbers: e below 1e-5 on entering the LIGO band (quoted from Andersson 2019).

## Historical notes

- **Albert Einstein (1916):** Derived the linearized description of gravitational waves, judged them far too weak to detect, and later doubted they were real rather than coordinate effects. — Sets up the chapter's emphasis on invariant, measurable effects. (SCH §9.1 p.202 (pdf 220))
- **Karl Schwarzschild (1916):** Found the solution now identified with black holes; Einstein did not accept it as describing real objects. — Part of the framing that GR's astrophysical role was long underestimated. (SCH §9.1 p.202 (pdf 220))
- **Russell Hulse, Joseph Taylor (1974-1993):** Discovered the binary pulsar PSR B1913+16 in 1974, whose orbital decay provided evidence for gravitational radiation; Nobel Prize 1993. — First strong, indirect confirmation that waves carry energy. (SCH §9.1 p.202 (pdf 220))
- **Rainer Weiss, Kip Thorne, Barry Barish (2015-2017):** LIGO made the first direct detection on 14 September 2015 (a merger of two unexpectedly massive black holes); Nobel Prize 2017. — Turned gravitational-wave physics into observational astronomy. (SCH §9.3 p.224 (pdf 242))
- **Richard Feynman, Hermann Bondi, Felix Pirani, Joseph Weber (1957):** At the Chapel Hill conference Feynman gave the sticky-bead argument that waves carry energy; Bondi and Pirani made similar points; Weber was inspired to start detector work. — Helped end decades of confusion about the reality and energy of waves, and provides the logic of §9.5. (SCH §9.7 p.255 (pdf 273))
- **Joseph Weber (1961-1970s):** Built the first resonant bar detectors and claimed detections in the late 1960s that other bars did not confirm. — The controversy clouded the field, but the teams that tested his claims seeded later bar and interferometer projects. (SCH §9.7 p.253 (pdf 271))
- **History (2005):** Interferometers first reached sensitivity to relative length changes near 1e-21, approaching 1e-22 by 2015. — Marks the technical milestone preceding detection. (SCH §9.2 p.207 (pdf 225))
- **History (2017):** Virgo joined LIGO in August 2017 and the network observed the first binary neutron star merger on 17 August 2017. — Opened multimessenger gravitational-wave astronomy and standard-siren cosmology. (SCH §9.3 p.224 (pdf 242))
- **Robert Forward, David Berman (1967):** Derived that a binary's gravitational luminosity depends only on the number of orbits left, and other scaling results, before pulsars were known. — An early, overlooked source of the standard-siren idea. (SCH §9.6 p.250 (pdf 268))
- **Bernard Schutz (1986):** Wrote down the distance formula for binary standard sirens and pointed out their use for measuring the Hubble constant; with Krolak (1987) discussed cosmological redshift of observed masses. — Foundation of gravitational-wave distance measurement used by LIGO-Virgo. (SCH §9.6 p.251 (pdf 269))
- **Philip Peters, Jon Mathews (1963-1964):** Computed radiation and orbital decay for eccentric binaries. — Basis of the eccentricity correction for the Hulse-Taylor pulsar and of circularization arguments. (SCH §9.6 p.252 (pdf 270))
- **History (2015-2017):** ESA's LISA Pathfinder, launched at the end of 2015, demonstrated drag-free free-fall performance better than LISA requires. — Retired a key technical risk for space interferometry. (SCH §9.3 p.229 (pdf 247))

## Notation and conventions

- **Units:** Geometrized units G = c = 1 throughout: masses, times and lengths in metres; luminosity dimensionless with unit c^5/G = 3.63e52 W. — Solar mass about 1.477 km; the book uses 1.4 solar masses = 2.07 km. (SCH §9.5 p.245 (pdf 263))
- **Metric signature and background:** Signature (-,+,+,+); indices raised and lowered with the Minkowski metric eta in linearized theory. (SCH §9.2 p.203 (pdf 221))
- **Trace-reversed perturbation:** h-bar_{alpha beta} = h_{alpha beta} - (1/2) eta_{alpha beta} h; equal to h in TT gauge. — The reading copy's OCR renders the bar as a tilde in places. (SCH §9.2 p.205 (pdf 223))
- **TT superscript and polarization symbols:** h^TT_{jk} denotes transverse-traceless components; h_+ denotes h_xx^TT = -h_yy^TT for a wave along z; the cross amplitude is written h_xy^TT. (SCH §9.3 p.221 (pdf 239))
- **Comma derivatives:** A comma denotes partial differentiation; subscript 0 is the time coordinate, so h_{xx,00} is the second time derivative. (SCH §9.2 p.208 (pdf 226))
- **Complex notation:** Oscillating solutions are written with complex exponentials; the physical quantity is the real part, which must be taken before forming products such as energies. — Stated in footnote 1. (SCH §9.2 p.203 (pdf 221))
- **Time averages:** Angle brackets denote averages over one period; double angle brackets in Exercise 9.50 denote averages over a period and a wavelength. (SCH §9.3 p.218 (pdf 236))
- **Quadrupole moment:** I^{lm} = integral of T^00 x^l x^m with no trace removal and no factor 3 (MTW convention); the trace-free version is printed as a barred I. — Other texts define Q_ij = integral rho (3 x_i x_j - r^2 delta_ij). (SCH §9.4 p.233 (pdf 251))
- **Frequencies:** Omega denotes the source or wave angular frequency in general formulas; in binary sections omega is the orbital angular frequency and the wave frequency is 2 omega. — Eq. 9.144 writes (M Omega) where the orbital omega is meant. (SCH §9.6 p.248 (pdf 266))
- **Mass symbols in binary sections:** m is each star's mass in §9.4; M is each star's mass in the Hulse-Taylor example of §9.5; in §9.6 M = m1 + m2 is the total mass, mu the reduced mass and script M the chirp mass. — Overloaded M is an easy trap across §§9.5-9.6. (SCH §9.5 p.245 (pdf 263))
- **Inclination:** iota is the angle between the orbital normal and the line of sight, face-on iota = 0; the x axis in the sky plane is chosen so that the orbit lies in the x-z plane when iota = pi/2. (SCH §9.4 p.236 (pdf 254))
- **Riemann index order in geodesic deviation:** d^2 xi^alpha/d tau^2 = R^alpha_{mu nu beta} U^mu U^nu xi^beta, equivalently -R^i_{0j0} xi^j for the tidal acceleration. — Sign follows the book's Chapter 6 convention. (SCH §9.2 p.208 (pdf 226))
- **Overloaded symbols:** xi is a gauge vector (9.14), a connecting vector (9.25) and a spring stretch (9.35); phi is a response phase, a polar angle and a Newtonian potential; R is a response amplitude, a source size and |x - y|; psi is a phase shift (9.119) and an orbital phase (9.159); A is a plane-wave amplitude tensor, a wave amplitude and a mass oscillation amplitude. — Tutors should disambiguate by context. (SCH §9.5 p.241 (pdf 259))

## Teaching gems

### Let the TT gauge 'prove' that nothing happens to free particles, then overturn the conclusion by computing the proper distance. · SCH §9.2 p.206 (pdf 224)

**Why it works:** A deliberately provoked paradox makes the coordinate-versus-invariant distinction unforgettable and answers Einstein's 'wavy coordinates' worry.

**App idea:** Split view: TT coordinate grid where particles sit still versus a proper-distance view where they oscillate, linked by one time slider.

### Rewrite the wave's action as a Newtonian-looking tidal force in a local inertial frame so that other forces can simply be added. · SCH §9.2 p.209 (pdf 227)

**Why it works:** Turns curvature into something learners can put into F = ma, and resolves the rigid-ruler question with force comparison.

**App idea:** Mass-spring chain under a wave with an adjustable internal stiffness; readouts of tidal versus elastic force.

### Differentiate the radar return time so the integral collapses to wave values at emission and reception. · SCH §9.3 p.222 (pdf 240)

**Why it works:** A small calculus trick yields a surprising, testable result and naturally leads to interferometers as two-arm clocks.

**App idea:** Space-time diagram of a photon bouncing between free masses in a wave, with the return-time derivative plotted live and the reflection-time term appearing as the wave direction tilts.

### Build the interferometer by placing arms inside the ring-of-particles picture. · SCH §9.3 p.223 (pdf 241)

**Why it works:** Links an abstract polarization diagram to real detector geometry, explaining sensitivity and blind spots in one image.

**App idea:** Drag arms over the Figure 9.1 ring and see the differential arm signal for each polarization and direction.

### Reuse one spring-and-masses toy as detector, emitter and energy calibrator. · SCH §9.5 p.240 (pdf 258)

**Why it works:** Continuity reduces cognitive load and demonstrates that absorption and emission are two faces of the same coupling.

**App idea:** A single oscillator widget with three modes (receive, transmit, absorb-and-reradiate) that share parameters.

### Derive the wave energy flux by making the detector absorb energy and then showing its parameters cancel. · SCH §9.5 p.243 (pdf 261)

**Why it works:** Delivers a field-theory result with only energy conservation and interference, and shows why the answer is a property of the wave.

**App idea:** Energy-ledger simulation where changing oscillator mass, damping or density never changes the computed dF/dA.

### Explain detectability by comparing forces, not amplitudes: waves fall as 1/r, tides as 1/r^3. · SCH §9.4 p.237 (pdf 255)

**Why it works:** Resolves the puzzle of detecting waves from sources whose static gravity is unmeasurable, with a one-line scaling.

**App idea:** Log-log plot of wave tidal acceleration and Newtonian tidal acceleration versus distance with a draggable crossover.

### Use scale-freedom to show luminosity depends only on orbits remaining, and that the chirp reveals the chirp mass. · SCH §9.6 p.250 (pdf 268)

**Why it works:** Dimensional reasoning produces striking, mass-independent predictions and sets up standard sirens with minimal algebra.

**App idea:** Chirp explorer: choose masses, watch waveform, luminosity and remaining-orbit counter; rescale masses to see curves collapse when plotted against orbits left.

### Frame gravitational-wave astronomy as listening rather than looking. · SCH §9.3 p.229 (pdf 247)

**Why it works:** Gives novices a coherent mental model for phase-coherent detection, poor directionality, networks and chirps.

**App idea:** Audio sonification of inspirals with adjustable chirp mass alongside a microphone-array localization mini-game.

### Bound any luminosity by c^5/G with a black-hole-formation argument. · SCH §9.5 p.245 (pdf 263)

**Why it works:** A short, units-based argument that applies to all radiation and puts merger luminosities in perspective.

**App idea:** Luminosity ladder from the Sun to quasars to merging black holes on a log scale capped by c^5/G.


## Gaps and pitfalls

- **Printed Christoffel symbol for the exact plane wave reads Gamma^v_xx = 2 f-dot/f (and 2 g-dot/g); for the metric -du dv + f^2 dx^2 + g^2 dy^2 it should be 2 f f-dot (and 2 g g-dot). The linearized solution is also printed as f approximately 1 - epsilon(a) instead of epsilon(u).** — Learners checking the calculation get inconsistent Riemann components and suspect their own algebra. *Suggestion:* Show the Christoffel computation explicitly with g^{uv} = -2 and flag the correction.
- **Eq. 9.58 as printed has h_+(z - t) multiplying dx^2 but h_+(t - z) multiplying dy^2.** — The two arguments differ by sign, which for a general waveform would not be the same wave. *Suggestion:* Use a single retarded-time argument h_+(t - z) in both terms.
- **Eq. 9.83 is printed with exponent -i Omega (r - t), opposite to Eq. 9.74's outgoing form, and Eq. 9.106 carries a minus sign that is inconsistent with Eqs. 9.83 and 9.107 once d^2 I/dt^2 = -Omega^2 D e^{-i Omega t} is used; the second line of Eq. 9.107 is labelled h_xx with a stray bracket where h_xy = (2/r) d^2 I_xy/dt^2 is meant.** — Sign and label slips in the central generation formula undermine confidence and propagate into polarization signs. *Suggestion:* Present h-bar_jk = (2/r) d^2 I_jk(t - r)/dt^2 and derive the TT components from it, noting the printed variants.
- **Eq. 9.88's middle line drops the factor m, and Eq. 9.92 prints A where A^2 is needed (Eq. 9.93 has A^2).** — Dimensional inconsistency between consecutive equations. *Suggestion:* Carry m and A^2 explicitly; use a dimension check as a teaching moment.
- **Eq. 9.124 is printed with 8 I^{xy} where 8 (I_xy)^2 is required.** — The flux formula looks non-quadratic, blocking the reader from verifying Eq. 9.126. *Suggestion:* Write both polarizations' squares explicitly before simplifying.
- **Eq. 9.147 converts dP/dt = -2.40263e-12 to -7.2e-15 s/yr; the correct conversion is about -7.6e-5 s/yr (multiply by 3.156e7 s). Also the text after Eq. 9.133 cites 'Eq. 9.70' for the amplitude estimate, which is Eq. 9.101.** — A reader comparing with the earlier -6e-6 s/yr estimate and the stated factor 12 finds a nine-order-of-magnitude contradiction. *Suggestion:* Recompute the conversion with the learner and check it against 12 times the circular estimate.
- **The Hulse-Taylor distance is printed as 8 kpc = 2.4e17 m; 8 kpc is about 2.5e20 m (footnote 3's own parsec value gives this). The quoted amplitude 1e-20 matches the mis-printed distance; with the correct distance 2 m l_0^2 omega^2/r gives about 4e-23 (our estimate). The laboratory-generator amplitude 1e-34/r is also about three orders of magnitude larger than the stated parameters give (2 m omega^2 l_0 A about 1.5e-37, so about 2e-37/r).** — Order-of-magnitude intuition built on these numbers will be off by large factors. *Suggestion:* Have learners redo both estimates in geometrized units as an exercise in unit conversion.
- **Several internal pointers are off by one section or exercise: §9.5 refers to 'the oscillator in §9.3' radiating and to 'spherical waves of §9.3' and 'binary in §9.3' where §9.4 is meant; Eq. 9.81 is attributed to Exercise 9.24 (which is about Eq. 9.53) and the detector initial condition to Exercise 9.23.** — Readers searching for the cited derivations cannot find them. *Suggestion:* Resolve references in the app to the actual locations (quadrupole examples are in §9.4) and derive the tensor virial identity directly from stress-energy conservation.
- **The TT gauge construction (Exercise 9.5), the Riemann components (Eq. 9.27, 'not hard'), the outgoing solution (Exercise 9.29) and the TT projection (Exercises 9.32-9.33) are all delegated to exercises.** — Novices may accept the two-polarization count and projection rules without understanding them. *Suggestion:* Offer a guided derivation mode that walks through constraint counting and the projector with live component tables.
- **The planar-sum derivation of the flux uses an exp(-epsilon r) convergence factor for an integral that does not converge, without discussing Fresnel-zone physics.** — The step can feel like a trick and hides why the result has a quarter-cycle phase lag. *Suggestion:* Show the phasor spiral (Cornu-like) and explain that distant zones cancel in pairs, leaving half the first zone.
- **Applying a derivation that assumed non-gravitational forces (Eq. 9.77) to self-gravitating binaries is justified only by citation, and the flux derivation is explicitly linear while used for relativistic mergers.** — Learners may either overtrust the quadrupole formula near merger or distrust it for binary pulsars. *Suggestion:* State the domain clearly: leading post-Newtonian order for well-separated binaries; numerical relativity near merger.
- **Complex notation is used for quadratic quantities (flux, luminosity) with only a footnote reminder to take real parts first; Eq. 9.136 relies on using the real part of Eq. 9.97.** — Squaring complex amplitudes directly gives wrong factors of 2 or oscillating terms. *Suggestion:* Model the time-average rule <Re(a e^{-i w t}) Re(b e^{-i w t})> = Re(a b*)/2 explicitly.
- **Symbol overloading: M means each star's mass in the Hulse-Taylor example but total mass in §9.6; Omega and omega switch roles; xi, phi, R and psi each carry several meanings.** — Students mix equal-mass and total-mass formulas, introducing factors of 2^{something}. *Suggestion:* In tutoring, restate symbol meanings at each formula and use distinct names (m_each, M_tot, omega_orb, omega_gw).
- **Instrument and project status is dated to mid-2021: SKA 'perhaps by 2025', LIGO-India 'around 2025', KAGRA 'just beginning', LISA 'mid-2030s', pulsar timing still seeking a first detection, 4 percent ordinary matter.** — Learners may think pulsar timing has seen nothing or LISA is unapproved. *Suggestion:* Add a timeline overlay: LISA adopted by ESA in 2024; pulsar timing arrays reported evidence for a nanohertz background in 2023; later LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA observing runs; modern baryon fraction about 5 percent.
- **The book gives the inclination-dependent amplitudes (Eq. 9.100, 9.152) without the TT projection along the line of sight, noting only that it must still be done.** — Readers may apply the formulas directly as detector strains. *Suggestion:* Provide the projected h_+ and h_x forms with (1 + cos^2 iota)/2 and cos iota factors and show a numerical check.
- **The antenna response of interferometers is described only qualitatively (half response for waves along an arm, zero for a cross wave overhead) with details deferred to Chapter 12.** — Learners lack a formula to reason about network sensitivity. *Suggestion:* Introduce F_+ and F_x pattern functions visually in the detector app, marked as beyond the chapter.
- **Geometrical-optics propagation through curved spacetime is asserted without derivation.** — Fine for most, but advanced learners may wonder about tails, dispersion and back-reaction. *Suggestion:* Mention the short-wave (Isaacson) expansion and when it fails, as optional depth.

## Tutor notes

- Open with a question: 'If a gravitational wave passes, do two free-floating marbles move apart? How would you know?' Let the learner discover that coordinates cannot answer it and a measurement of distance or light travel time can.
- Recommended order: wave equation and null k, gauge counting to two polarizations, TT coordinates paradox, proper distance, geodesic deviation as tidal force, ring picture, then detectors. Keep generation and energy for after the learner is comfortable with h as a strain.
- Check understanding of TT gauge by asking why particles stay at fixed coordinates yet move apart, and why this is not a contradiction.
- For polarization, ask the learner to predict the pattern after rotating the ring by 45 degrees, and contrast with light's 90 degrees; ask which polarization an L-shaped detector with arms on x and y misses for overhead waves.
- When the ruler question comes up, compare magnitudes: tidal acceleration h L omega^2 with interatomic forces; emphasize that detectors avoid rulers by timing light between free masses.
- For interferometers, stress 'two clocks compared' rather than 'wavelength ruler'; if asked whether the laser light is stretched, point to footnote 2 and the return-time derivation.
- Common learner question: 'Why is gravitational radiation quadrupolar?' Answer with conservation: constant total mass-energy kills monopole, fixed centre of mass kills dipole, and connect to electromagnetism's charge conservation.
- Quick estimation drills: h ~ (M/r) v^2 for a binary; strain times 4 km for mirror displacement; L ~ (32/5)(M omega)^{10/3} in units of c^5/G. Always convert masses to metres first.
- For the energy flux, narrate Feynman's sticky beads before any mathematics, then highlight that the detector parameters cancel; warn that the formula is an average valid in linearized theory.
- When discussing binaries, clarify omega (orbital) versus 2 omega (wave), and total versus individual mass; ask the learner to derive chirp mass for two 1.4 solar mass stars (about 1.22 solar masses).
- Standard sirens: ask 'What would you need to know to turn loudness into distance?' Guide to chirp mass from frequency drift, then inclination from polarization, then redshift degeneracy.
- Flag the printed slips (Eqs. 9.83, 9.92, 9.106-9.107, 9.124, 9.147, the 8 kpc conversion) proactively if the learner is working from the book, so they do not lose time doubting correct algebra.
- Learners at lower levels can skip the Helmholtz-Gauss derivation and accept h-bar_jk = (2/r) d^2 I_jk/dt^2 with the analogy to dipole radiation; advanced learners should do Exercise 9.29 or the projector derivation.
- Update dated statements when relevant: pulsar timing evidence for a nanohertz background (2023), LISA adoption (2024), and the growing catalogue of LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA events.
