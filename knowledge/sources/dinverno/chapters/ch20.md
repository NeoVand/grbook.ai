# DIV ch20 · Rotating black holes

> Builds the Kerr metric from Schwarzschild with the Newman-Janis complex-shift trick on a null tetrad, then reads off its rotation, ring singularity, two horizons, ergosphere, principal null congruences and global structure for sub-extremal, extremal and super-extremal spin, before closing the black-hole part of the book with Kerr-Newman, no-hair, the Penrose process, Komar and ADM mass, the singularity theorems and Hawking radiation.

**Pages:** printed 367–397 · pdf 382–412 · **Difficulty:** math 4/5, conceptual 5/5, novice-friendliness 2/5

The first half is heavy index and algebra work (complex tetrads, long line elements, geodesic eliminations) with most verification left to exercises; the Newman-Janis step is unmotivated magic for newcomers. Conceptually the chapter is dense with genuinely strange ideas - ring singularity, negative-r universes, closed timelike curves, naked singularities, ergosphere - presented mainly by analogy with RN and with only axis Penrose diagrams. The final three sections are descriptive surveys, easier to read but offering little derivation for the Komar/ADM, singularity-theorem and Hawking material.

## Role in the book

Final chapter of the black-hole sequence (ch15 Schwarzschild, ch16 classical tests, ch17 horizons and Eddington-Finkelstein charts, ch18 Kruskal and Penrose diagrams, ch19 Reissner-Nordstrom). It deliberately reuses the whole Schwarzschild/RN toolkit - redshift surfaces from g_00, horizons from g^11 = 0, null congruences, Eddington-Finkelstein extension, conformal diagrams - on the first non-spherical solution, so that genuinely rotational features (frame dragging, ergosphere, ring singularity, closed timelike curves) stand out against a familiar background. Its last three sections widen into a survey of global results (mass at infinity, singularity theorems, black-hole thermodynamics) that link back to the 3+1 formalism of ch14 and forward to the null focusing equation (23.44) of ch23 on radiation from isolated sources and black-body radiation (ch26).

## Learning objectives

- Reader can build a null tetrad (l, n, m, m-bar) from an orthonormal tetrad, verify its null and normalization conditions, and write g_ab and g^ab as sums of tetrad products.
- Reader can describe the Newman-Janis procedure (complexify r, rewrite the Schwarzschild tetrad, shift v and r by i a cos(theta), demand reality) and state honestly that it is a generating trick rather than a derivation from the field equations.
- Reader can write the Kerr metric in advanced Eddington-Finkelstein, Boyer-Lindquist and Kerr (Kerr-Schild) forms and state which one is convenient for which question.
- Reader can give three pieces of evidence that the Kerr parameter a encodes rotation: the t -> -t, phi -> -phi invariance, the dt dphi cross term matching a rotating-frame metric, and the 1/R expansion identifying angular momentum ma.
- Reader can explain why the Boyer-Lindquist r is not the Euclidean radius and derive R^2 = r^2 + a^2 sin^2(theta) and asymptotic flatness.
- Reader can locate the ring singularity, the infinite-redshift surfaces S_+/- (from g_00 = 0) and the horizons r_+/- (from g^11 = Delta = 0), and sketch how they nest, including the ergosphere.
- Reader can find the principal null congruences by exploiting the t and phi first integrals and the null condition, and use them to build Eddington-Finkelstein-type coordinates regular across r = r_+.
- Reader can show from the photon-orbit condition dr = dtheta = ds^2 = 0 that no observer can stay at rest relative to infinity inside S_+, and explain why S_+ is called the stationary limit surface and is crossable both ways.
- Reader can read the Penrose diagrams of Kerr for a^2 < m^2 and a^2 = m^2 (regions I, II, III, negative r, timelike ring, closed timelike curves) and describe the super-extremal case as a naked singularity motivating cosmic censorship.
- Reader can state the Kerr-Newman parameters, its horizon condition a^2 + epsilon^2 <= m^2, and the content (and status) of the no-hair/uniqueness result.
- Reader can describe the Penrose process qualitatively and say where the extracted energy comes from.
- Reader can compute total charge as a surface integral at infinity and use the same template to define and evaluate the Komar mass of Schwarzschild; reader can quote the ADM mass integral and the positive energy theorem.
- Reader can list the three ingredients of a pattern singularity theorem, state the timelike/null convergence and strong/weak energy conditions, and explain why the theorems prove geodesic incompleteness rather than infinite curvature.
- Reader can state the black-hole thermodynamic analogies (area-entropy, surface gravity-temperature) and evaluate the Hawking temperature T = hbar c^3 / (8 pi G k_B M), noting that it falls as mass grows.

## Teaching approach

A two-speed chapter. Sections 20.1-20.10 are a careful, component-level replay of the Schwarzschild and Reissner-Nordstrom programme on the Kerr metric: the metric is obtained by a stated trick rather than by solving the field equations, then interrogated with exactly the same diagnostics the reader already trusts (g_00 = 0 for infinite redshift, g^11 = 0 for null r = const surfaces, first integrals for null geodesics, a congruence-adapted coordinate change for extension, conformal diagrams by analogy with RN). Physical interpretation is built from suggestive arguments - discrete symmetries, a flat-space rotating-frame cross term, a large-R expansion - explicitly labelled as non-rigorous. Sections 20.11-20.14 switch to a descriptive survey of results the book cannot prove at its level (no-hair, Penrose process, positive energy, singularity theorems, Hawking radiation), with one fully worked calculation (Komar mass of Schwarzschild) motivated by an electromagnetic Gauss-law analogy. Many derivations are pushed into '(exercise)' markers keyed to the 18 end-of-chapter exercises.

**Style:** derivation-heavy, components-first, diagram-driven, spiral-revisit, analogy-driven, survey-overview, thought-experiment, computational-recipe

**Narrative arc**

1. Motivation: solving the vacuum equations directly for a rotating black hole is long, so obtain Kerr (and later Kerr-Newman) by a complex transformation instead; this needs null tetrads.
2. Formalism: frame metric, inverse frame metric, null vectors l and n from T +/- X, complex null m from Y + iZ, and the tetrad decomposition of g_ab and g^ab.
3. Construction: write the Schwarzschild inverse metric in advanced EF coordinates as a null tetrad, complexify r, apply v -> v + i a cos(theta), r -> r + i a cos(theta), and demand reality to get the Kerr tetrad.
4. Catalogue: present the resulting advanced EF line element, transform to Boyer-Lindquist, and quote Kerr's original Cartesian form as flat metric minus a null-vector square.
5. Interpretation: parameters m and a; stationarity and axisymmetry; discrete t,phi and t,a symmetries; dt dphi cross term compared with a rotating frame in flat space; r versus Euclidean R; asymptotic flatness; 1/R expansion suggesting J proportional to ma.
6. Singular structure: curvature invariant blows up at rho = 0, which is a ring; infinite-redshift surfaces S_+/- from g_00; horizons r_+/- from Delta = 0; three regions; ergosphere between r_+ and S_+ (Fig. 20.3 summary).
7. Light-cone structure: search for theta = const null geodesics, obtain the constraint between L and N, solve for principal outgoing/ingoing congruences, integrate to logarithms showing r = r_+ is a coordinate singularity.
8. Extension: pick coordinates in which the ingoing principal congruence becomes dt-bar = -dr, recovering the advanced EF form and the light-cone diagram (Fig. 20.4).
9. Stationary limit: photons forced to circle at fixed r, theta; counter-rotation becomes impossible inside S_+; spatial diagram of wavefronts (Fig. 20.5).
10. Global structure: maximal extensions by analogy with RN for a^2 < m^2 and a^2 = m^2 (Figs 20.6-20.7), negative r, timelike avoidable ring and closed timelike curves; super-extremal case as two sheets glued through the disc and a naked singularity (Figs 20.8-20.9); cosmic censorship.
11. Astrophysics: collapse of a rotating star, Kerr-Newman by the same trick, no-hair and final-state picture (Fig. 20.10), white holes as the time reverse, Penrose process and superradiance (Fig. 20.11).
12. Global mass: charge as a flux integral at infinity (Fig. 20.12), Komar mass by analogy with a Killing-vector current, normalization fixed by Schwarzschild, asymptotic Killing vectors, ADM mass and positive energy theorem.
13. Singularity theorems: history from Finkelstein to Penrose 1965 and Hawking-Penrose 1970, the three-ingredient pattern, energy and genericity conditions, and the caveat that incompleteness is not the same as infinite curvature.
14. Quantum coda: area theorem and thermodynamic analogy, entropy as lost information, Hawking temperature, pair-creation picture (Fig. 20.13), evaporation times and primordial black holes.

**Signature moves**

- Obtains a hard exact solution by an explicitly advertised 'trick' (Newman-Janis) on a null tetrad, then immediately cross-checks by reducing to Schwarzschild when a -> 0.
- Collects three coordinate forms of the same metric in one section and then chooses whichever form makes each property transparent (Boyer-Lindquist for symmetries and horizons, Kerr-Schild for asymptotics and the ring, advanced EF for extension).
- Uses discrete symmetries (t -> -t with phi -> -phi, or with a -> -a) as physical evidence of spin without any dynamics.
- Compares the Kerr dt dphi cross term with the cross term produced by transforming flat space to a rotating frame, while openly flagging that rigid rotation in relativity has not been treated.
- Reuses the exact diagnostic recipe of earlier chapters: g_00 = 0 gives infinite redshift, g^11 = 0 gives a null r = const hypersurface, radial null congruences give adapted coordinates.
- Derives principal null congruences by counting equations: four first-integral equations for three unknowns force an algebraic constraint that selects the congruences.
- Characterizes the stationary limit operationally with a photon constrained to circle at fixed r and theta and asks when dphi/dt can be non-positive.
- Draws global structure 'by analogy with Reissner-Nordstrom' rather than deriving Kruskal-like charts, leaning on Penrose diagrams already familiar from ch18-19.
- Motivates a gravitational mass integral from electromagnetic Gauss's law and fixes the normalization constant by demanding the Schwarzschild answer.
- Presents the singularity theorems as a template of three hypotheses (boundary/initial condition, curvature condition, causality condition) rather than proving any of them.
- Ends heavy mathematics with humour and cartoons (vase and television set, civilization living off a black hole) to fix the physical message.

## Section by section

### §20.1 Null tetrads — p.367 (pdf 382)

Explains why the chapter uses a shortcut to Kerr and introduces the frame formalism needed for it. A set of four independent vectors defines a frame metric by contraction with g_ab, whose inverse raises and lowers frame indices; the relation can be inverted to rebuild g_ab from the tetrad. Starting from an orthonormal tetrad, the combinations (T +/- X)/sqrt2 give two real null vectors with l.n = 1, and (Y +/- iZ)/sqrt2 give a complex conjugate pair of null vectors with m.m-bar = -1. The resulting null tetrad has an off-diagonal frame metric, and the spacetime metric becomes l n + n l - m m-bar - m-bar m.

**Concepts:** frame formalism, frame metric, null tetrad, complex null vector, Newman-Penrose formalism

**Key moves**
- Define g_ij = g_ab e_i^a e_j^b and argue invertibility from linear independence plus non-degeneracy of g_ab.
- Rotate the T-X pair by 45 degrees in the light-cone sense to obtain l and n, null and cross-normalized to 1 (Fig. 20.1).
- Complexify the transverse pair Y, Z into m and m-bar, each null, with m.m-bar = -1.
- Read off the null-tetrad frame metric (20.14) and hence the decompositions (20.15)-(20.16) of g_ab and g^ab.

### §20.2 The Kerr solution from a complex transformation — p.369 (pdf 384)

Carries out the Newman-Janis construction. The inverse Schwarzschild metric in advanced Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates is written as a null tetrad with l along the r direction, n containing the 1 - 2m/r factor and m spanning the angular directions. Allowing r to be complex, the tetrad is rewritten with 1/r replaced by the average of 1/r and 1/r-bar and with 1/r-bar in m, keeping l and n real. The formal shift v -> v + i a cos(theta), r -> r + i a cos(theta), followed by demanding the new v and r be real, produces a tetrad whose inverse metric is that of Kerr.

**Concepts:** Newman-Janis algorithm, null tetrad, Kerr solution, advanced Eddington-Finkelstein form of Kerr

**Key moves**
- List the nonzero contravariant components of Schwarzschild in advanced EF coordinates (20.17).
- Factor g^ab into the tetrad (20.18) using (20.16).
- Complexify r in a specific, non-unique way (20.19): real parts in l and n, conjugate r in m.
- Apply the complex shift (20.20) and impose reality of v' and r' to obtain the Kerr tetrad (20.21), which picks up a -i a sin(theta) component along the v and r directions.

### §20.3 The three main forms of the Kerr solution — p.370 (pdf 385)

Assembles the line element produced by the trick, in advanced EF form with functions rho^2 = r^2 + a^2 cos^2(theta). A transformation written in differentials (introducing Delta = r^2 - 2mr + a^2) converts it to the Boyer-Lindquist form, which is the Kerr analogue of Schwarzschild coordinates. Kerr's own Cartesian-type form is also quoted; it has the structure flat metric minus lambda times the square of a covector that is null in the flat metric, which is how Kerr originally found the solution, and it reduces to lambda = 2m/r with a radial null covector for Schwarzschild.

**Concepts:** Kerr solution, advanced Eddington-Finkelstein form of Kerr, Boyer-Lindquist coordinates, Kerr-Schild form

**Key moves**
- Present (20.22) with rho^2 (20.23) and rename phi to phi-bar in anticipation of the later coordinate change.
- Give the transformation to Boyer-Lindquist coordinates as differential relations (20.24)-(20.25), promising the explanation in §20.7.
- Write Boyer-Lindquist (20.27) as two perfect squares in (dt - a sin^2 theta dphi) and ((r^2+a^2) dphi - a dt).
- Quote the Kerr form (20.28) with Cartesian-type coordinates (20.29) and abstract it to the Kerr-Schild structure (20.30)-(20.31).

### §20.4 Basic properties of the Kerr solution — p.372 (pdf 387)

Uses the Boyer-Lindquist form to argue that Kerr is a stationary, axisymmetric field of a spinning mass. Setting a = 0 gives Schwarzschild, identifying m. Independence of t and phi gives two Killing vectors; the metric is invariant under simultaneous reversal of t and phi (or t and a) but not under either alone, as expected for a spinning body. The single dt dphi cross term is compared with the cross term that appears when flat space is written in a uniformly rotating frame. The Boyer-Lindquist r is shown to differ from the Euclidean radius R except asymptotically; the metric approaches Minkowski at large R, and expanding in 1/R shows an off-diagonal g_0alpha term proportional to ma, suggesting angular momentum ma, supported by Lense-Thirring and multipole-moment arguments.

**Concepts:** stationary spacetime, axisymmetric spacetime, Kerr spin parameter, frame dragging, Boyer-Lindquist radial coordinate, asymptotic flatness, Lense-Thirring effect

**Key moves**
- Read off Killing vectors d/dt and d/dphi and the circular orbits of d/dphi.
- Note invariance under (t, phi) -> (-t, -phi) and (t, a) -> (-t, -a): time reversal of a spinning body equals reversing its spin.
- Transform flat space in cylindrical coordinates to a rotating frame (20.37)-(20.39) and exhibit the resulting dphi' dt term, with a disclaimer about rigid rotation.
- Compute R^2 = r^2 + a^2 sin^2 theta from the Kerr form and expand for r >> a (20.41)-(20.42).
- Expand (20.28) in 1/R (20.43) to see the mass term and a rotation term proportional to ma.

### §20.5 Singularities and horizons — p.374 (pdf 389)

Locates the genuine singularity where the curvature invariant R^abcd R_abcd diverges, rho = 0, which in Kerr-form Cartesian coordinates is a ring of radius a in the equatorial plane. Infinite-redshift surfaces are taken where g_00 vanishes, giving two axisymmetric surfaces S_+/- that reduce to r = 2m and r = 0 for a = 0. The Killing vector d/dt is timelike outside S_+ and inside S_-, spacelike between. Horizons are found, as for Schwarzschild, where r = const surfaces become null, g^11 = -Delta/rho^2 = 0, giving r_+/- for a^2 < m^2, three regular regions, and an ergosphere between r_+ and S_+. A nine-point summary of Kerr properties closes the section.

**Concepts:** ring singularity, Kretschmann scalar, infinite-redshift surface, Kerr event horizons, ergosphere, stationary limit surface

**Key moves**
- Solve rho^2 = 0 as r = 0 and theta = pi/2 and translate via (20.29) to x^2 + y^2 = a^2, z = 0.
- Assert (without proof in stationary non-static case) that infinite redshift still occurs where g_00 = 0 and solve for r_{S+/-} (20.46).
- Evaluate the norm of the time-translation Killing vector as g_00 to classify where it is timelike, null or spacelike.
- Compute g^11 = -Delta/rho^2 and set Delta = 0 for the horizons (20.47); compare with S_+/- and note they touch only on the axis.

### §20.6 The principal null congruences — p.377 (pdf 392)

Since rotation drags light, there are no radial null geodesics; the analogue is a pair of congruences at constant theta. Using the Boyer-Lindquist Lagrangian, the ignorable t and phi give first integrals L and N, the null condition gives a third, and the theta Euler-Lagrange equation with theta fixed gives a fourth. Four equations in three unknown velocities force the constraint (N + aL sin^2 theta)(N - aL sin^2 theta) = 0; choosing one factor yields t-dot, r-dot = +/- L, phi-dot, so r is an affine parameter. Integrating dt/dr and dphi/dr gives logarithms diverging at r_+/-, identifying the outgoing and ingoing principal congruences and signalling that r = r_+ is a coordinate singularity of Boyer-Lindquist coordinates.

**Concepts:** principal null congruences, frame dragging, affine parameter, coordinate singularity

**Key moves**
- Argue from the non-removability of rotation that Kerr cannot be transformed into Schwarzschild, so radial null lines do not exist.
- Write first integrals (20.49)-(20.50), the null condition (20.51) and the theta equation (20.52).
- Use equation counting to derive the constraint (20.53) and restrict to (20.54).
- Solve (20.55)-(20.57) and integrate (20.58)-(20.59) by partial fractions over r_+ and r_- to (20.60)-(20.61).
- Use the sign of Delta in regions I, II, III to identify outgoing behaviour and obtain the ingoing congruence by t -> -t, phi -> -phi.

### §20.7 Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates — p.379 (pdf 394)

Mirrors the Schwarzschild construction: find new coordinates (t-bar, r, theta, phi-bar) in which the ingoing principal congruence is simply dt-bar = -dr at fixed angles. The needed shifts are dt-bar = dt + (2mr/Delta) dr and dphi-bar = dphi + (a/Delta) dr, and with advanced time v = t-bar + r the Boyer-Lindquist metric turns back into the advanced EF form produced by the Newman-Janis trick, which is regular through r_+. The corresponding two-dimensional spacetime diagram (Fig. 20.4) is compared with Reissner-Nordstrom.

**Concepts:** advanced Eddington-Finkelstein form of Kerr, principal null congruences, coordinate singularity

**Key moves**
- Write the ingoing congruence differentially (20.62)-(20.63) and state the target form (20.65).
- Choose the transformation (20.66)-(20.67) that cancels the dr parts along the congruence.
- Define v = t-bar + r (20.68) and recognize the result as (20.22), closing the loop opened in §20.3.

### §20.8 The stationary limit — p.381 (pdf 396)

Considers the non-geodesic null curves with dr = dtheta = 0 in region I, representing light forced to circle at fixed r and theta. Solving the Boyer-Lindquist null condition gives two values of dphi/dt; the co-rotating one is always positive, and the counter-rotating one is non-positive only outside S_+ and vanishes on it. Hence on S_+ even light aimed against the rotation cannot move relative to infinity, and inside the ergosphere everything must co-rotate. This justifies calling S_+ the stationary limit surface; it is timelike (so crossable in both directions) except at the poles where it is null and touches the horizon. The equatorial spatial diagram (Fig. 20.5) shows the tipping of wavefronts.

**Concepts:** stationary limit surface, ergosphere, frame dragging, static observer

**Key moves**
- Impose dr = dtheta = ds^2 = 0 and solve the quadratic for dphi/dt (20.70).
- Show the denominator of the minus-branch is positive for r > r_+.
- Reduce dphi/dt <= 0 to Delta^{1/2} >= a sin theta, i.e. r >= r_{S+} (20.71).
- Interpret: on S_+ a counter-rotating photon is momentarily stationary; inside it is swept forward.

### §20.9 Maximal extension for the case a^2 < m^2 — p.382 (pdf 397)

Describes, without full derivation, how advanced and retarded EF-type coordinates are patched together as for Reissner-Nordstrom to build the maximal analytic extension. The new feature is that r can be continued through the ring to negative values. Along the symmetry axis the conformal diagram (Fig. 20.6) is an infinite chain: stationary asymptotically flat regions I, non-stationary regions II where every point lies on a trapped surface, and regions III containing a timelike (hence avoidable) ring singularity and closed timelike curves. For a^2 = m^2 the horizons merge, regions II disappear, and the diagram resembles extreme RN (Fig. 20.7).

**Concepts:** maximal analytic extension of Kerr, Penrose diagram, trapped surface, closed timelike curves, extremal Kerr black hole, negative-r region

**Key moves**
- Introduce u_+/- and phi_+/- (advanced and retarded) and combine charts as in ch19.
- Restrict the conformal picture to the axis where horizons and infinite-redshift surfaces coincide.
- Classify regions I, II, III by stationarity, trapping and causality violation.
- Take the limit a^2 = m^2 to remove regions II.

### §20.10 Maximal extension for the case a^2 > m^2 — p.384 (pdf 399)

With a^2 > m^2, Delta never vanishes, so there are no horizons, only the ring. On a t = const slice the r = const surfaces are confocal ellipsoids that flatten to the disc bounded by the ring at r = 0. Continuing r to negative values through the disc means gluing the top of the disc to the bottom of a copy in a second sheet and vice versa (Fig. 20.8); passing through twice returns you. The negative-r sheet is asymptotically flat with negative mass. Near the ring d/dphi is timelike, giving closed timelike curves that can be deformed through any point, so causality fails badly. Only equatorial causal geodesics from the positive-r side hit the ring, but signals from it can spiral out (Fig. 20.9): a naked singularity, which motivates Penrose's cosmic censorship hypothesis.

**Concepts:** super-extremal Kerr, naked singularity, cosmic censorship hypothesis, closed timelike curves, negative-r region, geodesic incompleteness

**Key moves**
- Invert (20.29) as a quartic for r in terms of x, y, z and identify confocal ellipsoids degenerating to the disc.
- Construct the two-sheeted extension by identifying opposite faces of the disc.
- Show d/dphi becomes timelike near the ring, producing closed timelike curves.
- Observe the absence of horizons makes the ring visible from infinity and state cosmic censorship.

### §20.11 Rotating black holes — p.385 (pdf 400)

Imagines a rotating star with Kerr exterior collapsing: observers cross r_+ and then r_-, but unlike Schwarzschild the collapse may halt and the extension suggests emergence into a new asymptotic region (no realistic interior solution is known; a toroidal source is speculated). Applying the Newman-Janis trick to Reissner-Nordstrom gives the Kerr-Newman solution with mass, spin and charge, its stationary limit surface and outer horizon for a^2 + epsilon^2 <= m^2. Realistic collapse radiates away asymmetries once a horizon forms and settles to Kerr-Newman: the no-hair picture (Fig. 20.10), whose time reverse is a white hole spitting out objects. Penrose's process of splitting a particle in the ergosphere so that one fragment escapes with more energy than was sent in, at the cost of the hole's spin, is described (Fig. 20.11), together with its link to superradiance and Zel'dovich's suggestion.

**Concepts:** gravitational collapse to a rotating black hole, Kerr-Newman solution, no-hair theorem, white hole, Penrose process, superradiance

**Key moves**
- Contrast the collapse sequence with Schwarzschild: halting inside r_- is allowed.
- Reapply the complex trick to the RN seed to get (20.72) and its surfaces (20.73)-(20.74).
- State the asymptotic uniqueness result and the dynamical picture of a trapped surface forming inside an expanding, then settling, event horizon.
- Describe energy extraction in the ergosphere and trace the energy budget to the hole's angular momentum.

### §20.12 The definition of mass in general relativity — p.388 (pdf 403)

Since general relativity has no local gravitational energy density, mass is defined for isolated systems by surface integrals at infinity. Electromagnetism motivates the construction: with the sourced Maxwell equation, total charge in a volume on a spacelike hypersurface becomes, by the divergence theorem, a flux of F^ab through its boundary, which gives q for a point charge. Replacing the current by R^ab K_b for a timelike Killing vector K and using the Killing identity turns the volume integral into a boundary integral of the derivative of K; with factor 1/4pi this Komar integral gives m for Schwarzschild. Because only the boundary is used, K need only be an asymptotic Killing vector. The ADM mass, an alternative from the Hamiltonian formulation, agrees for time-independent perturbations, and the Schoen-Yau positive energy theorem guarantees non-negative ADM mass under the dominant energy condition.

**Concepts:** Gauss-law charge integral, Komar mass, asymptotic Killing vector, ADM mass, positive energy theorem, Killing vector Ricci identity, Eulerian observer

**Key moves**
- Write the sourced Maxwell equation (20.75) and total charge as a volume integral over V (20.76), convert to a surface flux (20.78), and push the surface to infinity (20.79).
- Evaluate with the Minkowski asymptotic metric to get (20.81) and check with a point charge (20.82).
- Define J^a = R^ab K_b and E_total (20.83)-(20.84); use (20.85) to write J as a divergence and apply the divergence theorem (20.86).
- For Schwarzschild compute unit normals (20.87)-(20.88), reduce the integrand to a Christoffel symbol (20.89), and integrate to m (20.90).
- Generalize to asymptotic Killing vectors (20.91) and quote the ADM boundary integral (20.92).

### §20.13 The singularity theorems — p.391 (pdf 406)

Recounts how singularities went from being dismissed as artefacts of symmetry (Schwarzschild 1916, Finkelstein 1958) to being expected generically after Penrose's 1965 theorem introduced trapped surfaces and geodesic incompleteness as the definition of a singular spacetime, followed by Hawking's cosmological version and the work of Hawking, Penrose, Ellis, Geroch and others. The general pattern: an initial/boundary condition, a curvature condition and a causality condition imply incomplete causal geodesics. For the 1970 Hawking-Penrose theorem these are a trapped surface, no closed timelike curves, and the timelike convergence condition, which via the Einstein equations becomes the strong energy condition; null versions use the null convergence condition implied by the weak energy condition; a genericity condition is also needed. The theorems predict incompleteness in models near Kerr-Newman collapse, Friedmann big bang/crunch and colliding plane waves, but do not establish curvature blow-up or the form of generic singularities.

**Concepts:** geodesic incompleteness, trapped surface, singularity theorems, Hawking-Penrose singularity theorem, timelike convergence condition, strong energy condition, null convergence condition, weak energy condition, generic condition, Raychaudhuri focusing

**Key moves**
- Define a singular spacetime by an inextendible causal geodesic of finite affine length.
- State the three-part template of a pattern singularity theorem.
- Translate the curvature condition (20.93) into matter terms (20.94)-(20.95) and the null version (20.96)-(20.97).
- Add the genericity condition and discuss what the theorems do not claim.

### §20.14 Black hole thermodynamics and Hawking radiation — p.394 (pdf 409)

A brief excursion beyond classical relativity. Horizon area grows when matter falls in and when holes merge, suggesting an analogy with entropy; Hawking and collaborators found analogues of the laws of thermodynamics, with the first law linking changes of mass and area through the surface gravity, which is uniform over the horizon like equilibrium temperature. Entropy is linked to the finite (by the uncertainty principle) number of possible progenitor configurations lost in collapse; finite entropy implies a finite temperature, which conflicts with a classical perfect absorber until Hawking showed that black holes emit thermal radiation with temperature inversely proportional to mass. A virtual-pair picture with one member falling in, reinterpreted as backward-in-time tunnelling (Fig. 20.13), explains the emission, which needs no rotation. Evaporation accelerates as mass drops; solar-mass holes last about 10^67 years, while primordial mini holes could be exploding now, though gamma-ray searches are inconclusive.

**Concepts:** black hole area theorem, black hole thermodynamics, surface gravity, Bekenstein-Hawking entropy, Hawking radiation, black hole evaporation, primordial black holes

**Key moves**
- Motivate area-entropy from monotonic growth under accretion and merger.
- Link entropy to information loss via no-hair and count of configurations.
- Resolve the equilibrium paradox by Hawking emission and quote T (20.98) with a numerical scale.
- Give a heuristic pair-creation/tunnelling picture and derive the runaway nature of evaporation.

## Concepts

### frame formalism

*technique · introduced* · also: tetrad formalism, frames

Describing tensors by their components along a chosen set of four linearly independent vector fields e_i^a (a tetrad) rather than along coordinate basis vectors, with frame indices i, j raised and lowered by the frame metric.

**How introduced:** Recalled from the orthonormal tetrad of §10.5 and generalized to arbitrary independent vector fields as preparation for null tetrads.

**Prerequisites:** tetrad, metric tensor

$$
g_{ij}=g_{ab}e_i{}^a e_j{}^b
$$

**Notes:** The book only defines the frame metric; no frame connection coefficients or spin coefficients are introduced.

**Where:** DIV §20.1 p.367 (pdf 382)

### frame metric

*mathematical-object · introduced* · also: tetrad metric, g_ij

The 4x4 matrix of scalars g_ij = g_ab e_i^a e_j^b formed from the inner products of tetrad vectors; it is invertible (inverse g^ij) and lets the metric be rebuilt as g_ab = g_ij e^i_a e^j_b.

**How introduced:** Defined at a point from four independent vectors; invertibility is argued from independence and non-degeneracy of g_ab.

**Prerequisites:** frame formalism

$$
g_{ij}g^{jk}=\delta_i^k
$$

$$
g_{ab}=g_{ij}e^i{}_a e^j{}_b
$$

**Where:** DIV §20.1 p.367 (pdf 382); DIV §20.1 p.368 (pdf 383)

### null tetrad

*mathematical-object · developed* · also: Newman-Penrose tetrad, complex null tetrad

A tetrad (l^a, n^a, m^a, m-bar^a) of two real future-pointing null vectors with l.n = 1 and a complex conjugate pair of null vectors orthogonal to them with m.m-bar = -1 (in signature +---), so that g_ab = l_a n_b + n_a l_b - m_a m-bar_b - m-bar_a m_b.

**How introduced:** Built from an orthonormal tetrad by taking (T +/- X)/sqrt2 and (Y +/- iZ)/sqrt2, with a sketch of l and n as the two light-cone directions in the T-X plane (Fig. 20.1).

**Prerequisites:** frame metric, null vector, orthonormal tetrad

$$
\ell^a\ell_a=n^an_a=m^am_a=0
$$

$$
\ell^an_a=1,\; m^a\bar m_a=-1
$$

$$
g^{ab}=\ell^an^b+\ell^bn^a-m^a\bar m^b-m^b\bar m^a
$$

**Where:** DIV §20.1 p.368 (pdf 383); DIV §20.1 p.369 (pdf 384)

### complex null vector

*mathematical-object · introduced* · also: m^a, complex null vector m

A complex vector m^a = (Y^a + i Z^a)/sqrt2 built from two orthogonal unit spacelike vectors; it satisfies m.m = 0 even though it is not a real light-like direction, and its conjugate completes the null tetrad.

**How introduced:** Introduced as an 'advantageous' complexification of the transverse spatial pair so that all four tetrad legs are null.

**Prerequisites:** null tetrad

$$
m^a=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(Y^a+iZ^a)
$$

**Where:** DIV §20.1 p.368 (pdf 383)

### Newman-Penrose formalism

*technique · mention* · also: NP formalism

A version of the frame formalism in which all components are taken along a null tetrad, widely used in gravitational radiation and exact-solution work.

**How introduced:** Mentioned as the systematic framework behind null tetrads; only the tetrad definition is developed here.

**Prerequisites:** null tetrad

**Where:** DIV §20.1 p.367 (pdf 382)

### Newman-Janis algorithm

*technique · core* · also: Newman-Janis trick, complex transformation trick, complex coordinate transformation

A solution-generating heuristic: write a static seed metric's inverse as a null tetrad in advanced null coordinates, allow r to be complex with a prescribed placement of r and r-bar, perform the complex shift v -> v + i a cos(theta), r -> r + i a cos(theta), and require the new v and r to be real; applied to Schwarzschild it yields Kerr, and to Reissner-Nordstrom it yields Kerr-Newman.

**How introduced:** Motivated as a shortcut avoiding a lengthy direct integration of the vacuum field equations; executed step by step on the Schwarzschild tetrad in advanced EF coordinates and later reused on RN.

**Prerequisites:** null tetrad, advanced Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates, Schwarzschild solution

$$
v\to v+ia\cos\theta,\quad r\to r+ia\cos\theta
$$

**Notes:** The book calls it a trick and offers no explanation of why it works; the complexification step is not unique and the method is not a general theorem.

**Where:** DIV §20.2 p.369 (pdf 384); DIV §20.2 p.370 (pdf 385); DIV §20.11 p.385 (pdf 400)

### Kerr solution

*solution-or-model · core* · also: Kerr metric, Kerr vacuum solution

The two-parameter (mass m, specific angular momentum a) stationary, axisymmetric, asymptotically flat vacuum solution of Einstein's equations describing the exterior of a rotating black hole; it reduces to Schwarzschild when a = 0.

**How introduced:** Obtained from Schwarzschild by the Newman-Janis trick, displayed in three coordinate forms, then interrogated with the same diagnostics used for Schwarzschild and RN.

**Prerequisites:** Schwarzschild solution, Newman-Janis algorithm

$$
ds^2=\frac{\Delta}{\rho^2}(dt-a\sin^2\theta\,d\phi)^2-\frac{\sin^2\theta}{\rho^2}[(r^2+a^2)d\phi-a\,dt]^2-\frac{\rho^2}{\Delta}dr^2-\rho^2d\theta^2
$$

**Notes:** A nine-item property summary is given at the end of §20.5.

**Where:** DIV §20.3 p.370 (pdf 385); DIV §20.4 p.372 (pdf 387); DIV §20.5 p.376 (pdf 391)

### Boyer-Lindquist coordinates

*convention · core* · also: canonical (t, phi) coordinates, Boyer-Lindquist form

Coordinates (t, r, theta, phi) in which the Kerr metric is independent of t and phi, has a single off-diagonal dt dphi term, and reduces to Schwarzschild coordinates at a = 0; they are singular where Delta = r^2 - 2mr + a^2 vanishes.

**How introduced:** Reached from the advanced EF form by differential relations dv = dt + (r^2 + a^2)/Delta dr and dphi-bar = dphi + a/Delta dr, whose origin is explained in §20.7.

**Prerequisites:** Kerr solution, coordinate transformation

$$
\Delta=r^2-2mr+a^2
$$

$$
\rho^2=r^2+a^2\cos^2\theta
$$

**Where:** DIV §20.3 p.370 (pdf 385); DIV §20.3 p.371 (pdf 386); DIV §20.4 p.372 (pdf 387)

### Kerr-Schild form

*solution-or-model · introduced* · also: Kerr form, Cartesian form of Kerr, Kerr-Schild metric

A way of writing a metric as the Minkowski metric minus lambda times the square of a covector l_a that is null with respect to the Minkowski metric; Kerr's original Cartesian-type form of his solution has this structure, with Schwarzschild as the case lambda = 2m/r, l_a = (1, x/r, y/r, z/r).

**How introduced:** Quoted as Kerr's original discovery form, then abstracted to the general structure and specialized back to Schwarzschild.

**Prerequisites:** Kerr solution, null vector, Minkowski metric

$$
ds^2=\eta_{ab}dx^adx^b-\lambda\,\ell_a\ell_b\,dx^adx^b,\quad \eta_{ab}\ell^a\ell^b=0
$$

**Notes:** The book calls it the 'Kerr form'; the standard name Kerr-Schild is not used. The printed components (20.33) have a^2 + y^2 in the denominators where a^2 + r^2 is meant.

**Where:** DIV §20.3 p.371 (pdf 386); DIV §20.3 p.372 (pdf 387)

### advanced Eddington-Finkelstein form of Kerr

*solution-or-model · developed* · also: ingoing Kerr coordinates, Kerr coordinates (v, r, theta, phi-bar)

The Kerr metric written in coordinates (v, r, theta, phi-bar) adapted to the ingoing principal null congruence, in which the metric is regular across the outer horizon r = r_+.

**How introduced:** Appears first as the direct output of the Newman-Janis trick (§20.3) and is then re-derived in §20.7 as the coordinate system in which ingoing principal null geodesics become lines of constant v at fixed theta and phi-bar.

**Prerequisites:** principal null congruences, Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates

$$
v=\bar t+r,\quad d\bar t=dt+\frac{2mr}{\Delta}dr,\quad d\bar\phi=d\phi+\frac{a}{\Delta}dr
$$

**Where:** DIV §20.3 p.370 (pdf 385); DIV §20.7 p.380 (pdf 395)

### Kerr spin parameter

*physical-quantity · core* · also: a, specific angular momentum, rotation parameter

The parameter a in the Kerr metric, equal to the angular momentum per unit mass J/m (dimension of length in geometrized units); J = ma is the angular momentum measured at infinity and the sign of a gives the sense of rotation.

**How introduced:** Inferred from three suggestive arguments: invariance under (t, a) -> (-t, -a), comparison with Lense-Thirring and multipole moments, and the ma coefficient in the 1/R expansion of g_0alpha.

**Prerequisites:** Kerr solution, angular momentum

$$
J=ma
$$

**Notes:** The text says a is 'related to the angular velocity'; strictly it is angular momentum per unit mass, not an angular velocity.

**Where:** DIV §20.4 p.372 (pdf 387); DIV §20.4 p.374 (pdf 389)

### stationary spacetime

*definition · developed* · also: stationarity

A spacetime admitting a Killing vector field that is timelike (at least in an asymptotic region), so the geometry does not change along its orbits; unlike a static spacetime it may contain time-space cross terms that cannot be removed.

**How introduced:** Read off from t-independence of the Boyer-Lindquist metric, with the caveat that d/dt is timelike only outside S_+.

**Prerequisites:** Killing vector

$$
X^a=\delta^a_0,\; X_aX^a=g_{00}
$$

**Where:** DIV §20.4 p.372 (pdf 387); DIV §20.5 p.375 (pdf 390)

### axisymmetric spacetime

*definition · introduced* · also: axial symmetry

A spacetime with a Killing vector field (here d/dphi) whose orbits are closed circles about an invariantly defined axis, so the geometry is unchanged under rotations about that axis.

**How introduced:** Read off from phi-independence of the Boyer-Lindquist metric; the orbits t, r, theta = const are identified as circles.

**Prerequisites:** Killing vector

**Where:** DIV §20.4 p.372 (pdf 387)

### discrete t-phi reflection symmetry

*principle · introduced* · also: t -> -t, phi -> -phi invariance, time reversal with spin reversal

The Kerr metric is unchanged by reversing t and phi together (or t and a together) but not by reversing either alone, which is the symmetry expected of the field of a spinning body.

**How introduced:** Used as the first physical clue that Kerr describes rotation: running a spinning object backward in time looks like the same object spinning the other way.

**Prerequisites:** Kerr solution

$$
t\to-t,\;\phi\to-\phi
$$

**Where:** DIV §20.4 p.372 (pdf 387); DIV §20.5 p.376 (pdf 391)

### frame dragging

*phenomenon · developed* · also: dragging of inertial frames, rotational dragging

The effect of a rotating mass on local inertial frames and light cones: near the source, freely falling frames and light are forced to rotate with it, signalled in the metric by an irremovable dt dphi term and culminating in compulsory co-rotation inside the ergosphere.

**How introduced:** Suggested by the dt dphi cross term compared with a flat metric in a rotating frame (§20.4), invoked to explain the absence of radial null geodesics (§20.6), and made operational at the stationary limit (§20.8).

**Prerequisites:** Kerr solution, Boyer-Lindquist coordinates

$$
ds^2=(1-a^2r^2)dt^2-2ar^2d\phi'dt-(dr^2+r^2d\phi'^2+dz^2)
$$

**Notes:** The term 'frame dragging' is used only loosely ('drag space around with it'); the book stresses that rotation cannot be transformed away, unlike in Newtonian theory.

**Where:** DIV §20.4 p.373 (pdf 388); DIV §20.6 p.377 (pdf 392); DIV §20.8 p.381 (pdf 396)

### Lense-Thirring effect

*phenomenon · mention* · also: Lense-Thirring solution

The weak-field gravitomagnetic field outside a slowly rotating body, which drags inertial frames and whose off-diagonal metric terms are fixed by the body's angular momentum.

**How introduced:** Mentioned as one independent argument identifying ma with the Kerr angular momentum.

**Prerequisites:** weak-field limit

**Where:** DIV §20.4 p.374 (pdf 389)

### Boyer-Lindquist radial coordinate

*convention · developed* · also: r in Kerr, spheroidal radius

The Kerr coordinate r is not the Euclidean distance: with Kerr-form Cartesian coordinates, R^2 = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = r^2 + a^2 sin^2 theta, so r = const surfaces are confocal oblate spheroids that coincide with spheres only asymptotically or when a = 0.

**How introduced:** Shown by computing R from the Kerr-form coordinate relations and expanding for r >> a; later used to see r = 0 as a disc and to extend r negative.

**Prerequisites:** Kerr-Schild form

$$
R^2=r^2+a^2\sin^2\theta
$$

$$
r^4-(x^2+y^2+z^2-a^2)r^2-a^2z^2=0
$$

**Notes:** When the book gives S_+ a 'radius' 2m at the equator and m + (m^2 - a^2)^{1/2} at the poles, these are values of the coordinate r, not Euclidean or proper radii; worth flagging given this very point.

**Where:** DIV §20.4 p.373 (pdf 388); DIV §20.10 p.384 (pdf 399)

### asymptotic flatness

*definition · developed* · also: asymptotically flat

The metric approaches the Minkowski metric (in suitable coordinates) at large distance from the source, so that spacetime looks flat far away and quantities like total mass can be defined at infinity.

**How introduced:** Checked for Kerr by letting R -> infinity in the Kerr form; used again as the setting for Komar and ADM mass.

**Prerequisites:** Minkowski metric

$$
g_{ab}\to\eta_{ab}\;\text{as}\;R\to\infty
$$

**Where:** DIV §20.4 p.373 (pdf 388); DIV §20.12 p.389 (pdf 404); DIV §20.12 p.391 (pdf 406)

### ring singularity

*phenomenon · core* · also: Kerr ring singularity

The curvature singularity of Kerr at rho^2 = r^2 + a^2 cos^2 theta = 0, i.e. r = 0 on the equator, which in Kerr-form Cartesian coordinates is a ring x^2 + y^2 = a^2 in the plane z = 0; it is timelike and can be avoided, and one can pass through the disc it bounds into a negative-r region.

**How introduced:** Found from divergence of the invariant R^abcd R_abcd at rho = 0 and translated into Cartesian terms; described as surprising.

**Prerequisites:** Kretschmann scalar, Boyer-Lindquist radial coordinate

$$
x^2+y^2=a^2,\;z=0

**Where:** DIV §20.5 p.374 (pdf 389); DIV §20.9 p.382 (pdf 397); DIV §20.10 p.384 (pdf 399)

### Kretschmann scalar

*mathematical-object · mention* · also: Riemann invariant R^abcd R_abcd

The curvature invariant formed by fully contracting the Riemann tensor with itself; its divergence signals a genuine curvature singularity independent of coordinates.

**How introduced:** Used without computation to certify that rho = 0 is intrinsic.

**Prerequisites:** Riemann tensor

$$
R^{abcd}R_{abcd}

**Where:** DIV §20.5 p.374 (pdf 389)

### infinite-redshift surface

*phenomenon · developed* · also: surface of infinite red shift, S_+ and S_-

A hypersurface on which light emitted by an observer at rest relative to infinity would arrive infinitely redshifted; in Kerr it lies where g_00 vanishes, giving S_+/-: r = m +/- (m^2 - a^2 cos^2 theta)^{1/2}.

**How introduced:** Taken over from the static case by assertion ('it can be shown') and then linked to the norm of the time-translation Killing vector.

**Prerequisites:** gravitational redshift, Killing vector

$$
g_{00}=(r^2-2mr+a^2\cos^2\theta)/\rho^2
$$

$$
r_{S\pm}=m\pm(m^2-a^2\cos^2\theta)^{1/2}

**Where:** DIV §20.5 p.374 (pdf 389); DIV §20.5 p.375 (pdf 390)

### Kerr event horizons

*phenomenon · core* · also: outer and inner horizons, r_+ and r_-

For a^2 < m^2, the null hypersurfaces r = r_+/- = m +/- (m^2 - a^2)^{1/2} where Delta = 0; r_+ is the event horizon of the black hole and r_- an inner (Cauchy-type) horizon; together they split Boyer-Lindquist r > 0 into regions I, II, III.

**How introduced:** Found, as for Schwarzschild, by asking where r = const surfaces become null, i.e. where g^11 = -Delta/rho^2 vanishes.

**Prerequisites:** Boyer-Lindquist coordinates, null hypersurface, event horizon

$$
g^{11}=-\Delta/\rho^2
$$

$$
r_\pm=m\pm(m^2-a^2)^{1/2}
$$

**Notes:** The g^11 = 0 criterion locates null r = const surfaces; the book does not use Killing horizons or discuss that the horizon generator is a combination of d/dt and d/dphi.

**Where:** DIV §20.5 p.375 (pdf 390); DIV §20.5 p.376 (pdf 391)

### ergosphere

*phenomenon · core*

The region between the outer event horizon r_+ and the stationary limit surface S_+, outside the black hole, where the time-translation Killing vector is spacelike so that no observer can remain at rest relative to infinity and everything is forced to co-rotate; negative-energy states exist there, enabling the Penrose process.

**How introduced:** Named when comparing S_+ with r_+ (they touch only on the axis), shaded in Fig. 20.3, made operational by the photon-orbit argument of §20.8, and exploited in §20.11.

**Prerequisites:** stationary limit surface, Kerr event horizons, frame dragging

**Where:** DIV §20.5 p.375 (pdf 390); DIV §20.8 p.381 (pdf 396); DIV §20.8 p.382 (pdf 397); DIV §20.11 p.387 (pdf 402)

### stationary limit surface

*phenomenon · core* · also: S_+, static limit

The outer infinite-redshift surface S_+ of Kerr: the boundary inside which no timelike or null curve can keep fixed r, theta, phi, because even a photon aimed against the rotation has dphi/dt >= 0 there; it is timelike except at the poles, so it can be crossed both inward and outward.

**How introduced:** Derived by considering photons forced to orbit at fixed r and theta and finding where the counter-rotating solution for dphi/dt reaches zero.

**Prerequisites:** infinite-redshift surface, frame dragging

$$
\frac{d\phi}{dt}\le 0\iff r\ge r_{S_+}
$$

**Where:** DIV §20.8 p.381 (pdf 396); DIV §20.8 p.382 (pdf 397)

### principal null congruences

*mathematical-object · core* · also: principal congruences of outgoing and ingoing null geodesics

The two families of null geodesics in Kerr at fixed theta with r-dot = +/- L, t-dot = (r^2 + a^2)L/Delta, phi-dot = aL/Delta; they are the most outgoing and most ingoing light rays at each point and play the role of radial null geodesics in Schwarzschild.

**How introduced:** Found by imposing theta = const on the Boyer-Lindquist geodesic equations, using first integrals and a four-equations-three-unknowns constraint.

**Prerequisites:** null geodesic, first integral, Boyer-Lindquist coordinates

$$
\dot t=(r^2+a^2)L/\Delta,\;\dot r=\pm L,\;\dot\phi=aL/\Delta
$$

**Notes:** Geometrically they are the repeated principal null directions of the Weyl tensor (Kerr is Petrov type D), a point the book does not make.

**Where:** DIV §20.6 p.377 (pdf 392); DIV §20.6 p.378 (pdf 393); DIV §20.6 p.379 (pdf 394)

### affine parameter

*definition · revisited*

A parameter along a geodesic for which the geodesic equation takes its standard form; here r itself is affine along the principal congruences because r-dot is constant.

**How introduced:** Noted in passing after solving r-dot = +/- L.

**Prerequisites:** geodesic

**Where:** DIV §20.6 p.378 (pdf 393)

### coordinate singularity

*definition · revisited*

A place where a metric's components blow up or degenerate because of a bad choice of coordinates, not because curvature diverges; it can be removed by a coordinate change.

**How introduced:** Recognized at r = r_+ because t and phi along the principal congruences diverge logarithmically while curvature is finite, then removed with EF-type coordinates.

**Prerequisites:** principal null congruences

**Where:** DIV §20.6 p.379 (pdf 394)

### static observer

*definition · introduced* · also: stationary observer relative to infinity

An observer whose world line follows the time-translation Killing vector, staying at fixed spatial Boyer-Lindquist coordinates; such observers exist only where that Killing vector is timelike, i.e. outside S_+.

**How introduced:** Implicit in the photon-orbit analysis: on S_+ a counter-rotating photon is only just stationary, so massive observers cannot be.

**Prerequisites:** stationary limit surface, Killing vector

### maximal analytic extension of Kerr

*solution-or-model · developed* · also: maximal extension

The largest spacetime obtained by patching together Eddington-Finkelstein-type charts (advanced and retarded) across the Kerr horizons and continuing r through the ring to negative values; for a^2 < m^2 it is an infinite chain of regions I, II, III as seen on the axis.

A conformally compactified spacetime diagram in which light rays run at 45 degrees and infinity is brought to finite boundaries (scri-plus, scri-minus), showing causal structure of an entire spacetime.

### trapped surface

A closed spacelike 2-surface so deep in a gravitational field that both outgoing and ingoing future-directed light rays orthogonal to it converge.

### closed timelike curves

Future-directed timelike curves that return to their starting event, allowing an observer to meet their own past; in Kerr they occur near the ring on the negative-r side, where d/dphi is timelike so circles of constant t, r, theta are timelike.

### extremal Kerr black hole

### geodesic incompleteness

*definition · developed* · also: singular spacetime (definition), incomplete causal geodesic

A spacetime is singular if some timelike or null geodesic cannot be extended beyond a finite proper time or affine parameter, and the spacetime cannot be extended to continue it.

**How introduced:** Stated as the modern criterion introduced in Penrose's 1965 paper; used for the ring in §20.10 and as the conclusion of singularity theorems.

**Prerequisites:** geodesic, affine parameter

**Where:** DIV §20.10 p.384 (pdf 399); DIV §20.13 p.391 (pdf 406); DIV §20.13 p.392 (pdf 407)

### Kerr-Newman solution

*solution-or-model · introduced* · also: charged rotating black hole, Kerr-Newman metric

The stationary axisymmetric electrovacuum solution with mass m, spin parameter a and charge epsilon; it has a stationary limit surface r = m + (m^2 - epsilon^2 - a^2 cos^2 theta)^{1/2} and, if a^2 + epsilon^2 <= m^2, an outer horizon r = m + (m^2 - epsilon^2 - a^2)^{1/2}.

**How introduced:** Obtained (as an exercise) by applying the Newman-Janis trick to Reissner-Nordstrom in advanced EF coordinates; presented as the most general black hole.

**Prerequisites:** Newman-Janis algorithm, Reissner-Nordstrom solution

$$
r=m+(m^2-\varepsilon^2-a^2)^{1/2}
$$

**Where:** DIV §20.1 p.367 (pdf 382); DIV §20.11 p.385 (pdf 400); DIV §20.11 p.386 (pdf 401)

### no-hair theorem

*theorem · developed* · also: black hole uniqueness theorem, a black hole has no hair

The collection of uniqueness results implying that a stationary black hole in asymptotically flat Einstein-Maxwell theory is described by Kerr-Newman, fixed entirely by mass, angular momentum and charge, so all other details of the collapsed matter are lost.

**How introduced:** Stated as a theorem that the exterior of a forming horizon approaches Kerr-Newman, then dramatized by Wheeler's picture of a vase and a television falling in.

**Prerequisites:** Kerr-Newman solution, event horizon

**Notes:** The rigorous theorems (Israel, Carter, Robinson, Mazur, Bunting) concern stationary black holes; that collapse dynamically settles to Kerr-Newman is a strongly supported but more delicate claim than the text suggests.

**Where:** DIV §20.11 p.386 (pdf 401); DIV §20.12 p.388 (pdf 403)

### gravitational collapse to a rotating black hole

*phenomenon · introduced* · also: rotating collapse, final state of collapse

The process in which a rotating body collapses inside its horizon; asymmetries are radiated away as a trapped surface forms inside a still-expanding event horizon that then settles to a stationary Kerr(-Newman) hole.

**How introduced:** Told as an idealized collapse through r_+ and r_- (with possible halting and re-emergence in the extension) and then as the realistic settling picture.

**Prerequisites:** Kerr event horizons, trapped surface, no-hair theorem

**Notes:** No physically realistic interior Kerr solution is known; the text speculates a toroidal source.

**Where:** DIV §20.11 p.385 (pdf 400); DIV §20.11 p.386 (pdf 401)

### white hole

*solution-or-model · mention*

The time reverse of a black hole: a region out of which matter and light emerge and which nothing can enter.

**How introduced:** Mentioned humorously as the time reversal of the no-hair cartoon.

**Prerequisites:** event horizon

**Where:** DIV §20.11 p.387 (pdf 402)

### Penrose process

*phenomenon · introduced* · also: energy extraction from a rotating black hole

A mechanism in which a particle entering the ergosphere splits so that one fragment falls into the hole on a negative-energy orbit while the other escapes with more energy than the original, the difference being paid for by a reduction of the hole's angular momentum and mass.

**How introduced:** Described qualitatively, with a fanciful engineering cartoon of a civilization living off the hole (Fig. 20.11).

**Prerequisites:** ergosphere, Killing vector energy

**Notes:** No calculation or efficiency figure is given; negative-energy orbits are not explained.

**Where:** DIV §20.11 p.387 (pdf 402)

### superradiance

*phenomenon · mention* · also: rotational superradiance

Amplification of waves scattered off a rotating body when the wave's angular phase velocity is below the body's rotation rate, extracting rotational energy; the wave analogue of the Penrose process.

**How introduced:** Linked to the Penrose process through Zel'dovich's 1971 suggestion from spinning metal spheres; used as a bridge to Hawking radiation.

**Prerequisites:** Penrose process

**Where:** DIV §20.11 p.387 (pdf 402); DIV §20.14 p.396 (pdf 411)

### Gauss-law charge integral

*technique · developed* · also: total charge as a surface integral

Using the sourced Maxwell equation and the divergence theorem to express the charge inside a region of a spacelike hypersurface as the flux of F^ab through its boundary 2-surface, and total charge as that flux through a sphere at infinity.

**How introduced:** Used as a warm-up and template for defining gravitational mass, checked on a point charge.

**Prerequisites:** Maxwell equations, divergence theorem, Eulerian observer

$$
Q_{\rm total}=\int_S F^{ab}n_am_b\sqrt{\sigma}\,d^2x

**Where:** DIV §20.12 p.388 (pdf 403); DIV §20.12 p.389 (pdf 404)

### Eulerian observer

*definition · revisited* · also: normal observer

An observer whose 4-velocity is the unit timelike normal n^a to a spacelike hypersurface in a 3+1 split.

**How introduced:** Recalled from ch14 to interpret j^a n_a as measured charge density.

**Prerequisites:** 3+1 decomposition

**Where:** DIV §20.12 p.388 (pdf 403)

### Killing vector Ricci identity

*identity · revisited* · also: Killing identity for second derivatives

For any Killing vector K^a, the divergence of its covariant derivative is governed by the Ricci tensor: nabla_a nabla_b K^a = R_ab K^a (sign depending on curvature conventions).

**How introduced:** Quoted from Exercise 7.14 to rewrite the gravitational 'current' R^ab K_b as a divergence.

**Prerequisites:** Killing vector, Ricci tensor

$$
\nabla_a(\nabla_bK^a)=R_{ab}K^a
$$

**Where:** DIV §20.12 p.389 (pdf 404); DIV §20.12 p.390 (pdf 405)

### Komar mass

*physical-quantity · developed* · also: Komar integral, Komar mass-energy

The total mass-energy of a stationary (or asymptotically stationary) asymptotically flat spacetime defined as (1/4pi) times the flux of the antisymmetric tensor nabla^a K^b through a 2-sphere at infinity, where K is the timelike (asymptotic) Killing vector; it gives m for Schwarzschild.

**How introduced:** Built by analogy with charge: replace j^a by R^ab K_b, integrate over a hypersurface, convert to a boundary term with the Killing identity, and fix the 1/4pi by matching Schwarzschild.

**Prerequisites:** Gauss-law charge integral, Killing vector Ricci identity, asymptotic flatness

$$
E_{\rm Komar}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_S(\nabla^aK^b)n_am_b\sqrt\sigma\,d^2x
$$

**Notes:** Sign bookkeeping in (20.89)-(20.90) is glossed; see gaps.

**Where:** DIV §20.12 p.389 (pdf 404); DIV §20.12 p.390 (pdf 405); DIV §20.12 p.391 (pdf 406)

### asymptotic Killing vector

*definition · introduced*

A vector field that approaches a Killing vector of Minkowski space at infinity, even if it is not a Killing vector of the full spacetime; enough for defining conserved quantities by surface integrals at infinity.

**How introduced:** Introduced to extend the Komar construction from strictly stationary to asymptotically flat spacetimes.

**Prerequisites:** Killing vector, asymptotic flatness

**Where:** DIV §20.12 p.391 (pdf 406)

### ADM mass

*physical-quantity · introduced* · also: ADM mass-energy, Arnowitt-Deser-Misner mass

The total mass-energy of an asymptotically flat spacetime computed from the fall-off of the spatial metric perturbation h_ij on a spacelike slice as (1/16pi) times a flux integral of d_j h^j_i - d_i h^j_j at infinity; it arises from the boundary term of the ADM Hamiltonian.

**How introduced:** Quoted as an alternative to Komar from the Hamiltonian viewpoint of §14.11, agreeing with Komar for time-independent h_ij.

**Prerequisites:** asymptotic flatness, ADM Hamiltonian

$$
E_{\rm ADM}=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_S(\partial_jh^j{}_i-\partial_ih^j{}_j)m^i\sqrt\sigma\,d^2x
$$

**Where:** DIV §20.12 p.391 (pdf 406)

### positive energy theorem

*theorem · mention* · also: positive mass theorem

If the dominant energy condition holds, every asymptotically flat spacetime has non-negative ADM mass, and zero ADM mass only for Minkowski space (Schoen and Yau).

**How introduced:** Stated without proof after the ADM mass.

**Prerequisites:** ADM mass, dominant energy condition

**Where:** DIV §20.12 p.391 (pdf 406)

### singularity theorems

*theorem · introduced* · also: pattern singularity theorem

Theorems showing that a spacetime obeying a suitable initial or boundary condition (e.g. a trapped surface), a curvature (energy) condition, and a causality condition must contain inextendible incomplete causal geodesics.

**How introduced:** Presented historically and then as Senovilla's three-ingredient template, with the Hawking-Penrose 1970 theorem as the worked instance.

**Prerequisites:** geodesic incompleteness, trapped surface, Raychaudhuri focusing

**Notes:** The book balances the theorems by noting that many exact solutions are geodesically complete (maximal and singularity-free); the theorems bite for models close to Kerr-Newman collapse, Friedmann big bang/crunch, or colliding plane waves, and say nothing definite about the structure of generic singularities.

**Where:** DIV §20.13 p.391 (pdf 406); DIV §20.13 p.392 (pdf 407); DIV §20.13 p.393 (pdf 408)

### Hawking-Penrose singularity theorem

*theorem · introduced*

The 1970 theorem that a spacetime with a closed trapped surface (or analogous condition), the timelike convergence condition, the generic condition and no closed timelike curves is causal-geodesically incomplete.

**How introduced:** Used to instantiate each slot of the general template.

**Prerequisites:** singularity theorems, generic condition, timelike convergence condition

**Where:** DIV §20.13 p.392 (pdf 407); DIV §20.13 p.393 (pdf 408)

### timelike convergence condition

*principle · introduced* · also: convergence condition

The requirement R_ab V^a V^b >= 0 for all non-spacelike V^a, which by Raychaudhuri's equation makes initially converging congruences of geodesics focus.

**How introduced:** Stated as the curvature ingredient of the Hawking-Penrose theorem, then rephrased as the strong energy condition.

**Prerequisites:** Ricci tensor, Raychaudhuri focusing

$$
R_{ab}V^aV^b\ge0
$$

**Where:** DIV §20.13 p.392 (pdf 407)

### strong energy condition

*principle · introduced*

T_ab V^a V^b >= (1/2) T g_ab V^a V^b for all non-spacelike V^a; for matter with density mu and principal stresses p_alpha it means mu + p_alpha >= 0 and mu + sum p_alpha >= 0.

**How introduced:** Obtained from the timelike convergence condition via the Einstein equations to give it a matter interpretation.

**Prerequisites:** energy-momentum tensor, timelike convergence condition

$$
\mu+p_\alpha\ge0,\;\mu+\textstyle\sum p_\alpha\ge0
$$

**Notes:** Violated by a positive cosmological constant or inflationary scalar fields, a point not mentioned.

**Where:** DIV §20.13 p.392 (pdf 407)

### null convergence condition

*principle · mention*

R_ab k^a k^b >= 0 for all null vectors k^a; it forces focusing of null geodesic congruences and underlies Penrose's 1965 theorem.

**How introduced:** Given as the null version of the convergence condition.

**Prerequisites:** timelike convergence condition

**Where:** DIV §20.13 p.393 (pdf 408)

### weak energy condition

*principle · introduced*

T^ab V_a V_b >= 0 for all non-spacelike V; for standard matter mu >= 0 and mu + p_alpha >= 0; it implies the null convergence condition.

**How introduced:** Stated as the physical justification for the null convergence condition and compared with the dominant energy condition.

**Prerequisites:** energy-momentum tensor

$$
\mu\ge0,\;\mu+p_\alpha\ge0
$$

**Where:** DIV §20.13 p.393 (pdf 408)

### generic condition

*principle · mention* · also: genericity condition

Every causal geodesic somewhere encounters curvature with a non-vanishing tidal component orthogonal to it, written as a non-zero antisymmetrized contraction of the Riemann tensor with the geodesic's tangent; it fails only in special, highly symmetric situations.

**How introduced:** Added as a technical hypothesis of the Hawking-Penrose theorem.

**Prerequisites:** Riemann tensor

$$
v_{[a}R_{b]cd[e}v_{f]}v^cv^d\ne0
$$

**Where:** DIV §20.13 p.393 (pdf 408)

### Raychaudhuri focusing

*theorem · mention* · also: Raychaudhuri effect, focusing theorem

Given the convergence condition, a congruence of geodesics orthogonal to a hypersurface that starts to converge will converge ever faster and focus within finite affine parameter, provided the geodesics extend that far.

**How introduced:** Invoked by forward reference to (23.44) to explain why the curvature condition matters.

**Prerequisites:** timelike convergence condition

**Where:** DIV §20.13 p.392 (pdf 407)

### black hole area theorem

*theorem · introduced* · also: area increase law, second law of black hole mechanics

Classically (given the null energy condition), the area of an event horizon never decreases; it grows under accretion, and a merged hole has more area than the sum of its progenitors.

**How introduced:** Stated as the observation that motivates the entropy analogy.

**Prerequisites:** event horizon

**Notes:** Hawking evaporation violates the classical energy condition and does shrink the area; the text does not reconcile this.

**Where:** DIV §20.14 p.394 (pdf 409)

### black hole thermodynamics

*principle · introduced* · also: laws of black hole mechanics

The exact correspondence between laws of black hole mechanics and thermodynamics: horizon area plays the role of entropy, surface gravity of temperature, and the first law relates mass change to area change weighted by surface gravity.

**How introduced:** Introduced through the area-entropy resemblance and uniformity of surface gravity over the horizon.

**Prerequisites:** black hole area theorem, surface gravity

$$
\delta M=\frac{\kappa}{8\pi}\delta A+\Omega_H\delta J+\Phi_H\delta Q\;(\text{standard form, not in text})
$$

**Where:** DIV §20.14 p.394 (pdf 409)

### surface gravity

*physical-quantity · mention* · also: kappa

A measure of gravitational field strength at a Killing horizon (the force per unit mass needed at infinity to hold a unit mass at the horizon), constant over a stationary horizon; kappa = 1/(4m) for Schwarzschild.

**How introduced:** Named, without a formula, as the proportionality factor in the first law and the analogue of temperature.

**Prerequisites:** Kerr event horizons, Killing vector

**Where:** DIV §20.14 p.394 (pdf 409)

### Bekenstein-Hawking entropy

*physical-quantity · mention* · also: black hole entropy

The entropy assigned to a black hole, proportional to its horizon area (S = k_B c^3 A / (4 G hbar)), interpreted as the logarithm of the number of internal configurations compatible with its mass, charge and spin.

**How introduced:** Motivated heuristically: no-hair means many possible progenitors, quantum mechanics makes the count finite, and its logarithm is the entropy.

**Prerequisites:** no-hair theorem, black hole thermodynamics

**Notes:** The area-entropy formula S = A/4 (geometric units) is not given in the text.

**Where:** DIV §20.14 p.394 (pdf 409)

### Hawking radiation

*phenomenon · introduced* · also: Hawking effect, black hole radiance

Thermal emission of particles by any black hole due to quantum field effects near the horizon, with black-body temperature T = hbar c^3 / (8 pi G k_B M) for a Schwarzschild hole of mass M.

**How introduced:** Introduced as the resolution of the paradox that finite entropy implies finite temperature yet classical holes cannot emit; pictured as separation of virtual pairs with backward-in-time reinterpretation.

**Prerequisites:** black hole thermodynamics, virtual particles

$$
T=\frac{\hbar c^3}{8\pi Gk_BM}
$$

**Where:** DIV §20.14 p.394 (pdf 409); DIV §20.14 p.395 (pdf 410); DIV §20.14 p.396 (pdf 411)

### black hole evaporation

*phenomenon · introduced*

The gradual loss of mass through Hawking radiation; since temperature rises as mass falls, emission runs away and the hole eventually disappears, over roughly 10^67 years for a solar mass.

**How introduced:** Deduced qualitatively from the inverse mass-temperature relation.

**Prerequisites:** Hawking radiation

**Where:** DIV §20.14 p.396 (pdf 411)

### primordial black holes

*solution-or-model · mention* · also: mini black holes

Black holes hypothetically formed in the early universe, with masses small enough that they would be finishing their evaporation today, possibly as bursts of high-energy gamma rays.

**How introduced:** Mentioned as the observationally interesting end of evaporation; searches reported inconclusive.

**Prerequisites:** black hole evaporation

**Where:** DIV §20.14 p.396 (pdf 411)

### (20.44) Ring singularity · central · DIV §20.5 p.374 (pdf 389)

$$
x^2+y^2=a^2,\quad z=0
$$

rho = 0 is a ring of radius a in the equatorial plane.

### (20.45) g_00 in Boyer-Lindquist · supporting · DIV §20.5 p.374 (pdf 389)

$$
g_{00}=\frac{r^2-2mr+a^2\cos^2\theta}{\rho^2}
$$

Norm of the time-translation Killing vector; its zeros are infinite-redshift surfaces.

### (20.46) Infinite-redshift surfaces · central · DIV §20.5 p.374 (pdf 389)

$$
r=r_{S\pm}=m\pm\big(m^2-a^2\cos^2\theta\big)^{1/2}
$$

S_+ is the stationary limit surface; S_- lies inside the inner horizon.

### Radial inverse metric component · derivation-step · DIV §20.5 p.375 (pdf 390)

$$
g^{11}=-\frac{\Delta}{\rho^2}=-\frac{r^2-2mr+a^2}{r^2+a^2\cos^2\theta}
$$

r = const is null where this vanishes.

### (20.47) Kerr horizons · central · DIV §20.5 p.375 (pdf 390)

$$
r=r_\pm=m\pm\big(m^2-a^2\big)^{1/2}
$$

Outer (event) and inner horizons for a^2 < m^2; they merge at a^2 = m^2 and disappear for a^2 > m^2.

### (20.48) Conditions for theta = const null geodesics · derivation-step · DIV §20.6 p.377 (pdf 392)

$$
\dot\theta=0,\qquad ds^2=0
$$

Printed compactly as theta-dot = ds^2 = 0; the dot is derivative with respect to an affine parameter.

### (20.49)-(20.50) First integrals from t and phi · derivation-step · DIV §20.6 p.377 (pdf 392)

$$
\frac{\Delta}{\rho^2}(\dot t-a\sin^2\theta\,\dot\phi)+\frac{a\sin^2\theta}{\rho^2}\big[(r^2+a^2)\dot\phi-a\dot t\big]=L,\qquad \frac{a\Delta\sin^2\theta}{\rho^2}(\dot t-a\sin^2\theta\,\dot\phi)+\frac{(r^2+a^2)\sin^2\theta}{\rho^2}\big[(r^2+a^2)\dot\phi-a\dot t\big]=N
$$

Conserved momenta conjugate to the ignorable coordinates t and phi (energy-like L and angular-momentum-like N).

**Symbols:** L, N constants

### (20.51) Null condition · derivation-step · DIV §20.6 p.377 (pdf 392)

$$
\frac{\Delta}{\rho^2}(\dot t-a\sin^2\theta\,\dot\phi)^2-\frac{\sin^2\theta}{\rho^2}\big[(r^2+a^2)\dot\phi-a\dot t\big]^2-\frac{\rho^2\dot r^2}{\Delta}=0
$$

The tangent vector is null.

### (20.52) theta equation at fixed theta · derivation-step · DIV §20.6 p.377 (pdf 392)

$$
\frac{a^2\Delta}{\rho^4}(\dot t-a\sin^2\theta\,\dot\phi)^2-\frac{2a\Delta\dot\phi}{\rho^2}(\dot t-a\sin^2\theta\,\dot\phi)-\frac{r^2+a^2}{\rho^4}\big[(r^2+a^2)\dot\phi-a\dot t\big]^2+\frac{a^2\dot r^2}{\Delta}=0
$$

Consistency of staying at constant theta; the fourth equation for three unknown velocities.

### (20.53)-(20.54) Constraint selecting the principal congruences · supporting · DIV §20.6 p.378 (pdf 393)

$$
(N+aL\sin^2\theta)(N-aL\sin^2\theta)=0,\qquad N=aL\sin^2\theta
$$

Overdetermination forces a relation between the constants; one branch is chosen.

### (20.55)-(20.57) Principal null congruence tangents · central · DIV §20.6 p.378 (pdf 393)

$$
\dot t=\frac{(r^2+a^2)L}{\Delta},\qquad \dot r=\pm L,\qquad \dot\phi=\frac{aL}{\Delta}
$$

Tangent components of the outgoing (+) and ingoing (-) principal null geodesics; r is affine.

### (20.58)-(20.59) Congruence slopes · supporting · DIV §20.6 p.378 (pdf 393)

$$
\frac{dt}{dr}=\frac{r^2+a^2}{\Delta},\qquad \frac{d\phi}{dr}=\frac{a}{\Delta}
$$

Both diverge at Delta = 0, signalling the Boyer-Lindquist coordinate singularity.

### (20.60) Outgoing congruence t(r) · supporting · DIV §20.6 p.378 (pdf 393)

$$
t=r+\Big(m+\frac{m^2}{(m^2-a^2)^{1/2}}\Big)\ln|r-r_+|+\Big(m-\frac{m^2}{(m^2-a^2)^{1/2}}\Big)\ln|r-r_-|+\text{const}
$$

Kerr analogue of the Schwarzschild tortoise relation; logarithmic divergence at both horizons.

### (20.61) Outgoing congruence phi(r) · supporting · DIV §20.6 p.378 (pdf 393)

$$
\phi=\frac{a}{2(m^2-a^2)^{1/2}}\ln\Big|\frac{r-r_+}{r-r_-}\Big|+\text{const}
$$

Light rays wind infinitely in phi as they approach a horizon in Boyer-Lindquist coordinates.

### (20.62)-(20.63) Ingoing congruence in differential form · derivation-step · DIV §20.7 p.379 (pdf 394)

$$
dt=-\frac{r^2+a^2}{\Delta}dr,\qquad d\phi=-\frac{a}{\Delta}dr

Starting point for adapted coordinates.

### (20.65) Target form of ingoing congruence · derivation-step · DIV §20.7 p.379 (pdf 394)

$$
d\bar t=-dr,\qquad d\theta=d\bar\phi=0
$$

Ingoing principal null lines become straight 45-degree lines at fixed angles.

### (20.66)-(20.68) Kerr Eddington-Finkelstein transformation · central · DIV §20.7 p.380 (pdf 395)

$$
d\bar t=dt+\frac{2mr}{\Delta}dr,\qquad d\bar\phi=d\phi+\frac{a}{\Delta}dr,\qquad v=\bar t+r
$$

Maps Boyer-Lindquist to the advanced EF form (20.22).

### (20.69) Constrained circular photon · derivation-step · DIV §20.8 p.381 (pdf 396)

$$
dr=d\theta=ds^2=0
$$

Null curves at fixed r and theta (not geodesics).

### (20.70) Angular velocity of circling photons · central · DIV §20.8 p.381 (pdf 396)

$$
\frac{d\phi}{dt}=\frac{a\sin\theta\pm\Delta^{1/2}}{(r^2+a^2)\sin\theta\pm a\Delta^{1/2}\sin^2\theta}
$$

Co-rotating (+) and counter-rotating (-) angular velocities of light held at fixed r, theta.

### (20.71) Stationary limit criterion · central · DIV §20.8 p.381 (pdf 396)

$$
\frac{d\phi}{dt}\le0\iff a\sin\theta-\Delta^{1/2}\le0\iff r\ge r_{S_+}

Counter-rotation relative to infinity is possible only outside S_+.

### Advanced and retarded Kerr null coordinates · supporting · DIV §20.9 p.382 (pdf 397)

du_\pm=dt\pm\frac{r^2+a^2}{\Delta}dr,\qquad d\phi_\pm=d\phi\pm\frac{a}{\Delta}dr

Charts combined to build the maximal extension.

### Kerr-form r in Cartesian terms · supporting · DIV §20.10 p.384 (pdf 399)

r^4-(x^2+y^2+z^2-a^2)r^2-a^2z^2=0

Implicit definition of r; r = const are confocal ellipsoids degenerating to the disc at r = 0.

### Reissner-Nordstrom seed in advanced EF · supporting · DIV §20.11 p.385 (pdf 400)

## Worked examples

### Example · challenging · DIV §20.2 p.369 (pdf 384)

**Problem:** Produce the Kerr metric from Schwarzschild without solving the field equations.

**Method:** Write Schwarzschild's inverse metric in advanced EF coordinates, factor it as a null tetrad, complexify r in a prescribed way, apply the complex shift in v and r by i a cos(theta), demand real v' and r', and rebuild g^ab from the new tetrad.

**Key insight:** Rotation enters through an imaginary displacement of the radial and null coordinates along the axis; the null tetrad is the natural object on which to perform it.

**Result:** Kerr tetrad (20.21) and advanced EF line element (20.22)

**Concepts:** Newman-Janis algorithm, null tetrad, Kerr solution

### Example · standard · DIV §20.3 p.370 (pdf 385)

**Problem:** Convert the Kerr metric from advanced EF to Boyer-Lindquist form.

**Method:** Use the differential transformation dv = dt + (r^2 + a^2)/Delta dr, dphi-bar = dphi + a/Delta dr and simplify, noticing that the line element can be grouped into two squares.

**Key insight:** Removing all dr cross terms isolates a single dt dphi term and makes t- and phi-independence obvious.

**Result:** (20.27)

**Concepts:** Boyer-Lindquist coordinates

### Example · intro · DIV §20.4 p.373 (pdf 388)

**Problem:** Show that writing flat space in a rotating frame generates a time-azimuth cross term like Kerr's.

**Method:** Substitute phi = phi' + at into the cylindrical Minkowski metric and expand r^2 dphi^2.

**Key insight:** A dt dphi term is the metric fingerprint of rotation, though flat-space rigid rotation is only suggestive.

**Result:** ds^2 = (1 - a^2 r^2) dt^2 - 2 a r^2 dphi' dt - (dr^2 + r^2 dphi'^2 + dz^2)

**Concepts:** frame dragging

### Example · intro · DIV §20.4 p.373 (pdf 388)

**Problem:** Relate the Boyer-Lindquist r to the Euclidean radius and check asymptotic flatness.

**Method:** Square and add the Kerr-form Cartesian coordinates (20.29) to get R^2, then expand for r >> a; let R -> infinity in (20.28).

**Key insight:** r labels oblate spheroids; the difference from R dies off as a^2/r.

**Result:** R^2 = r^2 + a^2 sin^2 theta; R = r + a^2 sin^2 theta/(2r) + ...

**Concepts:** Boyer-Lindquist radial coordinate, asymptotic flatness

### Example · standard · DIV §20.5 p.374 (pdf 389)

**Problem:** Locate the singularity, infinite-redshift surfaces and horizons of Kerr.

**Method:** Set rho^2 = 0 and map to Cartesian coordinates; set g_00 = 0 and solve the quadratic in r; compute g^11 and set Delta = 0.

**Key insight:** Two different conditions (g_00 = 0 and g^11 = 0) that coincide in Schwarzschild separate in Kerr, opening the ergosphere.

**Result:** Ring x^2 + y^2 = a^2, z = 0; r_{S+/-} = m +/- (m^2 - a^2 cos^2 theta)^{1/2}; r_+/- = m +/- (m^2 - a^2)^{1/2}

**Concepts:** ring singularity, infinite-redshift surface, Kerr event horizons, ergosphere

### Example · challenging · DIV §20.6 p.377 (pdf 392)

**Problem:** Find the Kerr analogues of radial null geodesics.

**Method:** At fixed theta, write first integrals L, N from t and phi, the null condition, and the theta equation; eliminate to find the constraint (N + aL sin^2 theta)(N - aL sin^2 theta) = 0; solve on one branch and integrate dt/dr and dphi/dr by partial fractions.

**Key insight:** An overdetermined system (four equations, three velocities) is what picks out the special congruences; r is automatically affine.

**Result:** (20.55)-(20.57) and (20.60)-(20.61)

**Concepts:** principal null congruences, affine parameter, coordinate singularity

### Example · standard · DIV §20.7 p.379 (pdf 394)

**Problem:** Build coordinates regular across r_+ from the ingoing principal congruence.

**Method:** Require dt-bar = -dr and dphi-bar = 0 along (20.62)-(20.63), which fixes dt-bar = dt + 2mr/Delta dr and dphi-bar = dphi + a/Delta dr; define v = t-bar + r.

**Key insight:** Adapting coordinates to infalling light removes the horizon's coordinate singularity, closing the loop with the Newman-Janis form.

**Result:** Boyer-Lindquist -> (20.22)

**Concepts:** advanced Eddington-Finkelstein form of Kerr

### Example · standard · DIV §20.8 p.381 (pdf 396)

**Problem:** Decide where an observer (or photon) can avoid rotating relative to infinity.

**Method:** Constrain a null curve to dr = dtheta = 0, solve the quadratic Boyer-Lindquist null condition for dphi/dt, show the minus-branch denominator is positive for r > r_+, and find when the numerator a sin theta - Delta^{1/2} is non-positive.

**Key insight:** The best a counter-rotating light ray can do reaches zero angular velocity exactly at S_+; massive observers do worse.

**Result:** dphi/dt <= 0 iff r >= r_{S+}

**Concepts:** stationary limit surface, ergosphere, static observer

### Example · challenging · DIV §20.11 p.385 (pdf 400)

**Problem:** Obtain the Kerr-Newman metric and its horizon and stationary limit (set as an exercise, results quoted).

**Method:** Apply the same complex shift to the Reissner-Nordstrom seed in advanced EF coordinates (the text leaves this as Exercise 20.16); the usual choice, not spelled out in the book, is to complexify the charge term epsilon^2/r^2 as epsilon^2/(r r-bar), which becomes epsilon^2/rho^2.

**Key insight:** Charge and spin enter additively in the horizon condition: a^2 + epsilon^2 <= m^2.

**Result:** (20.72)-(20.74)

**Concepts:** Kerr-Newman solution, Newman-Janis algorithm

### Example · intro · DIV §20.12 p.389 (pdf 404)

**Problem:** Check that the flux integral at infinity returns the charge of a point charge.

**Method:** Use Minkowski normals at large r to reduce the integrand to F^tr r^2 sin theta, insert F^tr = q/(4 pi r^2) and integrate over the sphere.

**Key insight:** Total charge is encoded entirely in the 1/r^2 tail of the field.

**Result:** Q_total = q

**Concepts:** Gauss-law charge integral

### Example · standard · DIV §20.12 p.390 (pdf 405)

**Problem:** Evaluate the Komar integral for Schwarzschild and fix its normalization.

**Method:** Take K = d/dt; normalize n^a and m^a with g_00 and g_11; the product of normalization factors cancels so the integrand is nabla_0 K_1; reduce to g_00 Gamma^0_01 (magnitude m/r^2) and integrate over the sphere.

**Key insight:** The r^2 from the area cancels the 1/r^2 of the field at every radius, so the answer is independent of where the sphere is placed in vacuum.

**Result:** E_total = m (up to a sign fixed by orientation)

**Concepts:** Komar mass, Killing vector Ricci identity

### Example · intro · DIV §20.14 p.395 (pdf 410)

**Problem:** Estimate the Hawking temperature and evaporation time scale.

**Method:** Insert constants into T = hbar c^3/(8 pi G k_B M) for M = M_sun; quote the lifetime scale.

**Key insight:** Temperature falls as mass rises, so stellar holes are far colder than the cosmic microwave background and evaporation is runaway only at small mass.

**Result:** About 6x10^-8 K for a solar mass; about 10^67 years to evaporate

**Concepts:** Hawking radiation, black hole evaporation


## Analogies and intuitions

### A flat spacetime written in rotating coordinates → frame dragging · useful · DIV §20.4 p.373 (pdf 388)

The dt dphi' cross term that appears when Minkowski space is expressed in a turning frame is offered as the reason to read Kerr's dt dphi term as rotation.

**Where it breaks down:** In flat space the cross term is removable by undoing the rotation; in Kerr it is not, and the authors admit rigid rotation in relativity is not treated.

**App idea:** Side-by-side metric panels: flat rotating coordinates versus Kerr, with a button that 'unrotates' flat space but fails for Kerr.

### A film of a spinning top run backwards → discrete t-phi reflection symmetry · strong · DIV §20.4 p.372 (pdf 387)

Reversing time for a spinning source looks like the same source spinning the opposite way, matching Kerr's invariance under t -> -t with phi -> -phi.

**Where it breaks down:** Symmetry is only suggestive; many non-rotating metrics with other cross terms could share it.

**App idea:** Play a rotating-disc video forward and backward next to a flipped mirror; the metric panel shows invariance only for the combined flip.

### Reissner-Nordstrom as a template → maximal analytic extension of Kerr · useful · DIV §20.9 p.382 (pdf 397)

Horizons, three regions, Penrose diagram and extreme case of Kerr are all described 'as for RN'.

**Where it breaks down:** Kerr is not spherically symmetric; the diagrams are only valid along the axis, and the ring allows passage to negative r which RN lacks.

### Gauss's law for electric charge → Komar mass · strong · DIV §20.12 p.388 (pdf 403)

Charge from a flux of F^ab at infinity is the model for mass from a flux of the Killing-vector derivative, with the constant chosen to give Schwarzschild m.

**Where it breaks down:** Gravity has no local energy density and needs a symmetry (Killing vector) to make the construction work; different constructions (Komar, ADM) can differ outside stationarity.

**App idea:** Toggle the same draggable Gaussian surface between an electric charge and a Schwarzschild mass.

### Horizon area as entropy, surface gravity as temperature → black hole thermodynamics · strong · DIV §20.14 p.394 (pdf 409)

Area never decreasing parallels the second law; uniform surface gravity parallels uniform equilibrium temperature; the first law links mass change to area change.

**Where it breaks down:** Classical area increase fails once Hawking radiation is included; the correspondence becomes exact only with quantum field theory.

### Superradiance from a spinning metal sphere → Penrose process · useful · DIV §20.11 p.387 (pdf 402)

The wave amplification off rotating conductors is cited as the analogue of extracting rotational energy from a Kerr hole, via Zel'dovich.

**Where it breaks down:** Superradiance is a wave phenomenon; the Penrose process is particle kinematics; Hawking radiation does not need rotation at all.

### Virtual pairs split by a horizon; an antiparticle as a particle running backwards in time → Hawking radiation · weak · DIV §20.14 p.395 (pdf 410)

One member of a vacuum pair falls in while the other escapes; reinterpreted, a particle tunnels out of the hole backwards in time and is scattered forward.

**Where it breaks down:** A heuristic cartoon, not a derivation; it does not explain the thermal spectrum or why T scales as 1/M.

### Vase of flowers and a television set disappearing into a black hole → no-hair theorem · strong · DIV §20.11 p.386 (pdf 401)

Wheeler's cartoon makes vivid that no details of the swallowed objects survive except m, a and charge; time-reversed, objects popping out would betray a white hole.

**Where it breaks down:** Hides the dynamical settling process and the conditions of the uniqueness theorems.

### A civilization living off a black hole → Penrose process · useful · DIV §20.11 p.387 (pdf 402)

Framework around a rotating hole sends matter into the ergosphere and harvests the returning energized fragments.

**Where it breaks down:** Engineering fantasy; the maximum extractable energy is limited by the irreducible mass (not stated in the text).


## Misconceptions addressed

### A co-rotating coordinate system should turn Kerr into Schwarzschild, just as a rotating Newtonian source can be viewed at rest. · DIV §20.6 p.377 (pdf 392)

**Correction:** No coordinate change maps Kerr to Schwarzschild; the rotation is encoded in curvature, and the nonlinear field equations tie the exterior field to the spinning source.

**Why tempting:** In Newtonian mechanics rotation is always frame-relative.

### The Boyer-Lindquist r is the ordinary distance from the centre. · DIV §20.4 p.373 (pdf 388)

**Correction:** r labels oblate spheroids; the Euclidean radius satisfies R^2 = r^2 + a^2 sin^2 theta, so r = 0 is a disc, not a point.

**Why tempting:** It reduces to the Schwarzschild r when a = 0 and has the same name.

### The surface of infinite redshift is always the event horizon. · DIV §20.5 p.375 (pdf 390)

**Correction:** In Kerr the stationary limit surface S_+ lies outside r_+ except at the poles; they coincide only in the static limit or on the axis.

**Why tempting:** In Schwarzschild both conditions give r = 2m.

### Once inside the stationary limit surface you are trapped. · DIV §20.8 p.381 (pdf 396)

**Correction:** S_+ is timelike (except at the poles), so particles cross it in both directions; only r_+ is a one-way surface.

**Why tempting:** Infinite redshift and horizon were the same thing before.

### The Kerr singularity is a point at the centre. · DIV §20.5 p.374 (pdf 389)

**Correction:** It is a ring of radius a in the equatorial plane, timelike and avoidable, bounding a disc through which one can pass to negative r.

**Why tempting:** Schwarzschild's r = 0 and Newtonian point masses.

### Nothing, not even energy, can ever be extracted from a black hole classically. · DIV §20.11 p.387 (pdf 402)

**Correction:** In the Penrose process energy is extracted from a rotating hole at the expense of its angular momentum, using the ergosphere, which lies outside the horizon.

**Why tempting:** The popular slogan that nothing escapes.

### Singularity theorems prove that curvature becomes infinite. · DIV §20.13 p.393 (pdf 408)

**Correction:** They prove causal geodesic incompleteness and inextendibility; infinite curvature is the most plausible but unproved explanation.

**Why tempting:** 'Singularity' suggests blow-up of some quantity.

### Singularities in exact solutions are artefacts of their symmetry and would vanish in realistic, asymmetric collapse. · DIV §20.13 p.391 (pdf 406)

**Correction:** Penrose's 1965 theorem and its successors show that incompleteness persists under generic perturbations given trapped surfaces and energy conditions.

**Why tempting:** That was the prevailing view before 1965.

### Only rotating black holes can radiate. · DIV §20.11 p.388 (pdf 403)

**Correction:** Hawking showed all black holes emit thermal radiation when quantum effects are included; rotation is not needed.

**Why tempting:** Energy extraction and superradiance were first discussed for rotating holes.

### The Kerr parameter a is an angular velocity. · DIV §20.4 p.374 (pdf 389)

**Correction:** a = J/m is angular momentum per unit mass (a length in geometrized units); the horizon's angular velocity is a different quantity, a/(r_+^2 + a^2).

**Why tempting:** The text itself says a is related to angular velocity, and the same letter is used for the angular velocity of the rotating frame in (20.37).

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### Heavier black holes are hotter. · DIV §20.14 p.395 (pdf 410)

**Correction:** Hawking temperature is inversely proportional to mass; a solar-mass hole is at about 6x10^-8 K and more massive ones are colder.

**Why tempting:** Intuition from stars, and the printed scaling (M/M_sun) x 6x10^-8 K in the text.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### The Newman-Janis trick is a derivation that always produces valid rotating solutions. · DIV §20.2 p.369 (pdf 384)

**Correction:** It is a heuristic that happens to work for Schwarzschild and RN seeds; it is not justified by the field equations, and the result must be checked.

**Why tempting:** It is presented as a clean procedure that gives the right answer.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### Horizon area can never decrease under any circumstances. · DIV §20.14 p.394 (pdf 409)

**Correction:** The area theorem assumes a classical energy condition; Hawking evaporation violates it, and the area shrinks as the hole loses mass, while total generalized entropy still increases.

**Why tempting:** The chapter states area increase as a law and later describes evaporation without reconciling them.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*


## Thought experiments

### Photon trying to hover against the spin · DIV §20.8 p.381 (pdf 396)

**Setup:** Light is guided to move at fixed r and theta around a Kerr hole, aimed opposite to the rotation, and we ask its angular velocity seen from infinity.

**Lesson:** At S_+ even light cannot make headway against the rotation, so no massive observer can be at rest there or inside.

**App idea:** Race a counter-rotating photon and rocket at chosen r; plot dphi/dt against r with the zero crossing at S_+.

### Collapse of a rotating star through both horizons · DIV §20.11 p.385 (pdf 400)

**Setup:** A spinning star with Kerr exterior collapses; follow an observer riding its surface through r_+, then r_-.

**Lesson:** Crossing r_+ is irreversible and crossing r_- is forced, but unlike Schwarzschild the collapse can halt in region III and the extension suggests emergence into a new universe.

### Travelling through the ring · DIV §20.10 p.384 (pdf 399)

**Setup:** An observer in the extended Kerr spacetime passes through the disc bounded by the ring singularity.

**Lesson:** They emerge where r < 0, in an asymptotically flat region whose mass looks negative, and near the ring can find closed timelike curves.

### Meeting yourself in the past · DIV §20.9 p.382 (pdf 397)

**Setup:** An observer follows one of the closed timelike curves in region III.

**Lesson:** Causality is violated: such an observer returns to their own earlier events, suggesting the inner Kerr regions are unphysical.

### Penrose process · DIV §20.11 p.387 (pdf 402)

**Setup:** A particle is fired into the ergosphere where it splits; one fragment falls inward, the other flies out.

**Lesson:** The escaping fragment can carry away more mass-energy than came in, drawn from the hole's rotational energy.

**App idea:** See Fig. 20.11 redesign.

### Objects popping out of nowhere · DIV §20.11 p.387 (pdf 402)

**Setup:** A space traveller sees a vase of flowers and a television set suddenly appear.

**Lesson:** Time-reversing the no-hair collapse describes a white hole, which would emit rather than absorb.

### Black hole in a thermal bath · DIV §20.14 p.394 (pdf 409)

**Setup:** Place a black hole with finite entropy (hence finite temperature) inside radiation at the same temperature.

**Lesson:** Classically it could only absorb, so no equilibrium is possible; the paradox is resolved only if the hole emits, which Hawking showed it does.


## Applications and observations

- **Final state of stellar collapse** (astrophysical-system, DIV §20.11 p.386 (pdf 401)): Collapse radiates away asymmetries once a horizon forms, leaving a stationary Kerr-Newman hole described by mass, spin and charge; realistic astrophysical holes are expected to be essentially uncharged Kerr. Key numbers: Horizon exists if a^2 + epsilon^2 <= m^2; for Kerr, r_+ ranges from 2m (a = 0) to m (a = m).
- **Ergosphere size** (numerical-estimate, DIV §20.5 p.375 (pdf 390)): S_+ reaches 2m at the equator for any spin and meets the horizon at the poles; the ergosphere is thickest for maximal spin (own reading of (20.46)-(20.47)). Key numbers: Extremal Kerr: equatorial ergosphere from r = m to r = 2m (Boyer-Lindquist).
- **Penrose process energy budget** (numerical-estimate, DIV §20.11 p.387 (pdf 402)): The text gives no numbers. Own addition: the rotational energy that can in principle be extracted is the difference between m and the irreducible mass, at most about 29% of the mass for an extremal hole. Key numbers: M_irr = sqrt(r_+^2 + a^2)/2; extremal: 1 - 1/sqrt2 ~ 0.29 (own estimate, not in text).
- **Hawking temperature of stellar black holes** (numerical-estimate, DIV §20.14 p.395 (pdf 410)): Evaluates T = hbar c^3/(8 pi G k_B M); stellar holes are far colder than the 2.7 K microwave background, so they currently absorb more than they emit (comparison is own). Key numbers: About 6x10^-8 K x (M_sun/M) (text prints the ratio inverted).
- **Evaporation lifetime** (numerical-estimate, DIV §20.14 p.396 (pdf 411)): Evaporation accelerates as mass decreases; a solar-mass hole would take about 10^67 years. Key numbers: ~10^67 yr for M_sun; lifetime scales as M^3 (scaling is own addition).
- **Primordial black holes as gamma-ray sources** (observation, DIV §20.14 p.396 (pdf 411)): Mini holes from the early universe could be finishing evaporation now and producing high-energy gamma bursts; comparisons with gamma-ray observations had been inconclusive. Key numbers: Text uses about 10 billion years since the big bang (current value 13.8 Gyr); holes near 5x10^11 kg would be ending now (own estimate).
- **No realistic interior for Kerr** (astrophysical-system, DIV §20.11 p.385 (pdf 400)): No physically realistic matter source is known that matches onto the Kerr exterior; a toroidal source is speculated because of the ring singularity.

## Historical notes

- **Roy Kerr (1963):** Kerr discovered the rotating vacuum solution by searching among metrics of the flat-plus-null-square type and wrote it in Cartesian-type coordinates. — First exact rotating black-hole solution; the year is general knowledge, not given in the text. (DIV §20.3 p.371 (pdf 386))
- **Ezra Newman, Allen Janis (1965):** Complex-transformation trick relating Schwarzschild to Kerr (and RN to Kerr-Newman) on a null tetrad. — Provides a short route to Kerr that this chapter adopts; year from general knowledge. (DIV §20.1 p.367 (pdf 382))
- **Robert Boyer, Richard Lindquist (1967):** Coordinates generalizing Schwarzschild coordinates to Kerr. — Standard coordinates for Kerr's properties; year from general knowledge. (DIV §20.3 p.371 (pdf 386))
- **Josef Lense, Hans Thirring (1918):** Weak-field exterior field of a rotating sphere. — Used as supporting evidence that ma is the Kerr angular momentum; year from general knowledge. (DIV §20.4 p.374 (pdf 389))
- **David Finkelstein (1958):** Reinterpretation of Schwarzschild as a black hole with a curvature singularity at r = 0. — Marked the modern acceptance of black holes after four decades of dismissing the Schwarzschild singularities. (DIV §20.13 p.391 (pdf 406))
- **Roger Penrose (1965):** First singularity theorem, introducing trapped surfaces and geodesic incompleteness. — Showed that departures from spherical symmetry do not avert collapse to a singularity. (DIV §20.13 p.391 (pdf 406))
- **Stephen Hawking, Roger Penrose, George Ellis, Robert Geroch (1965-1970):** Hawking's cosmological singularity result using a past trapped surface, followed by a series of papers culminating in the Hawking-Penrose theorem of 1970. — Established singularity theorems as a central achievement of modern GR. (DIV §20.13 p.392 (pdf 407))
- **Roger Penrose:** Proposal of energy extraction from the ergosphere of a rotating black hole and of the cosmic censorship hypothesis. — Showed black holes can be energy sources and framed the naked-singularity problem. (DIV §20.11 p.387 (pdf 402))
- **Yakov Zel'dovich (1971):** Suggested that rotating black holes, like spinning metal spheres, should amplify and emit radiation (superradiance). — A precursor of the discovery of Hawking radiation. (DIV §20.11 p.387 (pdf 402))
- **John Wheeler:** Coined the 'no hair' description with the cartoon of objects falling into a hole. — Popularized black-hole uniqueness. (DIV §20.11 p.386 (pdf 401))
- **Arthur Komar (1959):** Killing-vector surface integral for mass (year from general knowledge). — Standard definition of mass for stationary spacetimes. (DIV §20.12 p.389 (pdf 404))
- **Richard Arnowitt, Stanley Deser, Charles Misner (1959):** ADM Hamiltonian formulation giving a boundary-integral mass. — Mass for general asymptotically flat spacetimes. (DIV §20.12 p.391 (pdf 406))
- **Richard Schoen, Shing-Tung Yau (1979):** Proof of the positive energy theorem (year from general knowledge). — Guarantees stability-like positivity of total mass under the dominant energy condition. (DIV §20.12 p.391 (pdf 406))
- **Stephen Hawking (1974-1975):** Discovery that black holes emit thermal radiation (year from general knowledge). — Turned black-hole thermodynamics from analogy into physics and implied evaporation. (DIV §20.14 p.394 (pdf 409))

## Notation and conventions

- **Signature and units:** Signature (+, -, -, -); geometrized units G = c = 1 throughout, except SI units in the Hawking temperature (20.98). (DIV §20.1 p.368 (pdf 383))
- **Frame versus tensor indices:** Latin i, j label tetrad legs and are moved with the frame metric g_ij; Latin a, b are tensor indices moved with g_ab. — Later in §20.12, i, j = 1, 2, 3 denote spatial coordinate indices in the ADM integral. (DIV §20.1 p.367 (pdf 382))
- **Null tetrad normalization:** l.n = +1 and m.m-bar = -1, suited to signature (+---). — Books using (-+++) take l.n = -1 and m.m-bar = +1. (DIV §20.1 p.368 (pdf 383))
- **Kerr functions:** rho^2 = r^2 + a^2 cos^2 theta; Delta = r^2 - 2mr + a^2. — In ch19 Delta denoted the constant m^2 - epsilon^2; here it is the r-dependent horizon function. (DIV §20.3 p.371 (pdf 386))
- **Coordinate labels:** (x^0, x^1, x^2, x^3) = (v, r, theta, phi) in the tetrad construction and (t, r, theta, phi) in Boyer-Lindquist; bars mark advanced-type coordinates (t-bar, phi-bar) with v = t-bar + r. (DIV §20.3 p.370 (pdf 385))
- **Surface labels:** r_{S+/-} for infinite-redshift surfaces S_+/-; r_+/- for horizons (figures use superscripts r^+, r^-, S^+). (DIV §20.5 p.374 (pdf 389))
- **Charge symbol:** Black-hole charge is epsilon (geometrized Gaussian), not Q; Q denotes total electric charge only in §20.12. (DIV §20.11 p.385 (pdf 400))
- **Overloaded n and m:** n^a and m^a are null tetrad legs in §20.1-20.2 but unit timelike normal to Sigma and unit spacelike normal to the 2-surface in §20.12; m also denotes the mass. — Easy source of confusion when reading (20.86)-(20.88). (DIV §20.12 p.388 (pdf 403))
- **Overdot:** Derivative with respect to an affine parameter along a geodesic. (DIV §20.6 p.377 (pdf 392))
- **Charge units:** Lorentz-Heaviside units in the point-charge check, so F^tr = q/(4 pi r^2). (DIV §20.12 p.389 (pdf 404))

## Teaching gems

### Get a rotating solution by complex-shifting a static one on a null tetrad, then immediately sanity-check the a -> 0 limit. · DIV §20.2 p.369 (pdf 384)

**Why it works:** Replaces a notoriously long integration with a few steps and shows why null tetrads are worth learning; the limit check anchors trust.

**App idea:** Step-through animation: a symbolic panel shows the tetrad at each stage while a 3D view of r = const surfaces morphs from spheres to oblate spheroids as a is dialled up.

### Read spin off the metric without dynamics: invariance only under joint reversal of t and phi, plus a dt dphi term like a rotating frame. · DIV §20.4 p.372 (pdf 387)

**Why it works:** Uses symmetry reasoning learners already trust and a flat-space calculation they can do in two lines.

**App idea:** Symmetry toggles (t -> -t, phi -> -phi, a -> -a) applied to a live metric; the metric highlights which terms change sign, showing only the combined flips leave it unchanged.

### Separate two conditions that coincided for Schwarzschild: g_00 = 0 (infinite redshift) and g^11 = 0 (null r = const). Their gap is the ergosphere. · DIV §20.5 p.375 (pdf 390)

**Why it works:** Turns a new structure into a contrast with something familiar, and explains why the horizon and redshift surface are different notions.

**App idea:** Dual plot of g_00 and g^11 versus r at chosen theta with a spin slider; shaded band where the two differ maps onto the 3D ergosphere.

### Define the stationary limit operationally: force light to circle at fixed r and theta, aim it against the spin, and find where it stalls. · DIV §20.8 p.381 (pdf 396)

**Why it works:** A physical test replaces abstract Killing-vector language; the formula yields a clean inequality.

**App idea:** Plot the two branches of dphi/dt from (20.70) against r; the counter-rotating branch crosses zero at S_+, animated with a pair of photons on a ring.

### Wavefront diagram in the equatorial plane: whether an emitter sits inside its own light front encodes static, forced co-rotation, and forced infall. · DIV §20.8 p.382 (pdf 397)

**Why it works:** A purely spatial picture of light-cone tilting, readable without Penrose diagrams.

**App idea:** See Fig. 20.5 redesign: clickable flash-bulbs with spin slider.

### Picture r through a disc: confocal ellipsoids collapse to a disc at r = 0; glue its faces crosswise to a second copy of space to reach negative r. · DIV §20.10 p.384 (pdf 399)

**Why it works:** Makes an analytic continuation tangible and explains how the ring bounds a passage.

## Gaps and pitfalls

- **The Newman-Janis trick is presented without any reason why it works, and the choice of how to complexify r (which r become r-bar) is not justified.** — Learners may think it is a general derivation, or feel the result is conjured. *Suggestion:* Say plainly that it is a heuristic verified afterwards by checking the vacuum equations; show that other complexifications fail; mention it does not generate general rotating interior or non-vacuum solutions.
- **Printed misprints: (20.33) has a^2 + y^2 instead of a^2 + r^2; (20.43) has x dy - y dz instead of x dy - y dx; the RN seed on p.385 has dr^2 instead of dv^2; the normalization before (20.88) has (m^0)^2 instead of (m^1)^2; Exercise 20.2 cites (16.24) instead of (17.24).** — Students checking the algebra will get stuck or distrust correct reasoning. *Suggestion:* Show corrected equations in the app and flag them in tutor responses.
- **The Hawking temperature scale is printed as (M/M_sun) x 6x10^-8 K, i.e. proportional to mass, contradicting (20.98) and the preceding sentence.** — Reinforces the misconception that bigger black holes are hotter. *Suggestion:* Use T ~ 6x10^-8 K x (M_sun/M) and stress the negative heat capacity.
- **In (20.89)-(20.90) the sign of nabla_0 K_1 is dropped: a direct calculation gives -m/r^2, so a positive mass requires choosing the inward-pointing normal or an overall sign in the definition, none of which is discussed.** — A careful student obtains E = -m and cannot find the error. *Suggestion:* State orientation conventions for n^a and m^a explicitly or define the Komar integral with the conventional sign; also note the Komar normalization differs (factor 2) for angular momentum.
- **Infinite-redshift surfaces are identified with g_00 = 0 by assertion, and horizons with g^11 = 0, without explaining that the horizon generator is the combination d/dt + Omega_H d/dphi or defining surface gravity.** — The thermodynamics section then refers to surface gravity with no formula and no link to Kerr. *Suggestion:* Introduce the horizon angular velocity Omega_H = a/(r_+^2 + a^2) and kappa = (r_+ - r_-)/(2(r_+^2 + a^2)); show kappa -> 0 at extremality.
- **The statement that any spacetime with a forming horizon approaches Kerr-Newman is described as a proved theorem.** — Overstates what is rigorously known; the uniqueness theorems concern stationary holes and nonlinear stability of Kerr is only partly proved. *Suggestion:* Distinguish stationary uniqueness theorems from the final-state conjecture; mention recent stability results for slowly rotating Kerr.
- **Area increase is stated as a law and evaporation is described afterwards, but the conflict (evaporation shrinks the horizon) is never addressed, and the entropy formula S = A/4 is omitted.** — Learners are left with two incompatible statements and no quantitative entropy. *Suggestion:* Add the generalized second law and S_BH = k_B c^3 A/(4 G hbar).
- **The Penrose process is purely verbal: no negative Killing energy, no efficiency, no irreducible mass.** — Hard to see why the ergosphere is essential or how much energy is available. *Suggestion:* Show that E = p_a K^a can be negative only where K is spacelike, and give the 29% extraction bound for extremal Kerr.
- **Global structure for a^2 < m^2 is given only by analogy with RN and only along the axis; no Kruskal-type coordinates or off-axis discussion.** — Learners may think the diagram describes all of Kerr, and the negative-r and CTC claims appear unsupported. *Suggestion:* State that the diagram is a 2D slice through theta = 0 and show where the ring (which lies at theta = pi/2) enters.
- **The text calls a 'related to the angular velocity' and uses the same letter for a flat-space angular velocity in (20.37).** — Confuses angular momentum per unit mass with a rotation rate. *Suggestion:* Name a the specific angular momentum J/M and use Omega for angular velocities.
- **The claim that the 1/R terms in g_0alpha determine angular momentum is imprecise: in (20.43) the rotational term falls off as 1/R^2.** — Mismatch with the displayed expansion. *Suggestion:* Write g_0i ~ -2 epsilon_ijk J^j x^k/R^3 and compare with Lense-Thirring.
- **Symbols n^a and m^a are reused for tetrad legs and for hypersurface normals, and m for mass.** — Readers moving between §20.1 and §20.12 can misread (20.86)-(20.88). *Suggestion:* Use u^a or N^a for the timelike normal and s^a or r^a for the 2-surface normal in the app.
- **Dated or missing astrophysics: 'about 10 billion years' since the big bang; no mention of observational evidence for Kerr black holes (gravitational-wave ringdowns, spin measurements, horizon-scale imaging).** — Learners miss that Kerr is now tested, not merely a theoretical endpoint. *Suggestion:* Add 13.8 Gyr and a short modern-evidence panel (LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA remnant spins, EHT images of M87* and Sgr A*, X-ray reflection spin estimates).
- **The Hawking pair-creation picture is heuristic and says nothing about why the spectrum is thermal or why T is proportional to 1/M.** — Learners may believe tunnelling cartoons are the derivation. *Suggestion:* Give the scaling argument T ~ hbar kappa/(2 pi k_B c) with kappa = c^4/(4GM), labelling it as a result from quantum field theory in curved spacetime.

## Tutor notes

- Open with: 'What would you expect to change about a black hole if the star that formed it was spinning?' Collect guesses (flattening, dragging, stronger or weaker horizon) and return to them after §20.5.
- Recommended order: Boyer-Lindquist metric and its a -> 0 limit first, then the three rotation clues, then g_00 = 0 versus Delta = 0 and the ergosphere picture, then principal congruences and EF coordinates, then global structure; treat the Newman-Janis construction as optional enrichment for learners comfortable with tetrads.
- Whenever Kerr shows a new feature, ask the learner what the Schwarzschild analogue was; the chapter is built as a replay, so this keeps them oriented.
- Check for understanding: 'Can you be inside the ergosphere and still escape? Can you be inside the stationary limit surface and stay at rest?' (yes; no).
- Check for understanding: 'Where is the Kerr singularity, and what shape is r = 0?' Expect a ring of radius a and a disc; probe whether they know r is not Euclidean radius.
- When learners ask 'what is a?' answer J/M (a length in G = c = 1), give the bound a <= m for a horizon, and warn that the book loosely calls it an angular velocity.
- Common question: 'Is the inner region with negative r and time machines real?' Explain that it belongs to the idealized eternal solution; the inner horizon is believed unstable and realistic collapse is not expected to produce it.
- Correct the printed Hawking temperature scaling whenever Hawking radiation comes up: T is about 6x10^-8 K times M_sun/M; larger holes are colder.
- For §20.12, first redo the electric Gauss-law argument with the learner, then substitute R^ab K_b for j^a; point out the sign/orientation subtlety in the Schwarzschild check rather than letting it derail them.
- For singularity theorems, have the learner fill the three slots for Penrose 1965 (trapped surface, null convergence, a causality/global condition) and Hawking-Penrose 1970, then ask which slot Kerr's interior violates (causality: closed timelike curves).
- Useful numbers to have ready (own): r_+ = 2m at a = 0 and m at a = m; equatorial S_+ always at 2m; maximum extractable rotational energy about 29% of m; solar-mass Hawking temperature about 6x10^-8 K.
- Distinguish what the chapter proves (horizon locations, stationary limit criterion, Komar mass of Schwarzschild) from what it only reports (no-hair, Penrose process efficiencies, positive energy, singularity theorems, Hawking radiation) so learners calibrate their confidence.
