# DIV ch23 · Radiation from an isolated source

> Outlines Bondi's non-linear treatment of gravitational radiation from an axisymmetric, non-rotating isolated source: null hypersurfaces as characteristic wave fronts, radiation coordinates and the Bondi metric, the characteristic initial value problem, the news function and the exact mass-loss theorem, then surveys the algebraic tools (Petrov types, principal null directions, peeling, optical scalars, Goldberg-Sachs) used to describe radiation invariantly.

**Pages:** printed 461–478 · pdf 476–493 · **Difficulty:** math 5/5, conceptual 5/5, novice-friendliness 2/5

One of the most advanced chapters: characteristic PDE theory, asymptotic expansions, algebraic classification and null congruence kinematics are compressed into 15 pages. Only the short lemmas are proved; the asymptotic solution, Bianchi lemma, data count and all Petrov and peeling results are quoted. The physical storyline (mass loss iff news) is clear and the figures help, but a learner needs Chs 13, 14, 17, 18 and 20 fresh in mind, and symbols (θ, r, subscript 0) are overloaded.

## Role in the book

The last of the three gravitational-wave chapters. Chapter 21 derived waves and energy flux only in the linearized theory, and Chapter 22 found exact plane and cylindrical waves that have no source. This chapter closes the loop by asking whether a bounded, genuinely non-linear source really loses mass by radiating, and answers yes without any weak-field assumption. It pulls together machinery built much earlier (the Cauchy problem of Ch. 13, the 2+2 formalism of Ch. 14, null hypersurfaces of Ch. 17, Penrose diagrams and null infinity of Ch. 18, null tetrads and mass definitions of Ch. 20) and ends with classification tools and the null Raychaudhuri equation that point towards exact-solution theory and the singularity theorems, before the book turns to cosmology in Ch. 24.

## Learning objectives

- Reader can explain why the linearized quadrupole result leaves open whether gravitational waves really carry energy, and state the quiescent-radiating-quiescent source model used to settle it.
- Reader can show that a surface x^0 = const on which the Cauchy evolution equations cannot be solved for second time derivatives must have g^{00} = 0, i.e. is null, and so is a characteristic hypersurface.
- Reader can prove that the integral curves of l^a = g^{ab} ∂_b u for a family of null hypersurfaces are null geodesics with affine parameter.
- Reader can build radiation coordinates (u, r, θ, φ), derive g^{00} = g^{02} = g^{03} = 0, and explain the difference between Bondi's areal (luminosity) r and the Newman-Penrose affine r.
- Reader can write down the Bondi radiating metric, identify its four free functions V, U, β, γ, and say which encode lapse and shift in the 2+2 picture.
- Reader can sort the ten vacuum equations into symmetry, main (hypersurface plus dynamical), trivial and supplementary groups and describe the null-cone marching schema.
- Reader can list the free data of the characteristic problem (γ on N_0, news on scri+, M and d on the cut) and place them on a Penrose diagram.
- Reader can define the news function, mass aspect and Bondi mass, and derive m_{,u} = -½∫ n_{,u}^2 sin θ dθ from the supplementary equation with axis regularity.
- Reader can state the Petrov types, arrange them in Penrose's specialization diagram, and relate them to coincidences of principal null directions.
- Reader can state the peeling theorem and interpret each order in 1/r as a Petrov type, and describe Szekeres' test-particle pictures for types N, III and D.
- Reader can define the expansion, twist and shear of a null geodesic congruence, interpret them via Sachs' shadow picture, and state the Goldberg-Sachs theorem and the null Raychaudhuri equation.

## Teaching approach

A compressed research-level survey rather than a full derivation. The authors pose one sharp physical question (does a real, non-linear isolated source lose mass by radiating?), fix a deliberately simple model (axisymmetric, reflection-symmetric, quiescent before and after), and then walk through Bondi's programme step by step, proving only the short geometric lemmas (null hypersurfaces are characteristic, their generators are affinely parametrized null geodesics, coordinate conditions on the metric) while quoting the heavy asymptotic results and delegating derivations to exercises. Pictures carry much of the load: a world-tube diagram states the goal, cone sketches show the coordinates and the marching schema, and a Penrose diagram shows where the free data live. The second half switches from the specific metric to invariant algebra (Petrov, peeling, optical scalars), linking each abstract type to a physical picture of test particles or shadows.

**Style:** physical-motivation-first, derivation-heavy, survey-overview, diagram-driven, spiral-revisit

**Narrative arc**

1. Motivating doubt: linearized results and source-free exact waves cannot decide whether gravitational waves carry energy away from a real source.
2. Choose the simplest radiating model: after spherical and two-Killing-vector cases, allow one rotational Killing vector plus azimuthal reflection; state the goal pictorially as a world tube whose mass drops (Fig. 23.1).
3. Identify wave fronts: hyperbolic equations admit time-bomb solutions carried on characteristics; the Cauchy evolution equations fail exactly when g^{00} = 0, so characteristic surfaces are null hypersurfaces.
4. Build coordinates on those fronts: u labels null hypersurfaces, their generators are shown to be affinely parametrized null geodesics, r runs along rays, angles label rays at scri+.
5. Reduce the metric: ray conditions, areal choice of r and the two symmetries collapse g_ab to Bondi's four-function metric, reinterpreted in 2+2 lapse/shift language.
6. Set up the characteristic initial value problem: group the field equations, state the Bianchi lemma, split main equations into hypersurface and dynamical, and describe marching from one null cone to the next.
7. Go asymptotic: impose fall-off and an outgoing condition, expand in 1/r, record the free functions, and identify the news as the data that drives evolution; name the BMS group along the way.
8. Payoff: integrate the exact supplementary equation over angles to obtain the Bondi mass-loss formula and the news theorem; note Sachs' generalization and positivity of Bondi mass.
9. Change viewpoint to invariants: algebraic classification of the Weyl tensor (Petrov, Penrose diagram, Debever principal null directions), with known solutions assigned to types.
10. Physical meaning of the algebra: peeling expansion of curvature along outgoing rays and Szekeres' test-particle distortions for types N, III, D.
11. Close with null congruence kinematics: optical scalars, Sachs' shadow interpretation, Goldberg-Sachs, and the null Raychaudhuri equation as a bridge to the singularity theorems.

**Signature moves**

- States the final theorem pictorially before any mathematics: a hatched world tube narrows between two null cones with m_2 < m_1.
- Defines characteristic surfaces operationally as those where the Cauchy data fail to determine second time derivatives, and reuses the book's own evolution equation (13.23) to read off g^{00} = 0.
- Short line-by-line covariant proof that null-hypersurface generators are geodesics, pointing to exactly where torsion-freeness and nullness of the whole family are used.
- Reduces a general metric to Bondi form by successive coordinate choices and symmetries, each converted into vanishing components, with the algebra pushed into a single guided exercise.
- Uses a bookkeeping hierarchy of field equations (symmetry, main, trivial, supplementary; hypersurface vs dynamical) as the organizing structure for an otherwise unreadable calculation.
- Counts free data as functions of three, two and one variables and places each on a Penrose diagram, making 'what can you choose freely' visual.
- Turns a sign into a theorem: the mass-loss integrand is a square, so mass loss happens exactly when news is non-zero.
- Pairs each abstract algebraic type with a concrete physical picture (triangle of specialization, peeling arrows, deformed rings of particles, shadows on a screen).
- Frequent parenthetical 'exercise' markers and one open 'why?' prompt to hand verification to the reader.

## Section by section

### §23.1 Radiating isolated sources — p.461 (pdf 476)

Argues that neither linearized waves nor global exact wave solutions settle whether gravitational radiation transports energy, because non-linearity matters (colliding plane waves already form singularities) and exact waves lack sources. Proposes studying a bounded source that is static, radiates for a finite time, then settles, and expects its mass to drop. Since spherical sources cannot radiate and two-Killing-vector cases were done in Ch. 22, it adopts Bondi's assumptions: one hypersurface-orthogonal rotational Killing vector with azimuthal reflection invariance (no rotation), optionally equatorial reflection (dropped as unhelpful). It flags, but sets aside, the worry that backscatter might keep the source excited forever.

**Concepts:** radiating isolated source, axial symmetry, azimuthal reflection symmetry, non-linearity of the gravitational field

**Key moves**
- Motivation: energy transfer, not wave existence, is the physical test of whether gravitational waves are radiation like any other.
- Model: quiescent for a semi-infinite past, radiating for a finite interval, quiescent again; predicted mass drop drawn as Fig. 23.1.
- Symmetry ladder: spherical (no waves) -> two commuting spacelike Killing vectors (Ch. 22) -> one rotational Killing vector plus reflection (Bondi).
- Symmetries written as coordinate invariances (23.1)-(23.3), with (23.2) forbidding rotation and (23.3) omitted.
- Explicitly assumes that a return to quiescence is possible, deferring the backscatter question.

### §23.2 Characteristic hypersurfaces of Einstein's equations — p.462 (pdf 477)

Notes that the field equations are hyperbolic (wave equations in a suitable linearized gauge), and that hyperbolic systems allow 'time-bomb' solutions that switch on suddenly, with influences travelling along bicharacteristics lying in characteristic hypersurfaces that act as wave fronts and where different solutions can join and Cauchy data fail. Using the Cauchy evolution form g^{00} g_{ab,00} = 2M_{ab} from Ch. 13, the second time derivatives are undetermined exactly when g^{00} = 0, which is the null-hypersurface condition. A null hypersurface's normal is null, hence tangent, so the surface touches the light cone and is ruled by null geodesics that are the bicharacteristics.

**Concepts:** hyperbolic system of field equations, time-bomb solution, characteristic hypersurface, bicharacteristics, null hypersurface

**Key moves**
- Contrast hyperbolic with elliptic and parabolic equations via the existence of initially static solutions that later become dynamic.
- Characterize characteristic surfaces as those on which the Cauchy problem is not solvable.
- Read off g^{00} = 0 as the obstruction in (13.23) and recall from §17.1 that it makes x^0 = const null.
- Normal of a null surface is null, hence lies in the surface; surface is tangent to the local light cone (Fig. 23.2).
- Conclude that gravitational disturbances propagate along null geodesics, recovering the linearized picture non-linearly.

### §23.3 Radiation coordinates — p.463 (pdf 478)

Introduces u = x^0 labelling a non-intersecting family of null hypersurfaces, with covariant normal l_a = ∂_a u null. Shows by a short covariant calculation that the integral curves of l^a are null geodesics with affine parameter (null rays). Chooses x^1 = r as a radial parameter along the rays and, assuming asymptotic flatness, uses the polar angles on the spheres at future null infinity to label rays. These Bondi (radiation) coordinates are valid only near scri+, because rays traced inward generally focus and cross.

**Concepts:** radiation coordinates, null rays, affine parameter, asymptotic flatness, future null infinity

**Key moves**
- Normal covector l_a = δ_a^0 and its nullness (23.6).
- Absolute derivative of dx^a/dρ = g^{ab}∂_b u; swap ∇_c∂_b u = ∇_b∂_c u by symmetry of the connection; recognise a gradient of g^{cd}∂_c u ∂_d u, which vanishes because every member of the family is null (23.9).
- Coordinates: r along rays, θ and φ fixed on each ray by their values at scri+ (23.12).
- Honest limitation: caustics inside the source region mean the chart is only asymptotic (Fig. 23.3).

### §23.4 Bondi's radiating metric — p.465 (pdf 480)

Converts the coordinate construction into restrictions on the metric. Since the r-lines are the rays, l^a = g^{0a} is proportional to δ^a_1, giving g^{00} = g^{02} = g^{03} = 0, equivalently g_{11} = g_{12} = g_{13} = 0. Contrasts the Newman-Penrose affine choice (g_{01} = 1) with Bondi's luminosity-distance choice fixing the angular 2-metric determinant to r^4 sin^2θ so spheres of constant u and r have area 4πr^2. Axial symmetry removes φ dependence and reflection invariance kills the φ cross terms, leaving Bondi's metric with four functions V, U, β, γ of (u, r, θ). Reinterprets it as a 2+2 split in which the u = const surfaces are null, r = const surfaces timelike, U and β carry shift and V the lapse.

**Concepts:** Bondi radiating metric, luminosity distance parameter, affine parameter, axial symmetry, azimuthal reflection symmetry, 2+2 formalism

**Key moves**
- Tangent to a ray is δ^a_1, so l^a = λδ^a_1 and l^a = g^{0a} give (23.13).
- Inverse-metric zeros translate into covariant zeros (23.14), a matrix-inversion exercise.
- Radial gauge choice: affine (NP) versus areal determinant condition (23.16).
- Symmetry reductions (23.17)-(23.19).
- Assemble (23.20) with an exponential parametrization chosen to keep signature manifest.
- Translate into 2+2 language (link to §14.13, Fig. 14.9).

### §23.5 The characteristic initial value problem — p.467 (pdf 482)

Sets up evolution from data on a null hypersurface. Bondi's split of the ten vacuum equations: three components vanish identically by symmetry, four main equations, one trivial equation, two supplementary conditions. A lemma parallel to constraint propagation in the Cauchy problem: if the main equations hold everywhere, the Bianchi identities make the trivial equation hold algebraically and make the supplementary conditions hold everywhere once they hold on one r = const surface. The main equations divide into one dynamical equation (the only one with a u derivative) and three hypersurface equations acting within a null cone. For analytic solutions, one prescribes γ on N_0, solves the hypersurface equations for β, U, V, uses the dynamical equation for γ_{,u}, steps to the neighbouring cone and repeats; integration functions, including the news, are postponed.

**Concepts:** characteristic initial value problem, Bondi hierarchy of field equations, hypersurface equations, dynamical equation, contracted Bianchi identities, null-cone integration schema

**Key moves**
- Grouping (23.22)-(23.25).
- Lemma as the characteristic analogue of constraint preservation, with the roles of constraints played by the trivial and supplementary equations.
- Further split (23.26)-(23.27): dynamical versus hypersurface.
- Marching algorithm N_0 -> N_1 -> ... (Fig. 23.4).
- Flag that neglected integration functions will matter.

### §23.6 News and mass loss — p.468 (pdf 483)

Imposes asymptotic fall-off (23.28) and names the BMS group of form-preserving transformations as the asymptotic analogue of the Poincare group. Adds Bondi's outgoing condition (23.29), modelled on Sommerfeld's, while noting it cannot exclude weak incoming radiation. Expands γ, β, U, V in 1/r, introducing n, q, d, M; records the dynamical and (exact) supplementary equations. Data count: γ on N_0 (function of three variables), n_{,u} on scri+ (two variables), M and d on the cut N_0 ∩ scri+ (one variable). Since n_{,u} drives the evolution it is the news; M is the mass aspect, d and q dipole and quadrupole aspects; the angular average of M is the Bondi mass, matching ADM/Komar when stationary and Newton in the weak field. Integrating the M equation over the sphere gives the mass-loss formula: mass is lost if and only if there is news. The result is non-perturbative, coordinate independent (tied to the leading Weyl tensor), corresponds to the radiated power, holds in Sachs' general case with two news functions, and Bondi mass positivity was later proved.

**Concepts:** asymptotic expansion in 1/r, BMS group, outgoing radiation condition, news function, mass aspect, dipole aspect, quadrupole aspect, Bondi mass, Bondi mass-loss formula, positivity of Bondi mass, two news functions

**Key moves**
- Asymptotic conditions (23.28) plus (23.29).
- Expansions (23.30)-(23.31) with arbitrary functions n, q, d, M.
- Supplementary equations (23.32)-(23.33) are exact because they need hold on only one r.
- Free-data count (23.34)-(23.36) laid out on a Penrose diagram (Fig. 23.5).
- Identify M with mass via static limit and define m(u) (23.37).
- Multiply (23.32) by sin θ, integrate, drop the total-derivative term by axis regularity, obtain (23.38).
- Theorem: mass loss iff news; interpret as radiated power without linearization.

### §23.7 The Petrov classification — p.471 (pdf 486)

In vacuum the Riemann tensor equals the Weyl tensor, which is trace-free and has the Riemann symmetries. Viewed as a symmetric 6×6 matrix on bivector space it could be classified by eigenstructure; the extra symmetries reduce the problem to the root structure of a quartic, giving Petrov types I, II, D, III, N plus the conformally flat type O. Penrose's triangle arranges them by specialization; a spacetime's type is the most general type it attains, and types can only drop at special points. Type I is algebraically general, the rest special. Equivalently (Debever) one classifies principal null directions, of which there are between one and four, by their multiplicities. Schwarzschild, Reissner-Nordstrom and Kerr are type D, plane waves type N; realistic sources are type I but become type N asymptotically, with curvature falling like n_{,uu}/r. Mentions Plebanski (Ricci) and Karlhede classifications.

**Concepts:** Weyl tensor, Petrov classification, Penrose specialization diagram, algebraically general and special spacetimes, principal null directions, Plebanski classification, Karlhede classification

**Key moves**
- Vacuum reduction C_abcd = R_abcd via (23.39).
- Pair symmetries -> 6×6 symmetric matrix; extra symmetries (23.40) and cyclic identity reduce sextic to quartic.
- Table 23.1 (root multiplicities, eigenvector counts) and Fig. 23.6 hierarchy.
- Debever theorem and Table 23.2 (PND coincidences).
- Assign known solutions to types; Bondi solution type I with asymptotic type N.

### §23.8 The peeling theorem — p.473 (pdf 488)

Gives physical meaning to the algebra. Sachs expanded the curvature of an outgoing linearized wave in an affine parameter along rays: the 1/r term is type N, 1/r^2 type III, 1/r^3 type II, 1/r^4 type I (with the 0 subscript meaning parallel propagated along the ray). He then showed the same structure holds for asymptotically flat isolated radiating systems in full GR: far out the four principal null directions coincide along the ray, and moving inward they separate one by one. Szekeres' test-particle analysis: type N squeezes and stretches a ring transverse to the propagation direction; type III produces a planar distortion in a plane containing the propagation direction, tilted by 45°; type D stretches a sphere into a prolate ellipsoid along the propagation direction, the familiar Schwarzschild tidal effect (Coulomb-like). Types I and II give nothing simple.

**Concepts:** peeling theorem, wave zone, principal null directions, Szekeres test-particle interpretation, transverse wave, longitudinal wave component, Coulomb-type field

**Key moves**
- Expansion (23.41) with each order labelled by its Petrov type.
- Extension from linearized to full non-linear isolated systems (Sachs).
- Pictorial statement: principal null directions peel off inward (Fig. 23.7).
- Physical reading via orthonormal triads and deformed rings/spheres (Figs 23.8-23.10), cross-linked to Fig. 21.2 and §17.10.

### §23.9 The optical scalars — p.474 (pdf 489)

For an affinely parametrized null geodesic congruence defines expansion, twist and shear. Sachs' interpretation: an object in the beam casts a shadow on a nearby screen whose size, rotation and distortion over distance dr are set by θ, ω and |σ|, independent of screen velocity. Shear is singled out by the Goldberg-Sachs theorem: a vacuum spacetime is algebraically special exactly when it has a shear-free null geodesic congruence; in isolated systems the news is related to shear. Petrov types, optical scalars and Killing vectors are the main invariant tools for finding exact solutions (all vacuum type D solutions are known) though many solutions remain poorly understood or pathological. Finally, the Ricci identity on l^a yields propagation equations such as the complex null Raychaudhuri equation, key to singularity theorems.

**Concepts:** null geodesic congruence, optical scalars, expansion of a null congruence, twist of a null congruence, shear of a null congruence, Sachs shadow interpretation, Goldberg-Sachs theorem, null Raychaudhuri equation, Ricci identity

**Key moves**
- Rescale l^a to affine form (exercise 23.10).
- Define θ, ω, |σ| from symmetric, antisymmetric and trace parts of l_{a;b}.
- Shadow-on-a-screen picture (Fig. 23.11).
- Goldberg-Sachs theorem links congruence kinematics to Petrov type.
- Status report on exact solutions classified by these tools.
- Ricci identity (23.42) -> propagation equation for z = -θ + iω (23.44).

## Concepts

### radiating isolated source

*solution-or-model · core* · also: bounded isolated source, quiescent radiating model

A bounded matter distribution in an otherwise empty, asymptotically flat spacetime that is stationary for all early times, emits gravitational radiation for a finite interval, and is stationary again afterwards; used to test whether radiation carries away mass-energy.

**How introduced:** Motivated by doubts about linearized theory and by the unphysical, source-free nature of exact plane waves; drawn as a world tube with outgoing radiation bands and a smaller final mass.

**Prerequisites:** asymptotic flatness, gravitational waves (linearized)

$$
m_2 < m_1
$$

**Notes:** The assumption that the source can become quiescent again is explicitly not justified; backscatter could in principle keep it ringing.

**Where:** DIV §23.1 p.461 (pdf 476); DIV §23.1 p.462 (pdf 477)

### energy transfer by gravitational waves

*phenomenon · core* · also: radiated energy, mass-energy carried by waves

Whether gravitational waves remove mass-energy from the body that emits them; the book takes this, not the mere existence of wave solutions, as the test of whether gravitational radiation is radiation in the same sense as light.

**How introduced:** Set in bold in the opening paragraph as the reason to study bounded sources rather than source-free exact waves; it becomes the target proved by the Bondi mass-loss formula in §23.6, where the rate of mass decrease is identified with the radiated power dE/dt.

**Prerequisites:** gravitational energy (linearized), radiating isolated source

$$
m_{,0}=-\tfrac12\int_0^\pi n_{,0}^2\sin\theta\,d\theta
$$

**Notes:** Unlike the §21.9 flux formula, the non-linear result does not need a weak field.

**Where:** DIV §23.1 p.461 (pdf 476); DIV §23.6 p.471 (pdf 486)

### non-linearity of the gravitational field

*principle · mention* · also: non-linear effects

The Einstein equations are non-linear in the metric, so solutions do not superpose and effects absent in the linearized theory (e.g. singularity formation from colliding plane waves, wave self-interaction) can occur.

**How introduced:** Opening argument for why linearized conclusions about energy transport need independent confirmation.

**Prerequisites:** linearized field equations

**Where:** DIV §23.1 p.461 (pdf 476)

### axial symmetry

*definition · developed* · also: axisymmetry, rotational Killing vector

Existence of a spacelike Killing vector ∂/∂φ with closed circular orbits, so the metric in adapted coordinates is unchanged under φ -> φ + const and its components do not depend on φ. Bondi also asks that this Killing vector be hypersurface orthogonal, which is the geometric form of the no-rotation condition that the discrete reflection φ -> -φ enforces.

**How introduced:** The next simplest symmetry after the spherical and two-Killing-vector cases; imposed on the radiation coordinates and yielding ∂g_ab/∂φ = 0.

**Prerequisites:** Killing vector

$$
\phi\to\phi+\text{const}
$$

$$
\partial g_{ab}/\partial\phi=0
$$

**Where:** DIV §23.1 p.461 (pdf 476); DIV §23.4 p.466 (pdf 481)

### azimuthal reflection symmetry

*definition · developed* · also: azimuth reflection invariance, non-rotation condition, hypersurface-orthogonal rotational Killing vector

Invariance of the metric under φ -> -φ. Because a rotation would single out a sense of circulation about the axis, this symmetry excludes rotation; in adapted coordinates it forces all cross terms between dφ and the other coordinate differentials to vanish.

**How introduced:** Stated as (23.2) with a 'why?' prompt to the reader, then turned into g_03 = g_13 = g_23 = 0 in §23.4.

**Prerequisites:** axial symmetry

$$
\phi\to-\phi
$$

$$
g_{03}=g_{13}=g_{23}=0
$$

**Notes:** The book leaves the 'why?' unanswered: under φ -> -φ a term like g_03 du dφ changes sign, and angular momentum about the axis flips sign, so invariance requires both to vanish.

**Where:** DIV §23.1 p.461 (pdf 476); DIV §23.4 p.466 (pdf 481)

### hyperbolic system of field equations

*principle · introduced* · also: hyperbolicity of Einstein's equations

The vacuum Einstein equations, suitably gauge-fixed, form a hyperbolic system whose solutions propagate at finite speed along characteristics, unlike elliptic or parabolic systems.

**How introduced:** Seen most easily in the linearized theory where a gauge reduces them to wave equations; used to motivate searching for wave fronts.

**Prerequisites:** Cauchy problem, linearized field equations

**Where:** DIV §23.2 p.462 (pdf 477)

### time-bomb solution

*phenomenon · introduced* · also: initially static then dynamic solution

A solution of a hyperbolic system that is exactly static up to some moment and then becomes dynamic, possible because data can be joined continuously across characteristic surfaces; elliptic and parabolic equations forbid this.

**How introduced:** Borrowed from Bondi to dramatize the special character of hyperbolic equations.

**Prerequisites:** hyperbolic system of field equations

**Where:** DIV §23.2 p.463 (pdf 478)

### characteristic hypersurface

*definition · core* · also: characteristic surface, wave front

A hypersurface on which Cauchy data do not determine the normal (second) derivatives of the field, so distinct solutions can meet continuously across it; for Einstein's equations these are exactly the null hypersurfaces.

**How introduced:** Derived from the Cauchy evolution equations (13.23): the coefficient g^{00} of g_{ab,00} vanishes precisely on null surfaces x^0 = const.

**Prerequisites:** Cauchy problem, null hypersurface

$$
g^{00}g_{\alpha\beta,00}=2M_{\alpha\beta}
$$

### null hypersurface

*definition · core* · also: null surface, lightlike hypersurface

A hypersurface whose normal vector is null; the normal therefore also lies in the surface, the surface is everywhere tangent to the local light cone, and it is generated by null geodesics.

### null rays

*theorem · developed* · also: null geodesic generators, affinely parametrized null geodesics

For a family of null hypersurfaces u = const, the curves with tangent l^a = g^{ab}∂_b u satisfy l^b∇_b l^a = 0: they are null geodesics and the parameter along them is affine.

### radiation coordinates

### luminosity distance parameter

*convention · developed* · also: areal radius, Bondi r

### Bondi radiating metric

*solution-or-model · core* · also: Bondi-van der Burg-Metzner metric, Bondi-Sachs metric (axisymmetric)

The general axisymmetric, non-rotating line element in radiation coordinates, containing four free functions V, U, β, γ of (u, r, θ): ds^2 = (V e^{2β}/r - U^2 r^2 e^{2γ}) du^2 + 2e^{2β} du dr + 2U r^2 e^{2γ} du dθ - r^2(e^{2γ}dθ^2 + e^{-2γ} sin^2θ dφ^2).

**How introduced:** Obtained by stacking the ray conditions, the areal gauge and the two symmetries on a general metric; interpreted in 2+2 language.

**Prerequisites:** radiation coordinates, luminosity distance parameter, axial symmetry, azimuthal reflection symmetry

$$
ds^2=\left(\frac{V}{r}e^{2\beta}-U^2r^2e^{2\gamma}\right)du^2+2e^{2\beta}du\,dr+2Ur^2e^{2\gamma}du\,d\theta-r^2(e^{2\gamma}d\theta^2+e^{-2\gamma}\sin^2\theta\,d\phi^2)

**Notes:** γ carries the single radiative degree of freedom allowed by the symmetry; V/r -> 1 - 2M/r reproduces Schwarzschild-like behaviour.

**Where:** DIV §23.4 p.466 (pdf 481)

### 2+2 formalism

*technique · revisited* · also: double null / characteristic decomposition

Splitting spacetime into a two-parameter family of spacelike 2-surfaces and the 2-dimensional orthogonal directions, with lapse- and shift-like functions describing how neighbouring 2-surfaces are connected.

**How introduced:** Bondi's metric is presented as an instance: 2-surfaces S_{u,r} with metric r^2(e^{2γ}dθ^2 + e^{-2γ}sin^2θ dφ^2), null u-surfaces, timelike r-surfaces, U and β as shifts, V as lapse.

**Prerequisites:** 3+1 formalism, lapse and shift

### characteristic initial value problem

*technique · core* · also: null initial value problem, characteristic evolution

Posing data for the field equations on a null hypersurface (plus suitable data at its boundary, here at null infinity) rather than on a spacelike Cauchy surface, and evolving from one null hypersurface to the next.

**How introduced:** Introduced as the natural replacement for the Cauchy problem once characteristic surfaces are known to be wave fronts; organized by Bondi's grouping of the equations.

**Prerequisites:** Cauchy problem, characteristic hypersurface, Bondi radiating metric

### Bondi hierarchy of field equations

Bondi's partition of the ten vacuum Ricci equations for the axisymmetric metric: three identically satisfied by symmetry (R_03, R_13, R_23), four main equations (R_11, R_12, R_22, R_33), one trivial (R_01) and two supplementary conditions (R_00, R_02).

**Prerequisites:** Ricci tensor, Bondi radiating metric

R_{01}=0

R_{00}=R_{02}=0

### contracted Bianchi identities

*identity · developed* · also: Bianchi lemma, constraint propagation

The identities ∇_b G^{ab} = 0, which here imply that if the main equations hold everywhere then the trivial equation holds algebraically and the supplementary conditions hold everywhere provided they hold on one r = const surface.

**How introduced:** Presented as an analogue of the Cauchy-problem theorem that constraints preserved by evolution need only be imposed initially; proof deferred to Exercise 23.6.

**Prerequisites:** Einstein tensor, Cauchy problem

$$
\nabla_b G^{ab}=0

### hypersurface equations

### null-cone integration schema

### asymptotic expansion in 1/r

### Bondi mass

*physical-quantity · core* · also: Bondi-Sachs mass, mass at null infinity

The total mass-energy of an isolated system measured at a cut u = const of future null infinity, given in the axisymmetric case by m(u) = ½∫_0^π M(u, θ) sin θ dθ; it agrees with the ADM and Komar masses for stationary spacetimes and with Newtonian mass in the weak-field limit.

### Bondi mass-loss formula

*theorem · core* · also: news theorem, mass-loss theorem

For an asymptotically flat isolated system the Bondi mass obeys dm/du = -½∫_0^π (n_{,u})^2 sin θ dθ ≤ 0, so the mass is constant exactly when there is no news and strictly decreases when there is; the loss equals the energy radiated in gravitational waves, with no weak-field approximation.

**How introduced:** Obtained by multiplying the exact supplementary equation for M_{,u} by sin θ, integrating over θ, and discarding the total-derivative term using regularity on the axis.

### peeling theorem

*theorem · core* · also: peeling-off theorem, Sachs peeling

Along an outgoing null geodesic with affine parameter r in an asymptotically flat radiating spacetime, the curvature falls off as N/r + III/r^2 + II/r^3 + I/r^4 + O(r^{-5}), where the coefficient of r^{-k} has Petrov type with the ray direction as a principal null direction of multiplicity 5 - k; far away the field is type N and principal null directions separate as one moves inward.

**How introduced:** Stated first for Sachs' linearized retarded waves and then for full isolated systems; visualized with bundles of arrows peeling apart (Fig. 23.7).

**Prerequisites:** Petrov classification, principal null directions, affine parameter, asymptotic flatness

$$
R=\frac{N_0}{r}+\frac{III_0}{r^2}+\frac{II_0}{r^3}+\frac{I_0}{r^4}+\frac{I'_0}{r^5}+O(r^{-6})
$$

**Notes:** The r^{-5} coefficient I'_0 is different in kind: its single principal null direction is not along the ray, so it carries no peeling structure. Sachs also found that algebraically special fields do not admit an expansion as general as (23.41). The text presents peeling as holding for Bondi-Sachs asymptotics; later work (beyond the text) shows it can fail for generic physically reasonable initial data.

**Where:** DIV §23.8 p.473 (pdf 488); DIV §23.8 p.474 (pdf 489)

**Prerequisites:** shear of a null congruence, algebraically general and special spacetimes, principal null directions

**Where:** DIV §23.9 p.475 (pdf 490)

### null Raychaudhuri equation

*law · introduced* · also: Sachs equation, optical scalar propagation equation

The propagation equation along an affinely parametrized null congruence for the complex quantity z = -θ + iω: Dz/Dr = z^2 + |σ|^2 + ½R_ab l^a l^b; its real part shows that shear and positive R_ab l^a l^b drive focusing.

**How introduced:** Derived in outline from the Ricci identity applied to l^a; flagged as central to singularity theorems.

**Prerequisites:** optical scalars, Ricci identity, Ricci tensor

$$
\frac{Dz}{Dr}=z^2+|\sigma|^2+\tfrac12R_{ab}\ell^a\ell^b
$$

**Where:** DIV §23.9 p.475 (pdf 490); DIV §23.9 p.476 (pdf 491)

### Ricci identity

*identity · revisited* · also: commutator of covariant derivatives

For a vector field, the difference of second covariant derivatives taken in opposite orders equals a contraction of the Riemann tensor with the vector: l^a_{;bc} - l^a_{;cb} = R^a_{dcb} l^d in this book's convention.

**How introduced:** Invoked without proof to derive propagation equations for the optical scalars.

**Prerequisites:** Riemann tensor, covariant derivative

$$
\ell^a{}_{;bc}-\ell^a{}_{;cb}=R^a{}_{dcb}\ell^d
$$

**Where:** DIV §23.9 p.475 (pdf 490)

### ADM mass

*physical-quantity · mention* · also: mass at spatial infinity

The total mass of an asymptotically flat spacetime defined at spatial infinity from the asymptotic metric; conserved and shown positive, unlike the Bondi mass which can decrease.

**How introduced:** Recalled as a consistency check for the Bondi mass and as the positivity precedent.

**Prerequisites:** asymptotic flatness

**Where:** DIV §23.6 p.470 (pdf 485); DIV §23.6 p.471 (pdf 486)

### Komar mass

*physical-quantity · mention* · also: Komar integral

A mass defined for stationary spacetimes as a surface integral of the derivative of the timelike Killing vector; coincides with the Bondi mass during stationary epochs.

**How introduced:** Recalled only as a consistency check.

**Prerequisites:** Killing vector

**Where:** DIV §23.6 p.470 (pdf 485)

## Key equations

### (23.1) Axial symmetry · supporting · DIV §23.1 p.461 (pdf 476)

$$
\phi\rightarrow\phi'=\phi+\text{const}
$$

The metric is invariant under rotations about the symmetry axis.

**Symbols:** φ azimuthal angle

### (23.2) Azimuthal reflection · supporting · DIV §23.1 p.461 (pdf 476)

$$
\phi\rightarrow\phi'=-\phi
$$

Reflection invariance that rules out rotation of the source.

### (23.3) Equatorial reflection (not used) · derivation-step · DIV §23.1 p.462 (pdf 477)

$$
\theta\rightarrow\theta'=\pi-\theta
$$

Optional extra symmetry through the equatorial plane; discarded since it does not simplify much.

**Symbols:** θ polar angle

### Cauchy evolution equations (recalled 13.23) · supporting · DIV §23.2 p.463 (pdf 478)

$$
g^{00}g_{\alpha\beta,00}=2M_{\alpha\beta}
$$

Second time derivatives of the spatial metric can be solved for only if g^{00} ≠ 0; the failure g^{00} = 0 characterizes wave fronts.

**Symbols:** M_{αβ} terms with at most first time derivatives; Greek indices spatial

### (23.4) Null hypersurface family · supporting · DIV §23.3 p.463 (pdf 478)

$$
x^0=u=\text{const}
$$

Retarded-time coordinate whose level sets are non-intersecting null hypersurfaces.

**Symbols:** u retarded time

### (23.5) Normal covector · derivation-step · DIV §23.3 p.464 (pdf 479)

$$
\ell_a=\partial_a u=(1,0,0,0)=\delta_a^0
$$

Gradient of u is the normal to the u = const surfaces.

**Symbols:** l_a normal

### (23.6) Nullness of the normal · supporting · DIV §23.3 p.464 (pdf 479)

$$
\ell_a\ell^a=g^{ab}\partial_a u\,\partial_b u=0
$$

The surfaces are null, so the normal is also tangent to them; equivalently g^{00} = 0.

### (23.7)-(23.8) Bicharacteristic curves · derivation-step · DIV §23.3 p.464 (pdf 479)

$$
x^a=x^a(\rho),\qquad \frac{dx^a}{d\rho}=\ell^a=g^{ab}\partial_b u
$$

Rays are integral curves of the raised normal.

**Symbols:** ρ parameter along ray

### (23.9) Rays are affinely parametrized null geodesics · central · DIV §23.3 p.464 (pdf 479)

$$
\frac{D}{D\rho}\left(\frac{dx^a}{d\rho}\right)=\frac{dx^c}{d\rho}\nabla_c(g^{ab}\partial_b u)=g^{ab}g^{cd}\partial_d u\,\nabla_b\partial_c u=\tfrac12 g^{ab}\nabla_b\left(g^{cd}\partial_c u\,\partial_d u\right)=0
$$

Uses metric compatibility, the symmetry ∇_c∂_b u = ∇_b∂_c u of a torsion-free connection, and the vanishing of the gradient of l_a l^a (null on every surface of the family).

**Symbols:** D/Dρ absolute derivative

### (23.10) Radial coordinate · derivation-step · DIV §23.3 p.464 (pdf 479)

$$
x^1=r
$$

r is a parameter along each null ray.

### (23.11) Asymptotic flatness · supporting · DIV §23.3 p.464 (pdf 479)

$$
\lim_{r\to\infty}g_{ab}=\eta_{ab}
$$

Metric tends to Minkowski along outgoing rays.

**Symbols:** η_ab Minkowski metric

### (23.12) Angular ray labels · supporting · DIV §23.3 p.465 (pdf 480)

$$
x^2=\theta,\quad x^3=\phi
$$

Polar angles on the spheres at scri+ label the rays.

### Ray tangent · derivation-step · DIV §23.4 p.465 (pdf 480)

$$
\frac{dx^a}{dr}=(0,1,0,0)=\delta^a_1,\qquad \ell^a=\lambda\delta^a_1=g^{0a}
$$

Tangent to r-lines must be proportional to l^a, which is the 0-row of the inverse metric.

**Symbols:** λ proportionality factor

### (23.13) Contravariant ray conditions · supporting · DIV §23.4 p.465 (pdf 480)

$$
g^{00}=g^{02}=g^{03}=0
$$

Consequence of the r-lines being the null rays.

### (23.14) Covariant ray conditions · supporting · DIV §23.4 p.466 (pdf 481)

$$
g_{11}=g_{12}=g_{13}=0
$$

Equivalent covariant statement, obtained by inverting a symmetric 4×4 matrix with the zeros of (23.13).

### (23.15) Newman-Penrose affine gauge · derivation-step · DIV §23.4 p.466 (pdf 481)

$$
g_{01}=g^{01}=1
$$

If r is taken affine, λ = 1.

### (23.16) Bondi areal (luminosity) gauge · central · DIV §23.4 p.466 (pdf 481)

$$
\begin{vmatrix}g_{22}&g_{23}\\g_{23}&g_{33}\end{vmatrix}=r^4\sin^2\theta
$$

Fixes r so that u, r = const spheres have area 4πr^2.

### (23.17) Axial symmetry on metric · derivation-step · DIV §23.4 p.466 (pdf 481)

$$
\frac{\partial g_{ab}}{\partial\phi}=0
$$

No φ dependence.

### (23.18)-(23.19) Reflection invariance on metric · derivation-step · DIV §23.4 p.466 (pdf 481)

$$
g_{03}=g_{13}=g_{23}=0\;\Longleftrightarrow\;g^{03}=g^{13}=g^{23}=0
$$

No cross terms between dφ and du, dr, dθ.

### (23.20) Bondi radiating metric · central · DIV §23.4 p.466 (pdf 481)

$$
ds^2=\left(\frac{V}{r}e^{2\beta}-U^2r^2e^{2\gamma}\right)du^2+2e^{2\beta}du\,dr+2Ur^2e^{2\gamma}du\,d\theta-r^2\left(e^{2\gamma}d\theta^2+e^{-2\gamma}\sin^2\theta\,d\phi^2\right)
$$

General axisymmetric non-rotating metric in radiation coordinates with signature (+,-,-,-).

**Symbols:** V, U, β, γ free functions

### (23.21) Free functions · supporting · DIV §23.4 p.466 (pdf 481)

$$
V=V(u,r,\theta),\;U=U(u,r,\theta),\;\beta=\beta(u,r,\theta),\;\gamma=\gamma(u,r,\theta)
$$

Four functions of three variables remain after all gauge and symmetry choices.

### 2-surface metric · supporting · DIV §23.4 p.466 (pdf 481)

$$
d\sigma^2=r^2\left(e^{2\gamma}d\theta^2+e^{-2\gamma}\sin^2\theta\,d\phi^2\right)
$$

Metric on S_{u,r} in the 2+2 interpretation; determinant r^4 sin^2θ independent of γ.

### (23.22) Symmetry conditions · supporting · DIV §23.5 p.467 (pdf 482)

$$
R_{03}=R_{13}=R_{23}\equiv0
$$

Vanish identically because of the symmetries.

**Symbols:** R_ab Ricci tensor

### (23.23) Main equations · central · DIV §23.5 p.467 (pdf 482)

$$
R_{11}=R_{12}=R_{22}=R_{33}=0
$$

The equations that must be solved everywhere.

### (23.24) Trivial equation · supporting · DIV §23.5 p.467 (pdf 482)

$$
R_{01}=0
$$

Holds algebraically once the main equations hold.

### (23.25) Supplementary conditions · supporting · DIV §23.5 p.467 (pdf 482)

$$
R_{00}=R_{02}=0
$$

Need only be imposed on one r = const surface.

### (23.26) Dynamical equation · supporting · DIV §23.5 p.468 (pdf 483)

$$
R_{33}=0
$$

Only main equation with a u derivative; propagates γ to the next null hypersurface.

### (23.27) Hypersurface equations · supporting · DIV §23.5 p.468 (pdf 483)

$$
R_{11}=R_{12}=g^{22}R_{22}+g^{33}R_{33}=0
$$

Equations internal to a null hypersurface determining β, U, V from γ.

### (23.28) Asymptotic fall-off · supporting · DIV §23.6 p.469 (pdf 484)

$$
g_{00}=1+O(r^{-1}),\;g_{01}=1+O(r^{-1}),\;g_{02}=O(1),\;g_{22}=-r^2+O(r),\;g_{33}=-r^2\sin^2\theta+O(r)
$$

Strengthened asymptotic flatness conditions; transformations preserving these and (23.20) form the BMS group.

### (23.29) Bondi outgoing condition · supporting · DIV §23.6 p.469 (pdf 484)

$$
\lim_{r\to\infty}\left[\frac{\partial(r^2\gamma)}{\partial r}\right]_{u=\text{const}}=0
$$

Sommerfeld-like attempt to exclude incoming radiation; too weak to exclude weak incoming waves.

### (23.30) Expansion of γ · central · DIV §23.6 p.469 (pdf 484)

$$
\gamma=\frac{n}{r}+\frac{q}{r^3}+O(r^{-4})
$$

No 1/r^2 term; n(u, θ) carries the radiation, q(u, θ) the quadrupole aspect.

**Symbols:** n, q functions of (u, θ); n is c in Bondi's original notation

### (23.31) Hypersurface-equation results · central · DIV §23.6 p.469 (pdf 484)

$$
\beta=-\frac{n^2}{4r^2}+O(r^{-3}),\quad U=-\frac{n_{,2}+2n\cot\theta}{r^2}+\frac{2d+3nn_{,2}+4n^2\cot\theta}{r^3}+O(r^{-4}),\quad V=r-2M+O(r^{-1})
$$

β, U, V determined from γ up to integration functions d and M.

**Symbols:** d dipole aspect, M mass aspect; comma-2 is ∂_θ

### Dynamical equation at leading order · derivation-step · DIV §23.6 p.469 (pdf 484)

$$
4q_{,0}=2Mn-d_{,2}+d\cot\theta
$$

Evolution of the quadrupole aspect.

**Symbols:** comma-0 is ∂_u

### (23.32) Mass-aspect evolution · central · DIV §23.6 p.470 (pdf 485)

$$
M_{,0}=-n_{,0}^2+\tfrac12\left(n_{,22}+3n_{,2}\cot\theta-2n\right)_{,0}
$$

Exact supplementary result: mass aspect changes by minus the news squared plus a total angular derivative term.

### (23.33) Dipole-aspect evolution · supporting · DIV §23.6 p.470 (pdf 485)

$$
-3d_{,0}=M_{,2}+3nn_{,02}+4nn_{,0}\cot\theta+n_{,0}n_{,2}
$$

Second exact supplementary result.

### (23.34)-(23.36) Free data · central · DIV §23.6 p.470 (pdf 485)

$$
\gamma(u_0,r,\theta)\ \text{on }N_0;\quad n_{,0}(u,\theta)\ \text{on }\mathscr{I}^+;\quad M(u_0,\theta),\ d(u_0,\theta)\ \text{on }N_0\cap\mathscr{I}^+
$$

One function of three variables, one of two, two of one; everything else follows.

### (23.37) Bondi mass · central · DIV §23.6 p.470 (pdf 485)

$$
m(u)=\tfrac12\int_0^\pi M(u,\theta)\sin\theta\,d\theta
$$

Angular average of the mass aspect; total mass at a cut of scri+.

### (23.38) Bondi mass-loss formula · central · DIV §23.6 p.471 (pdf 486)

$$
m_{,0}=-\tfrac12\int_0^\pi n_{,0}^2\sin\theta\,d\theta
$$

Mass is non-increasing and strictly decreases whenever news is non-zero; mass loss iff news.

### (23.39) Weyl tensor decomposition · supporting · DIV §23.7 p.471 (pdf 486)

$$
C_{abcd}=R_{abcd}-g_{a[c}R_{d]b}+g_{b[c}R_{d]a}-\tfrac13 g_{a[d}g_{c]b}R
$$

Weyl tensor as Riemann minus its trace parts; in vacuum C = R.

**Symbols:** brackets antisymmetrize with weight ½

### (23.40) Trace-free property · supporting · DIV §23.7 p.472 (pdf 487)

$$
C^a{}_{bac}\equiv0
$$

All contractions of Weyl vanish.

### Weyl symmetries (Ex. 23.9) · supporting · DIV §23.7 p.472 (pdf 487)

$$
C_{abcd}=-C_{abdc}=-C_{bacd}=C_{cdab},\qquad C_{a[bcd]}=0
$$

Pair antisymmetry and pair exchange symmetry let C act as a symmetric 6×6 matrix on bivectors; the cyclic identity and tracelessness reduce the classification to a quartic.

### Principal null direction condition (Debever) · central · DIV §23.7 p.472 (pdf 487)

$$
\ell_{[a}R_{b]ef[c}\ell_{d]}\ell^e\ell^f=0,\qquad \ell^a\ell_a=0,\ \ell^a\neq0
$$

Defines between one and four principal null directions at each point of a vacuum spacetime (C replaces R otherwise).

### Asymptotic curvature of Bondi solution · supporting · DIV §23.7 p.473 (pdf 488)

$$
R_{abcd}\sim\frac{n_{,00}}{r}+O(r^{-2})
$$

Leading curvature is type N and set by the u derivative of the news.

### (23.41) Peeling expansion · central · DIV §23.8 p.473 (pdf 488)

$$
R=\frac{N_0}{r}+\frac{III_0}{r^2}+\frac{II_0}{r^3}+\frac{I_0}{r^4}+\frac{I'_0}{r^5}+O(r^{-6})
$$

Successive orders in affine distance are of Petrov types N, III, II, I; subscript 0 means parallel propagated along the ray.

**Symbols:** indices suppressed

### Affinely parametrized null congruence · supporting · DIV §23.9 p.474 (pdf 489)

$$
\ell^a{}_{;b}\ell^b=0
$$

Achievable by rescaling l^a (Exercise 23.10).

### Optical scalars · central · DIV §23.9 p.475 (pdf 490)

$$
\theta=\tfrac12\ell^a{}_{;a},\qquad \omega=\left\{\tfrac12\ell_{[a;b]}\ell^{a;b}\right\}^{1/2},\qquad |\sigma|=\left\{\tfrac12\ell_{(a;b)}\ell^{a;b}-\theta^2\right\}^{1/2}
$$

Expansion, twist and shear of the congruence.

**Symbols:** θ here is expansion, not the polar angle

### (23.42) Ricci identity · supporting · DIV §23.9 p.475 (pdf 490)

$$
\ell^a{}_{;bc}-\ell^a{}_{;cb}=R^a{}_{dcb}\ell^d
$$

Starting point for optical scalar propagation equations.

### (23.43) Complex expansion-twist · derivation-step · DIV §23.9 p.475 (pdf 490)

$$
z=-\theta+i\omega
$$

Combines expansion and twist into one complex scalar.

### (23.44) Null Raychaudhuri (Sachs) equation · central · DIV §23.9 p.475 (pdf 490)

$$
\frac{Dz}{Dr}=z^2+|\sigma|^2+\tfrac12R_{ab}\ell^a\ell^b
$$

Real part: θ_{,r} = -θ^2 + ω^2 - |σ|^2 - ½R_ab l^a l^b, so shear and matter focus the beam; imaginary part: ω_{,r} = -2θω.

**Symbols:** r affine parameter along the congruence

## Worked examples

### Example · standard · DIV §23.2 p.463 (pdf 478)

**Problem:** Find which hypersurfaces x^0 = const are characteristic for the vacuum Einstein equations.

**Method:** Take the Cauchy evolution form in which g^{00} multiplies the second time derivatives of the spatial metric; ask when these cannot be solved for; recall the causal meaning of g^{00}.

**Key insight:** The obstruction to the Cauchy problem is geometric: it happens exactly when the surface is null.

**Result:** g^{00} = 0, i.e. null hypersurfaces are the characteristics.

**Concepts:** characteristic hypersurface, null hypersurface, Cauchy problem

### Example · standard · DIV §23.3 p.464 (pdf 479)

**Problem:** Show that the curves tangent to l^a = g^{ab}∂_b u, for a family of null hypersurfaces u = const, are affinely parametrized null geodesics.

**Method:** Differentiate the tangent along the curve with the absolute derivative; move g^{ab} through by metric compatibility; swap the order of the two covariant derivatives of the scalar u (torsion-free); substitute the tangent again for dx^c/dρ; recognise half the gradient of g^{cd}∂_c u ∂_d u, which vanishes identically.

**Key insight:** Nullness must hold for the whole family, not just one surface, for the gradient term to vanish and give an affine parameter.

**Result:** D(dx^a/dρ)/Dρ = 0.

**Concepts:** null rays, affine parameter, null hypersurface

### Example · standard · DIV §23.4 p.465 (pdf 480)

**Problem:** Turn the statement 'the r-coordinate lines are the null rays' into conditions on the metric.

**Method:** The tangent to an r-line is δ^a_1, so l^a = λδ^a_1; but l^a = g^{ab}δ_b^0 = g^{0a}; compare components; then invert a symmetric matrix with those zeros (Ex. 23.3).

**Key insight:** A coordinate choice becomes a pattern of vanishing inverse-metric components, which is equivalent to a pattern of vanishing covariant components.

**Result:** g^{00} = g^{02} = g^{03} = 0 and g_{11} = g_{12} = g_{13} = 0; affine r gives g_{01} = 1.

**Concepts:** radiation coordinates, Bondi radiating metric

### Example · challenging · DIV §23.4 p.466 (pdf 481)

**Problem:** Reduce a general metric in radiation coordinates to Bondi's axisymmetric form.

**Method:** Stack conditions: ray conditions (23.14), areal gauge (23.16), φ independence (23.17), reflection zeros (23.18); count what remains (four functions of u, r, θ) and parametrize with exponentials that keep the signature (Ex. 23.3 completes the details).

**Key insight:** Gauge and symmetry together leave exactly one radiative function γ plus three functions fixed by constraints.

**Result:** Metric (23.20) with V, U, β, γ.

**Concepts:** Bondi radiating metric, luminosity distance parameter, axial symmetry, azimuthal reflection symmetry

### Example · challenging · DIV §23.6 p.470 (pdf 485)

**Problem:** Count the free data needed to determine the Bondi solution.

**Method:** Follow the integration schema and track every function of integration produced by the hypersurface and dynamical equations and by the supplementary conditions in the 1/r expansion.

**Key insight:** Apart from γ on the initial cone and two functions on the initial cut, the only remaining freedom is the news along scri+, so it must be what drives change.

**Result:** γ(u_0, r, θ) on N_0; n_{,u}(u, θ) on scri+; M(u_0, θ), d(u_0, θ) on N_0 ∩ scri+.

**Concepts:** characteristic initial value problem, news function

### Example · standard · DIV §23.6 p.471 (pdf 486)

**Problem:** Derive the Bondi mass-loss formula from the supplementary equation for M.

**Method:** Multiply (23.32) by sin θ and integrate from 0 to π. The bracket term is a u derivative of (n_{,22} + 3n_{,2} cot θ - 2n) sin θ, which (dossier author's completion) equals d/dθ[n_{,2} sin θ + 2n cos θ]; it integrates to boundary values that vanish because regularity requires n = 0 on the axis (Ex. 23.8). Use m = ½∫M sin θ dθ.

**Key insight:** Everything except the news-squared term is a total derivative on the sphere, so only the sign-definite part survives.

**Result:** m_{,u} = -½∫_0^π n_{,u}^2 sin θ dθ ≤ 0; mass loss iff news.

**Concepts:** Bondi mass-loss formula, Bondi mass, news function, mass aspect

### Example · challenging · DIV §23.9 p.475 (pdf 490)

**Problem:** Obtain the propagation equation for expansion and twist along a null congruence.

**Method:** Apply the Ricci identity to l^a, contract with l^b and use the geodesic condition and definitions of θ, ω, σ; combine expansion and twist into z = -θ + iω. Only the outline and result are given.

**Key insight:** The same curvature that tidally distorts particle clouds focuses light beams; with R_ab l^a l^b ≥ 0 and no twist, beams must converge.

**Result:** Dz/Dr = z^2 + |σ|^2 + ½R_ab l^a l^b.

**Concepts:** null Raychaudhuri equation, Ricci identity, optical scalars


## Analogies and intuitions

### Characteristic hypersurfaces as wave fronts → characteristic hypersurface · strong · DIV §23.2 p.463 (pdf 478)

Surfaces where the equations fail to determine the evolution are exactly where disturbances can switch on, so they act like the fronts of propagating waves.

**Where it breaks down:** A wave front in optics is a surface of constant phase of a particular solution; characteristics are properties of the equations, existing whether or not waves are present.

**App idea:** Toy 1+1 wave equation where the learner inserts a kink in data and sees it travel only along characteristic lines.

### Time-bomb solutions → hyperbolic system of field equations · useful · DIV §23.2 p.463 (pdf 478)

Dramatizes that hyperbolic systems permit perfect stillness followed by sudden activity, which elliptic or diffusion equations forbid.

**Where it breaks down:** Gives no quantitative criterion; the precise statement is about non-uniqueness of Cauchy data across characteristics.

**App idea:** Side-by-side: a string (wave equation) that is flat until a pulse arrives versus a heated rod (diffusion) where any change is felt everywhere instantly.

### Sommerfeld radiation condition in electromagnetism → outgoing radiation condition · useful · DIV §23.6 p.469 (pdf 484)

Bondi's asymptotic condition on γ is modelled on the condition that selects outgoing electromagnetic waves.

**Where it breaks down:** The text notes the gravitational version is too weak to exclude sufficiently weak incoming radiation.

### BMS group as the Poincare group of isolated radiating systems → BMS group · useful · DIV §23.6 p.469 (pdf 484)

Gives the role of the asymptotic symmetry group by comparison with the symmetry group of special relativity.

**Where it breaks down:** BMS is infinite-dimensional (supertranslations), so e.g. angular momentum is not uniquely defined in the same way as in Poincare-invariant theory.

### Luminosity distance: choose r so spheres have the familiar area → luminosity distance parameter · strong · DIV §23.4 p.466 (pdf 481)

Bondi's r is defined so that a sphere of radius r has area 4πr^2, just as in flat space or Schwarzschild's areal radius, making flux-times-area reasoning natural.

**Where it breaks down:** It is not the distance along the ray and is not affine; it fails where rays cross.

**App idea:** Sphere-area readout comparing areal r with affine distance along a lensed ray.

### Petrov classification as eigenvalue classification of a matrix → Petrov classification · useful · DIV §23.7 p.472 (pdf 487)

The Weyl tensor is viewed as a symmetric 6×6 matrix on bivectors, so its types are like degenerate-eigenvalue cases of a matrix.

**Where it breaks down:** Lorentzian signature makes the matrix non-diagonalizable in some types; the extra symmetries reduce to a quartic, and eigenvector counts depend on formalism.

**App idea:** Root-merging plotter for the quartic.

### Coulomb-type field → Coulomb-type field · useful · DIV §23.8 p.474 (pdf 489)

Type D fields of isolated masses are compared to the electrostatic field of a charge: non-radiative and set by a monopole-like parameter.

**Where it breaks down:** Gravitational tidal effects are quadrupolar (stretch along one axis, squeeze two), not a force along field lines.

### Shadow of an object on a screen → optical scalars · strong · DIV §23.9 p.475 (pdf 490)

Expansion, twist and shear become the enlargement, rotation and distortion of a shadow cast by the light beam a short distance downstream.

**Where it breaks down:** Only valid infinitesimally; finite beams can form caustics where the picture breaks down.

**App idea:** Shadow-screen simulator (see Fig. 23.11 redesign).

### Peeling: directions come apart like layers peeling off → peeling theorem · strong · DIV §23.8 p.474 (pdf 489)

Moving inward from the wave zone, the four coincident principal null directions separate one at a time, pictured as arrow bundles peeling apart.

**Where it breaks down:** The coefficients are ordered by powers of 1/r, not by physical onion layers; peeling fails for some reasonable initial data.

**App idea:** Peeling slider along a ray (see Fig. 23.7 redesign).

### Lapse and shift in the 2+2 picture → 2+2 formalism · weak · DIV §23.4 p.466 (pdf 481)

Bondi's functions V, U, β are read as lapse- and shift-like quantities, reusing 3+1 intuition from Ch. 14.

**Where it breaks down:** The u-surfaces are null, so there is no unit normal and the 3+1 meaning of lapse does not transfer directly.


## Misconceptions addressed

### Since the quadrupole formula came from linearized theory, the energy carried by gravitational waves might be an artifact of linearization. · DIV §23.6 p.471 (pdf 486)

**Correction:** Bondi's analysis shows in the full non-linear theory that an isolated system loses mass exactly when it emits news.

**Why tempting:** Gravitational energy is not locally defined and early debates questioned whether waves were physical.

### Exact plane-wave solutions are good models for radiation from real sources. · DIV §23.1 p.461 (pdf 476)

**Correction:** They are global, source-free vacuum solutions; realistic radiation needs a bounded source and asymptotic analysis.

**Why tempting:** They are exact and look like linearized plane waves.

### A pulsating spherically symmetric star can emit gravitational waves. · DIV §23.1 p.461 (pdf 476)

**Correction:** Spherically symmetric vacuum exteriors are static (Birkhoff), so axisymmetry is the simplest radiating case.

**Why tempting:** Pulsating spheres radiate sound and a spherical EM charge distribution seems similar.

### A vector normal to a surface cannot lie in that surface. · DIV §23.2 p.463 (pdf 478)

**Correction:** For a null hypersurface the normal is null, hence orthogonal to itself, so it is tangent to the surface and generates its rays.

**Why tempting:** Euclidean intuition where normal and tangent are always distinct.

### Bondi coordinates cover the whole spacetime. · DIV §23.3 p.465 (pdf 480)

**Correction:** They work only near future null infinity; rays traced inward focus and cross, breaking the chart.

**Why tempting:** They resemble spherical polar coordinates in flat space.

### Bondi's r is the affine distance along the ray. · DIV §23.4 p.466 (pdf 481)

**Correction:** Bondi's r is an areal (luminosity) coordinate; Newman and Penrose instead used an affine parameter. The peeling expansion and the Raychaudhuri equation use the affine one.

**Why tempting:** Both are called r and agree at leading order far away.

### Bondi's radiation condition removes every trace of incoming radiation. · DIV §23.6 p.469 (pdf 484)

**Correction:** It is too weak to exclude sufficiently weak incoming radiation.

**Why tempting:** Its electromagnetic analogue is regarded as selecting purely outgoing waves.

### Bondi mass and ADM mass are the same quantity. · DIV §23.6 p.470 (pdf 485)

**Correction:** They coincide for stationary spacetimes; the ADM mass (at spatial infinity) is fixed, while the Bondi mass (at a cut of scri+) decreases as radiation escapes, approaching the ADM mass in the far past.

**Why tempting:** Both are called total mass and agree in the static cases learners know.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### Since mass can decrease, a radiating system could end up with negative mass. · DIV §23.6 p.471 (pdf 486)

**Correction:** Under the dominant energy condition the Bondi mass is non-negative (Ludvigsen-Vickers).

**Why tempting:** The mass-loss formula alone places no lower bound.

### The mass-loss result depends on axial symmetry. · DIV §23.6 p.471 (pdf 486)

**Correction:** Sachs removed the symmetry; the result persists with two news functions.

**Why tempting:** The chapter's derivation uses axisymmetry throughout.

### A realistic radiating spacetime is Petrov type N because gravitational waves are type N. · DIV §23.7 p.473 (pdf 488)

**Correction:** Realistic solutions are algebraically general (type I); only the leading 1/r part of the curvature is type N.

**Why tempting:** Plane waves are type N and far-field radiation looks plane.

### A spacetime could be type D in one region and type I at a special point. · DIV §23.7 p.472 (pdf 487)

**Correction:** The type is defined as the least special type attained; at special points it can only become more special.

**Why tempting:** Pointwise classification suggests types can vary freely.

### The rotating Kerr black hole is algebraically general because it is less symmetric than Schwarzschild. · DIV §23.7 p.473 (pdf 488)

**Correction:** Kerr, Schwarzschild and Reissner-Nordstrom are all type D.

**Why tempting:** Less symmetry is expected to mean less algebraic specialness.

### How a light beam's shadow looks depends on how fast the screen moves. · DIV §23.9 p.475 (pdf 490)

**Correction:** Sachs showed the shadow's size, shape and orientation depend only on the screen's location.

**Why tempting:** Aberration and length contraction change most observed shapes.


## Thought experiments

### Quiet-loud-quiet source · DIV §23.1 p.462 (pdf 477)

**Setup:** A bounded axisymmetric body sits static forever in the past, pulsates and radiates for a while, then settles to a new static state.

**Lesson:** If gravitational waves are real radiation, the late static mass must be smaller; the Bondi formula confirms it.

**App idea:** Fig. 23.1 redesign with editable news burst.

### Endless ringing from backscatter · DIV §23.1 p.462 (pdf 477)

**Lesson:** Whether a source can ever become quiescent again is a genuine question the model assumes away.

**App idea:** Toy wave equation with a potential barrier showing a decaying but persistent tail.

### Time bomb in a hyperbolic system · DIV §23.2 p.463 (pdf 478)

**Setup:** A field is exactly static until a moment, then begins to change, with influence spreading along characteristics.

**Lesson:** Hyperbolic equations permit sudden onsets joined across characteristic surfaces, identifying those surfaces as wave fronts.

**Setup:** An observer surrounds themselves with a ring or sphere of free particles and aligns a triad with the field in type N, III and D regions.

## Applications and observations

- **Known black-hole solutions as type D** (astrophysical-system, DIV §23.7 p.473 (pdf 488)): Schwarzschild, Reissner-Nordstrom and Kerr exteriors are algebraically special type D, each with two repeated principal null directions; their tidal effect on a sphere of particles is the Coulomb-type elongation. Key numbers: Type D; PND coincidence [2 2].
- **Tidal stretching for radial infall into Schwarzschild** (astrophysical-system, DIV §23.8 p.474 (pdf 489)): Szekeres' type D picture reproduces the radial stretching and transverse compression discussed in §17.10. Key numbers: Dossier author's addition: tidal eigenvalues proportional to (2, -1, -1) m/r^3 in geometric units.
- **Gravitational-wave detections as Bondi mass loss (dossier author's addition)** (detection-event, DIV §23.6 p.471 (pdf 486)): Not in the chapter. The mass radiated by a compact binary merger is precisely a Bondi mass decrease between early and late cuts of scri+; it gives a concrete example for the theorem. Key numbers: GW150914: roughly 3 solar masses radiated from a system of about 65 solar masses (published LIGO estimate, quoted from memory; not in the text).
- **How strongly type N dominates at detector distances (own estimate)** (numerical-estimate, DIV §23.8 p.473 (pdf 488)): In the peeling expansion successive terms are suppressed by roughly (source scale)/r. For a stellar-mass merger at cosmological distance this ratio is minute, so detectors see a pure type N transverse field. Key numbers: Own estimate: source scale ~10^3 km, distance ~400 Mpc ~ 10^22 km, ratio ~10^-19.
- **Characteristic evolution in numerical relativity (dossier author's addition)** (technology, DIV §23.5 p.468 (pdf 483)): Beyond the text: the Bondi-Sachs characteristic formulation and news at scri+ are used to extract gravitational waveforms from numerical simulations; the marching schema of Fig. 23.4 is its conceptual core.

## Notation and conventions

- **Metric signature:** (+, -, -, -): g_00 -> 1 and angular components -> -r^2 at large r. — Consistent with (23.20) and (23.28). (DIV §23.6 p.469 (pdf 484))
- **Coordinate index labels in Bondi coordinates:** x^0 = u, x^1 = r, x^2 = θ, x^3 = φ; so a comma-0 subscript means ∂/∂u and comma-2 means ∂/∂θ, e.g. n_{,0} is the news and n_{,22} the second θ derivative. — Easy to misread n_{,0} as a component. (DIV §23.6 p.469 (pdf 484))
- **Covariant and absolute derivatives:** Semicolon or ∇ for covariant derivative, D/Dρ for the absolute derivative along a curve, comma for partial derivative. (DIV §23.3 p.464 (pdf 479))
- **Normal vector symbol:** l^a (script ell) denotes the null normal/tangent; (23.8) prints a plain l^a for the same object. — Typographic inconsistency only. (DIV §23.3 p.464 (pdf 479))
- **Symmetrization and antisymmetrization brackets:** Round brackets ( ) and square brackets [ ] around indices, with the usual normalizing factors, as in l_(a;b), l_[a;b] and (23.39). (DIV §23.9 p.475 (pdf 490))
- **Bondi functions and notation change:** γ = n/r + q/r^3 + ...; the book says it slightly changes the original notation. — In Bondi-van der Burg-Metzner the leading coefficient is c and the news is c_{,0} (from the original paper, not stated in the text). (DIV §23.6 p.469 (pdf 484))
- **M versus m:** Capital M(u, θ) is the mass aspect; lowercase m(u) is the Bondi mass, its angular average. (DIV §23.6 p.470 (pdf 485))
- **Overloaded θ:** θ is both the polar angle of radiation coordinates and the expansion optical scalar in §23.9. — Context decides; Exercise 23.11 involves both. (DIV §23.9 p.475 (pdf 490))
- **Null infinity symbol:** Script ℐ+ (scri plus) for future null infinity; i+, i-, i^0 for timelike and spatial infinity on the Penrose diagram. (DIV §23.6 p.470 (pdf 485))
- **Petrov type O label:** Written as 'O' in the text, drawn as '0' in Fig. 23.6. (DIV §23.7 p.472 (pdf 487))
- **Peeling subscript 0:** In N_0, III_0, II_0, I_0 the subscript 0 means the coefficient has vanishing absolute derivative along l^a, not an index or u derivative. — Conflicts visually with the comma-0 u-derivative convention of §23.6. (DIV §23.8 p.473 (pdf 488))
- **Ricci identity sign:** l^a_{;bc} - l^a_{;cb} = R^a_{dcb} l^d. — Index order must be matched to the book's Riemann convention from Ch. 6 before comparing with other texts. (DIV §23.9 p.475 (pdf 490))
- **Star-equals:** Exercise 23.11 writes l^a with a starred equals sign, marking an equality valid only in the stated (Bondi) coordinates. (DIV p.476 (pdf 491))

## Gaps and pitfalls

- **The 'why?' about reflection symmetry forbidding rotation is never answered.** — Learners may accept (23.18) as arbitrary. *Suggestion:* Explain that φ -> -φ reverses the sense of rotation and flips the sign of any dφ cross term, so invariance forces zero angular momentum about the axis and zero g_03, g_13, g_23.
- **Most results are quoted: the asymptotic solution (23.30)-(23.33), the Bianchi lemma, the data count, and the claim that M is mass.** — Hard to tell which steps are routine and which are deep; risk of treating the expansions as definitions. *Suggestion:* Label each quoted result by difficulty, provide a computer-algebra notebook for the Ricci components (Ex. 23.7), and check M against Schwarzschild in Bondi coordinates (V = r - 2m).
- **The proof in (23.9) silently needs every surface of the family to be null, not just one.** — Learners may think a single null surface's normal field is automatically affinely geodesic. *Suggestion:* Point out that for one null surface the gradient term is only proportional to the normal (geodesic but not necessarily affine), while a whole null family makes it vanish.
- **The peeling paragraph refers to Fig. 23.6 where the peeling picture is Fig. 23.7.** — Readers look at the Petrov hierarchy instead of the arrow bundles. *Suggestion:* Point to Fig. 23.7 for peeling, and optionally to Fig. 23.6 for the path N -> III -> II -> I through the hierarchy.
- **Fig. 23.11(c) labels the ellipse semi-axes with 2θ ± |σ|, while panel (a) uses θ for expansion alone.** — Confusion about whether the expansion enters with a factor 2 and whether shear changes area. *Suggestion:* Present pure shear as axes 1 ± |σ|dr (area preserved to first order) and add θdr to both only when expansion is present.
- **The statement that the mass-loss integrand is 'essentially Ψ_abcd Ψ^abcd' is loose and uses an undefined symbol; the leading Weyl coefficient is proportional to n_{,uu}, i.e. the u-derivative of the news, not the news itself.** — May believe mass loss is a local curvature-squared density. *Suggestion:* Say instead that the news is a u-integral of the leading (type N) Weyl component at scri+, and that n_{,u}^2 is the invariant flux.
- **Table 23.1's 'distinct eigenvectors' row equals the count of distinct principal null directions; in the common complex 3×3 (Segre) treatment the eigenvector counts differ (e.g. D has three, N two).** — Conflicting numbers when reading other sources. *Suggestion:* Teach types primarily via PND multiplicities (Table 23.2) and flag that eigenvector counts depend on the matrix formalism.
- **The radial parameter r changes meaning: Bondi areal r in §23.4-23.6, affine r in peeling and in D/Dr of (23.44).** — Misapplying peeling orders or the Raychaudhuri equation in Bondi's r. *Suggestion:* Use distinct symbols (r for areal, λ for affine) in the app and note they agree at leading order.
- **Minor typos: 'Sommerfield' for Sommerfeld, 'dominant energy' missing 'condition', 'family of two surface', 'it lead to' in Ex. 23.3.** — Small distractions, and a failed search for 'Sommerfield'. *Suggestion:* Use corrected terms in the course.
- **Dated framing: peeling is presented as universal and BMS as a passing mention; since then peeling is known to fail for some reasonable data, BMS supertranslations tie to gravitational-wave memory, and waves have been detected directly.** — Misses modern context and observational confirmation of mass loss. *Suggestion:* Add a short 'since then' panel: detections as Bondi mass loss, memory effect and BMS, and caveats on peeling.
- **The model assumes a return to quiescence and analyticity without justification.** — May think the theorem proves sources settle down. *Suggestion:* State clearly that the mass-loss formula holds regardless, but the before-and-after picture of Fig. 23.1 relies on an assumption.

## Tutor notes

- Open with: 'The quadrupole formula was derived by linearizing. How could we be sure a real, strongly gravitating source really loses mass?' Let the learner propose what a convincing non-linear test would need (a bounded source, a place to measure mass far away).
- Before any formulas, sketch Fig. 23.1 and ask what should happen to the mass; keep returning to it as the goal.
- Order of explanation: characteristics = null surfaces -> rays are affine null geodesics -> Bondi coordinates -> metric reduction -> equation hierarchy and marching -> 1/r expansion -> news -> mass loss; then the invariant tools (Petrov, peeling, Szekeres, optical scalars).
- Check understanding: 'Why does g^{00} = 0 make the Cauchy problem fail, and why does that mean the surface is null?' and 'Why can a normal vector lie inside its own surface?'
- Answer the book's 'why?' about reflection symmetry if the learner asks; it is a good check of understanding of Killing vectors and cross terms.
- Stress the two meanings of r (areal vs affine) and of θ (angle vs expansion) whenever switching sections.
- When presenting the news theorem, have the learner do the angular integral themselves: show the total-derivative term vanishes with n = 0 on the axis.
- Good sanity check: Schwarzschild in Bondi coordinates has γ = β = U = 0, V = r - 2m, so M = m and the news vanishes.
- Common learner question: 'Is the Bondi mass the same as ADM mass?' Answer: same for stationary spacetimes; ADM is fixed at spatial infinity, Bondi is measured on scri+ and drops as radiation leaves.
- Common learner question: 'If waves are type N, why is a real radiating spacetime type I?' Use peeling: type N is only the leading 1/r piece.
- For Petrov types, start from principal null directions on a sphere of directions and merging them (Table 23.2) before mentioning quartics or 6×6 matrices.
- Use Szekeres' particle pictures to connect type N to LIGO-style ring distortions and type D to spaghettification; learners remember these.
- For advanced learners, mention modern links: BMS supertranslations and memory, characteristic extraction in numerical relativity, and the Raychaudhuri equation in singularity theorems.
