# DIV ch25 · The classical cosmological models

> Integrates the dust Friedmann equation for the flat and Λ = 0 cases, sorts every (k, Λ) combination into a catalogue of scale-factor histories, then studies the special solutions (Einstein static, de Sitter, radiation-filled, steady state), cosmological horizons, the Lorentzian spaces of constant curvature (de Sitter and anti-de Sitter) as embedded hyperboloids, the Penrose diagrams of all of these, and ends with a qualitative inflation-radiation-matter-vacuum history.

**Pages:** printed 511–538 · pdf 526–553 · **Difficulty:** math 4/5, conceptual 4/5, novice-friendliness 2/5

The first half (§25.1-25.6) is calculus-level ODE work and accessible, though several misprints ((25.1), p = 3ρ, the constants C and D) can derail a careful reader. The second half moves quickly into global structure: embeddings in indefinite-signature 5-spaces, conformal compactification and horizon taxonomy, with many results asserted ('it can be shown') and figures carrying much of the argument. Heavy reliance on Chapters 18 and 24. Equation misprints cluster in §25.7-25.11 and there is a sign inconsistency in the naming of K.

## Role in the book

Middle chapter of the book's three-chapter cosmology part. Chapter 24 built the Robertson-Walker metric, derived Friedmann's equation for dust, and defined redshift, luminosity distance, Hubble and deceleration parameters; this chapter takes that equation as given and solves it, turning abstract parameters (C, Λ, k) into explicit histories R(t). It then reuses the global-structure machinery of Chapter 18 (conformal compactification onto the Einstein static cylinder, Penrose diagrams) and the horizon ideas of Chapters 3 and 17 to describe the causal structure of cosmological spacetimes. It closes with a qualitative history of the universe that sets up Chapter 26, where multi-component models, observations, ΛCDM and inflation are treated properly.

## Learning objectives

- Reader can integrate the dust Friedmann equation in closed form for k = 0 with Λ > 0, Λ = 0, Λ < 0 and for Λ = 0 with k = ±1, choosing the substitution that makes each integral elementary.
- Reader can compute H(t) and q(t) from an explicit solution, e.g. obtain H = 2/(3t) and q = 1/2 for the Einstein-de Sitter model.
- Reader can predict the qualitative shape of R(t) from Ṙ² = F(R) by locating zeros of F (turning points) and deciding which term dominates at small and at large R.
- Reader can explain why every big bang model starts like R ∝ t^{2/3} (dust) or t^{1/2} (radiation) and why any Λ > 0 eventually drives exponential expansion in an ever-expanding model.
- Reader can reproduce the classification of Friedmann models by k and Λ, including the critical values Λ_c = 4/(9C²) and R_c = 3C/2 for k = +1 and the named special cases (Lemaître, Einstein static, Eddington-Lemaître, bouncing, oscillating).
- Reader can derive the Einstein static universe from the static Friedmann equations and state why, without Λ, a static closed universe needs negative pressure.
- Reader can show that the de Sitter metric written with exponential scale factor is actually stationary, by transforming to static coordinates.
- Reader can reconstruct the steady-state line element from the perfect cosmological principle and show that Hubble's law d_L = zT_0 is exact and q = -1 there.
- Reader can compute the event horizon of a comoving observer in de Sitter space, the reception time and redshift of a comoving source approaching it, and interpret the divergence of both.
- Reader can distinguish particle horizons, observer-dependent future event horizons and absolute (black hole) horizons, and relate their existence to whether scri-minus and scri-plus are spacelike or null.
- Reader can state the constant-curvature condition, derive K = R/12 and G_ab = -3Kg_ab, and visualise de Sitter and anti-de Sitter space as hyperboloids in flat five-dimensional spaces, including which part the flat (steady-state) slicing covers.
- Reader can use conformal time to map Robertson-Walker, de Sitter and anti-de Sitter spacetimes onto regions of the Einstein static cylinder and read off from Penrose diagrams that the big bang is spacelike, the steady-state patch is past-incomplete, and AdS infinity is timelike.
- Reader can narrate the standard sequence inflation, radiation domination, matter domination, vacuum domination using the scalings ρ ∝ R^{-4}, R^{-3}, R^0.

## Teaching approach

A mathematically explicit catalogue chapter. The authors take Friedmann's equation from Chapter 24 as a black box and work it like an ODE problem: exact integrations by clever substitutions for a few representative cases, then qualitative reasoning from Ṙ² = F(R) to fill in the rest, collected into one tabular figure of sketches. The second half changes register from dynamics to geometry and causal structure: special solutions are examined for coordinate artefacts (de Sitter is stationary), horizons are derived from limits of past light cones and pictured with explorer stories, constant-curvature spacetimes are shown as hyperboloids in five dimensions, and everything is placed on the Einstein static cylinder to produce Penrose diagrams. The chapter ends with a verbal, equation-light synthesis of cosmic history.

**Style:** derivation-heavy, computational-recipe, survey-overview, diagram-driven, geometric-first, thought-experiment, historical-narrative

**Narrative arc**

1. Restate Friedmann's dust equation with its parameter ranges and admit that the general solution needs elliptic functions, so restrict to representative exact cases.
2. Solve k = 0 for Λ > 0, Λ < 0, Λ = 0 by substitutions, meeting the Einstein-de Sitter model and its H and q.
3. Switch to qualitative analysis: small-R dominance gives universal t^{2/3} start, zeros of F(R) give turning points, large-R dominance gives exponential growth.
4. Repeat for Λ = 0 with k = ±1 (cycloid-type implicit solutions, H and q) and the qualitative G(R) argument.
5. Collect all cases into the classification figure, introducing the critical Λ_c and R_c for k = +1 and naming the special models.
6. Step back historically: static Friedmann equations show why Einstein needed Λ; the Einstein static and de Sitter models are derived and de Sitter is shown to be the late-time attractor and secretly stationary.
7. Add radiation pressure: equation of state, ρ ∝ R^{-4}, faster early expansion R ∝ t^{1/2}, Friedmann equation with D/R².
8. Present the steady-state theory from the perfect cosmological principle, recover the de Sitter metric, exact Hubble law and q = -1.
9. Use the same metric to derive the event horizon of a comoving observer, follow a source to the horizon (infinite redshift, recession velocity to c), tell the explorer story and compare with Rindler horizons.
10. Generalise to a taxonomy of particle and event horizons controlled by the character of scri-minus and scri-plus.
11. Treat de Sitter and anti-de Sitter as Lorentzian constant curvature spaces: embeddings, global coordinates, the half covered by flat slicing.
12. Conformally compactify Robertson-Walker (Λ = 0), de Sitter and anti-de Sitter onto the Einstein static cylinder and draw their Penrose diagrams; note AdS/CFT in passing.
13. Summarise the realistic picture: inflation, radiation, matter and vacuum eras, pointing to Chapter 26 for detail.

**Signature moves**

- Turns an ODE whose general solution is elliptic into a small set of elementary integrals by tailored substitutions (u ∝ R³ with cosh, u² = R/C with sin), then justifies not doing the rest by showing that dominance arguments recover all qualitative shapes.
- Reads dynamics off a 'Ṙ² = function of R' form exactly like a particle energy equation: zeros are turning points, dominant terms give asymptotics; Exercise 25.7 makes the Newtonian particle-in-a-channel analogy explicit.
- Packs the entire model space into one grid of hand sketches (Fig. 25.1) labelled with a Roman-numeral scheme that later text refers back to (e.g. models I(i), II(i), III(i), III(iic), III(iiib) all tend to de Sitter).
- Repeatedly unmasks coordinate effects: the exponentially expanding de Sitter metric is transformed into a manifestly static form; the flat slicing is shown to cover only half the hyperboloid; the periodic AdS time is unwrapped.
- Obtains horizons by taking a limit of an explicit past light cone equation, then attaches physical observables (reception time, luminosity distance, redshift, geodesic recession speed) to the approach to the horizon.
- Uses a narrative 'curtain' and an explorer who can see past the horizon but can never report back to make observer-dependence of event horizons vivid.
- Reuses one geometric stage, the Einstein static cylinder, to draw conformal diagrams of every model, so different spacetimes become different shaded regions of the same cylinder.

## Section by section

### §25.1 The flat space models — p.511 (pdf 526)

Sets the chapter's task of solving the dust Friedmann equation, then treats k = 0. For positive Λ the substitution u = 2ΛR³/(3C) and a cosh substitution give R³ proportional to cosh(√(3Λ)t) - 1; negative Λ gives the analogous 1 - cos form; Λ = 0 gives the Einstein-de Sitter model R ∝ t^{2/3} with H = 2/(3t), q = 1/2. A qualitative argument shows all big bang models start like Einstein-de Sitter, negative Λ produces a maximum radius where F(R) vanishes, and positive Λ gives exponential growth at late times.

**Concepts:** Friedmann equation for dust, big bang initial condition, Einstein-de Sitter model, Hubble parameter, deceleration parameter, qualitative analysis of the Friedmann equation, early-time universality of expansion, late-time exponential expansion

**Key moves**
- Note that the general solution requires elliptic functions and restrict to k = 0 and Λ = 0 cases, citing Landsberg and Evans for completeness.
- For k = 0, Λ > 0 set u = 2ΛR³/(3C) so that u̇² = 3Λ(2u + u²); complete the square, put u + 1 = cosh w and integrate with R(0) = 0 to reach (25.5).
- For Λ < 0 flip the sign of the substitution to obtain the trigonometric solution (25.7).
- For Λ = 0 separate R^{1/2}dR = C^{1/2}dt to get R = (9Ct²/4)^{1/3}, the Einstein-de Sitter model, and compute H = 2/(3t), q = 1/2.
- Argue that near R = 0 the C/R term dominates, so every big bang model behaves as t^{2/3} initially.
- Write Ṙ² = F(R) and read off: F has a zero at R_m = [3C/(-Λ)]^{1/3} when Λ < 0 (expansion halts); for Λ ≥ 0 growth is unbounded and for Λ > 0 the ΛR²/3 term gives R ∼ exp[(Λ/3)^{1/2}t].

### §25.2 Models with vanishing cosmological constant — p.514 (pdf 529)

With Λ = 0 the equation Ṙ² = C/R - k is solved for k = +1 using u² = R/C and u = sin θ, giving an implicit cycloid-type relation between R and t; the k = -1 case gives the hyperbolic analogue. H and q are expressed as functions of R for the closed case, with q = ½(1 - R/C)^{-1} exceeding ½. The qualitative form Ṙ² = G(R) shows the closed model turns round at R = C while the flat and open models expand forever, the open one asymptotically linearly in t.

**Concepts:** Friedmann equation for dust, closed Friedmann model, open Friedmann model, Hubble parameter, deceleration parameter, qualitative analysis of the Friedmann equation

**Key moves**
- Substitute u² = R/C so the equation becomes separable with integrand u²/(1 - u²)^{1/2}.
- Evaluate with u = sin θ, using sin²θ = (1 - cos 2θ)/2, to get C[arcsin (R/C)^{1/2} - (R/C)^{1/2}(1 - R/C)^{1/2}] = t, eq (25.19).
- State the k = -1 analogue with sinh^{-1}, eq (25.20), as an exercise.
- Differentiate implicitly to express H and q in terms of R for k = +1, eqs (25.21)-(25.22).
- Use G(R) = C/R - k: G vanishes at R = C for k = +1 (recollapse); for k = -1, Ṙ² → 1 at large R so R ∼ t.

### §25.3 Classification of Friedmann models — p.516 (pdf 531)

All the dust models are gathered in a single tabular figure organised by k (rows) and the sign of Λ (columns), with the k = +1, Λ > 0 cell split further. Open (k = -1, 0) models with Λ > 0 expand forever with an inflection ('kink'), with Λ = 0 expand forever (linearly or as t^{2/3}), and with Λ < 0 recollapse. For k = +1 a critical constant Λ_c = 4/(9C²) and radius R_c = 3C/2 separate Lemaître's model (Λ > Λ_c), the Einstein static model and its two asymptotic companions (Λ = Λ_c), and, for 0 < Λ < Λ_c, either an oscillating model or a bouncing model with no singularity; Λ ≤ 0 closed models oscillate. Cyclic repetition of oscillating models is mentioned.

**Concepts:** classification of Friedmann models, critical cosmological constant, Lemaître model, Einstein static universe, Eddington-Lemaître model, bouncing cosmological model, oscillating Friedmann model, big crunch, inflection (coasting) phase in Friedmann models, open and closed spatial topology

**Key moves**
- Organise models into Case I (k = -1), II (k = 0), III (k = +1) with sub-cases by sign or size of Λ: 3, 3 and 8 sub-cases.
- Point out that all Λ < 0 models recollapse, calling them oscillating, and allow an indefinite sequence of cycles (Fig. 25.2).
- Introduce the critical values Λ_c = 4/(9C²), R_c = 3C/2 for k = +1 (the double root of F(R) - 1).
- Explain that the nearer Λ is to Λ_c from above, the longer the Lemaître model lingers near R_c.
- At Λ = Λ_c distinguish three solutions by an integration constant: static, big bang approaching static, and static-in-the-past expanding to exponential (Eddington-Lemaître).
- For 0 < Λ < Λ_c distinguish the oscillating branch from the time-symmetric bounce that stays away from R = 0.

### §25.4 The Einstein static model and the de Sitter model — p.519 (pdf 534)

Setting R = a constant in the Robertson-Walker field equations shows that without Λ a static non-empty universe requires k = 1 and negative pressure, which is why Einstein added Λ; with Λ = 1/a² and k = 1 one gets dust of density 1/(4πa²), the Einstein static universe, now mainly useful as the canvas for Penrose diagrams. The de Sitter model (p = ρ = k = 0) has exponential scale factor, is the late-time limit of many models, and has a self-similar metric with no preferred time origin. A coordinate transformation turns it into a static form, so its apparent expansion is a coordinate effect.

**Concepts:** Einstein static universe, cosmological constant, de Sitter model, self-similarity of exponential expansion, static coordinates of de Sitter space, late-time exponential expansion

**Key moves**
- Put R = a = constant and Λ = 0 in (24.52)-(24.53): 8πρ = 3k/a², 8πp = -k/a², so a non-trivial static model needs k = 1 and p < 0.
- Restore Λ: 8πρ = 3k/a² - Λ, 8πp = Λ - k/a²; choose k = 1, Λ = 1/a² for a pressure-free static closed universe with ρ = 1/(4πa²).
- Recall the historical sequence: galaxy recession led Einstein to abandon Λ, but Λ now returns as dark energy.
- Set p = ρ = k = 0 to get Ṙ/R = (Λ/3)^{1/2}, R = exp[(Λ/3)^{1/2}t], and the flat-slicing line element with α = (3/Λ)^{1/2}.
- Observe invariance under t → t + const with rescaled spatial coordinates, i.e. no natural origin of time.
- Apply transformation (25.33) to reach the static form (25.34), exposing time independence of the metric components.

### §25.5 Early epochs of the universe — p.521 (pdf 536)

Radiation should dominate gravity early on, so the equation of state 3p = ρ is imposed. With Λ = 0 the field equations reduce to R̈/R + Ṙ²/R² + k/R² = 0, giving R ∝ t^{1/2} at early times, faster than dust. The authors explain this as extra gravity from pressure, seen most clearly by imagining the collapse in reverse. The energy equation shows ρR⁴ is constant for radiation, and the Friedmann equation for radiation acquires a D/R² term.

**Concepts:** radiation equation of state, radiation-dominated expansion, scaling of density with scale factor, Friedmann equation for radiation, pressure as a source of gravity

**Key moves**
- Impose 3p = ρ in (24.52)-(24.53) with Λ = 0 to obtain R̈/R + Ṙ²/R² + k/R² = 0.
- Drop the k term at early times to get R ∼ t^{1/2}.
- Compare with dust t^{2/3} and interpret via pressure contributing to gravity (argue with time-reversed collapse).
- Use the energy equation (24.54) with p = 0 to get ρ ∝ R^{-3} and with p = ρ/3 to get ρR⁴ = constant.
- Substitute into (24.52) to write Ṙ² = D/R² + ΛR²/3 - k with D = (8/3)πR⁴ρ.

### §25.6 The steady-state theory — p.522 (pdf 537)

Summarises the 1948 Bondi-Gold-Hoyle theory, which buys simplicity and falsifiability at the cost of modifying energy conservation. From the perfect cosmological principle (the large-scale universe looks the same at all times) the observable spatial curvature kR^{-2} and Hubble parameter must be constant, forcing k = 0 and R = exp(t/T_0): the de Sitter line element. Light propagation then gives d_L proportional to the radial coordinate, 1 + z = exp[(t_0 - t_1)/T_0], an exactly linear Hubble law d_L = zT_0, and q = -1.

**Concepts:** steady-state theory, perfect cosmological principle, stationarity, de Sitter model, exact Hubble law in the steady-state model, luminosity distance, cosmological redshift

**Key moves**
- State the perfect cosmological principle and note it implies the ordinary cosmological principle, hence the Robertson-Walker form.
- Argue that the 3-curvature ∝ kR^{-2} is observable, so constancy with growing R forces k = 0.
- Argue that H = Ṙ/R is observable and hence constant, giving R = exp(t/T_0).
- Integrate radial null rays: r = T_0(e^{-t/T_0} - e^{-t_0/T_0}) for rays arriving at r = 0 at t_0.
- Combine d_L = r_1 e^{t_0/T_0} with 1 + z = R(t_0)/R(t_1) to get d_L = zT_0 exactly; compute q = -1.

### §25.7 The event horizon of the de Sitter universe — p.523 (pdf 538)

Letting the reception time go to infinity in the past light cone equation gives the surface r = T_0 e^{-t/T_0}, beyond which the observer at r = 0 can never receive signals: an event horizon. A comoving particle at r = r_1 crosses it at t_1 = T_0 ln(T_0/r_1); signals it emits just before arrive ever later, with luminosity distance and redshift diverging, while the geodesic distance stays finite and the geodesic recession velocity tends to 1. Homogeneity makes this true for every comoving observer. An explorer can see beyond the observer's horizon but can never report back; the uniformly accelerated observer in Minkowski space gives a flat-space analogue.

**Concepts:** cosmological event horizon, reception time divergence at the horizon, infinite redshift at a horizon, geodesic recession velocity, observer dependence of event horizons, Rindler horizon

**Key moves**
- Take t_0 → ∞ in the past light cone (25.43) to obtain the horizon hypersurface (25.49).
- Find the crossing time t_1 = T_0 ln(T_0/r_1) for a substratum particle P at r = r_1.
- Integrate the null ray from (t, r_1) to r = 0 to get reception time τ = -T_0 ln(e^{-t/T_0} - e^{-t_1/T_0}).
- Express d_L and z in terms of emission time and show both diverge as t → t_1, while P reaches the horizon in finite proper time.
- Compute geodesic distance l = r_1 e^{t/T_0} (finite at P_1) and dl/dt = e^{(t - t_1)/T_0} → 1.
- Introduce the curtain image and the explorer E who crosses the horizon at E_1; relate to the Minkowski horizon of a hyperbolic observer x² - t² = const.

### §25.8 Particle and event horizons — p.526 (pdf 541)

Defines two kinds of horizon using conformal-diagram language. If scri-minus is spacelike, an observer's past light cone at any moment divides comoving particles into those already seen and not yet seen; the boundary is the particle horizon. If scri-minus is null (Minkowski) every particle is visible. If scri-plus is spacelike, the past light cone of the observer's endpoint on scri-plus is the future event horizon. If scri-plus is null, inertial observers have none but accelerated observers whose world-lines end on scri-plus do. Such horizons depend on the observer, in contrast to the absolute event horizons of black holes.

**Concepts:** particle horizon, cosmological event horizon, conformal infinity (scri), observer dependence of event horizons, absolute event horizon, Rindler horizon

**Key moves**
- Define the particle horizon of O at P from the intersection of P's past light cone with the family of particle world-lines when scri-minus is spacelike.
- Contrast with null scri-minus, where the past cone reaches i^- and contains all particle histories.
- Define the future event horizon as the past light cone of O's endpoint on a spacelike scri-plus.
- For null scri-plus, show geodesic observers reach i^+ and have no event horizon while a uniformly accelerated observer ends on scri-plus and has one.
- Stress observer dependence versus the absolute horizons of black holes.

### §25.9 Lorentzian constant curvature space-times — p.527 (pdf 542)

Motivated by vacuum energy dominating late times, spacetimes with R_abcd = K(g_ac g_bd - g_ad g_bc) are studied. Contracting gives K = R/12 and G_ab = -3Kg_ab, so they are vacuum solutions with Λ = -3K, isotropic about every point. K = 0 is Minkowski space. De Sitter space is the hyperboloid -v̂² + ŵ² + x̂² + ŷ² + ẑ² = T_0² in a flat five-dimensional space with one timelike direction, topology R × S³, global metric dt̂² - T_0²cosh²(t̂/T_0)dΩ₃²; the steady-state (flat slicing) coordinates cover only the half ŵ + v̂ > 0. Anti-de Sitter space is the hyperboloid with two timelike embedding directions, metric T_0²[cosh²ρ d(t̂/T_0)² - dρ² - sinh²ρ dΩ²], whose periodic time is unwrapped to give topology R⁴.

**Concepts:** constant curvature spacetime, Einstein space, de Sitter space, anti-de Sitter space, hyperboloid embedding of de Sitter space, global coordinates of de Sitter space, flat slicing of de Sitter space, universal covering of anti-de Sitter space, vacuum domination

**Key moves**
- Argue that since dust dilutes as R^{-3} and radiation as R^{-4}, any Λ eventually dominates, so vacuum Λ solutions matter physically.
- Contract the constant curvature condition twice to find K = R/12, then G_ab = -3Kg_ab, i.e. a vacuum solution with Λ = -3K.
- Dispose of K = 0 as Minkowski space.
- Embed de Sitter as a hyperboloid, parametrise with (t̂, χ, θ, φ) to get closed S³ slices contracting then re-expanding as cosh.
- Introduce t = T_0 ln[(ŵ + v̂)/T_0] and scaled Cartesian coordinates to recover (25.31), and note they fail for ŵ + v̂ ≤ 0.
- Embed AdS with two timelike directions, parametrise, observe 2π-periodic time and pass to the covering space.

### §25.10 Conformal structure of Robertson-Walker space-times — p.530 (pdf 545)

Writing the Robertson-Walker metric with radial coordinate χ and conformal time dτ = dt/R shows ds² = R²(τ) ds̄² with ds̄² a static metric; for k = +1 that static metric is the Einstein static universe itself. For Λ = 0 the flat model maps to the upper half of the Minkowski compactification, the closed model to a band of the cylinder, and the open model to a smaller triangular region. Part of each boundary is now the initial singularity rather than infinity. Penrose diagrams follow: expanding models are triangles with a spacelike big bang at the bottom, the closed model is a rectangle with spacelike bang and crunch. Matter-filled Robertson-Walker universes are stated to be inextendible.

**Concepts:** conformal time, conformal compactification, Einstein static universe, Penrose diagram of Robertson-Walker models, big bang singularity as a spacelike boundary, inextendibility of Robertson-Walker models

**Key moves**
- Rewrite RW with χ, f(χ) = χ, sin χ, sinh χ for k = 0, +1, -1.
- Define conformal time τ by dτ = dt/R to factor out R²(τ).
- Recognise the k = +1 unphysical metric as the Einstein static cylinder (18.27) and map k = 0 onto the t' > 0 half of the Minkowski picture (Fig. 18.7).
- Specify the k = -1 region by inequalities on t' ± r' and t' ≥ 0.
- Draw shaded regions on the cylinder (Fig. 25.13) and the 2D Penrose diagrams (Figs 25.14, 25.15), noting that singular boundaries behave like any other boundary in the diagram.

### §25.11 Conformal structure of de Sitter and anti-de Sitter space-time — p.531 (pdf 546)

The substitution t' = 2 arctan[exp(t̂/T_0)] - π/2 gives cosh(t̂/T_0) = 1/cos t' and makes de Sitter conformal to the band -π/2 < t' < π/2 of the Einstein static cylinder, a square Penrose diagram with spacelike scri-plus and scri-minus; the steady-state model is the half-triangle bounded by a null surface and so is geodesically incomplete to the past. For anti-de Sitter the radial substitution χ = 2 arctan(e^ρ) - π/2 gives cosh ρ = 1/cos χ, mapping AdS to half of the cylinder, 0 ≤ χ < π/2, whose boundary χ = π/2 is timelike. This timelike infinity underlies the AdS/CFT correspondence, mentioned as an active research topic.

**Concepts:** conformal compactification, Penrose diagram of de Sitter space, geodesic incompleteness of the steady-state model, timelike conformal boundary of anti-de Sitter space, AdS/CFT correspondence

**Key moves**
- Define t' by an arctan of an exponential so that dt̂ = T_0 cosh(t̂/T_0) dt' and cosh(t̂/T_0) = sec t'.
- Factor the global de Sitter metric as T_0² sec²t' times the Einstein static metric with t' restricted to (-π/2, π/2).
- Identify the flat-slicing patch as half the square, bounded by a null surface through i^-, so the steady-state model is past-incomplete.
- For AdS define χ from ρ the same way, getting T_0² sec²χ times the Einstein static metric with 0 ≤ χ ≤ π/2.
- Conclude spatial slices are open hemispheres (topology R³), spacetime is R⁴, and infinity χ = π/2 is timelike.

### §25.12 Our model of the universe — p.534 (pdf 549)

Summarises the domains of validity of the models. Homogeneity and isotropy hold only on large scales. Early on radiation dominates with R ∝ t^{1/2} and ρ ∝ R^{-4}; because radiation dilutes faster than dust (R^{-3}), matter eventually dominates with R ∝ t^{2/3}; in an ever-expanding universe any non-zero Λ, interpretable as vacuum energy with p = -ρ, eventually dominates, giving exponential growth towards de Sitter. An earlier exponential phase, inflation, driven by an inflaton field with T_ab ≈ V(φ)g_ab, precedes the radiation era. The sequence inflation → radiation → matter → vacuum is offered as the basic model, with real transitions being gradual and details deferred to the next chapter.

**Concepts:** cosmic history sequence, radiation-dominated expansion, matter domination, vacuum domination, vacuum energy, inflation, dark energy, scaling of density with scale factor

**Key moves**
- Recall observational support for large-scale homogeneity and isotropy and its breakdown on galactic scales.
- Assign radiation-dominated, matter-dominated and vacuum-dominated laws R ∝ t^{1/2}, t^{2/3}, e^{αt}.
- Use the different dilution rates to argue that the order of the eras is inevitable in an expanding universe.
- Interpret Λ as a perfect fluid with p = -ρ (energy-momentum proportional to g_ab).
- Add an early inflationary exponential stage driven by a slowly varying potential V(φ).
- State the four-stage history and warn that the eras overlap in reality.

## Concepts

### Friedmann equation for dust

*law · core* · also: Friedmann's equation, (25.1)

First-order equation for the scale factor of a homogeneous isotropic universe filled with pressure-free matter: Ṙ² = C/R + ΛR²/3 - k, with C > 0 fixed by the conserved mass, Λ the cosmological constant and k the curvature index.

**How introduced:** Imported from Chapter 24 as the object to be solved; the chapter treats it as a non-linear ODE whose general solution is elliptic and attacks it by special cases and qualitative arguments.

**Prerequisites:** scale factor, cosmological constant, curvature index k, Friedmann constant C

$$
\dot R^2 = \frac{C}{R} + \tfrac13\Lambda R^2 - k
$$

**Notes:** The book's (25.1) misprints the Λ term as ΛṘ/3; the correct term ΛR²/3 appears in (25.3) and (24.60).

**Where:** DIV §25.1 p.511 (pdf 526); DIV §25.4 p.519 (pdf 534); DIV §25.12 p.534 (pdf 549)

### scale factor

*physical-quantity · revisited* · also: R(t), expansion factor, cosmic scale factor

The function R(t) multiplying the spatial part of the Robertson-Walker metric; ratios of R at two times give how much all comoving distances have grown.

**How introduced:** Taken over from Chapter 24; every model in the chapter is described by the graph of R against cosmic time t.

**Prerequisites:** Robertson-Walker metric

$$
ds^2 = dt^2 - R^2(t)\,[d\chi^2 + f^2(\chi)\,d\Omega^2]
$$

**Notes:** R is also used for the Ricci scalar in §25.9 (K = R/12); context distinguishes them.

**Where:** DIV §25.1 p.511 (pdf 526); DIV §25.3 p.517 (pdf 532)

### Friedmann constant C

*physical-quantity · revisited* · also: C, mass constant

Positive constant C = (8/3)πρR³ in the dust Friedmann equation, conserved because ρR³ is constant for dust; it sets the strength of gravitational attraction relative to curvature and Λ.

**How introduced:** Recalled with the requirement C > 0; it sets the characteristic scales Λ_c = 4/(9C²) and R_c = 3C/2 and the recollapse radius R = C for k = +1, Λ = 0.

**Prerequisites:** Friedmann equation for dust

### cosmological constant

*physical-quantity · developed* · also: Λ, cosmological term, cosmic repulsion

Constant Λ in the field equations G_ab - Λg_ab = 8πT_ab (book's sign convention); in Friedmann's equation it contributes ΛR²/3, acting as repulsion for Λ > 0 and attraction for Λ < 0, and it can be reinterpreted as vacuum energy with p = -ρ.

**How introduced:** Allowed any real value in the classification; its history (introduced by Einstein for a static universe, abandoned after Hubble, revived as dark energy) is retold in §25.4, and its late-time dominance motivates §25.9 and §25.12.

### Einstein-de Sitter model

### deceleration parameter

*physical-quantity · revisited* · also: q(t)

Dimensionless measure q = -RR̈/Ṙ² of whether the expansion is slowing (q > 0) or speeding up (q < 0).

**How introduced:** Evaluated for Einstein-de Sitter (1/2), the closed dust model (greater than 1/2 and growing) and the steady-state model (-1).

### qualitative analysis of the Friedmann equation

*technique · core* · also: F(R) and G(R) method, effective potential method

Writing the Friedmann equation as Ṙ² = F(R) and inferring the history from F: R can only move where F ≥ 0, zeros of F are turning points, and the dominant term of F at small or large R gives power-law or exponential asymptotics.

**How introduced:** Introduced right after the exact k = 0 solutions as a cheaper route that generalises to all cases; repeated with G(R) = C/R - k for Λ = 0; it underpins the sketches of Fig. 25.1.

**Prerequisites:** Friedmann equation for dust

$$
\dot R^2 = F(R) = \frac{C}{R} + \tfrac13\Lambda R^2
$$

$$
\dot R^2 = G(R) = \frac{C}{R} - k
$$

**Notes:** Equivalent to one-dimensional particle motion in a potential; Exercise 25.7 draws the Newtonian analogy.

**Where:** DIV §25.1 p.513 (pdf 528); DIV §25.2 p.515 (pdf 530); DIV §25.3 p.516 (pdf 531)

### early-time universality of expansion

*principle · developed* · also: all big bang models start like Einstein-de Sitter

Near R = 0 the matter term C/R dominates the curvature and Λ terms, so every dust big bang model grows as t^{2/3} initially regardless of k and Λ (t^{1/2} for radiation).

**How introduced:** Argued by term dominance in (25.1) and integrated as in the Einstein-de Sitter case; revisited for radiation in §25.5 and in the summary §25.12.

**Prerequisites:** Friedmann equation for dust, Einstein-de Sitter model

$$
\dot R^2 \sim C/R \Rightarrow R \sim (\tfrac94 C t^2)^{1/3}
$$

**Where:** DIV §25.1 p.513 (pdf 528); DIV §25.5 p.521 (pdf 536); DIV §25.12 p.534 (pdf 549)

### late-time exponential expansion

*phenomenon · developed* · also: de Sitter attractor, asymptotic de Sitter behaviour

In any ever-expanding model with Λ > 0, the ΛR²/3 term eventually dominates and R grows like exp[(Λ/3)^{1/2}t], approaching the de Sitter model.

**How introduced:** Found for k = 0 by dominance; §25.4 then observes that the Λ > 0 ever-expanding cases of every row (the kinked open and flat models, Lemaître's model, the Eddington-Lemaître model and the bounce) all approach it.

**Prerequisites:** Friedmann equation for dust, cosmological constant, de Sitter model

\dot R^2 \sim \tfrac13 \Lambda R^2 \Rightarrow R \sim \exp[(\Lambda/3)^{1/2} t]

### closed Friedmann model

*solution-or-model · developed* · also: k = +1 dust model, model III(iv)

Positively curved dust model; with Λ = 0 it expands from a big bang to maximum scale factor R = C and recollapses, with the implicit solution C[arcsin(R/C)^{1/2} - (R/C)^{1/2}(1 - R/C)^{1/2}] = t.

**How introduced:** Solved by the substitutions u² = R/C and u = sin θ; qualitatively from G(R) = C/R - 1 vanishing at R = C.

**Prerequisites:** Friedmann equation for dust, curvature index k

$$
C\left[\sin^{-1}(R/C)^{1/2} - (R/C)^{1/2}(1-R/C)^{1/2}\right] = t
$$

$$
q = \tfrac12 (1 - R/C)^{-1}
$$

**Notes:** Parametrically R = C sin²θ, t = C(θ - sin θ cos θ): a cycloid; maximum at t = πC/2, crunch at t = πC.

**Where:** DIV §25.2 p.514 (pdf 529); DIV §25.2 p.515 (pdf 530)

### open Friedmann model

*solution-or-model · developed* · also: k = -1 dust model, model I(ii)

Negatively curved dust model; with Λ = 0 it expands forever, starting as t^{2/3} and approaching R ≈ t since Ṙ² → 1 at large R.

**How introduced:** Solution (25.20) stated as an exercise; asymptotics from G(R) = C/R + 1.

**Notes:** With the book's field equations (13.5) this gives Λ = -3K; combined with (24.52) de Sitter then has K = -1/α² < 0, whereas the text labels K > 0 as de Sitter (see gaps).

## Worked examples

### Example · standard · DIV §25.1 p.511 (pdf 526)

**Problem:** Solve the flat dust Friedmann equation with positive Λ subject to a big bang start.

**Method:** Set u = 2ΛR³/(3C) to get u̇² = 3Λ(2u + u²). Take the positive root and write 2u + u² = (u + 1)² - 1. Substitute u + 1 = cosh w, so the integral becomes ∫dw, and invert.

**Key insight:** Choosing the variable ∝ R³ makes both terms of F(R) quadratic in u, so the integral becomes standard.

**Result:** R³ = (3C/2Λ)[cosh((3Λ)^{1/2}t) - 1]; the Λ < 0 analogue has 1 - cos.

**Concepts:** Friedmann equation for dust, big bang initial condition, late-time exponential expansion

### Example · intro · DIV §25.1 p.512 (pdf 527)

**Problem:** Find R(t), H and q for the flat, Λ = 0 dust model.

**Method:** Separate Ṙ² = C/R as R^{1/2}dR = C^{1/2}dt, integrate from R = 0 at t = 0, then differentiate.

**Key insight:** The simplest big bang model already has universal early-time behaviour and constant q.

**Result:** R = (9Ct²/4)^{1/3}, H = 2/(3t), q = 1/2.

**Concepts:** Einstein-de Sitter model, Hubble parameter, deceleration parameter

### Example · standard · DIV §25.2 p.514 (pdf 529)

**Problem:** Solve the closed (k = +1), Λ = 0 dust model and find H and q.

**Method:** Substitute u² = R/C to get a separable equation with integrand 2u²/(1 - u²)^{1/2}. Put u = sin θ, use the double-angle identity, and express the result back in R. Differentiate implicitly for H and q.

**Key insight:** The trigonometric substitution exposes the cycloid: expansion to R = C and symmetric recollapse.

**Result:** C[arcsin(R/C)^{1/2} - (R/C)^{1/2}(1 - R/C)^{1/2}] = t, H = C^{-1}(R/C)^{-3/2}(1 - R/C)^{1/2}, q = ½(1 - R/C)^{-1}.

**Concepts:** closed Friedmann model, Hubble parameter, deceleration parameter

### Example · intro · DIV §25.4 p.519 (pdf 534)

**Problem:** Can the Robertson-Walker field equations admit a static universe filled with ordinary matter?

**Method:** Set R = a constant in (24.52)-(24.53), first with Λ = 0 and then with Λ free, and impose ρ ≥ 0 and p = 0.

**Key insight:** Without Λ a static universe needs negative pressure; with Λ = 1/a² and k = 1 dust can sit still. This is the logic that led Einstein to Λ.

**Result:** Λ = 0: 8πρ = 3k/a², 8πp = -k/a² (needs k = 1, p < 0). With Λ: k = 1, Λ = 1/a², ρ = 1/(4πa²), p = 0.

**Concepts:** Einstein static universe, cosmological constant

### Example · challenging · DIV §25.4 p.520 (pdf 535)

**Problem:** Show that the exponentially expanding de Sitter metric is actually stationary.

**Method:** Apply the transformation (25.33): t̄ = t - (α/2)ln[1 - α^{-2}r²e^{2t/α}], x̄^i = x^i e^{t/α}. Substitute into (25.31) and drop bars.

**Key insight:** Physical coordinates x̄ = e^{t/α}x absorb the expansion; the logarithmic time shift removes the cross terms, leaving metric components that do not depend on t.

**Result:** ds² = (1 - r²/α²)dt² - dx² - dy² - dz² - α^{-2}(x dx + y dy + z dz)²/(1 - r²/α²), eq (25.34).

**Concepts:** static coordinates of de Sitter space, stationarity, de Sitter model

### Example · standard · DIV §25.5 p.521 (pdf 536)

**Problem:** How do density and scale factor behave in a radiation-filled universe?

**Method:** Insert 3p = ρ into the field equations with Λ = 0 to get R̈/R + Ṙ²/R² + k/R² = 0 and drop k at early times. Put p = ρ/3 in the energy equation (24.54) to get d(ρR⁴)/dt = 0, then substitute into (24.52).

**Key insight:** Radiation loses energy to redshift as well as dilution, and its pressure adds to gravity, so the early expansion law changes from t^{2/3} to t^{1/2}.

**Result:** ρ ∝ R^{-4}, R ∼ t^{1/2}, Ṙ² = D/R² + ΛR²/3 - k with D = (8/3)πR⁴ρ.

**Concepts:** radiation-dominated expansion, scaling of density with scale factor, Friedmann equation for radiation

### Example · standard · DIV §25.6 p.522 (pdf 537)

**Problem:** Derive the steady-state metric from the perfect cosmological principle and deduce its Hubble law and deceleration parameter.

**Method:** The 3-curvature scales as kR^{-2}. The book says it could be observed, for example through how galaxy counts grow with distance, so it must stay constant, which forces k = 0. The observable H must also be constant, so R = e^{t/T_0}. Integrate radial null rays to get r(t), use d_L = r_1R(t_0) and 1 + z = R(t_0)/R(t_1), then eliminate r_1.

**Key insight:** Demanding that observables, not coordinates, be time-independent pins down the geometry uniquely without any field equations.

**Result:** ds² = dt² - e^{2t/T_0}[dr² + r²dΩ²], d_L = zT_0 exactly, q = -1.

**Concepts:** steady-state theory, perfect cosmological principle, exact Hubble law in the steady-state model

### Example · standard · DIV §25.7 p.523 (pdf 538)

**Problem:** For a comoving observer at r = 0 in de Sitter space, find the event horizon and describe what is seen of a comoving particle at r = r_1.

**Method:** Take t_0 → ∞ in the past light cone to get r = T_0e^{-t/T_0}; solve for the crossing time t_1. Integrate a null ray from (t, r_1) to r = 0 for the reception time τ. Evaluate d_L = r_1e^{τ/T_0}, z = d_L/T_0, proper distance l = r_1e^{t/T_0} and its time derivative.

**Key insight:** Everything O observes diverges (arrival time, redshift, apparent distance) while the particle itself crosses at finite proper time and finite proper distance, where its geodesic recession speed equals light speed.

**Result:** t_1 = T_0 ln(T_0/r_1); τ = -T_0 ln(e^{-t/T_0} - e^{-t_1/T_0}) → ∞; d_L, z → ∞; l(t_1) = T_0; dl/dt = e^{(t - t_1)/T_0} → 1.

**Concepts:** cosmological event horizon, reception time divergence at the horizon, infinite redshift at a horizon, geodesic recession velocity

### Example · intro · DIV §25.9 p.527 (pdf 542)

**Problem:** Find the scalar curvature and Einstein tensor of a four-dimensional constant curvature spacetime.

**Method:** Contract R_abcd = K(g_ac g_bd - g_ad g_bc) on a and c to get R_bd = 3Kg_bd, contract again for R = 12K, and form G_ab = R_ab - ½Rg_ab.

**Key insight:** Maximal symmetry automatically makes the spacetime a vacuum solution with a cosmological constant, so the constant-curvature classification is also a classification of Λ-vacua.

**Result:** K = R/12, G_ab = -3Kg_ab, hence Λ = -3K with G_ab - Λg_ab = 0.

**Concepts:** constant curvature spacetime, Einstein space

### Example · challenging · DIV §25.9 p.528 (pdf 543)

**Problem:** Obtain global and flat-slicing coordinates on the de Sitter hyperboloid and the global metric of anti-de Sitter space.

**Method:** Parametrise the quadric (25.59) with hyperbolic and spherical functions, check the constraint, and pull back the 5D metric to get (25.62). Define t = T_0 ln[(ŵ + v̂)/T_0] and x^i = T_0x̂^i/(ŵ + v̂) to get (25.31). Repeat with two timelike directions for AdS, (25.64)-(25.67).

**Key insight:** Embedding makes the maximal symmetry and the global topology visible, and shows that coordinate patches can cover only part of the manifold.

**Result:** ds² = dt̂² - T_0²cosh²(t̂/T_0)dΩ₃² (all of de Sitter); flat coordinates only where ŵ + v̂ > 0; AdS: T_0²[cosh²ρ d(t̂/T_0)² - dρ² - sinh²ρ dΩ²] with t̂ unwrapped.

**Concepts:** hyperboloid embedding of de Sitter space, global coordinates of de Sitter space, flat slicing of de Sitter space, anti-de Sitter space

### Example · standard · DIV §25.11 p.531 (pdf 546)

**Problem:** Construct the conformal diagrams of de Sitter and anti-de Sitter space.

**Method:** For de Sitter set t' = 2arctan(e^{t̂/T_0}) - π/2 so that dt̂ = T_0 cosh(t̂/T_0)dt' and cosh(t̂/T_0) = sec t'; factor the metric as T_0² sec²t' times the Einstein static metric. For AdS apply the same trick to the radial coordinate, χ = 2arctan(e^ρ) - π/2, obtaining T_0² sec²χ times the Einstein static metric.

**Key insight:** The same 'double arctan of an exponential' (the Gudermannian function) compactifies an infinite hyperbolic range into a finite angular range; whether it acts on time or radius decides whether infinity is spacelike (dS) or timelike (AdS).

**Result:** de Sitter: band -π/2 < t' < π/2 of the cylinder (square Penrose diagram, steady-state = half). AdS: 0 ≤ χ < π/2 for all t̂, timelike boundary at χ = π/2, topology R⁴.

**Concepts:** conformal compactification, Penrose diagram of de Sitter space, timelike conformal boundary of anti-de Sitter space


## Analogies and intuitions

### An event horizon is a curtain that hides what lies behind it; an explorer can pull the curtain aside, but only by leaving home for ever. → cosmological event horizon · strong · DIV §25.7 p.525 (pdf 540)

Used to convey both that information beyond the horizon is inaccessible to O and that those events are nonetheless real and observable to others at the cost of never returning.

**Where it breaks down:** A curtain is a physical object at a place; the horizon is a null surface defined by O's entire future, not a barrier that anyone passing it would notice locally.

**App idea:** Animate a translucent curtain attached to the horizon surface in the (t, r) diagram that re-forms around E's position when the viewpoint switches from O to E.

### Friedmann's equation read as a particle's energy equation: Ṙ² plays kinetic energy and -F(R) a potential, so turning points are where 'kinetic energy' vanishes. → qualitative analysis of the Friedmann equation · strong · DIV §25.1 p.513 (pdf 528)

Implicit in the F(R) and G(R) reasoning and made explicit in Exercise 25.7, where a particle falling in a straight channel toward a mass obeys ẋ² = 2GM/x - 2GM/x_1 and its escape or return mirrors open and closed models.

**Where it breaks down:** The 'energy' constant is -k, quantised to three values by normalisation. Λ gives a potential with no Newtonian counterpart in the simple channel model, and the analogy says nothing about spatial geometry.

**App idea:** Potential-well view: plot V(R) = -C/R - ΛR²/6 with a horizontal line at -k/2 and a ball rolling on it, synchronised with the R(t) plot.

### An exponential curve is self-similar: zooming to any portion and rescaling looks the same, so no moment along it is special. → self-similarity of exponential expansion · useful · DIV §25.4 p.520 (pdf 535)

Explains why de Sitter's time origin is conventional and prepares for the perfect cosmological principle.

**Where it breaks down:** Self-similarity of R(t) is a statement about the metric's symmetry; it does not by itself show the full spacetime is time-independent, which needs the static coordinates.

**App idea:** Zoomable exponential vs power-law comparison.

### Run a collapse backwards in time: more gravity means faster collapse, so its time reverse is faster expansion from the singularity. → pressure as a source of gravity · useful · DIV §25.5 p.521 (pdf 536)

Used to make sense of the claim that radiation, with pressure, expands faster near the big bang than dust.

**Where it breaks down:** 'Faster' needs care: at equal density radiation decelerates more, and the model reaches R = 0 more quickly when run backwards; comparing t^{1/2} with t^{2/3} directly depends on normalisation.

**App idea:** Split-screen reversed-time movie of dust and radiation collapses starting from the same R and Ṙ.

### The uniformly accelerated observer in Minkowski space as a flat-space model for a cosmological horizon. → cosmological event horizon · strong · DIV §25.7 p.525 (pdf 540)

Shows that horizons arise from an observer's world-line relative to the causal structure rather than from curvature, then used to classify horizons by the nature of 𝒥⁺.

**Where it breaks down:** The Rindler horizon disappears if the observer stops accelerating; the de Sitter horizon exists for every geodesic comoving observer.

**App idea:** Side-by-side diagrams: Rindler hyperbola in Minkowski and a comoving observer in de Sitter (static patch), with matched horizon shading.

### Visualising a curved spacetime as a surface (hyperboloid) sitting inside a flat higher-dimensional space. → hyperboloid embedding of de Sitter space · strong · DIV §25.9 p.528 (pdf 543)

Makes the global topology (cylinder R × S³), the contracting-then-expanding 3-spheres, and the partial coverage of flat coordinates visible at a glance.

**Where it breaks down:** The embedding space has indefinite signature, so Euclidean intuitions about distances on the drawn surface are misleading; the picture suppresses two or three dimensions.

**App idea:** 3D rotatable hyperboloid with light cones drawn tangent to the surface to counter Euclidean intuition.

### The Einstein static cylinder as a common stage on which every model becomes a shaded region. → conformal compactification · useful · DIV §25.10 p.530 (pdf 545)

Different cosmologies are compared by the shape of their region (band, triangle, half-cylinder), which directly reveals spacelike, null or timelike infinity.

**Where it breaks down:** Conformal maps preserve light cones but not distances or time intervals; the size of a region is not physically meaningful.

**App idea:** Cylinder painter with a model gallery.


## Misconceptions addressed

### Because the de Sitter metric contains e^{2t/α}, de Sitter space is intrinsically evolving. · DIV §25.4 p.521 (pdf 536)

**Correction:** Transforming to (25.34) gives time-independent components; the apparent expansion reflects the choice of comoving slicing. Comoving observers still separate, but the geometry is stationary.

**Why tempting:** Metrics written in comoving coordinates make any time dependence look physical.

### Pressure pushes outward, so a radiation-filled universe should resist collapse and expand more gently than dust. · DIV §25.5 p.521 (pdf 536)

**Correction:** In general relativity pressure is a source of gravity, adding to the attraction, which is why radiation models behave as t^{1/2} near the singularity.

**Why tempting:** Everyday pressure pushes on walls; a homogeneous universe has no pressure gradients, and only the gravitating effect remains.

### Closed universes always recollapse and open or flat universes always expand for ever. · DIV §25.3 p.516 (pdf 531)

**Correction:** That link holds only for Λ = 0. With Λ > Λ_c a closed model expands for ever, and with Λ < 0 every model, including open ones, recollapses.

**Why tempting:** Many introductory treatments set Λ = 0 and equate geometry with destiny.

### Event horizons exist only around black holes and are the same for everyone. · DIV §25.8 p.526 (pdf 541)

**Correction:** Cosmological and acceleration horizons depend on the observer's world-line; black hole horizons are the special absolute (observer-independent) kind.

**Why tempting:** Horizons are usually first met in black-hole contexts.

### A galaxy crossing our cosmological horizon suddenly vanishes at some finite time we could observe. · DIV §25.7 p.525 (pdf 540)

**Correction:** Its light arrives ever later and ever more redshifted; we never see the crossing itself, though the galaxy passes the horizon in finite proper time.

**Why tempting:** People picture the horizon like the edge of a field of view that objects step behind.

### Events beyond an observer's horizon can be ignored as physically meaningless. · DIV §25.7 p.525 (pdf 540)

**Correction:** They are real: an explorer can see them and the observer's own horizon moves if the observer moves; only communication back is impossible.

**Why tempting:** Operationalist instinct that the unobservable does not exist.

### A recession velocity reaching the speed of light violates relativity. · DIV §25.7 p.525 (pdf 540)

**Correction:** The geodesic recession velocity dl/dt is a rate of change of proper distance along a cosmic-time slice, not a velocity measured locally against light; it reaches 1 at the horizon without contradiction.

**Why tempting:** Special relativity is remembered as 'nothing can exceed c' without the qualifier 'locally'.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### The big bang happened at a point in space. · DIV §25.10 p.531 (pdf 546)

**Correction:** The book never discusses this idea directly, but its Penrose diagrams answer it: the initial singularity is a spacelike surface, and every comoving world-line begins on it at a different place.

**Why tempting:** Popular images of an explosion from a centre.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### A coordinate system that covers all of an observer's world-lines covers the whole spacetime. · DIV §25.9 p.529 (pdf 544)

**Correction:** The flat (steady-state) coordinates cover all comoving histories from t = -∞ yet only half of de Sitter space; geodesics leave the patch through a null boundary.

**Why tempting:** Coordinate time running from -∞ to +∞ sounds complete.

### An eternal steady-state universe with no beginning is automatically complete. · DIV §25.11 p.532 (pdf 547)

**Correction:** Its Penrose diagram shows a null past boundary: some geodesics end at finite affine parameter, so it is past-incomplete.

**Why tempting:** No big bang singularity suggests nothing is missing.

### The periodic time in the AdS embedding means time is physically cyclic there. · DIV §25.9 p.529 (pdf 544)

**Correction:** The periodicity is an artefact of the embedding; one takes the covering space with t̂ over all reals, topology R⁴.

**Why tempting:** The formulas in (25.66) are manifestly periodic.

### The Einstein static universe is a viable static cosmos once Λ is tuned. · DIV §25.3 p.518 (pdf 533)

**Correction:** It requires exact fine-tuning (Λ = Λ_c and R = R_c with Ṙ = R̈ = 0) and any perturbation sends it toward the Eddington-Lemaître or the collapsing branch; it is also contradicted by observed expansion.

**Why tempting:** The algebra gives a perfectly valid solution, and the book presents it as balanced.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### Cosmic eras are cleanly separated, with radiation absent in the matter era and so on. · DIV §25.12 p.535 (pdf 550)

**Correction:** All components coexist; each era is defined by which term dominates the Friedmann equation, and transitions are gradual.

**Why tempting:** Textbook tables list eras with sharp boundaries.

### Spatial curvature k = +1 or 0 or -1 fixes whether space is finite. · DIV §25.3 p.516 (pdf 531)

**Correction:** Local geometry does not fix global topology; flat or hyperbolic spaces can be compact after identifications. The book's 'open/closed topology' assumes the simplest topologies.

**Why tempting:** The book states topology directly from k.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*


## Thought experiments

### Watching a comoving galaxy slip over the de Sitter horizon · DIV §25.7 p.524 (pdf 539)

**Setup:** Observer O at r = 0 monitors substratum particle P at r_1, receiving its signals and measuring their redshift and apparent distance.

**Lesson:** O's received signals take unboundedly long, with redshift and luminosity distance diverging, while P crosses the horizon at finite proper time; the recession speed reaches light speed there.

**Setup:** O sends explorer E outward with instructions to report everything; E crosses O's horizon at event E_1.

**Lesson:** E can learn about events hidden from O but can never send that knowledge back; horizons are observer-relative and knowledge beyond them is available only one way.

**App idea:** Two-observer horizon game of Fig. 25.5.

### The uniformly accelerated observer in flat spacetime · DIV §25.7 p.525 (pdf 540)

**Setup:** An observer on x² - t² = const in Minkowski space and light emitted from events in the region t > x.

**Lesson:** Such light never reaches the observer, so a horizon exists without curvature; inertial observers have none.

**App idea:** Rindler toggle of Fig. 25.6.

### Particle in a straight channel toward a fixed mass · DIV §25.3 p.536 (pdf 551)

**Lesson:** Stronger gravity (with pressure) gives faster collapse; the reversed film is a faster initial expansion.


## Applications and observations

- **Galaxy recession and abandonment of the static universe** (observation, DIV §25.4 p.519 (pdf 534)): Recession of distant galaxies measured by Slipher and Hubble showed expansion, ending the static Einstein model as a cosmology.
- **Age of an Einstein-de Sitter universe** (numerical-estimate, DIV §25.1 p.513 (pdf 528)): From H = 2/(3t), the age is two-thirds of the Hubble time. Our own estimate, not in the text: with H_0 ≈ 70 km/s/Mpc (Hubble time ≈ 14.0 Gyr) this gives about 9.3 Gyr, younger than the oldest stars. This is one historical motivation for Λ, taken up in Chapter 26. Key numbers: t_0 = 2/(3H_0) ≈ 9.3 Gyr for H_0 = 70 km/s/Mpc (our estimate)
- **Steady-state predictions: exact Hubble law and q = -1** (observation, DIV §25.6 p.523 (pdf 538)): The theory predicts d_L = zT_0 at all redshifts and q = -1, making it easy to test; the text says it conflicts with much present-day observation (details such as the microwave background and evolving radio-source counts are not given here). Key numbers: q = -1; d_L = zT_0
- **Size of our cosmological event horizon** (numerical-estimate, DIV §25.7 p.523 (pdf 538)): If the universe approaches de Sitter with the observed Λ, the asymptotic horizon distance is α = (3/Λ)^{1/2}. Our own estimate: Λ ≈ 1.1 × 10^{-52} m^{-2} gives α ≈ 1.6 × 10^{26} m ≈ 17 billion light years. Key numbers: α = (3/Λ)^{1/2} ≈ 5 Gpc ≈ 17 Gly (our estimate)
- **Matter-radiation transition** (numerical-estimate, DIV §25.12 p.534 (pdf 549)): Because ρ_rad ∝ R^{-4} and ρ_matter ∝ R^{-3}, radiation must dominate early and matter later. Our own addition from standard ΛCDM values: equality occurs at redshift about 3400, and matter-Λ equality near z ≈ 0.3. Key numbers: z_eq ≈ 3400; matter-Λ equality z ≈ 0.3 (external values)
- **Vacuum energy as the source of Λ** (astrophysical-system, DIV §25.12 p.534 (pdf 549)): A small Λ could arise from quantum zero-point energy, modelled as a fluid with p = -ρ; its constant density guarantees late-time domination in any expanding universe. Key numbers: ρ_vac = Λ/(8π) in geometric units
- **AdS/CFT in string theory** (technology, DIV §25.11 p.534 (pdf 549)): Anti-de Sitter space's timelike boundary makes it the arena for a duality between bulk quantum gravity and a boundary conformal field theory; flagged as an active research area.

## Historical notes

- **Albert Einstein (1916-1917):** Believing the universe static and homogeneous, Einstein found the field equations required negative pressure for a static closed universe and added the cosmological constant, obtaining the Einstein static universe. — Origin of Λ; the static model survives today as the conformal backdrop for Penrose diagrams. (DIV §25.4 p.519 (pdf 534))
- **Vesto Slipher, Edwin Hubble (early 20th century):** Observed that distant galaxies recede, showing the universe expands. — Led Einstein to drop Λ and accept expanding models. (DIV §25.4 p.519 (pdf 534))
- **Willem de Sitter (1917):** Found the empty exponentially expanding solution with Λ, the earliest expanding cosmological model. — Became the late-time attractor of Λ > 0 models, the steady-state metric, and a prototype of inflation. (DIV §25.4 p.519 (pdf 534))
- **Georges Lemaître, Arthur Eddington:** Closed models with Λ near the critical value (Lemaître's coasting model, the Eddington-Lemaître model emerging from the static state) are named after them. — Early explorations of Λ-dominated histories; the book only uses the names as labels. (DIV §25.3 p.518 (pdf 533))
- **Hermann Bondi, Thomas Gold, Fred Hoyle (1948):** Proposed the steady-state theory based on the perfect cosmological principle with continuous matter creation. — A sharply falsifiable rival to big bang cosmology, now considered mainly historical because observations disagree with it. (DIV §25.6 p.522 (pdf 537))
- **History:** The cosmological constant returned to cosmology in the form of dark energy. — Justifies keeping Λ throughout the classification. (DIV §25.4 p.519 (pdf 534))
- **P. T. Landsberg, D. A. Evans (1977):** Gave a systematic treatment of the solutions of Friedmann's equation in all cases. — Cited as the complete reference the chapter does not reproduce. (DIV §25.1 p.511 (pdf 526))
- **History:** The AdS/CFT correspondence links quantum gravity in anti-de Sitter space to conformal field theory on its boundary. — Explains the modern interest in a spacetime that is not a realistic cosmology. (DIV §25.11 p.534 (pdf 549))

## Notation and conventions

- **Metric signature and units:** Signature (+,-,-,-) with geometric units G = c = 1, so ds² = dt² - R²(...) and density appears as 8πρ. (DIV §25.1 p.511 (pdf 526))
- **Field equations with Λ:** G_ab - Λg_ab = 8πT_ab (13.5), so Λ > 0 is repulsive in Friedmann's equation and a constant-curvature vacuum has Λ = -3K. — Combined with (24.52) this implies de Sitter (Λ > 0) has K = -1/α² in this convention; the book nevertheless calls K > 0 the de Sitter case. (DIV §25.9 p.527 (pdf 542))
- **Scale factor symbol R:** R(t) denotes the scale factor throughout; R also denotes the Ricci scalar in K = R/12 and R_abcd the Riemann tensor. — Watch the overload in §25.9. (DIV §25.9 p.527 (pdf 542))
- **Friedmann constants C and D:** C = (8/3)πρR³ for dust; D = (8/3)πρR⁴ for radiation. — C is reused in (25.37) for the constant ρR⁴, and the text's 'C = (8/3)πD' should read D = (8/3)πC. (DIV §25.5 p.521 (pdf 536))
- **Hubble and de Sitter time scales:** α = (3/Λ)^{1/2} in §25.4; T_0 (constant Hubble time) in the steady-state and horizon sections; T_0 is also the radius of the de Sitter and AdS hyperboloids. — With α = T_0 all three agree. (DIV §25.6 p.522 (pdf 537))
- **Model labels:** Roman numerals I, II, III for k = -1, 0, +1 and lower-case (i)-(v) with letters (a)-(c) for sub-cases, e.g. III(iic) Eddington-Lemaître, III(iiib) bouncing. (DIV §25.3 p.516 (pdf 531))
- **Hatted embedding coordinates:** (û, v̂, ŵ, x̂, ŷ, ẑ) are Cartesian coordinates on the flat five-dimensional embedding space; t̂ is the global time on the hyperboloid, distinct from the flat-slicing time t. (DIV §25.9 p.528 (pdf 543))
- **Radial coordinate χ and function f(χ):** RW metric written dt² - R²[dχ² + f²(χ)dΩ²] with f = χ, sin χ, sinh χ; for AdS χ is instead the compactified radial coordinate. — The text gives χ up to 2π for k = +1; the correct range is 0 to π. (DIV §25.10 p.530 (pdf 545))
- **Primed ESU coordinates:** t', r' denote time and polar angle on the Einstein static cylinder (18.27); in §25.11 r' is identified with χ. (DIV §25.11 p.532 (pdf 547))
- **τ overloaded:** τ is the reception time of a signal in §25.7 but conformal time dτ = dt/R in §25.10. (DIV §25.10 p.530 (pdf 545))
- **Script I for null infinity:** 𝒥⁺, 𝒥⁻ (scri) with i⁺, i⁻, i⁰ for timelike and spatial infinity; the extraction renders script I as J. — In Figs 25.14 and 25.15 the big bang and crunch surfaces are also labelled with 𝒥⁻/𝒥⁺ though they are singularities, not infinity. (DIV §25.8 p.526 (pdf 541))
- **Hubble and deceleration parameters:** H = Ṙ/R, q = -RR̈/Ṙ² as in (24.73), (24.76). (DIV §25.1 p.513 (pdf 528))
- **Luminosity distance:** d_L = r_1R(t_0) following (24.72). (DIV §25.6 p.523 (pdf 538))
- **Five-dimensional embedding spaces:** Called 'flat five-dimensional Euclidean space' in the text but carrying indefinite metrics: (+,-,-,-,-) for de Sitter and (+,+,-,-,-) for anti-de Sitter. — The Fig. 25.11 caption correctly says five-dimensional Minkowski space-time. (DIV §25.9 p.528 (pdf 543))

## Gaps and pitfalls

- **The printed (25.1) has ΛṘ/3 in place of ΛR²/3, and the inline equation at the start of §25.2 has Ṙ where Ṙ² is meant.** — The master equation of the chapter appears dimensionally inconsistent at first sight. *Suggestion:* Always present Ṙ² = C/R + ΛR²/3 - k and check against (25.3) and (24.60).
- **At R_m (Λ < 0) and at R = C (k = +1, Λ = 0) the book says Ṙ vanishes at 'a local minimum'. The phrase can only fairly refer to Ṙ² reaching its least possible value, zero. For R(t) this is the maximum radius, the turning point where expansion becomes collapse, and F(R) itself has no minimum there.** — Learners may think the model bounces off a minimum radius. *Suggestion:* Say 'turning point, maximum of R(t)', point out that F(R) decreases monotonically through its zero, and show the arch on a plot.
- **The text writes p = 3ρ (in §25.5 and §25.12) for radiation, contradicting (25.35) 3p = ρ; and sets C = (8/3)πD, which contradicts (25.39).** — Doing the algebra with p = 3ρ gives ρ ∝ R^{-12}; the constants will not match. *Suggestion:* Use p = ρ/3 and D = (8/3)πρR⁴ consistently; avoid reusing C for radiation.
- **Equation misprints in the horizon section: (25.48) repeats t_0 in both exponentials and (25.55) omits the division by T_0 in the exponent.** — The horizon limit and recession velocity cannot be reproduced from the printed forms. *Suggestion:* Use r = T_0(e^{-t/T_0} - e^{-t_0/T_0}) and dl/dt = e^{(t - t_1)/T_0}.

## Tutor notes

- Open with: 'If you know how fast the universe is expanding today and what it contains, can you predict its whole history?' Then show that Friedmann's equation is a one-dimensional energy equation.
- Before any integrals, have the learner sketch R(t) using only the dominance argument (C/R at small R, ΛR²/3 at large R, zeros of F); then reveal the exact solutions as confirmation.
- Correct the misprint in (25.1) immediately and flag the p = 3ρ typo before the radiation section, so learners trust their algebra.
- Use Exercise 25.7 (particle in a channel) as the bridge for learners with mechanics background; for others, use a ball thrown upward: escape, marginal, fall back.
- Check understanding: 'Which models with k = +1 expand for ever?' (Λ > Λ_c, and the Eddington-Lemaître case). 'Can a flat universe recollapse?' (yes, if Λ < 0).
- For the Einstein static universe ask the learner to set Ṙ = R̈ = 0 themselves; then ask what happens if R is nudged slightly larger, to motivate instability.
- When presenting de Sitter, ask 'Is this universe changing?' before showing the static coordinates; use it as a lesson on coordinate versus invariant statements.
- For horizons, first draw the past light cone of a finite t_0, then let t_0 grow on screen; ask the learner to predict what the limit surface looks like.
- Common question: 'If the galaxy recedes at the speed of light at the horizon, doesn't that break relativity?' Answer: proper-distance rate of change on a cosmic time slice is not a local velocity; local light cones are respected.
- Common question: 'Will we see galaxies disappear?' Answer: they fade and redshift without limit; we never see them cross.
- Distinguish particle horizon (what we could have seen so far, set by the past) from event horizon (what we will ever see, set by the future); link each to the shape of the Penrose diagram's bottom and top edges.
- When introducing the hyperboloid, remind learners that the embedding space has a minus sign, so the 'waist' is not a bottleneck in a Euclidean sense; show light cones on the surface.
- For conformal diagrams, stress that only light-cone structure is preserved; sizes of regions are not physical, but whether boundaries are spacelike, null or timelike is.
- Point out the K sign convention issue if the learner is computing curvature; recommend classifying by the sign of Λ.
- Finish by asking the learner to narrate the four-era history and justify its order from ρ ∝ R^{-4}, R^{-3}, R^0; then signpost Chapter 26 for real data.
