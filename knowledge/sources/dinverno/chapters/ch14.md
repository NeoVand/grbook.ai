# DIV ch14 · The 3+1 and 2+2 formalisms

> Rebuilds the Cauchy problem of Chapter 13 geometrically: a spacelike hypersurface is described by its induced metric and extrinsic curvature, the Gauss, Codazzi and Ricci equations recover spacetime curvature from them, a foliation plus lapse and shift turns vacuum Einstein equations into four constraints and first-order evolution equations for (gamma, K), numerical-relativity practice (conformal initial data, slicing and shift gauges) is surveyed, and the 2+2 double-foliation alternative is sketched with its lapse matrix, two shifts and conformal 2-structure.

**Pages:** printed 239–268 · pdf 254–283 · **Difficulty:** math 5/5, conceptual 4/5, novice-friendliness 2/5

Among the most technical chapters of the book: long projector-laden index derivations (Gauss, Codazzi, Ricci), Lie derivatives, and block-matrix inversion, with many steps delegated to exercises. Conceptually the layering (slice, foliation, fibration) is clear, but distinguishing intrinsic from extrinsic geometry, gauge from dynamics, and h from gamma sign conventions is demanding. §14.12 and §14.13-14.14 shift to quoted results with little derivation. Several misprints and sign inconsistencies (14.76, 14.87, 14.89-90, 14.121, 14.123, 14.142, the h/gamma remark) will derail careful learners who try to verify equations.

## Role in the book

Chapter 13 showed in special coordinates that Einstein's equations split into constraints and evolution equations and argued existence via Cauchy-Kowalevskaya. Chapter 14 is the coordinate-free, geometric sequel: it supplies the machinery (projection operators, induced connection, extrinsic curvature, Lie derivatives along the normal) that numerical relativity actually uses and that underlies the ADM Hamiltonian picture of 'geometrodynamics'. It closes the 'structure of the field equations' block before the book turns to exact solutions in Chapter 15 (whose spherically symmetric metric already appears in Exercise 14.4), and the 2+2 and characteristic sections plant the null-hypersurface ideas that Chapter 23 (Bondi's radiating metric, characteristic initial value problem) develops. In style it is markedly more differential-geometric than the book's early chapters, and its further reading says the ordering follows Gourgoulhon's lecture notes.

## Learning objectives

- Reader can build the projection operator B_a^b from the unit normal of a spacelike hypersurface and prove it is idempotent and annihilates the normal.
- Reader can define the induced metric h_ab = g_ab - n_a n_b, explain why its spatial part is negative definite in the (+,-,-,-) signature, and pass to the positive definite gamma_ab = -h_ab.
- Reader can define the induced covariant derivative by double projection and argue that it is the Levi-Civita connection of h_ab.
- Reader can define the extrinsic curvature as minus the doubly projected gradient of the unit normal, show it is symmetric and tangential, and interpret it as the normal part of the spacetime derivative of tangent fields (the Gauss formula).
- Reader can state the Gauss, Codazzi and Ricci equations, say which projections of the Riemann tensor each one supplies, and explain why the Ricci equation needs a foliation.
- Reader can contract the Gauss and Codazzi equations to obtain the Hamiltonian and momentum constraints and explain why these contain no normal time derivatives.
- Reader can define a foliation by a time function, the lapse N, the Eulerian observer, and prove that N = d tau / d t.
- Reader can derive K_ab = -(1/2) L_n h_ab and the acceleration relation A_a = -D_a ln N.
- Reader can decompose a rigging vector T = N n + beta into lapse and shift and write the 3+1 line element and inverse metric in adapted coordinates.
- Reader can write the vacuum ADM evolution equations for gamma_{mu nu} and K_{mu nu}, identify lapse and shift as non-dynamical gauge functions, and outline the constrain-then-evolve procedure.
- Reader can summarize the York-O Murchadha conformal method for solving the constraints and the simplifications from conformal flatness, maximal slicing and vanishing TT data.
- Reader can compare slicing conditions (geodesic, Lagrangian, maximal) and shift conditions (Eulerian, Lagrangian, isothermal, radial) in terms of singularity avoidance and simplicity.
- Reader can reproduce the counting argument that reduces 10 metric functions to 2 gravitational degrees of freedom.
- Reader can describe the 2+2 construction (two transvecting vector fields, double-null and null-timelike foliations), the lapse matrix N_AB, the two shift vectors, and the conformal 2-structure as carrier of the free data.

## Teaching approach

Geometry-before-coordinates, following Gourgoulhon: the authors first study one hypersurface in isolation (intrinsic and extrinsic geometry, Gauss-Codazzi), then add a family of hypersurfaces (foliation, acceleration, Ricci equation, lapse), and only then add a threading congruence (rigging vector, shift) that produces coordinates and PDEs. Each layer is introduced exactly when the previous layer cannot supply the next curvature component or time derivative. Derivations are fully index-explicit with projection operators, with many small steps left as exercises, and results are restated in adapted coordinates using the starred equality. The later sections switch register to a literature survey (numerical relativity gauges, conformal data) and to an author-driven overview of the 2+2 formalism, where only the metric decomposition is done in detail.

**Style:** geometric-first, derivation-heavy, axiomatic-mathematical, computational-recipe, survey-overview, spiral-revisit

**Narrative arc**

1. Motivating problem: Chapter 13 did the Cauchy problem in special coordinates; can the same split be phrased geometrically, as data living on a slice?
2. Model one spacelike hypersurface: unit normal, projection operator, induced metric, sign flip to a positive definite 3-metric.
3. Intrinsic calculus: induced covariant derivative by double projection, proved to be the metric connection of h_ab.
4. Extrinsic data: define K_ab from the tangential derivative of the normal, show symmetry, and package the tangential/normal split of nabla_Y X.
5. Reconstruction question: how much spacetime curvature do (gamma, K) determine? State Gauss and Codazzi, contract them to the two constraint equations, then prove both in separate calculational sections.
6. Add a family of slices: Cauchy surfaces and global hyperbolicity justify M = Sigma x R; the normal can now be differentiated off the slice, giving the acceleration and the Ricci equation for the last curvature component.
7. Introduce the time function and lapse; interpret N as proper time per coordinate time via an Eulerian observer; show K is minus half the normal Lie derivative of h.
8. Relax the normal-observer restriction: arbitrary threading curves give the rigging vector, decomposed into lapse and shift; derive the 3+1 metric and line element.
9. Dynamics: project G_ab = 0 three ways, convert L_n into L_T = partial_t, and obtain first-order evolution equations for (gamma, K) plus four constraints; lapse and shift carry no evolution equation.
10. Procedure and history: solve constraints, pick gauge, evolve; constraints are preserved by Bianchi identities; Darmois, Lichnerowicz, Choquet-Bruhat, ADM.
11. Application survey: conformal-TT construction of initial data and the menu of slicing and shift conditions used in numerical codes.
12. Critique and alternative: 3+1 hides the two true degrees of freedom and breaks down on null slices; counting argument; 2+2 double foliation with lapse matrix, two shifts and conformal 2-structure; pointer to Bondi's metric in Chapter 23.

**Signature moves**

- Projection-operator bookkeeping: every hypersurface object is manufactured by sandwiching a spacetime object between copies of B_a^b, and the identities B n = 0 and B B = B do almost all the work in the long derivations.
- Layered geometry: single hypersurface, then foliation, then fibration, each layer unlocking exactly one new ingredient (K, then A and the Ricci equation, then shift and coordinates).
- Starred equality (*=) to flag results that hold only in adapted coordinates, keeping the covariant statement and its coordinate shadow side by side.
- Announce the key equations first (Gauss 14.23, Codazzi 14.25, Ricci 14.53) and extract their physics (the constraints) before proving them in dedicated calculation sections.
- Physical reading of formal objects via observers: the normal as an observer's 4-velocity, A^a as that observer's acceleration, the lapse as proper time elapsed per tick of coordinate time.
- Comparative metric decompositions: the 2+2 contravariant and covariant metrics are laid out as block matrices explicitly to be compared line by line with the 3+1 ones.
- Degree-of-freedom accounting as a narrative device to motivate a better formalism (2+2) rather than as an isolated fact.
- Frequent 'exercise' and 'check' markers inside derivations, turning the text into a guided calculation that the exercise list then completes.

## Section by section

### §14.1 The geometry of submanifolds — p.239 (pdf 254)

A one-page roadmap. It announces that the chapter redoes the Cauchy problem geometrically: embed a hypersurface, identify the induced metric and extrinsic curvature as the data that rebuild spacetime curvature, slice spacetime with a time function (foliation, lapse), thread it with timelike curves (fibration, shift), and read Einstein's equations as first-order evolution of (metric, extrinsic curvature). It names ADM (1959) and Wheeler's geometrodynamics and previews the 2+2 approach for null data and explicit degrees of freedom.

**Concepts:** induced metric, extrinsic curvature, foliation, lapse function, fibration, shift vector, ADM formalism, geometrodynamics, 2+2 formalism

**Key moves**
- Frames lapse and shift as the four free functions matching the fourfold coordinate freedom.
- Positions GR as a dynamical system with dynamical variables (h, K) evolving in a time function.

### §14.2 The induced metric — p.240 (pdf 255)

For a spacelike hypersurface with future-pointing unit timelike normal (n.n = +1 in this signature), the operator delta minus n n projects spacetime vectors and covectors into the surface; it kills the normal and squares to itself. Projecting g gives the induced metric h = g - n n, which agrees with g on tangent vectors. In coordinates where the surface is t = 0, h_{alpha beta} are just the spatial components of g and have signature (-,-,-), so the book introduces the positive definite gamma = -h.

**Concepts:** unit normal to a spacelike hypersurface, projection operator onto a hypersurface, induced metric, adapted coordinates, positive definite spatial metric gamma

**Key moves**
- Verify tangency of B X by contracting with n.
- Derive h_ab = g_ab - n_a n_b from the double projection.
- Flip the sign to gamma_ab = -h_ab because of the (+,-,-,-) signature.

### §14.3 The induced covariant derivative — p.241 (pdf 256)

Extend a tangent field off the surface, take the tangential derivative (independent of the extension), and project the free index back into the surface to define D_a; the same recipe works on any tensor. D annihilates h and is torsion-free, so it is the Levi-Civita derivative of h (and of gamma). In adapted coordinates the spatial 4D Christoffel symbols equal the 3D ones. The normal component of the tangential derivative defines the extrinsic curvature K_ab = -B B nabla n, which is tangential and symmetric, and the full split nabla_Y X = D_Y X + K(X,Y) n follows.

**Concepts:** induced covariant derivative, Levi-Civita connection uniqueness, extrinsic curvature, symmetry of the extrinsic curvature, Gauss formula for covariant derivatives

**Key moves**
- Show D_e h_ab = 0 using B n = 0.
- Invoke uniqueness of the torsion-free metric-compatible connection.
- Use n.X = 0 to move the derivative from X onto n, defining K.
- Split Y.nabla X into tangential (D) and normal (K) parts, eq. (14.21).

### §14.4 The Gauss-Codazzi equations — p.243 (pdf 258)

States (proofs deferred) the Gauss equation, which gives the fully tangential projection of the Riemann tensor as 3D curvature plus quadratic K terms, and the Codazzi equation, which gives the three-tangential-one-normal projection as antisymmetrized D K. Together these give every Riemann component except R_{alpha 0 beta 0}, which needs normal derivatives of n and hence a foliation. Contracting Gauss twice yields G_ab n^a n^b in terms of (3)R and K (the Hamiltonian constraint); contracting Codazzi yields the mixed projection of G (the momentum constraint). Both involve only the slice data and their tangential derivatives, so they constrain initial data.

**Concepts:** Gauss equation, Codazzi equation, Hamiltonian constraint, momentum constraint, constraint equations

**Key moves**
- Use g^{ac} = h^{ac} + n^a n^c to contract the Gauss equation.
- Contract a second time and match against the normal-normal Einstein component, which is the normal-normal Ricci component minus half the scalar curvature.
- Contract Codazzi on a and c to reach B n G.
- Remark on whether the contractions can be done with gamma instead of h (see gaps: this remark is not correct for the (3)R term).

### §14.5 Calculating the Gauss equation — p.245 (pdf 260)

Start from the 3D Ricci identity for a tangent field Z, expand D_a D_b Z by writing each D as projected nabla, and differentiate the projectors themselves. Derivatives of B produce n times K, so after antisymmetrizing in a and b one term drops out and the rest leave the projected 4D commutator plus two K K products. Replacing Z by the projection of an arbitrary field and stripping it gives the Gauss equation.

**Concepts:** Ricci identity (commutator of covariant derivatives), Gauss equation, projection operator onto a hypersurface, extrinsic curvature

**Key moves**
- Differentiate the projector: only its n n part contributes, giving two terms each carrying one derivative of the normal (14.37).
- Show the projected derivative of B equals n^d K_ab (14.38) and n_c K_a^c (14.39).
- Use n.Z = 0 to trade n_e nabla Z^e for Z^e K_eb (14.41).
- Antisymmetrize and strip the arbitrary vector.

### §14.6 Calculating the Codazzi equation — p.246 (pdf 261)

Differentiate K_ab = -B B nabla n with the projected derivative, expand the product rule over the projectors, observe that the two projector-derivative terms are symmetric under a<->c and cancel on antisymmetrization, and recognize the remaining commutator of 4D derivatives acting on n as the Riemann tensor contracted with n.

**Concepts:** Codazzi equation, Ricci identity (commutator of covariant derivatives), extrinsic curvature

**Key moves**
- Apply the definition (14.13) of D to K.
- Cancel terms symmetric in the antisymmetrized pair.
- Replace the commutator acting on n_k by Riemann contracted with n.

### §14.7 The geometry of foliations — p.247 (pdf 262)

Defines a Cauchy surface (met exactly once by every endless causal curve) and global hyperbolicity, whose key consequence is the product topology M = Sigma x R. A foliation is the family of level sets of a scalar with nowhere-vanishing gradient, filling M without intersection. With a family of spacelike slices the normal can be differentiated along itself, giving the acceleration A = nabla_n n of normal observers, which is orthogonal to n. The Ricci equation, supplying the last curvature projection n R n, is stated in terms of L_n K, D A, A A and K K.

**Concepts:** Cauchy surface, global hyperbolicity, foliation, time function, acceleration of normal observers, Ricci equation

**Key moves**
- Motivate foliations from the topology theorem for globally hyperbolic spacetimes.
- Differentiate n.n = 1 to show A is spatial.
- Identify A as the new datum needed for R_{alpha 0 beta 0}.

### §14.8 Derivation of the Ricci equation — p.248 (pdf 263)

With A in hand, the full gradient of the normal is nabla_a n_b = -K_ab + n_a A_b. Expanding L_n K with this decomposition, rewriting n.nabla K through second derivatives of n, and splitting nabla A into D A plus normal pieces, all normal-index terms cancel and the commutator of derivatives on n becomes Riemann contracted twice with n. The result is the Ricci equation, a purely tangential tensor identity.

**Concepts:** decomposition of the gradient of the normal, Lie derivative, Ricci equation, acceleration of normal observers

**Key moves**
- Establish nabla_a n_b = -K_ab + n_a A_b (14.54).
- Write L_n K with the torsion-free formula (14.55).
- Combine (14.57)-(14.59) and cancel the n_a A^c K terms.
- Use symmetries of Riemann to show n n R is tangential.

### §14.9 The lapse function — p.249 (pdf 264)

When the gradient of the scalar is timelike the scalar is a time function t; the normal is N times the raised gradient, and the lapse N = 1/sqrt(g^{ab} t_a t_b) is positive. The Eulerian observer (worldline along n) regards each slice as simultaneous. Stepping a proper time d tau along n moves to the slice t + d tau/N, so N = d tau/dt. The normal Lie derivative of h equals -2K, giving K = -(1/2) L_n h and, in coordinates carried by Eulerian observers, gamma-dot = 2 N K. The acceleration is minus the spatial gradient of ln N, which simplifies the Ricci equation; combined with the contracted Gauss equation it becomes an evolution equation for K.

**Concepts:** time function, lapse function, Eulerian observer, extrinsic curvature as rate of change of the induced metric, acceleration of normal observers, evolution equation for the extrinsic curvature

**Key moves**
- Taylor-expand t along the normal to get N = d tau/dt (14.67).
- Compute L_n g and L_n(n n) to get L_n h = -2K (14.71).
- Use n = (1/N) partial_t in Eulerian-adapted coordinates for gamma-dot = 2NK.
- Show D A - A A = -(1/N) D D N and substitute into the Ricci equation.
- Swap n n Riemann for projected Ricci via the contracted Gauss equation (14.77).

### §14.10 The 3+1 decomposition of the metric — p.252 (pdf 267)

Normal observers are too restrictive, so any congruence of curves transverse to the slices (a fibration) is allowed. Its t-parametrized tangent T, the rigging vector, splits into a normal part (the lapse N, fixed by the foliation) and a tangential part, the shift beta. Coordinates constant along the threading curves make T = partial_t. Substituting into g^{ab} = h^{ab} + n n gives the block form of the inverse metric, inversion gives the covariant metric, and hence the 3+1 line element. The inverse metric's spatial block is -gamma^{mu nu} only when the shift vanishes.

**Concepts:** fibration, rigging vector, shift vector, lapse function, 3+1 decomposition of the metric

**Key moves**
- Split T = N n + beta with N = T.n, beta = B T (14.78).
- Express n = (T - beta)/N and substitute into the inverse metric (14.82).
- Use T^a *= delta^a_0 to read off components (14.87).
- Invert with the determinant relation det gamma^{mu nu} = -N^2 det g^{ab} (14.89)-(14.90).

### §14.11 The 3+1 decomposition of the vacuum Einstein equations — p.253 (pdf 268)

Recasts GR as the time history of a 3-geometry: lapse and shift (four functions) are freely chosen kinematical data, the six components of gamma are dynamical. G_ab = 0 is projected normal-normal (Hamiltonian constraint), normal-tangential (momentum constraint), and tangential-tangential (conveniently using R_ab = 0). The Ricci-equation result gives L_n K; L_n h = -2K gives the metric's evolution. Converting to L_T (partial_t in adapted coordinates) splits each Lie derivative into an N-scaled normal part and a shift Lie derivative expressed with D, yielding first-order PDEs for gamma_{mu nu} and K_{mu nu}. The Cauchy procedure, preservation of constraints by the Bianchi identities, the history from Darmois to ADM (with p^{ab}), and the difficulty of existence proofs for non-analytic data close the section.

**Concepts:** constraint equations, Hamiltonian constraint, momentum constraint, ADM evolution equations, gauge freedom in the 3+1 split, constraint propagation, ADM formalism, canonical momentum conjugate to the 3-metric, well-posed initial value problem

**Key moves**
- Project Einstein's equations into three pieces, using R_ab for the spatial projection.
- Show L_{Nn} h = N L_n h because n.h = 0 kills the gradient-of-N terms.
- Expand L_beta with D because beta is tangent and D h = 0.
- Solve (14.102) for K to get (14.103), making the system second order in gamma.
- Stress that N and beta have no evolution equations and correspond to g_{0a} in Chapter 13.

### §14.12 The 3+1 equations and numerical relativity — p.257 (pdf 272)

A quoted-results survey. Initial data: the York-O Murchadha conformal approach writes gamma = psi^4 gamma-hat, splits K into trace and trace-free parts, scales the trace-free part by psi^-10, decomposes it into a transverse-traceless piece plus a conformal-Killing-type derivative of a vector potential, and turns the constraints into four coupled quasilinear elliptic equations for psi and X. Flat conformal metric, maximal slice and zero TT data decouple and linearize the momentum constraint, enabling analytic black-hole data with chosen momenta and spins. Gauge: lapse choices (geodesic and Lagrangian slicing are singularity seeking; maximal slicing avoids focusing at the cost of an elliptic solve) and shift choices (Eulerian, Lagrangian with grid distortion, isothermal and radial gauges that zero metric components).

**Concepts:** numerical relativity, conformal method for initial data, conformal factor, transverse-traceless decomposition, trace-free extrinsic curvature, maximal slicing, geodesic slicing, Lagrangian slicing, singularity avoidance, shift gauge conditions

**Key moves**
- Choose conformal weights (psi^4 on gamma, psi^-10 on the trace-free K) so the divergence scales simply (14.112).
- Reverse-decompose the TT part so the free data is an arbitrary traceless symmetric T-hat.
- List the three free-data choices that decouple the system.
- Tie lapse choice to singularity avoidance and shift choice to simplicity of the equations.

### §14.13 The 2+2 and characteristic approaches — p.261 (pdf 276)

Two limitations of 3+1 motivate an alternative: the free data are hidden behind constraints, and spacelike slicing fails on null surfaces. A counting argument reduces ten metric components to four data (two configuration and two velocity), i.e. two degrees of freedom, but 3+1 never exhibits them and carries six evolution equations. The 2+2 approach starts from a spacelike 2-surface and two transvecting vector fields that drag it into two families of 3-surfaces; if both are null one gets a double-null foliation, if one is null and one timelike a null-timelike foliation. Advantages: the conformal 2-geometry carries the two degrees of freedom, the free data are unconstrained and obey ODEs along the vector fields, and null and characteristic initial value problems (radiation, null infinity, cosmological past light cones) are handled naturally.

**Concepts:** gravitational degrees of freedom, 2+2 formalism, double null foliation, null-timelike foliation, characteristic initial value problem, conformal 2-structure

**Key moves**
- Run the 10 -> 6 -> 12 -> 8 -> 5 -> 4 -> 2 count.
- Pose the two questions: which are the free data, and why six evolution equations?
- Construct foliations by dragging an initial 2-surface along two vector fields.
- List the physical arenas where null data are natural.

### §14.14 The 2+2 metric decomposition — p.263 (pdf 278)

Two scalar functions define intersecting hypersurface families whose intersections are spacelike 2-surfaces S(u, v). Their normal covectors give a dual dyad n_A spanning the orthogonal 2-space {T}, which is generally not integrable because the dyad's Lie bracket need not vanish. The Gram matrix N_AB of the dyad is the orthogonal 2-metric in dyad components and generalizes the lapse; projectors onto {S} and {T} are built from the dyad. Connecting vectors E_A = n_A + b_A with arbitrary shifts b_A can always be chosen to commute, giving coordinates (x^0, x^1, x^2, x^3). The contravariant and covariant metrics then take block forms with a 2x2 lapse matrix and two shift vectors, compared explicitly with the 3+1 forms. Extracting the determinant of the 2-metric isolates the conformal 2-structure, which carries the two degrees of freedom; the Bondi-type coordinates of Chapter 23 are previewed.

**Concepts:** 2+2 formalism, integrable distribution, vector dyad, lapse matrix, 2+2 shift vectors, 2+2 decomposition of the metric, conformal 2-structure, Bondi-type coordinates

**Key moves**
- Define dual dyads with n^A_a n_B^a = delta^A_B.
- Note [n_0, n_1] != 0 generically, so {T} is not surface-forming.
- Use freedom in b_A to make [E_0, E_1] = 0 and adopt coordinates along E_A.
- Read off block matrices (14.138)-(14.139).
- Divide out the area element to get the conformal 2-structure.

## Concepts

### spacelike hypersurface

*mathematical-object · core* · also: 3-surface, slice, Sigma

A three-dimensional submanifold of spacetime all of whose tangent vectors are spacelike; equivalently its normal is timelike.

**How introduced:** Taken as the arena on which initial data live, as the geometric replacement for the t = 0 surface of Chapter 13's coordinate treatment.

**Prerequisites:** manifold, metric signature

**Where:** DIV §14.1 p.239 (pdf 254); DIV §14.2 p.240 (pdf 255)

### unit normal to a spacelike hypersurface

*mathematical-object · core* · also: future-pointing unit normal, n^a

The future-directed timelike vector field orthogonal to every tangent vector of the hypersurface, normalized so that n_a n^a = +1 in the (+,-,-,-) signature.

**How introduced:** First object constructed for a hypersurface; later reinterpreted as the 4-velocity of Eulerian observers.

**Prerequisites:** spacelike hypersurface, metric signature

$$
n_a n^a = 1
$$

**Where:** DIV §14.2 p.240 (pdf 255); DIV §14.9 p.249 (pdf 264)

### projection operator onto a hypersurface

*operation · core* · also: spatial projector, B_a^b, first fundamental form (mixed)

The mixed tensor B_a^b = delta_a^b - n_a n^b, which removes the normal component of any vector or covector; it annihilates n and is idempotent.

**How introduced:** Built immediately from the normal and verified to produce tangent vectors; it is then the workhorse for defining every induced object by sandwiching.

**Prerequisites:** unit normal to a spacelike hypersurface, Kronecker delta

$$
B_b{}^a = \delta_b^a - n_b n^a
$$

$$
B_a{}^b n_b = 0
$$

$$
B_a{}^c B_c{}^b = B_a{}^b
$$

**Notes:** In the 2+2 section the same letter B denotes the projector onto the 2-surfaces.

**Where:** DIV §14.2 p.240 (pdf 255); DIV §14.5 p.245 (pdf 260)

### induced metric

*mathematical-object · core* · also: first fundamental form, spatial metric, h_ab, 3-metric

The metric that the spacetime metric induces on the hypersurface, h_ab = B_a^c B_b^d g_cd = g_ab - n_a n_b; it agrees with g on pairs of tangent vectors.

**How introduced:** Obtained by projecting g; in adapted coordinates shown to be simply the spatial block g_{alpha beta}.

**Prerequisites:** projection operator onto a hypersurface, metric tensor

$$
h_{ab} = g_{ab} - n_a n_b
$$

**Where:** DIV §14.2 p.240 (pdf 255); DIV §14.2 p.241 (pdf 256)

### positive definite spatial metric gamma

*convention · developed* · also: gamma_ab, Riemannian 3-metric

In the (+,-,-,-) signature the induced metric is negative definite, so the book defines gamma_ab = -h_ab to work with a positive definite Riemannian metric on the slice.

**How introduced:** Introduced right after noting h_{alpha beta} has signature (-,-,-); the ADM variables are then written in terms of gamma.

**Prerequisites:** induced metric, metric signature

$$
\gamma_{ab} = -h_{ab}
$$

**Notes:** Christoffel symbols and Riemann R^a_{bcd} and Ricci R_ab are unchanged by h -> -h, but the Ricci scalar and any single contraction with the inverse metric change sign; see gaps_and_pitfalls.

**Where:** DIV §14.2 p.241 (pdf 256); DIV §14.10 p.253 (pdf 268)

### adapted coordinates

*technique · developed* · also: coordinates adapted to the hypersurface, starred equality

Coordinates (t, x^1, x^2, x^3) in which the hypersurface (or each leaf) is t = constant and the x^alpha are coordinates on it; results valid only in such charts are marked with a starred equals sign.

**How introduced:** Used after each covariant result to show its component form, e.g. h_{alpha beta} equals g_{alpha beta} and the 4D and 3D Christoffel symbols agree.

**Prerequisites:** spacelike hypersurface

$$
(x^a) = (t, x^\alpha)
$$

**Where:** DIV §14.2 p.240 (pdf 255); DIV §14.3 p.242 (pdf 257); DIV §14.10 p.252 (pdf 267)

### induced covariant derivative

*operation · core* · also: intrinsic covariant derivative, D_a, 3-covariant derivative

For tensor fields tangent to the hypersurface, D_a is the spacetime covariant derivative with every index (including the derivative index) projected into the hypersurface; it depends only on the field on the surface.

**How introduced:** Defined in two steps: tangential derivative (independent of extension) followed by projecting the free index back into the surface, then generalized to arbitrary tangent tensors.

**Prerequisites:** projection operator onto a hypersurface, covariant derivative

$$
D_a X^b := B_d{}^b B_a{}^c \nabla_c X^d
$$

**Where:** DIV §14.3 p.241 (pdf 256); DIV §14.3 p.242 (pdf 257)

### Levi-Civita connection uniqueness

*theorem · introduced* · also: fundamental theorem of Riemannian geometry, metric connection

There is exactly one torsion-free covariant derivative that annihilates a given (pseudo-)metric; its coefficients are the Christoffel symbols of that metric.

**How introduced:** Invoked to conclude that D is the metric derivative of h, after showing D h = 0 and that D is torsion-free (the latter left as Exercise 14.3).

**Prerequisites:** torsion-free connection, metric compatibility

$$
D_e h_{ab} = 0
$$

**Where:** DIV §14.3 p.241 (pdf 256); DIV §14.3 p.242 (pdf 257)

### extrinsic curvature

*mathematical-object · core* · also: second fundamental form, K_ab

The symmetric tangential tensor obtained by projecting both indices of the spacetime gradient of the unit normal into the hypersurface and changing sign; it measures how the unit normal changes as one moves along the hypersurface, i.e. how the hypersurface bends inside spacetime.

**How introduced:** Arises as the normal component of the tangential derivative of a tangent vector field, after moving the derivative onto n using n.X = 0; later re-expressed as minus half the normal Lie derivative of h when a foliation exists.

**Prerequisites:** unit normal to a spacelike hypersurface, projection operator onto a hypersurface, covariant derivative

$$
K_{ab} := -B_a{}^c B_b{}^d \nabla_c n_d
$$

$$
K_{ab} n^b = 0
$$

$$
K_{ab} = -\tfrac12 \mathcal{L}_n h_{ab}
$$

**Notes:** Sign convention: with this definition, n future pointing and signature (+,-,-,-); numerical-relativity texts in (-,+,+,+) use K_ij = -(1/2) L_n gamma_ij, which is the negative of the book's K.

**Where:** DIV §14.3 p.242 (pdf 257); DIV §14.3 p.243 (pdf 258); DIV §14.9 p.251 (pdf 266)

### symmetry of the extrinsic curvature

*theorem · introduced* · also: K_ab = K_ba

The extrinsic curvature of a hypersurface is a symmetric tensor, a consequence of the torsion-free spacetime connection together with the hypersurface-orthogonality of n.

**How introduced:** Asserted in one line from torsion-freeness; a learner should note that it uses the fact that n is normal to a family of surfaces or, for a single surface, that commutators of tangent fields stay tangent (Exercise 14.3(i)).

**Prerequisites:** extrinsic curvature, torsion-free connection, Lie bracket

$$
K_{ab} = K_{ba}
$$

**Where:** DIV §14.3 p.242 (pdf 257); DIV §14.3 p.243 (pdf 258)

### Gauss formula for covariant derivatives

*identity · developed* · also: tangential-normal split of nabla_Y X, Gauss-Weingarten formula

For vector fields tangent to a hypersurface, the spacetime derivative of X along Y equals the intrinsic derivative D_Y X plus K(X,Y) times the unit normal.

**How introduced:** Presented as an alternative, very useful reading of K: the part of the 4D derivative that sticks out of the surface.

**Prerequisites:** induced covariant derivative, extrinsic curvature

$$
\nabla_Y X = D_Y X + K(X,Y)\,\boldsymbol{n}
$$

**Where:** DIV §14.3 p.243 (pdf 258)

### Ricci identity (commutator of covariant derivatives)

*identity · revisited* · also: curvature as commutator

The antisymmetrized second covariant derivative of a vector equals the Riemann tensor acting on it; applied both to D on the slice and to nabla in spacetime.

**How introduced:** Used as the starting point of both the Gauss and Codazzi derivations.

**Prerequisites:** Riemann tensor, covariant derivative

$$
D_a D_b Z^c - D_b D_a Z^c = {}^{(3)}R^c{}_{dab} Z^d
$$

**Where:** DIV §14.5 p.245 (pdf 260); DIV §14.6 p.246 (pdf 261)

### Gauss equation

*identity · core* · also: Gauss theorem egregium generalized, first Gauss-Codazzi equation

The fully tangential projection of the spacetime Riemann tensor equals the intrinsic Riemann tensor of the hypersurface plus terms quadratic in the extrinsic curvature.

**How introduced:** Announced as the first reconstruction result, then proved in §14.5 by expanding the 3D Ricci identity with projected derivatives.

**Prerequisites:** induced metric, extrinsic curvature, Ricci identity (commutator of covariant derivatives), Riemann tensor

$$
B_a{}^e B_b{}^f B_c{}^g B_d{}^h R_{efgh} = {}^{(3)}R_{abcd} + K_{ad}K_{bc} - K_{ac}K_{bd}
$$

**Where:** DIV §14.4 p.243 (pdf 258); DIV §14.5 p.246 (pdf 261)

### Codazzi equation

*identity · core* · also: Codazzi-Mainardi equation, second Gauss-Codazzi equation

The projection of the spacetime Riemann tensor with three indices tangent to the hypersurface and one along the normal equals an antisymmetrized intrinsic derivative of the extrinsic curvature.

**How introduced:** Announced with Gauss, proved in §14.6 by differentiating K and antisymmetrizing.

**Prerequisites:** extrinsic curvature, induced covariant derivative, Riemann tensor

$$
B_a{}^e B_b{}^f B_c{}^g n^h R_{efgh} = D_b K_{ac} - D_a K_{bc}
$$

**Where:** DIV §14.4 p.243 (pdf 258); DIV §14.6 p.246 (pdf 261)

### constraint equations

*law · core* · also: initial value constraints, Einstein constraints

The four components G_ab n^a n^b and B_a^c n^d G_cd of Einstein's equations, which involve only the induced metric, extrinsic curvature and their tangential derivatives, and so restrict which data may be posed on a slice.

**How introduced:** Obtained by contracting the Gauss and Codazzi equations; linked back to the constraints found in coordinates in §13.6.

**Prerequisites:** Gauss equation, Codazzi equation, Einstein tensor

**Where:** DIV §14.4 p.244 (pdf 259); DIV §14.4 p.245 (pdf 260); DIV §14.11 p.254 (pdf 269)

### Hamiltonian constraint

*law · core* · also: scalar constraint, energy constraint, normal-normal constraint

The normal-normal projection of the vacuum Einstein equations, relating the scalar curvature of the slice to quadratic invariants of the extrinsic curvature.

**How introduced:** Derived from the twice-contracted Gauss equation; its name is explained later by the ADM Hamiltonian formulation.

**Prerequisites:** constraint equations, Gauss equation, Ricci scalar

$$
{}^{(3)}R - (K^a{}_a)(K^b{}_b) + K_{ab}K^{ab} = 0
$$

**Notes:** As printed, (3)R and all traces are computed with h. If gamma is used instead, (3)R changes sign and the standard form (3)R + K^2 - K_ab K^ab = 0 results.

**Where:** DIV §14.4 p.244 (pdf 259); DIV §14.11 p.254 (pdf 269); DIV §14.11 p.257 (pdf 272)

### momentum constraint

*law · core* · also: vector constraint, diffeomorphism constraint

The mixed normal-tangential projection of the vacuum Einstein equations, requiring the divergence of the extrinsic curvature to equal the gradient of its trace.

**How introduced:** Derived by contracting the Codazzi equation once; named after its role in ADM's Hamiltonian formulation.

**Prerequisites:** constraint equations, Codazzi equation

$$
D_b K^b{}_a - D_a K^b{}_b = 0
$$

**Where:** DIV §14.4 p.244 (pdf 259); DIV §14.11 p.254 (pdf 269)

### Cauchy surface

*definition · introduced* · also: Cauchy hypersurface

A spacelike hypersurface met exactly once by every endless timelike or null curve.

**How introduced:** Opens the foliation section with a sketch (Fig. 14.1) of worldlines crossing a slice once.

**Prerequisites:** spacelike hypersurface, causal curve

**Where:** DIV §14.7 p.247 (pdf 262)

### global hyperbolicity

*definition · introduced* · also: globally hyperbolic spacetime

The property of possessing a Cauchy surface; it guarantees unique global solutions of the wave equation and implies the manifold is a product of a 3-manifold with the real line.

**How introduced:** Named via the hyperbolic wave equation and used to justify slicing spacetime into level surfaces of a time function.

**Prerequisites:** Cauchy surface

$$
M = \Sigma \times \mathbb{R}
$$

**Notes:** The product-topology result is quoted without proof or attribution (it is Geroch's theorem, later refined by Bernal and Sanchez).

**Where:** DIV §14.7 p.247 (pdf 262)

### foliation

*definition · core* · also: slicing, 3+1 slicing, family of leaves Sigma_t

A decomposition of spacetime into non-intersecting level sets of a smooth scalar with nowhere-vanishing gradient, so each event lies on exactly one leaf.

**How introduced:** Introduced after global hyperbolicity; drawn as a stack of wavy sheets (Fig. 14.2); allows derivatives of the normal off a single slice.

**Prerequisites:** spacelike hypersurface, gradient of a scalar

$$
\Sigma_t = \{x \in M : \phi(x) = t\}
$$

$$
M = \bigcup_t \Sigma_t
$$

**Where:** DIV §14.1 p.239 (pdf 254); DIV §14.7 p.247 (pdf 262)

### time function

*definition · developed* · also: global time, t(x)

A scalar field on spacetime whose gradient is everywhere timelike (future directed after raising), so its level sets are spacelike slices.

**How introduced:** Specialization of the foliation scalar once the leaves are required to be spacelike.

**Prerequisites:** foliation

$$
w^a = g^{ab}\partial_b t

**Where:** DIV §14.7 p.247 (pdf 262); DIV §14.9 p.249 (pdf 264)

### acceleration of normal observers

*physical-quantity · developed* · also: A^a, 4-acceleration of Eulerian observers, geodesic curvature of the normal curves

The vector A^a = n^b nabla_b n^a, the 4-acceleration of observers moving along the unit normal; it is orthogonal to n and vanishes exactly when the normal curves are geodesics.

**How introduced:** Appears as the new object made possible by a foliation, since n can now be differentiated along itself; identified as the missing ingredient for the last curvature projection.

**Prerequisites:** foliation, unit normal to a spacelike hypersurface

A^a = n^b\nabla_b n^a

$$
n_a A^a = 0

A_a = -D_a \ln N

### decomposition of the gradient of the normal

*identity · developed* · also: nabla_a n_b split

For a foliation the full spacetime gradient of the unit normal splits into minus the extrinsic curvature plus the normal times the acceleration.

**How introduced:** Derived (as Exercise 14.7) by inserting delta = B + n n on each index; it is the key substitution in the Ricci-equation and L_n h calculations.

**Prerequisites:** extrinsic curvature, acceleration of normal observers

\nabla_a n_b = -K_{ab} + n_a A_b

### Lie derivative

*operation · revisited* · also: L_n, L_T

The derivative of a tensor field along the flow of a vector field; with a torsion-free connection it can be written using covariant derivatives with extra terms involving derivatives of the vector.

**How introduced:** Relied on from Chapter 6 to express rates of change of h and K along the normal and along the rigging vector; L_T becomes partial_t in adapted coordinates.

**Prerequisites:** covariant derivative, vector field

\mathcal{L}_n K_{ab} = n^c\nabla_c K_{ab} + (\nabla_a n^c)K_{cb} + (\nabla_b n^c)K_{ac}

### Ricci equation

*identity · core* · also: third Gauss-Codazzi-Ricci equation, normal-normal curvature projection

The projection of the spacetime Riemann tensor with two indices along the normal equals the normal Lie derivative of the extrinsic curvature plus acceleration and K-squared terms; it is the only curvature relation that requires a foliation.

**How introduced:** Stated at the end of §14.7 as the answer to the missing R_{alpha 0 beta 0} component, derived in §14.8, simplified with the lapse in §14.9.

**Prerequisites:** extrinsic curvature, acceleration of normal observers, Lie derivative, foliation

$$
B_a{}^c n^d B_b{}^e n^f R_{cdef} = \mathcal{L}_n K_{ab} + D_a A_b - A_a A_b + K_a{}^c K_{cb}

**Where:** DIV §14.7 p.248 (pdf 263); DIV §14.8 p.249 (pdf 264)

### lapse function

*physical-quantity · core* · also: lapse, N, alpha

The positive scalar relating the unit normal to the raised gradient of the time function; physically, the proper time an Eulerian observer ages per unit of coordinate time between neighbouring leaves.

**How introduced:** Defined from n = N grad t, computed as 1/sqrt(g^{ab} t_a t_b), then interpreted via a small step along the normal (Fig. 14.3).

**Prerequisites:** time function, unit normal to a spacelike hypersurface, proper time

$$
n^a = N g^{ab}\partial_b t
$$

$$
N = \left(g^{ab} t_{,a} t_{,b}\right)^{-1/2}
$$

$$
N = \frac{d\tau}{dt}
$$

**Notes:** The text switches between N and alpha for the lapse in §14.11-14.12.

**Where:** DIV §14.9 p.249 (pdf 264); DIV §14.9 p.250 (pdf 265); DIV §14.10 p.252 (pdf 267)

### Eulerian observer

*definition · developed* · also: normal observer, hypersurface-orthogonal observer

An observer whose worldline is an integral curve of the unit normal to the foliation, so that each leaf is a surface of simultaneity for them.

**How introduced:** Introduced to give n a physical meaning and to interpret the lapse and acceleration.

### extrinsic curvature as rate of change of the induced metric

*identity · core* · also: K = -1/2 L_n h, velocity of the 3-metric

For a foliation, the extrinsic curvature equals minus one half of the Lie derivative of the induced metric along the unit normal, so in coordinates following Eulerian observers gamma-dot = 2 N K.

**How introduced:** Derived by expanding L_n(g - n n) with nabla g = 0 and the gradient decomposition; the book notes numerical relativists often take this as the definition, but it needs a foliation whereas the §14.3 definition needs only one surface.

**Prerequisites:** extrinsic curvature, Lie derivative, foliation, lapse function

\mathcal{L}_n h_{ab} = -2K_{ab}

\dot\gamma_{\alpha\beta} \overset{*}{=} 2N K_{\alpha\beta}

### evolution equation for the extrinsic curvature

*law · core* · also: K propagation equation, Ricci equation in evolution form

An equation giving the normal Lie derivative of K in terms of the projected spacetime Ricci tensor, the slice's Ricci tensor, K-quadratic terms, and the Hessian of the lapse divided by the lapse.

**How introduced:** Obtained by substituting A = -D ln N into the Ricci equation and eliminating n n Riemann with the contracted Gauss equation.

**Prerequisites:** Ricci equation, lapse function, Gauss equation

$$
\mathcal{L}_n K_{ab} = B_a{}^c B_b{}^d R_{cd} - {}^{(3)}R_{ab} + K_c{}^c K_{ab} + \tfrac{1}{N}D_a D_b N - 2K_{ac}K_b{}^c

### fibration

*definition · developed* · also: threading, congruence of threading curves, time lines

A congruence of curves, nowhere tangent to the leaves, with exactly one curve through each event; used to identify points on different leaves.

**How introduced:** Introduced because using only normal observers to build coordinates is physically and mathematically restrictive.

**Prerequisites:** foliation, congruence of curves

### rigging vector

*mathematical-object · developed* · also: time vector T^a, evolution vector, partial_t

The tangent vector T^a to the fibration curves parametrized by the time function, normalized so that moving a leaf by dt along T lands on the leaf t + dt; in adapted coordinates T = partial_t.

**How introduced:** Defined from the fibration and immediately decomposed into normal and tangential parts (Fig. 14.4).

T^a = N n^a + \beta^a

T^a \overset{*}{=} \delta^a_0

### shift vector

*physical-quantity · core* · also: shift, beta^a

The part of the rigging vector tangent to the leaves; it measures how far the threading curve drifts sideways within the slice, relative to the normal, per unit coordinate time.

**How introduced:** Obtained by projecting T into the slice; presented with the lapse as the four freely specifiable gauge functions.

### 3+1 decomposition of the metric

*identity · core* · also: ADM metric, lapse-shift form of the line element

In coordinates adapted to a foliation and fibration, the spacetime metric is determined by the lapse, the shift and the spatial metric; the inverse metric and line element take block forms built from N, beta and gamma.

**How introduced:** Derived from g^{ab} = h^{ab} + n^a n^b by substituting n = (T - beta)/N, then inverted.

**Prerequisites:** lapse function, shift vector, induced metric, adapted coordinates

$$
g^{ab} = \frac{1}{N^2}(T^a - \beta^a)(T^b - \beta^b) + h^{ab}
$$

$$
ds^2 = (N^2 - \beta^\sigma\beta_\sigma)dt^2 - 2\beta_\mu dt\,dx^\mu - \gamma_{\mu\nu}dx^\mu dx^\nu
$$

**Notes:** The printed book has +beta^sigma beta_sigma in g_00 and -beta^mu/N in g^{0mu}; see gaps for why these appear to be misprints.

**Where:** DIV §14.10 p.252 (pdf 267); DIV §14.10 p.253 (pdf 268)

### ADM evolution equations

*law · core* · also: 3+1 evolution equations, York-ADM equations

The first-order-in-time PDE system giving partial_t of the spatial metric in terms of lapse, extrinsic curvature and shift derivatives, and partial_t of the extrinsic curvature in terms of the Hessian of the lapse, shift Lie-derivative terms, the slice's Ricci tensor and K-quadratic terms.

**How introduced:** Obtained by replacing L_n by L_T = L_{Nn} + L_beta, using n.h = 0 and n.K = 0 to drop gradient-of-N terms and D to expand shift terms.

**Prerequisites:** Lie derivative, lapse function, shift vector, evolution equation for the extrinsic curvature, extrinsic curvature as rate of change of the induced metric

$$
\partial_t \gamma_{\mu\nu} \overset{*}{=} 2NK_{\mu\nu} - D_\mu\beta_\nu - D_\nu\beta_\mu
$$

$$
\partial_t K_{\mu\nu} \overset{*}{=} D_\mu D_\nu N + \mathcal{L}_\beta K_{\mu\nu} - N({}^{(3)}R_{\mu\nu} + 2K_{\mu\rho}K^\rho{}_\nu - K^\rho{}_\rho K_{\mu\nu})
$$

**Notes:** Signs of the shift terms and K-quadratic terms depend on whether indices are moved with h or gamma; the book is not consistent on this.

**Where:** DIV §14.11 p.255 (pdf 270); DIV §14.11 p.256 (pdf 271)

### maximal slicing

*technique · developed* · also: K = 0 slicing, maximal hypersurface

A slicing in which the trace of the extrinsic curvature vanishes on every leaf, enforced by solving an elliptic equation for the lapse; it prevents focusing of normal observers and so avoids singularities.

**How introduced:** Listed as the singularity-avoiding lapse choice, with its elliptic lapse equation; also used as a simplifying choice for initial data.

**Prerequisites:** lapse function, extrinsic curvature, gauge freedom in the 3+1 split

$$
K = K^\alpha{}_\alpha = 0,\quad \partial_t K = 0
$$

**Where:** DIV §14.12 p.259 (pdf 274); DIV §14.12 p.260 (pdf 275)

### geodesic slicing

*technique · introduced* · also: Gaussian normal coordinates, N = 1, beta = 0

The gauge with unit lapse and zero shift, so coordinate observers are freely falling normal observers and slices are geodesically parallel; it tends to run into singularities.

**How introduced:** First item in the lapse menu; also historically Darmois's 1927 case.

**Prerequisites:** lapse function, shift vector, Eulerian observer

$$
N = 1,\ \beta^a = 0
$$

**Notes:** The book puts 'Gaussian normal coordinates' in bold when it describes Darmois's 1927 case (p. 257): unit lapse, zero shift, slices labelled by the proper time of freely falling Eulerian observers.

**Where:** DIV §14.11 p.257 (pdf 272); DIV §14.12 p.260 (pdf 275)

### Lagrangian slicing

*technique · mention* · also: comoving slicing

In spherical symmetry, choosing the lapse so that fluid worldlines are orthogonal to the slices and the zero-shift coordinates move with the matter; fluid worldlines focus toward singularities, so it is singularity seeking.

**How introduced:** Second lapse option, with the condition N U^0 = 1.

**Prerequisites:** lapse function, perfect fluid 4-velocity

$$
NU^0 = 1,\ \beta^\mu = 0
$$

**Where:** DIV §14.12 p.260 (pdf 275)

### singularity avoidance

*principle · introduced* · also: singularity-seeking versus singularity-avoiding slicings

The practical requirement that the lapse slow down evolution in regions approaching a singularity so that the numerical slices do not hit it.

**How introduced:** The first motivation offered for choosing gauge conditions, used to classify slicings.

**Prerequisites:** lapse function, maximal slicing

**Where:** DIV §14.12 p.260 (pdf 275)

### shift gauge conditions

*technique · introduced* · also: Eulerian gauge, Lagrangian gauge, isothermal gauge, radial gauge

Rules fixing the shift: zero shift (Eulerian); shift equal to the fluid 3-velocity so the grid follows matter (Lagrangian, which distorts under vorticity); or shifts chosen to zero selected components of the 3-metric (isothermal, radial).

**How introduced:** Listed after lapse choices, each with a pro and con.

**Prerequisites:** shift vector, gauge freedom in the 3+1 split

$$
\beta^\mu = U^\mu/U^0
$$

$$
d\sigma^2 = A^2dr^2 + r^2B^{-2}d\theta^2 + r^2B^2(\sin\theta\,d\phi + \xi\,d\theta)^2
$$

$$
d\sigma^2 = \gamma_{xx}dx^2 + \gamma_{yy}dy^2 + \gamma_{zz}dz^2
$$

$$
\text{isothermal: } \gamma_{r\theta} = 0,\ \gamma^{rr} = \gamma^{\theta\theta}
$$

$$
\text{radial: } \gamma_{r\theta} = \gamma_{r\phi} = 0,\ \gamma_{\theta\theta}\gamma_{\phi\phi} - \gamma_{\theta\phi}^2 = r^4\sin^2\theta
$$

**Notes:** Three shift conditions can remove three 3-metric components, e.g. making the spatial line element diagonal (14.124).

**Where:** DIV §14.12 p.260 (pdf 275); DIV §14.12 p.261 (pdf 276)

### gravitational degrees of freedom

*principle · core* · also: two polarizations, true dynamical data

The vacuum gravitational field carries two degrees of freedom per spatial point, corresponding to four freely specifiable initial data (two configuration variables and two velocities) once constraints and all coordinate freedoms are removed.

**How introduced:** Established by an explicit subtraction count within 3+1, then used to criticize 3+1 for not exhibiting these data geometrically.

**Prerequisites:** constraint equations, gauge freedom in the 3+1 split

**Where:** DIV §14.13 p.261 (pdf 276); DIV §14.13 p.262 (pdf 277)

### characteristic initial value problem

*definition · introduced* · also: null initial value problem, characteristic data

An initial value problem in which data are posed on null hypersurfaces rather than on a spacelike Cauchy surface.

**How introduced:** Presented as the setting where 3+1 fails and 2+2 excels, with physical motivations (radiation along null directions, null infinity, past light cone in cosmology).

**Prerequisites:** null hypersurface, Cauchy surface

**Where:** DIV §14.13 p.262 (pdf 277); DIV p.268 (pdf 283)

### 2+2 formalism

*technique · introduced* · also: double foliation formalism, 2+(1+1) formalism

A covariant splitting of spacetime into a two-parameter family of spacelike 2-surfaces and their orthogonal 2-spaces, using two families of hypersurfaces, projection operators and Lie derivatives along two vector fields.

**How introduced:** Motivated by the two limitations of 3+1, constructed by dragging an initial 2-surface along two transvecting vector fields, attributed to d'Inverno and Smallwood (1980).

**Prerequisites:** foliation, projection operator onto a hypersurface, Lie derivative

**Where:** DIV §14.1 p.239 (pdf 254); DIV §14.13 p.262 (pdf 277); DIV §14.14 p.263 (pdf 278)

### double null foliation

*definition · introduced* · also: double-null slicing

A 2+2 foliation in which both vector fields dragging the initial 2-surface are null, producing two families of null hypersurfaces.

**How introduced:** Illustrated by two ruled sheets emanating from an initial 2-surface (Fig. 14.6).

**Prerequisites:** 2+2 formalism, null hypersurface

**Where:** DIV §14.13 p.262 (pdf 277)

### null-timelike foliation

*definition · introduced* · also: null-timelike slicing

A 2+2 foliation with one null and one timelike dragging vector field, producing null cones threaded by a timelike tube.

**How introduced:** Illustrated by nested cones around a central cylinder (Fig. 14.7).

**Prerequisites:** 2+2 formalism, null hypersurface

**Where:** DIV §14.13 p.262 (pdf 277)

### integrable distribution

*theorem · introduced* · also: surface-forming, holonomic, Frobenius condition

A field of 2-planes is tangent to a family of 2-surfaces exactly when the Lie bracket of any two fields spanning it stays in the span; for the dyad here the book uses vanishing bracket as the criterion.

**How introduced:** Used to show the orthogonal 2-spaces {T} are generally not tangent to surfaces, because [n_0, n_1] is generically non-zero.

**Prerequisites:** Lie bracket, vector dyad

$$
[\boldsymbol n_0, \boldsymbol n_1] \neq 0
$$

**Where:** DIV §14.14 p.263 (pdf 278); DIV §14.14 p.264 (pdf 279)

### vector dyad

*mathematical-object · introduced* · also: dual dyad n_A^a, n^A_a

A pair of vectors n_A spanning the 2-space orthogonal to the 2-surfaces, dual to the gradients of the two foliation scalars.

**How introduced:** Constructed from the normals to the two hypersurface families.

**Prerequisites:** 2+2 formalism

$$
n^a_A n^B_a = \delta^B_A
$$

$$
n^A_a = \partial_a\phi^A
$$

**Where:** DIV §14.14 p.263 (pdf 278)

### lapse matrix

*mathematical-object · introduced* · also: N_AB, 2x2 lapse

The Gram matrix of the dyad, equal to the dyad components of the metric on the orthogonal 2-space; its diagonal entries play the role of lapses for the two foliations.

**How introduced:** Defined from the dyad and shown to coincide with h_AB, then compared with the scalar lapse of 3+1.

**Prerequisites:** vector dyad, lapse function

$$
N_{AB} = g_{ab}n^a_A n^b_B
$$

**Where:** DIV §14.14 p.264 (pdf 279); DIV §14.14 p.265 (pdf 280)

### 2+2 shift vectors

*mathematical-object · introduced* · also: b_A^a, connecting vectors E_A

Two vectors tangent to the 2-surfaces that, added to the dyad, give commuting connecting vectors E_A between neighbouring 2-surfaces; they generalize the 3+1 shift.

**How introduced:** Defined as the freedom left after fixing duality with the normals; chosen so that [E_0, E_1] = 0 to allow coordinates.

**Prerequisites:** vector dyad, shift vector, Lie bracket

$$
E^a_A = n^a_A + b^a_A
$$

$$
[E_0, E_1] = 0
$$

**Where:** DIV §14.14 p.264 (pdf 279); DIV §14.14 p.265 (pdf 280)

### 2+2 decomposition of the metric

*identity · developed* · also: 2+2 line element

In coordinates with x^0, x^1 given by the two foliation scalars and x^2, x^3 constant along the connecting vectors, the metric has a block form built from the lapse matrix, two shift vectors and the 2-metric of the surfaces.

**How introduced:** Worked out explicitly so it can be compared with the 3+1 block forms.

**Prerequisites:** lapse matrix, 2+2 shift vectors, 3+1 decomposition of the metric

$$
g_{ab} = \begin{pmatrix} N_{AB} + {}^2g_{ij}b^i_A b^j_B & {}^2g_{ij}b^j_A \\ {}^2g_{ij}b^j_B & {}^2g_{ij}\end{pmatrix}
$$

**Where:** DIV §14.14 p.265 (pdf 280)

### conformal 2-structure

*mathematical-object · introduced* · also: conformal 2-geometry, unimodular 2-metric

The metric of the spacelike 2-surfaces with its overall scale (area element) divided out, leaving two functions that are claimed to carry the two gravitational degrees of freedom.

**How introduced:** Introduced at the end of §14.14, with attribution to d'Inverno and Stachel (1978) and the remark that its Euler-Lagrange equations reproduce the two dynamical Einstein equations.

**Prerequisites:** 2+2 decomposition of the metric, gravitational degrees of freedom

$$
\gamma = |{}^2g_{ij}|
$$

**Where:** DIV §14.13 p.262 (pdf 277); DIV §14.14 p.265 (pdf 280); DIV §14.14 p.266 (pdf 281)

### Bondi-type coordinates

*convention · mention* · also: radiation coordinates, null cone coordinates

Coordinates built on outgoing null cones from a timelike geodesic: a retarded time labelling the cones, a radial coordinate along generators, and two angles.

**How introduced:** Only previewed in Fig. 14.9 as an application of 2+2 to be developed in Chapter 23.

**Prerequisites:** null-timelike foliation, characteristic initial value problem

**Where:** DIV §14.14 p.266 (pdf 281)

## Key equations

### (14.1) Normalization of the normal · supporting · DIV §14.2 p.240 (pdf 255)

$$
n_a n^a = 1
$$

The normal to a spacelike hypersurface is a unit timelike vector; positive norm because the signature is (+,-,-,-).

**Symbols:** n^a future-pointing unit normal

### (14.2) Spatial projection operator · central · DIV §14.2 p.240 (pdf 255)

$$
B_b{}^a = \delta_b^a - n_b n^a
$$

Removes the component along n, mapping any vector into the hypersurface.

**Symbols:** B projector; delta Kronecker delta

### (14.3) · supporting · DIV §14.2 p.240 (pdf 255)

$$
B_a{}^b n_b = 0
$$

The projector annihilates the normal, which makes projected objects tangent.

### (14.4) Idempotence · supporting · DIV §14.2 p.240 (pdf 255)

$$
B_a{}^c B_c{}^b = B_a{}^b
$$

Projecting twice is the same as projecting once.

### (14.5)-(14.6) Induced metric · central · DIV §14.2 p.240 (pdf 255)

$$
h_{ab} := B_a{}^c B_b{}^d g_{cd} = g_{ab} - n_a n_b
$$

The induced metric is the projected spacetime metric, equivalently g with the normal-normal part removed.

**Symbols:** h induced metric; g spacetime metric

### (14.9) · supporting · DIV §14.2 p.241 (pdf 256)

$$
h_{\alpha\beta}\bar X^\alpha \bar Y^\beta \overset{*}{=} g_{\alpha\beta}\bar X^\alpha\bar Y^\beta
$$

In adapted coordinates the induced metric's components are the spatial block of g.

**Symbols:** *= holds in adapted coordinates; Greek indices 1-3

### (14.10) Positive definite 3-metric · central · DIV §14.2 p.241 (pdf 256)

$$
\gamma_{ab} = -h_{ab}
$$

Sign flip giving a Riemannian metric on the slice in the (+,-,-,-) signature.

### (14.12) Induced covariant derivative · central · DIV §14.3 p.241 (pdf 256)

$$
D_a X^b := B_d{}^b B_a{}^c \nabla_c X^d
$$

Tangential derivative with the result projected back into the hypersurface.

**Symbols:** D induced derivative; nabla spacetime derivative

### (14.13) · supporting · DIV §14.3 p.241 (pdf 256)

$$
D_e T^{a_1\cdots a_k}{}_{b_1\cdots b_\ell} := B_{c_1}{}^{a_1}\cdots B_{c_k}{}^{a_k} B_{b_1}{}^{d_1}\cdots B_{b_\ell}{}^{d_\ell} B_e{}^f \nabla_f T^{c_1\cdots c_k}{}_{d_1\cdots d_\ell}
$$

General tensor version: project every index, including the derivative index.

### Metric compatibility of D · derivation-step · DIV §14.3 p.241 (pdf 256)

$$
D_e h_{ab} = 0
$$

Follows from nabla g = 0 and B n = 0; with torsion-freeness it identifies D as the Levi-Civita derivative of h.

### (14.16) · supporting · DIV §14.3 p.242 (pdf 257)

$$
\Gamma^\beta_{\alpha\gamma} \overset{*}{=} {}^{(3)}\Gamma^\beta_{\alpha\gamma}
$$

In adapted coordinates the purely spatial 4D Christoffel symbols coincide with those of the 3-metric (and of gamma, since Christoffel symbols are unchanged by an overall sign of the metric).

### (14.18) Extrinsic curvature · central · DIV §14.3 p.242 (pdf 257)

$$
K_{ab} := -B_a{}^c B_b{}^d \nabla_c n_d
$$

Tangential rate of change of the unit normal along the hypersurface; measures bending of the slice in spacetime.

**Symbols:** K extrinsic curvature

### (14.19)-(14.20) · supporting · DIV §14.3 p.243 (pdf 258)

$$
K_{ab}n^a = K_{ab}n^b = 0,\qquad K_{ab} = K_{ba}
$$

K is a symmetric tensor living in the hypersurface.

### (14.21)-(14.22) Gauss formula · central · DIV §14.3 p.243 (pdf 258)

$$
Y^b\nabla_b X^a = Y^b D_b X^a + (K_{cd}X^cY^d)\,n^a \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \nabla_Y X = D_Y X + K(X,Y)\,\boldsymbol n
$$

Spacetime derivative of tangent fields = intrinsic derivative + extrinsic curvature times the normal.

### (14.23) Gauss equation · central · DIV §14.4 p.243 (pdf 258)

$$
B_a{}^e B_b{}^f B_c{}^g B_d{}^h R_{efgh} = {}^{(3)}R_{abcd} + K_{ad}K_{bc} - K_{ac}K_{bd}
$$

Fully tangential spacetime curvature equals intrinsic curvature plus quadratic extrinsic terms.

**Symbols:** (3)R intrinsic Riemann of h

### (14.25) Codazzi equation · central · DIV §14.4 p.243 (pdf 258)

$$
B_a{}^e B_b{}^f B_c{}^g n^h R_{efgh} = D_b K_{ac} - D_a K_{bc}
$$

Curvature with one normal index equals an antisymmetrized intrinsic derivative of K.

### (14.26) · supporting · DIV §14.4 p.243 (pdf 258)

$$
n^d R_{\alpha\beta\gamma d} \overset{*}{=} D_\beta K_{\alpha\gamma} - D_\alpha K_{\beta\gamma}
$$

Codazzi in adapted coordinates.

### (14.27) Once-contracted Gauss equation · derivation-step · DIV §14.4 p.244 (pdf 259)

$$
B_b{}^f B_d{}^h R_{fh} - n^e n^g B_b{}^f B_d{}^h R_{efgh} = {}^{(3)}R_{bd} - K^a{}_a K_{bd} + K^a{}_d K_{ba}
$$

Contraction with g^{ac} = h^{ac} + n^a n^c; later used to trade n n Riemann for projected Ricci.

### (14.28) · derivation-step · DIV §14.4 p.244 (pdf 259)

$$
R - 2n^b n^d R_{bd} = {}^{(3)}R - K^a{}_a K^b{}_b + K^{ab}K_{ab}
$$

Twice-contracted Gauss equation (Exercise 14.6).

### (14.29) · central · DIV §14.4 p.244 (pdf 259)

$$
G_{ab}n^a n^b = R_{ab}n^a n^b - \tfrac12 R = -\tfrac12\left({}^{(3)}R - K^a{}_a K^b{}_b + K_{ab}K^{ab}\right)
$$

The normal-normal Einstein component expressed entirely in slice data.

### (14.30) Hamiltonian constraint (vacuum) · central · DIV §14.4 p.244 (pdf 259)

$$
{}^{(3)}R - (K^a{}_a)(K^b{}_b) + K_{ab}K^{ab} = 0
$$

Vacuum constraint on (h, K) from G_ab n^a n^b = 0. Contractions here use h; with gamma the (3)R term flips sign giving the familiar (3)R + K^2 - K_ab K^ab = 0.

### (14.32) · derivation-step · DIV §14.4 p.244 (pdf 259)

$$
B_b{}^c n^d R_{cd} = D_b K^a{}_a - D_a K^a{}_b
$$

Contracted Codazzi equation.

### (14.34) Momentum constraint (vacuum) · central · DIV §14.4 p.244 (pdf 259)

$$
D_b K^b{}_a - D_a K^b{}_b = 0
$$

Vacuum constraint from B_a^c n^d G_cd = 0.

### (14.36) 3D Ricci identity · derivation-step · DIV §14.5 p.245 (pdf 260)

$$
D_aD_bZ^c - D_bD_aZ^c = {}^{(3)}R^c{}_{dab}Z^d
$$

Starting point of the Gauss derivation; also fixes the book's Riemann index convention.

### (14.37)-(14.38) Derivative of the projector · derivation-step · DIV §14.5 p.245 (pdf 260)

$$
\nabla_f B_g{}^d = -n_g\nabla_f n^d - n^d\nabla_f n_g,\qquad B_a{}^f B_b{}^g\nabla_f B_g{}^d = n^d K_{ab}
$$

Differentiating the projector generates the normal times K; the key algebraic lemma of §14.5.

### (14.41) · derivation-step · DIV §14.5 p.245 (pdf 260)

$$
B_b{}^d n_e\nabla_d Z^e = -Z^e B_b{}^d\nabla_d n_e = Z^e K_{eb}
$$

Tangency of Z moves the derivative onto the normal.

### (14.44) Gauss equation (derived form) · supporting · DIV §14.5 p.246 (pdf 261)

$$
{}^{(3)}R_{abcd} = B_a{}^e B_b{}^f B_c{}^g B_d{}^h R_{efgh} + K_{ac}K_{bd} - K_{ad}K_{bc}
$$

Same content as (14.23), solved for the intrinsic curvature.

### (14.45) · derivation-step · DIV §14.6 p.246 (pdf 261)

$$
D_aK_{bc} - D_cK_{ba} = B_a{}^j B_b{}^k B_c{}^g(\nabla_g\nabla_j n_k - \nabla_j\nabla_g n_k) = -B_a{}^j B_b{}^k B_c{}^g R^\ell{}_{kgj}n_\ell

Final step of the Codazzi derivation.

### (14.46) Topology of globally hyperbolic spacetimes · supporting · DIV §14.7 p.247 (pdf 262)

M = \Sigma\times\mathbb R

A spacetime with a Cauchy surface is a product of that 3-manifold and a time line.

\Sigma_t = \{x\in M : \phi(x) = t\},\qquad M = \bigcup_{t\in\mathbb R}\Sigma_t

A^a = n^b\nabla_b n^a

Acceleration of the normal congruence; zero iff normals are geodesics.

### (14.52) · supporting · DIV §14.7 p.248 (pdf 263)

n_a A^a = 0

The acceleration is tangent to the leaves.

B_a{}^c n^d B_b{}^e n^f R_{cdef} = \mathcal L_n K_{ab} + D_aA_b - A_aA_b + K_a{}^cK_{cb}

### (14.98)-(14.99) Geometric evolution pair (vacuum) · central · DIV §14.11 p.255 (pdf 270)

$$
\mathcal L_nK_{ab} = -{}^{(3)}R_{ab} + K_c{}^cK_{ab} - 2K_{ac}K^c{}_b + \frac1N D_aD_bN,\qquad \mathcal L_nh_{ab} = -2K_{ab}
$$

Normal evolution of (h, K) once R_ab = 0 is used.

### Lapse factors out of normal Lie derivatives of tangential tensors · derivation-step · DIV §14.11 p.255 (pdf 270)

$$
\mathcal L_{Nn}h_{ab} = N\mathcal L_nh_{ab},\qquad \mathcal L_{Nn}K_{ab} = N\mathcal L_nK_{ab}
$$

The gradient-of-N terms are contracted with n^c h_cb or n^c K_cb and vanish.

### (14.101) · derivation-step · DIV §14.11 p.255 (pdf 270)

$$
\mathcal L_Th_{ab} = -2NK_{ab} + D_a\beta_b + D_b\beta_a
$$

Metric evolution along the rigging vector.

### (14.102)-(14.103) ADM metric evolution · central · DIV §14.11 p.255 (pdf 270)

$$
\frac{\partial\gamma_{\mu\nu}}{\partial t} \overset{*}{=} 2NK_{\mu\nu} - D_\mu\beta_\nu - D_\nu\beta_\mu,\qquad K_{\mu\nu} = \frac1{2N}\left(\frac{\partial\gamma_{\mu\nu}}{\partial t} + D_\mu\beta_\nu + D_\nu\beta_\mu\right)
$$

First evolution equation, solved for K. Transcribed as printed: the shift-term signs are correct if beta_nu is lowered with h (as in 14.101); if lowered with gamma, as §14.10 instructs, they become + in (14.102) and - in (14.103).

### (14.106)-(14.107) ADM curvature evolution · central · DIV §14.11 p.256 (pdf 271)

$$
\frac{\partial K_{\mu\nu}}{\partial t} \overset{*}{=} D_\mu D_\nu N + \beta^\rho D_\rho K_{\mu\nu} + (D_\mu\beta^\rho)K_{\rho\nu} + (D_\nu\beta^\rho)K_{\mu\rho} - N\left({}^{(3)}R_{\mu\nu} + 2K_{\mu\rho}K^\rho{}_\nu - K^\rho{}_\rho K_{\mu\nu}\right)
$$

Second evolution equation: lapse Hessian, shift Lie-derivative (advection) terms, and curvature source. K-quadratic signs as printed correspond to raising with h.

### ADM conjugate momentum · supporting · DIV §14.11 p.257 (pdf 272)

$$
p^{ab} := \sqrt{\gamma}\,(K\gamma^{ab} - K^{ab})
$$

Variable conjugate to gamma_ab in the ADM Hamiltonian formulation; origin of the constraint names.

**Symbols:** gamma determinant of gamma_ab; K trace

### (14.108)-(14.109) Conformal decomposition of the 3-metric · central · DIV §14.12 p.258 (pdf 273)

$$
\gamma_{\mu\nu} = \psi^4\hat\gamma_{\mu\nu},\qquad R = \psi^{-4}\hat R - 8\psi^{-5}\hat D^\mu\hat D_\mu\psi
$$

Conformal factor and the transformation of the scalar curvature that turns the Hamiltonian constraint into an elliptic equation for psi.

**Symbols:** hat: background conformal quantities

### (14.110)-(14.112) · supporting · DIV §14.12 p.258 (pdf 273)

$$
A^{\mu\nu} = K^{\mu\nu} - \tfrac13\gamma^{\mu\nu}K,\qquad A^{\mu\nu} = \psi^{-10}\hat A^{\mu\nu},\qquad D_\nu A^{\mu\nu} = \psi^{-10}\hat D_\nu\hat A^{\mu\nu}
$$

Trace-free part and the conformal weight that makes its divergence scale simply.

### (14.113)-(14.115) TT decomposition · supporting · DIV §14.12 p.258 (pdf 273)

$$
\hat A^{\mu\nu} = \hat A^{\mu\nu}_{TT} + (\hat\ell W)^{\mu\nu},\quad (\hat\ell W)^{\mu\nu} = \hat D^\mu W^\nu + \hat D^\nu W^\mu - \tfrac23\hat\gamma^{\mu\nu}\hat D_\sigma W^\sigma,\quad \hat D_\nu\hat A^{\mu\nu}_{TT} = 0
$$

Split into transverse-traceless and longitudinal parts.

### (14.116) · derivation-step · DIV §14.12 p.259 (pdf 274)

$$
\hat A^{\mu\nu}_{TT} = \hat T^{\mu\nu} - (\hat\ell V)^{\mu\nu},\qquad \hat A^{\mu\nu} = \hat T^{\mu\nu} + (\hat\ell X)^{\mu\nu},\ X = W - V
$$

Reverse decomposition so that the free datum is any traceless symmetric T-hat.

### (14.117)-(14.118) Conformal constraint equations · central · DIV §14.12 p.259 (pdf 274)

$$
\hat\Delta\psi = \tfrac18\hat R\psi + \tfrac1{12}K^2\psi^5 - \tfrac18\left(\hat T^{\mu\nu} + (\hat\ell X)^{\mu\nu}\right)^2\psi^{-7},\qquad \hat D_\nu(\hat\ell X)^{\mu\nu} = -\hat D_\nu\hat T^{\mu\nu} + \tfrac23\psi^6\hat D^\mu K
$$

Four coupled quasilinear elliptic equations for psi and X.

### (14.119)-(14.120) Reconstruction of physical data · supporting · DIV §14.12 p.259 (pdf 274)

$$
\gamma_{\mu\nu} = \psi^4\hat\gamma_{\mu\nu},\qquad K^{\mu\nu} = \left(\hat T^{\mu\nu} + (\hat\ell X)^{\mu\nu}\right)\psi^{-10} + \tfrac13K\psi^{-4}\hat\gamma^{\mu\nu}
$$

Physical initial data satisfying the constraints.

### (14.121)-(14.122) Simplest conformal data · supporting · DIV §14.12 p.259 (pdf 274)

$$
\hat\Delta\psi = -\tfrac18(\hat\ell X)^{\mu\nu}(\hat\ell X)_{\mu\nu}\psi^{-7},\qquad (\hat\Delta_\ell X)^\mu = 0
$$

Flat conformal metric, maximal slice, zero T-hat: momentum constraint decouples and is linear. Corrected coefficient: the book prints -18, but setting R-hat = K = T-hat = 0 in (14.117) gives -1/8.

### (14.123) Maximal slicing condition on the lapse · supporting · DIV §14.12 p.260 (pdf 275)

$$
\gamma^{\mu\nu}D_\mu D_\nu\alpha - \alpha\,{}^{(3)}R = 4\pi(S - 3\rho)
$$

Elliptic equation for the lapse keeping K = 0; as printed. Matter terms rho, S are not defined in this vacuum chapter and a factor alpha appears to be missing on the right (the standard form, derived from K = dK/dt = 0 with the Hamiltonian constraint, has 4 pi alpha (S - 3 rho)).

**Symbols:** alpha lapse; rho energy density; S trace of spatial stress

### (14.125) Radial gauge 3-metric · supporting · DIV §14.12 p.261 (pdf 276)

$$
d\sigma^2 = A^2dr^2 + r^2B^{-2}d\theta^2 + r^2B^2(\sin\theta\,d\phi + \xi\,d\theta)^2
$$

Spatial metric form in the radial gauge, useful for radiation in asymptotically flat spacetimes.

### (14.127) Dual dyad · supporting · DIV §14.14 p.263 (pdf 278)

$$
n^a_A n^B_a = \delta^B_A,\qquad n^A_a = \partial_a\phi^A,\quad A,B = 0,1
$$

Vectors dual to the gradients of the two foliation scalars span the orthogonal 2-space.

### (14.128) Non-integrability · supporting · DIV §14.14 p.264 (pdf 279)

$$
[\boldsymbol n_0,\boldsymbol n_1] \neq 0\ \text{in general}
$$

The orthogonal 2-spaces are not generally surface-forming.

### (14.129) Lapse matrix · central · DIV §14.14 p.264 (pdf 279)

$$
N_{AB} = g_{ab}n^a_An^b_B
$$

Gram matrix of the dyad; generalizes the lapse.

### (14.130)-(14.131); Exercise 14.12(i) Raising dyad indices and the induced 2-metrics · supporting · DIV §14.14 p.264 (pdf 279)

$$
n^a_A = g^{ab}N_{AB}n^B_b,\quad n^A_a = g_{ab}N^{AB}n^b_B,\qquad {}^2g_{ab} = B_{ab},\quad h_{AB} = N_{AB},\qquad {}^2g^{ab} = g^{ab} - N^{AB}n^a_An^b_B
$$

The lapse matrix and its inverse convert between the dyad vectors and the gradient covectors. Projecting g gives the 2-metric of the surfaces and the metric on the orthogonal 2-space, whose dyad components are exactly N_AB. The diagonal entries N_00 and N_11 act as the lapses of the 2-surfaces inside the two hypersurface families. The last relation, set as Exercise 14.12(i), is the starting point for the contravariant 2+2 metric.

**Symbols:** A, B = 0, 1 dyad labels; N^{AB} inverse of N_AB

### (14.132)-(14.133) 2+2 projectors · supporting · DIV §14.14 p.264 (pdf 279)

$$
B_b{}^a = \delta_b^a - n^a_An^A_b,\qquad T_b{}^a = n^a_An^A_b
$$

Projectors onto the 2-surfaces and onto their orthogonal 2-spaces.

### (14.135), (14.137) Connecting vectors and shifts · supporting · DIV §14.14 p.264 (pdf 279)

$$
E^a_A = n^a_A + b^a_A,\quad n^A_ab^a_B = 0,\qquad [E_0,E_1] = 0
$$

Shifts tangent to the 2-surfaces chosen so the connecting vectors commute.

### (14.138) 2+2 inverse metric · central · DIV §14.14 p.265 (pdf 280)

$$
g^{ab} = \begin{pmatrix} N^{AB} & -N^{AB}b^i_B \\ -N^{AB}b^i_B & {}^2g^{ij} + N^{AB}b^i_Ab^j_B\end{pmatrix}
$$

Contravariant metric with lapse matrix and two shifts; i, j = 2, 3.

### (14.139) 2+2 metric · central · DIV §14.14 p.265 (pdf 280)

$$
g_{ab} = \begin{pmatrix} N_{AB} + {}^2g_{ij}b^i_Ab^j_B & {}^2g_{ij}b^j_A \\ {}^2g_{ij}b^j_B & {}^2g_{ij}\end{pmatrix}
$$

Covariant metric (the book's text calls it 'contravariant' by slip); compare with (14.89).

### (14.141)-(14.142) Conformal 2-structure · central · DIV §14.14 p.265 (pdf 280)

$$
\gamma = |{}^2g_{ij}|,\qquad {}^2\bar g_{ij} = \gamma^{-1/2}\,{}^2g_{ij}
$$

Divide the 2-metric by the square root of its determinant to leave a unit-determinant conformal structure. The book prints gamma^{-2}; for a 2x2 metric only the power -1/2 yields a scale-free (unit-determinant) object, so the printed exponent is likely a misprint.

## Worked examples

### Example · intro · DIV §14.2 p.240 (pdf 255)

**Problem:** Show that B applied to any vector gives a vector orthogonal to n, and that B is a projector.

**Method:** Contract B_b^a X^b with n_a and use n.n = 1; for idempotence multiply two copies of delta - n n.

**Key insight:** Both properties reduce to the single fact B n = 0, which in turn is just the normalization of n.

**Result:** X-bar . n = 0; B B = B.

**Concepts:** projection operator onto a hypersurface, unit normal to a spacelike hypersurface

### Example · standard · DIV §14.3 p.241 (pdf 256)

**Problem:** Show that the induced derivative D annihilates the induced metric and hence is the Levi-Civita derivative of h.

**Method:** Write h = g - n n inside the projected derivative, drop nabla g, expand nabla(n n) by Leibniz, and note each term leaves a bare n contracted with a projector.

**Key insight:** Projection kills anything carrying a free normal index, so metric compatibility is inherited; torsion-freeness (Exercise 14.3) then gives uniqueness.

**Result:** D_e h_ab = 0; D is the metric derivative of h (and of gamma).

**Concepts:** induced covariant derivative, Levi-Civita connection uniqueness, induced metric

### Example · standard · DIV §14.3 p.242 (pdf 257)

**Problem:** Relate the 4D and 3D Christoffel symbols in adapted coordinates and split the tangential derivative into tangent and normal parts.

**Method:** Compare the coordinate form of D X in terms of 4D Gamma with its form as the 3-metric derivative; for the normal part, differentiate n.X = 0.

**Key insight:** Tangential projection of nabla sees only intrinsic geometry; the leftover normal part is exactly K(X, Y), yielding the Gauss formula.

**Result:** Gamma^beta_{alpha gamma} *= (3)Gamma^beta_{alpha gamma}; nabla_Y X = D_Y X + K(X,Y) n.

**Concepts:** adapted coordinates, extrinsic curvature, Gauss formula for covariant derivatives

### Example · standard · DIV §14.4 p.244 (pdf 259)

**Problem:** Express the normal-normal and normal-tangential components of the Einstein tensor in terms of slice data.

**Method:** Contract the Gauss equation with g^{ac} = h^{ac} + n^a n^c, contract again, and compare with G_ab n^a n^b = R_ab n n - R/2; contract Codazzi once and note the g_cd R term dies under B n.

**Key insight:** The four components of G with at least one normal index contain no second normal derivatives, so they are constraints rather than evolution equations.

**Result:** Hamiltonian constraint (14.30) and momentum constraint (14.34).

**Concepts:** Hamiltonian constraint, momentum constraint, Gauss equation, Codazzi equation

### Example · challenging · DIV §14.5 p.245 (pdf 260)

**Problem:** Derive the Gauss equation.

**Method:** Expand D_a D_b Z^c with projectors, differentiate the projectors using (14.37), evaluate the two projector-derivative terms as n K, use n.Z = 0 to get Z K, antisymmetrize, then strip an arbitrary vector.

**Key insight:** All the extrinsic information enters through derivatives of the projectors; antisymmetrization removes the term proportional to K_ab n^d.

**Result:** (3)R_abcd = BBBB R_efgh + K_ac K_bd - K_ad K_bc.

**Concepts:** Gauss equation, Ricci identity (commutator of covariant derivatives)

### Example · challenging · DIV §14.6 p.246 (pdf 261)

**Problem:** Derive the Codazzi equation.

**Method:** Apply the projected derivative to K = -B B nabla n, expand the projectors by Leibniz, cancel symmetric terms under a<->c, and convert the commutator of nabla on n into Riemann.

**Key insight:** The normal index of the resulting Riemann component comes from the covector being differentiated.

**Result:** D_a K_bc - D_c K_ba = -B B B R^l_{kgj} n_l.

**Concepts:** Codazzi equation, extrinsic curvature

### Example · challenging · DIV §14.8 p.248 (pdf 263)

**Problem:** Derive the Ricci equation for the normal-normal curvature component.

**Method:** Decompose nabla n = -K + n A, substitute in the Lie-derivative formula for L_n K, rewrite n.nabla K through second derivatives of n, split nabla A into D A plus normal pieces, and replace the commutator on n by Riemann.

**Key insight:** Only with a foliation can n be differentiated along itself; the acceleration is exactly the extra datum that closes the curvature reconstruction.

**Result:** L_n K_ab = n n R_dbca + A_a A_b - D_a A_b - K_a^c K_cb.

**Concepts:** Ricci equation, decomposition of the gradient of the normal, acceleration of normal observers

### Example · intro · DIV §14.9 p.250 (pdf 265)

**Problem:** Show that the lapse equals proper time per coordinate time for Eulerian observers.

**Method:** Move from event x by delta tau along n; Taylor expand t to first order; use n^b partial_b t = w_b n^b = 1/N.

**Key insight:** The gradient of t contracted with the unit normal counts how many coordinate-time units one proper-time unit covers.

**Result:** delta t = delta tau / N, so N = d tau / dt.

**Concepts:** lapse function, Eulerian observer

### Example · standard · DIV §14.9 p.250 (pdf 265)

**Problem:** Show that the extrinsic curvature is minus half the normal Lie derivative of the induced metric.

**Method:** Write h = g - n n; L_n g = nabla_a n_b + nabla_b n_a; L_n(n n) = A n + n A (Exercise 14.8); substitute the gradient decomposition.

**Key insight:** The acceleration pieces cancel against those in nabla n, leaving only K: the extrinsic curvature is the 'velocity' of the 3-metric.

**Result:** L_n h_ab = -2 K_ab; in Eulerian coordinates gamma-dot = 2 N K.

**Concepts:** extrinsic curvature as rate of change of the induced metric, Lie derivative

### Example · standard · DIV §14.9 p.251 (pdf 266)

**Problem:** Remove the acceleration from the Ricci equation using the lapse.

**Method:** Use A = -D ln N to show D_a A_b - A_a A_b = -(1/N) D_a D_b N (the (DN)(DN)/N^2 terms cancel), then substitute and use the contracted Gauss equation.

**Key insight:** Everything about how slices are spaced is encoded in the Hessian of the lapse divided by the lapse.

**Result:** Evolution equation (14.77) for K along n.

**Concepts:** evolution equation for the extrinsic curvature, lapse function

### Example · standard · DIV §14.10 p.252 (pdf 267)

**Problem:** Construct the 3+1 metric from lapse, shift and 3-metric.

**Method:** Split T = N n + beta, solve for n, substitute into g^{ab} = h^{ab} + n^a n^b, evaluate in coordinates with T = partial_t, compute the determinant relation, and invert blockwise.

**Key insight:** The coordinate threads determine g_{0 mu}: zero shift gives a block-diagonal metric; the spatial block of the inverse metric equals -gamma^{mu nu} only for zero shift.

**Result:** g^{ab} and g_ab block forms and the line element (14.90) (with sign corrections noted under key_equations).

**Concepts:** 3+1 decomposition of the metric, shift vector, lapse function

### Example · standard · DIV §14.11 p.255 (pdf 270)

**Problem:** Convert the geometric evolution equations into coordinate PDEs for gamma and K.

**Method:** Split L_T = L_{Nn} + L_beta; show the gradient-of-N terms vanish because they contract n with tangential tensors; expand L_beta with D because beta is tangent; evaluate in adapted coordinates.

**Key insight:** Lie derivatives along the coordinate time vector are just partial_t, so the geometric statements become an explicit first-order system; lapse and shift appear only as given coefficients.

**Result:** (14.102), (14.103), (14.107).

**Concepts:** ADM evolution equations, Lie derivative, gauge freedom in the 3+1 split

### Example · standard · DIV §14.12 p.259 (pdf 274)

**Problem:** Construct constraint-satisfying initial data with the simplest conformal choices.

**Method:** Take conformally flat Cartesian gamma-hat, maximal K = 0 and zero free TT data T-hat; substitute into the conformal Hamiltonian and momentum constraints.

**Key insight:** The momentum constraint becomes a linear, decoupled vector Laplace equation for X, after which the Hamiltonian constraint is a single nonlinear scalar equation for psi; analytic momentum solutions exist for boosted and spinning black holes.

**Result:** Delta-hat psi = -(1/8)(ell X)^2 psi^-7 (book prints -18) and Delta_ell X = 0.

**Concepts:** conformal method for initial data, maximal slicing, transverse-traceless decomposition

### Example · intro · DIV §14.13 p.261 (pdf 276)

**Problem:** Count the true gravitational degrees of freedom.

**Method:** Start with 10 metric functions; remove 4 coordinate choices (6 left); initial data are gamma and K (12) minus 4 constraints (8); remove 3 spatial coordinate choices on the slice (5); remove 1 embedding (slicing) condition such as K = 0 (4); interpret as 2 q plus 2 q-dot.

**Key insight:** Every gauge freedom removes data twice (once as a choice, once via the constraint it generates), so 10 components leave only 2 dynamical degrees of freedom.

**Result:** Two degrees of freedom; the 3+1 formalism nonetheless carries six evolution equations and does not exhibit which data are free.

**Concepts:** gravitational degrees of freedom, constraint equations, gauge freedom in the 3+1 split

### Example · standard · DIV §14.14 p.264 (pdf 279)

**Problem:** Decompose the metric in 2+2 form.

**Method:** Define the dual dyad, the Gram matrix N_AB, projectors onto the 2-surfaces and orthogonal spaces, connecting vectors E_A with commuting shifts b_A, and adapted coordinates; read off contravariant components and verify the covariant form by block multiplication (Exercise 14.12).

**Key insight:** The single lapse becomes a 2x2 lapse matrix and the single shift becomes two shift vectors, but the structure of the block matrices mirrors 3+1.

**Result:** (14.138) and (14.139).

**Concepts:** 2+2 decomposition of the metric, lapse matrix, 2+2 shift vectors


## Analogies and intuitions

### A slice of spacetime as an 'instant of time' and spacetime as the time history of a 3-geometry (geometrodynamics) → ADM evolution equations · strong · DIV §14.11 p.253 (pdf 268)

Recasts Einstein's static 4D equations as ordinary-looking dynamics: the configuration is the 3-metric, its velocity is (essentially) the extrinsic curvature, and the field equations push the pair forward in t.

**Where it breaks down:** Which slices count as 'instants' is a free choice (the lapse and shift), so there is no preferred universal time; different slicings of the same spacetime give different-looking histories.

**App idea:** Film-strip view: a 3-geometry rendered as an embedded surface frame by frame, with a toggle that re-slices the same spacetime and shows a different film of the same 4-geometry.

### The unit normal as the 4-velocity of an observer, its acceleration as that observer's felt acceleration, and the lapse as that observer's clock rate → Eulerian observer · strong · DIV §14.9 p.249 (pdf 264)

Gives physical meaning to n^a, A^a = n.nabla n and N: slices are the observer's simultaneity surfaces, and N converts coordinate time into proper time.

**Where it breaks down:** Eulerian observers are a mathematical congruence, not the grid of the actual coordinates when the shift is non-zero; they may not correspond to any physically preferred matter.

**App idea:** Clock-carrying observers on a slicing with a painted lapse; tick counters and acceleration arrows update.

### Lagrangian mechanics bookkeeping: two coordinates q and two velocities q-dot → gravitational degrees of freedom · useful · DIV §14.13 p.262 (pdf 277)

The four freely specifiable data left after the count are read as two configuration pieces (in the metric) and two velocity pieces (in the extrinsic curvature), i.e. two degrees of freedom.

**Where it breaks down:** The count is per spatial point in a field theory; which components are the q's is not identified by 3+1 (the complaint that motivates 2+2).

**App idea:** Subtraction ladder animation from 10 down to 2 with each removal labelled by its reason.

### Eulerian versus Lagrangian descriptions of fluid flow → shift gauge conditions · useful · DIV §14.12 p.260 (pdf 275)

Implicit in the naming: Eulerian gauge (zero shift) keeps the grid normal to slices like a fixed laboratory mesh, while Lagrangian slicing and gauge move the coordinates with the matter, like following fluid parcels.

**Where it breaks down:** In GR the 'fixed mesh' still moves in spacetime along the normal and is not attached to any background; and grid tangling under vorticity is a practical, not conceptual, failure.

**App idea:** Side-by-side grids in a rotating fluid flow: Eulerian grid stays regular, Lagrangian grid twists and tangles over time.

### Projection of vectors onto a plane, as in taking the shadow of an arrow → projection operator onto a hypersurface · useful · DIV §14.2 p.240 (pdf 255)

B = delta - n n behaves like removing the component along a unit normal; the book stresses idempotence 'as one would expect from a projection operator'.

**Where it breaks down:** With a timelike normal in Lorentzian signature the 'orthogonal complement' is defined by the spacetime metric, not by Euclidean perpendicularity, so pictures drawn on paper mislead about angles.

**App idea:** Minkowski-diagram projector: drag a vector and a tilted slice; the projected part and removed normal part are drawn with Lorentzian orthogonality shown correctly.

### The origin of 'globally hyperbolic' in the hyperbolic wave equation → global hyperbolicity · weak · DIV §14.7 p.247 (pdf 262)

The name is explained by the existence of unique global solutions to the wave equation, linking a causal-structure definition to familiar PDE behaviour.

**Where it breaks down:** The name refers to a property of spacetime, not to any hyperbolic geometry of the slices.

### Promoting the scalar lapse to a 2x2 matrix and the single shift to a pair of shifts → lapse matrix · strong · DIV §14.14 p.265 (pdf 280)

The authors explicitly ask the reader to compare the 2+2 block metrics with the 3+1 ones, so familiar 3+1 roles transfer to the new objects.

**Where it breaks down:** In the null cases the lapse matrix has zero diagonal entries, so its 'lapse' interpretation as a proper-time rate breaks down.

**App idea:** Toggle between 3+1 and 2+2 block matrices with matching colours for lapse, shift and spatial metric blocks.


## Misconceptions addressed

### The extrinsic curvature is defined as minus half the time derivative (Lie derivative along n) of the spatial metric. · DIV §14.9 p.251 (pdf 266)

**Correction:** That identity holds only when a foliation exists; the primary definition uses the tangential derivative of the normal and makes sense for a single hypersurface.

**Why tempting:** Numerical-relativity treatments often start from the Lie-derivative formula because they always have a foliation.

### The spatial part of the inverse spacetime metric equals minus the inverse spatial metric. · DIV §14.10 p.253 (pdf 268)

**Correction:** g^{alpha beta} = -gamma^{alpha beta} + beta^alpha beta^beta / N^2, so the equality holds only when the shift vanishes (while g_{alpha beta} = -gamma_{alpha beta} always holds in adapted coordinates).

**Why tempting:** The covariant spatial block does equal -gamma, so learners assume inverting blocks commutes.

### Lapse and shift are physical fields that must be evolved along with the metric. · DIV §14.11 p.256 (pdf 271)

**Correction:** Einstein's equations give no evolution equations for them; they are gauge functions chosen freely (or by imposed conditions) to fix coordinates.

**Why tempting:** They appear in every equation and in the metric, alongside dynamical variables.

### Knowing the induced metric and extrinsic curvature of one slice determines the full spacetime curvature there. · DIV §14.4 p.244 (pdf 259)

**Correction:** Gauss and Codazzi fix every component except the normal-normal ones R_{alpha 0 beta 0}, which need derivatives of the normal off the slice (the Ricci equation, requiring a foliation).

**Why tempting:** The chapter opens by saying (gamma, K) are the key data, and they do suffice as initial data once the field equations are imposed.

### Ten metric components with ten field equations means ten independent gravitational degrees of freedom. · DIV §14.13 p.261 (pdf 276)

**Correction:** Coordinate freedom, constraints and slicing freedom reduce this to four free data, i.e. two degrees of freedom per point.

**Why tempting:** Counting unknowns and equations works in simpler field theories without gauge symmetry.

### Sign differences between this chapter and numerical-relativity papers mean one of them is wrong. · DIV §14.9 p.249 (pdf 264)

**Correction:** Most differences come from the (+,-,-,-) versus (-,+,+,+) signature, which flips the orientation convention for the normal (n = N grad t versus n = -N grad t) and the sign of K; always translate conventions before comparing.

**Why tempting:** The equations look almost identical apart from signs.

### The 2-planes orthogonal to a family of 2-surfaces automatically mesh together into orthogonal 2-surfaces. · DIV §14.14 p.264 (pdf 279)

**Correction:** They do so only if the dyad spanning them has vanishing (or in-span) Lie bracket; generically they are not integrable.

**Why tempting:** In 3+1, the orthogonal complement is one-dimensional and always integrates to curves, and in flat-space pictures orthogonal planes look like they stack.

### If spacetime is flat the slices must have zero curvature, and a curved slice means a curved spacetime. · DIV §14.4 p.243 (pdf 258)

**Correction:** Intrinsic curvature of a slice and extrinsic curvature both depend on how it is embedded: hyperboloidal slices of Minkowski space have negative intrinsic curvature balanced by non-zero K in the Gauss equation, while flat slices of curved spacetimes exist (e.g. Painleve-Gullstrand slices of Schwarzschild).

**Why tempting:** Learners conflate the curvature of the 4-geometry with that of the 3-geometry of a slice.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### Once constraints hold initially, a numerical evolution will keep them satisfied automatically. · DIV §14.11 p.257 (pdf 272)

**Correction:** The Bianchi identities preserve them for exact solutions of the evolution equations; numerically, truncation errors excite constraint violations that can grow, which is why constraint monitoring and damping formulations are used.

**Why tempting:** The book states the exact preservation result without numerical caveats.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### A foliation reveals the physically true simultaneity of spacetime. · DIV §14.9 p.249 (pdf 264)

**Correction:** The leaves are simultaneous only for the Eulerian observers of that chosen slicing; a different time function gives different leaves for the same spacetime.

**Why tempting:** The 'instant of time' language and the stacked-sheet picture suggest a universal present.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### Singularity-avoiding slicings remove singularities from the spacetime. · DIV §14.12 p.260 (pdf 275)

**Correction:** They only slow the evolution of slices near the singularity (lapse collapsing toward zero) so the computational slices never reach it; the spacetime is unchanged.

**Why tempting:** The phrase 'avoidance' sounds like a physical effect.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*


## Thought experiments

### A small proper-time step along the normal · DIV §14.9 p.250 (pdf 265)

**Setup:** An Eulerian observer at an event on one leaf waits a short proper time and asks which leaf they are now on.

**Lesson:** The coordinate-time label advances by the proper time divided by N, so the lapse is the ratio of elapsed proper time to elapsed coordinate time.

**App idea:** Two adjustable slices with an observer clock; varying N changes the proper time ticks needed to reach the next slice.

### Endless worldlines crossing a candidate initial surface · DIV §14.7 p.247 (pdf 262)

**Setup:** Draw every endless timelike and null curve through a region and count how many times each crosses a proposed spacelike slice.

**Lesson:** Only if every curve crosses exactly once does the slice qualify as a Cauchy surface capable of determining the whole spacetime.

**App idea:** Crossing counter overlay on a user-deformable slice.

### Dragging a 2-surface along two vector fields · DIV §14.13 p.262 (pdf 277)

**Setup:** Choose a spacelike 2-surface in a bare manifold and two vector fields crossing it; flow the surface along each field.

**Lesson:** The flows generate two foliations of 3-surfaces whose causal character (double null, null-timelike) is set by the chosen vector fields, which is the constructive starting point of 2+2.

**App idea:** 3D flow tool sweeping sheets from an initial patch with causal colour coding.

### Freely falling slicing during collapse · DIV §14.12 p.260 (pdf 275)

**Setup:** Evolve a collapsing star with unit lapse and zero shift, so coordinate observers fall freely, versus maximal slicing.

**Lesson:** Freely falling (and comoving) observers converge on the singularity so the slicing crashes into it; keeping K = 0 prevents focusing and holds the slices back.

**App idea:** Oppenheimer-Snyder style collapse diagram with slices drawn for geodesic versus maximal slicing, showing the lapse collapsing in the latter.

### Coordinates riding on a swirling fluid · DIV §14.12 p.260 (pdf 275)

**Setup:** Set the shift equal to the fluid 3-velocity in a two- or three-dimensional flow with vorticity.

**Lesson:** The grid follows the matter and becomes severely twisted, destroying numerical accuracy; comoving coordinates work well only in one-dimensional flows.

**App idea:** Grid advected by a vortex flow with a distortion meter.


## Applications and observations

- **Numerical relativity of gravitational collapse and gravitational waves** (technology, DIV §14.12 p.257 (pdf 272)): The 3+1 equations are the framework for computer solutions of Einstein's equations and underpin the modern understanding of collapse and gravitational radiation. (Tutor context, not in the book: the long-standing binary black hole problem was solved in 2005 by Pretorius and by the Campanelli et al. and Baker et al. groups, and such simulations inform waveform models used in LIGO-Virgo detections starting with GW150914 in 2015; production codes use BSSN or generalized-harmonic reformulations rather than raw ADM.) Key numbers: Our additions: first full binary black hole merger simulations 2005; first detection 14 September 2015.
- **Analytic initial data for black holes with chosen momentum and spin** (numerical-estimate, DIV §14.12 p.260 (pdf 275)): With a conformally flat metric, maximal slicing and no free TT data, the momentum constraint is linear and decoupled and can be solved analytically for one or more black holes with specified linear and angular momenta; the Hamiltonian constraint is then solved for the conformal factor. (The book does not name them; these are the Bowen-York solutions.) Key numbers: Choices: gamma-hat = diag(1,1,1), K = 0, T-hat = 0.
- **Early stellar collapse codes** (technology, DIV §14.12 p.260 (pdf 275)): Used the Eulerian gauge (zero shift), with coordinate lines normal to the slices.
- **Radial gauge for radiation in asymptotically flat spacetimes** (technology, DIV §14.12 p.261 (pdf 276)): Shift chosen to set gamma_{r theta} = gamma_{r phi} = 0 and fix the angular determinant to r^4 sin^2 theta, giving a three-function spatial metric suited to extracting gravitational radiation. Key numbers: Three metric functions A, B, xi.
- **Characteristic evolution for radiation, null infinity and cosmology** (technology, DIV §14.13 p.262 (pdf 277)): Null initial value problems are natural because gravitational radiation travels along null geodesics, null infinity is a null hypersurface (so it can be included in the computational domain and radiation defined unambiguously), and cosmological information arrives along our past light cone.
- **Counting gravitational degrees of freedom** (numerical-estimate, DIV §14.13 p.261 (pdf 276)): Subtraction chain from metric components to dynamical degrees of freedom in vacuum. Key numbers: 10 components -> 6 after 4 coordinate choices; 12 initial data (gamma, K) -> 8 after 4 constraints -> 5 after 3 spatial coordinate choices -> 4 after 1 slicing condition = 2 q + 2 q-dot -> 2 degrees of freedom.

## Historical notes

- **Georges Darmois (1927):** Wrote Einstein's equations as a constraint-plus-evolution system on hypersurfaces in the special case of unit lapse and zero shift (Gaussian normal coordinates). — First geometric initial value formulation; also gave an analytic existence argument. (DIV §14.11 p.257 (pdf 272))
- **Andre Lichnerowicz (1939):** Extended the formulation to a general lapse with zero shift; his lemma on constraint preservation is invoked in the degree-of-freedom count. — Generalized slicing and established that constraints propagate via the Bianchi identities. (DIV §14.11 p.257 (pdf 272))
- **Yvonne Choquet-Bruhat (1948):** Treated the general case with arbitrary lapse and shift (date as given in the book). — Completed the geometric 3+1 system; her later work underpins well-posedness for non-analytic data. (DIV §14.11 p.257 (pdf 272))
- **Richard Arnowitt, Stanley Deser, Charles Misner (1959):** Derived a Hamiltonian formulation of GR with the metric and a momentum built from the extrinsic curvature as canonical variables. — Source of the names 'Hamiltonian constraint', 'momentum constraint' and 'ADM formalism'. (DIV §14.11 p.257 (pdf 272))
- **John Archibald Wheeler (1963):** Coined 'geometrodynamics' for GR viewed as evolving 3-geometry. — Frames the conceptual picture of the chapter. (DIV §14.1 p.239 (pdf 254))
- **James York, Niall O Murchadha:** Developed the conformal approach that turns the constraints into elliptic equations for a conformal factor and vector potential. — Standard method for constructing initial data in numerical relativity. (DIV §14.12 p.258 (pdf 273))
- **Ray d'Inverno, John Stachel (1978):** Identified the conformal 2-structure as carrying the gravitational degrees of freedom. — Geometric identification of the two free data, one of the book author's research contributions. (DIV §14.13 p.262 (pdf 277))
- **Ray d'Inverno, James Smallwood (1980):** Gave a covariant 2+2 formulation of the initial value problem. — Basis of the 2+2 formalism sketched in §14.13-14.14. (DIV §14.13 p.262 (pdf 277))
- **Patrick Brady, Sebastien Droz, Werner Israel, Sharon Morsink (1996):** Published an alternative covariant double-null (2+2) splitting of Einstein's equations. — Recommended alternative treatment. (DIV p.268 (pdf 283))
- **Eric Gourgoulhon (2012):** Lecture notes on the 3+1 formalism that study hypersurface geometry before foliations and lapse/shift. — The chapter's sequencing explicitly follows this approach. (DIV p.268 (pdf 283))

## Notation and conventions

- **Metric signature and normalization:** (+,-,-,-); the future-pointing unit normal satisfies n_a n^a = +1; the induced metric h_ab is negative definite on the slice. — Numerical-relativity literature uses (-,+,+,+), where the future-pointing normal is n^a = -N grad^a t and many signs (K, constraint terms) differ; the book warns about this twice. (DIV §14.2 p.240 (pdf 255))
- **gamma_ab = -h_ab:** Positive definite spatial metric used for ADM variables; in §14.10 Greek indices are raised and lowered with gamma. — Many printed equations (constraints, K evolution) have signs appropriate to contractions with h; see gaps. (DIV §14.2 p.241 (pdf 256))
- **Starred equality *=:** An equality valid only in a particular (adapted) coordinate system. — Carried over from earlier chapters. (DIV §14.2 p.241 (pdf 256))
- **Index ranges:** Latin a, b, ... = 0-3 spacetime; Greek alpha, beta, mu, nu = 1-3 on the slice; in 2+2, bold A, B = 0, 1 label the two foliations/dyad and i, j = 2, 3 label coordinates on the 2-surfaces. (DIV §14.2 p.241 (pdf 256))
- **Extrinsic curvature sign:** K is minus the projected gradient of the future-pointing normal, equivalently minus half the normal Lie derivative of h; with zero shift the coordinate-time derivative of gamma is 2NK. — Opposite in sign to the common (-,+,+,+) convention K_ij = -(1/2) L_n gamma_ij. (DIV §14.3 p.242 (pdf 257))
- **Riemann tensor convention:** [D_a, D_b] Z^c = (3)R^c_{dab} Z^d, the same convention as the 4D tensor in earlier chapters. (DIV §14.5 p.245 (pdf 260))
- **Prefix superscripts:** (3)R, (3)R_ab, (3)Gamma denote objects built from the 3-metric; ^2g_ij the 2-metric of the 2+2 surfaces and ^2g-bar_ij its conformal structure; hats denote conformal background quantities. (DIV §14.3 p.242 (pdf 257))
- **Lapse and shift symbols:** Lapse N (also written alpha in §14.10-14.12); shift beta^a; rigging vector T^a = N n^a + beta^a; 2+2 lapse matrix N_AB and shifts b_A^a with connecting vectors E_A. — The switch between N and alpha is unannounced. (DIV §14.10 p.252 (pdf 267))
- **Lie derivative:** Written L with a bold vector subscript (L_n, L_T, L_beta, L_{Nn}). (DIV §14.8 p.248 (pdf 263))
- **Symbol reuse:** A^a is the acceleration but A^{mu nu} is the trace-free extrinsic curvature; T^a is the rigging vector, T_b^a the 2+2 projector and T-hat^{mu nu} the free TT data; gamma is the 3-metric and, in §14.14, the determinant of the 2-metric; h_ab is the induced 3-metric and, in §14.14, the metric on the orthogonal 2-space; B_a^b projects onto the 3-slice and, in §14.14, onto the 2-surface. — Flag these for learners moving between sections. (DIV §14.12 p.258 (pdf 273))

## Teaching gems

### Build the geometry in three layers: one slice (h, K), then a stack of slices (acceleration, lapse, Ricci equation), then threads through the stack (shift, coordinates), each layer adding exactly the object the previous one could not supply. · DIV §14.1 p.239 (pdf 254)

**Why it works:** Learners see why lapse and shift exist instead of meeting them as unexplained metric functions, and can locate every quantity by the minimal structure it needs.

**App idea:** Three-stage builder: stage 1 shows a single draggable slice with normals and K; stage 2 unlocks a stack and clocks; stage 3 unlocks threading curves and shows the resulting line element.

### Read K as the part of the spacetime derivative of a tangent vector that sticks out of the slice: nabla_Y X = D_Y X + K(X, Y) n. · DIV §14.3 p.243 (pdf 258)

**Why it works:** Turns an abstract definition with projectors into a picture familiar from curves and surfaces in 3D: moving along a curved surface, the velocity changes partly out of the surface.

**App idea:** Drag a tangent vector along a curve on an embedded surface (Euclidean then Minkowski); decompose its rate of change into tangential and normal arrows with the normal length equal to K(X, X).

### Curvature reconstruction scorecard: Gauss gives the all-tangential Riemann components, Codazzi those with one normal index, and Ricci those with two; only the last needs more than a single slice. · DIV §14.4 p.244 (pdf 259)

**Why it works:** Organizes three intimidating equations as a complete partition of Riemann by number of normal indices, which also explains why constraints come from the first two.

**App idea:** Interactive Riemann component table (in adapted coordinates) coloured by which equation determines each entry; hovering an entry shows the relevant formula.

### Constraints are exactly the Einstein components with a normal index, and they contain only slice data and tangential derivatives, so they restrict data instead of evolving it. · DIV §14.4 p.245 (pdf 260)

**Why it works:** Connects the abstract counting of constraints with an operational test: look for second time derivatives.

## Gaps and pitfalls

- **The note after (14.31) claims the Hamiltonian constraint is unchanged if gamma = -h is used for the contractions. This holds for K^2 and K_ab K^ab but not for (3)R: the Ricci tensor is invariant under h -> -h while the scalar (3)R = h^{ab}R_ab flips sign. Consequently (14.93), which says (3)R is that of gamma with gamma raising, has the wrong sign of (3)R relative to (14.30).** — A careful learner checking a known case (e.g. hyperboloidal slices of Minkowski space, or a comparison with numerical-relativity texts) will find the constraint fails and lose trust in the derivation. *Suggestion:* Teach one convention: with gamma and its scalar curvature, the vacuum Hamiltonian constraint is (3)R + K^2 - K_ab K^ab = 0 (K sign irrelevant here). Verify with the hyperboloid slice t^2 - r^2 = tau^2: (3)R = -6/tau^2, K_ab = gamma_ab/tau up to sign.
- **Metric block misprints: in (14.87) the off-diagonal inverse-metric entry is printed -beta^mu/N but substitution into (14.82) gives -beta^mu/N^2; in (14.89)-(14.90) g_00 is printed N^2 + beta^sigma beta_sigma, but g(T, T) = N^2 + h_{mu nu} beta^mu beta^nu = N^2 - beta^sigma beta_sigma with gamma lowering, and only that sign inverts (14.87).** — Block multiplication checks fail; learners may think the shift contributes positively to g_00, contrary to e.g. the Painleve-Gullstrand or rotating-frame metrics. *Suggestion:* Use g^{0 mu} = -beta^mu/N^2 and ds^2 = (N^2 - beta_sigma beta^sigma) dt^2 - 2 beta_mu dt dx^mu - gamma_{mu nu} dx^mu dx^nu; verify on flat space in rotating coordinates.
- **(14.76) contains an extra -2K_ac K_b^c term that does not follow from (14.61) and the preceding simplification; (14.77), where the -2KK term legitimately appears after using the contracted Gauss equation, is correct.** — Exercise 14.11 (derive 14.77 from 14.76 and 14.27) cannot be done consistently from the printed 14.76. *Suggestion:* As the intermediate step, keep only three terms on the right: the doubly normal-contracted Riemann tensor, a single K-squared product, and the lapse Hessian divided by the lapse (see the corrected (14.76) under key_equations).
- **Shift-term and K-quadratic signs in (14.102), (14.103) and (14.107) are those obtained when indices are moved with h, although §14.10 says Greek indices are moved with gamma. With gamma lowering, (14.102) should read partial_t gamma = 2NK + D beta + D beta and the K K terms in (14.107) change sign.** — Implementing the equations in code, or comparing with Gourgoulhon or Baumgarte-Shapiro, produces sign mismatches that look like errors. *Suggestion:* State the convention explicitly in the app; preferably present the evolution system in the (-,+,+,+) numerical-relativity form alongside a conversion table (K_book = -K_NR, traces with gamma).
- **(14.121) prints the coefficient -18; setting R-hat = K = T-hat = 0 in (14.117) gives -1/8 (a lost fraction bar).** — Wrong factor of 144 if used to build black-hole initial data. *Suggestion:* Use Delta-hat psi = -(1/8)(ell-hat X)^{mu nu}(ell-hat X)_{mu nu} psi^{-7}.
- **The maximal-slicing lapse equation (14.123) introduces matter terms rho and S that are never defined in this vacuum-only chapter and appears to lack a factor of the lapse on the right-hand side; no derivation is given.** — Learners cannot reproduce or use the equation, and may not realise it is linear homogeneous in the lapse in vacuum. *Suggestion:* Derive it: set K = 0 and partial_t K = 0 in the trace of the K evolution equation and use the Hamiltonian constraint; in vacuum it reduces to a homogeneous elliptic equation D^2 N = N K_ab K^ab (sign per convention).
- **(14.142) prints the conformal 2-structure as gamma^{-2} times the 2-metric with gamma its determinant, which is not scale-free; a unit-determinant structure requires the power -1/2. The same section also calls (14.139) the 'contravariant' metric and reuses gamma and h for new objects.** — The central claim that the conformal structure carries two functions (a symmetric 2x2 matrix with fixed determinant) becomes opaque. *Suggestion:* Write ^2g-bar_ij = |^2g|^{-1/2} ^2g_ij and count: 3 components minus 1 scale = 2.
- **Symmetry of K (14.20) is asserted in one line from torsion-freeness; for a single hypersurface the argument really uses that the Lie bracket of tangent fields is tangent, which is only proved later as Exercise 14.3(i).** — Learners may think symmetry holds for the projected gradient of any unit timelike field, which is false for twisting congruences. *Suggestion:* Show K(X,Y) - K(Y,X) = n.[X,Y] (up to sign) and note it vanishes because the bracket is tangent; contrast with a rotating congruence where it does not.
- **The product-topology result for globally hyperbolic spacetimes (14.46) is quoted without proof or attribution, and the notion of an endless causal curve is not defined.** — The logical dependence of the whole 3+1 split on a global causal assumption stays invisible. *Suggestion:* Cite Geroch (1970) and the smooth-splitting refinement (Bernal and Sanchez 2003), and show a non-globally-hyperbolic example (anti-de Sitter or Minkowski with a point removed).
- **§14.12 is dated and purely descriptive: it omits why raw ADM is numerically unstable (weak hyperbolicity), the BSSN and generalized-harmonic reformulations, moving-puncture gauges (1+log slicing, Gamma-driver shift), the 2005 binary black hole breakthrough, and gravitational-wave detections.** — Learners may believe the printed ADM system with maximal slicing is how modern codes work. *Suggestion:* Add a short 'what changed' panel with the modern pipeline and a link to Baumgarte-Shapiro or Alcubierre's textbooks; keep the book's gauge menu as historical background.
- **Section 14.13 refers to 'the conformal approach of the last chapter', but that approach is in §14.12 of this chapter; the 2+2 claims (unconstrained data, ODEs along vector fields, Euler-Lagrange equations reproducing the dynamical equations) are asserted without derivation.** — Confusing pointer; the 2+2 advantages must be taken on trust. *Suggestion:* Point to §14.12, and illustrate the null-data idea with a simple characteristic problem (the 1+1 wave equation on a double-null grid) before invoking 2+2 GR.
- **Exercise hints and statements contain slips: the hint in 14.5 gives n^a = (e^{nu/2}, 0, 0, 0), which are the covariant components n_a; 14.3(ii) has X_b where X^b is meant.** — Unit-normal normalization fails and later K components come out with wrong factors. *Suggestion:* Use n_a = (e^{nu/2}, 0, 0, 0) and n^a = (e^{-nu/2}, 0, 0, 0).
- **Notation drift: lapse written both N and alpha; A denotes both acceleration and trace-free K; T denotes the rigging vector, a projector, and the free TT data; B and h change meaning in §14.14.** — Retrieval and cross-section reading become error prone, especially for a voice tutor quoting symbols. *Suggestion:* In the app, rename consistently (e.g. alpha for lapse, a^a for acceleration, A-tilde for trace-free K, t^a for the evolution vector) and show a glossary.

## Tutor notes

- Open with: 'If you were handed a snapshot of space at one instant, what would you need besides its shape to predict the future?' Steer toward the idea that you need how the shape is changing, which is the extrinsic curvature.
- Start from surfaces in ordinary 3D space (a cylinder: intrinsically flat, extrinsically bent; a sphere: both) before introducing Lorentzian signs; then say that K for a slice plays the role of the second fundamental form.
- Explain in the book's order but compress the calculations: projector, induced metric, induced derivative, K, then the curvature scorecard (Gauss/Codazzi/Ricci by number of normal indices), then constraints, then lapse, shift and evolution.
- Keep a visible convention card throughout: signature (+,-,-,-), n.n = +1, gamma = -h, K = -(1/2) L_n h. Warn early that several printed equations mix h and gamma contractions, and give the (-,+,+,+) forms when the learner uses other sources.
- Check for understanding: ask the learner to explain why the constraints contain no second time derivatives, and why the Ricci equation needs a family of slices rather than one.
- Check for understanding: 'In flat Minkowski space with hyperboloidal slices, is K zero? Is (3)R zero? How can both be non-zero while spacetime is flat?' (Answer via the Gauss equation.)
- For the lapse, have the learner derive N = d tau/dt themselves with one Taylor step, then connect to the Newtonian limit: N is roughly 1 + Phi, and A = -D ln N gives the acceleration needed to hover (sign per signature).
- Use the lapse-shift triangle to derive the line element; then test with examples: Schwarzschild (N = sqrt(1-2M/r), zero shift), Painleve-Gullstrand (N = 1, flat slices, non-zero radial shift), rotating coordinates in flat space (shift = -Omega x r).
- When the learner asks 'which equations do I solve?', lay out the pipeline: choose free data, solve 4 elliptic constraints, choose lapse and shift, evolve 12 first-order equations, monitor constraints.
- Common question: 'Why not evolve lapse and shift too?' Answer: they are coordinate choices; in modern codes they are often given their own gauge-driver equations, but those are choices, not Einstein's equations.
- Common question: 'Where are gravitational waves in these variables?' Use the degree-of-freedom count and point to the TT part of K and, in 2+2, the conformal 2-structure; tie to the two polarizations in Chapter 21.
- Common question: 'Why is 3+1 bad for null infinity?' Explain that spacelike slices never reach null infinity and radiation travels along null directions, so characteristic (null) slicing is natural; preview Chapter 23.
- For advanced learners, assign the component calculation of Exercise 14.4 (gamma and K for the spherically symmetric slice) and the verification of the 3+1 inverse metric by block multiplication; expect them to find the printed sign issues.
- For novices, skip §14.5, §14.6 and §14.8 proofs; state the three equations with the scorecard picture and move to lapse, shift and the evolution idea.
