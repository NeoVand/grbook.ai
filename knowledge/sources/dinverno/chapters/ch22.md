# DIV ch22 · Exact gravitational waves

> Leaves linearized theory behind and builds exact vacuum wave spacetimes by imposing two commuting spacelike symmetries: cylindrical Einstein-Rosen waves (a flat-space wave equation plus quadratures), plane waves in Rosen and Brinkmann coordinates, delta-function impulsive waves, and the Penrose-Khan and Szekeres collisions in which two plane waves focus each other into a spacelike singularity.

**Pages:** printed 451–460 · pdf 466–475 · **Difficulty:** math 4/5, conceptual 4/5, novice-friendliness 2/5

Short but dense. Sections 22.2-22.4 are explicit calculations accessible to a reader comfortable with Chapter 6 tensor calculus and Bessel functions, though the Einstein-Rosen field equations and the pulse's gamma are quoted without derivation and contain two misprints. Sections 22.5-22.6 are research summaries: the Penrose-Khan functions arrive unmotivated and the causal and singularity arguments rely on the diagram, so novices need substantial scaffolding (coordinate vs curvature singularities, caustics, spacelike singularities).

## Role in the book

Chapter 21 treated gravitational radiation as a small ripple on flat space-time, where waves superpose and never interact. This short chapter is the book's first look at what the full non-linear field equations say about radiation. It reuses the Killing-vector machinery of Chapter 7, Birkhoff's theorem from Chapter 15, the conformal flatness of 2-metrics from Section 6.13 and the h22 wave of Section 21.4, and it sets up the double-null, characteristic-data viewpoint that Chapter 23 (Bondi radiation, characteristic initial value problem, Petrov types) develops systematically. It also echoes the black-hole chapters by showing that singularities are not merely artefacts of spherical symmetry: colliding waves generate them too.

## Learning objectives

- Reader can explain why spherical symmetry cannot support vacuum gravitational waves (Birkhoff) and why two commuting spacelike Killing vectors are the natural next symmetry assumption.
- Reader can tell cylindrical from plane symmetry by the topology (circle versus line) of the Killing orbits.
- Reader can reduce a whole-cylinder-symmetric metric to the Einstein-Rosen form (22.4) using a new radial coordinate built from the Killing-orbit areas and the conformal flatness of the (t, rho) 2-metric.
- Reader can state the Einstein-Rosen vacuum equations, recognise (22.5) as the flat cylindrical wave equation, and check the integrability condition that lets gamma be found by quadrature.
- Reader can build Bessel-mode and Weber-Wheeler pulse solutions for psi and explain why the second-kind Bessel function is discarded.
- Reader can start from the linearized h22 wave, promote it to the Rosen ansatz (22.10), compute its connection and curvature, and derive the single vacuum condition f''/f + g''/g = 0.
- Reader can transform a Rosen plane wave into Brinkmann form (22.17) and use that form to explain why co-propagating plane waves superpose exactly while counter-propagating ones do not.
- Reader can manipulate step and delta distributions (theta' = delta, u delta = 0) and verify that f = 1 + u theta, g = 1 - u theta is an impulsive plane wave with delta-function Riemann curvature and vanishing Ricci tensor.
- Reader can distinguish impulsive waves from sandwich waves on a space-time diagram and describe what a geodesic observer feels crossing each.
- Reader can read the Penrose-Khan diagram: identify the four regions, the flat versus curved parts, the fold (caustic) singularities at u = 1 and v = 1, and the spacelike curvature singularity u^2 + v^2 = 1.
- Reader can explain why the singularity formed in colliding plane waves is unavoidable (spacelike) and why Szekeres' more general sandwich-wave collisions showed it is not an artefact of the impulsive idealization.
- Reader can describe the colliding-wave problem as a characteristic initial value problem with data on two intersecting null hypersurfaces.

## Teaching approach

A compact, derivation-led survey. The authors let symmetry do the work: each section adds a symmetry assumption, adapts coordinates to it, and watches the vacuum field equations collapse to something solvable (a flat wave equation, a single ODE, a distributional identity). Linearized theory from Chapter 21 is used as the seed for the exact ansatz rather than as a separate topic. Several steps are delegated to exercises, and the final two sections switch to an outline of research results (Penrose-Khan, Szekeres), carried mainly by two space-time diagrams.

**Style:** derivation-heavy, diagram-driven, survey-overview, axiomatic-mathematical, historical-narrative

**Narrative arc**

1. Motivating question: can the full non-linear equations describe radiation exactly? Spherical symmetry is ruled out at once by Birkhoff's theorem.
2. Symmetry ladder: drop to two commuting spacelike Killing vectors; the orbit topology (S^1 x R versus R x R) splits the problem into cylindrical and plane waves.
3. Cylindrical model: adapt coordinates, impose reflection (whole-cylinder) symmetry, use conformal flatness of the 2-metric to reach the Einstein-Rosen metric.
4. Formalism payoff: the vacuum equations become a flat cylindrical wave equation for psi plus two first-order equations for gamma, consistent because of the wave equation.
5. Solutions and history: Bessel modes, then the Weber-Wheeler pulse that settled Einstein and Rosen's doubts about regularity and energy.
6. Plane model: take the linearized h22 metric in double null coordinates, square its coefficients to make an exact ansatz, compute curvature, find one ODE and an arbitrary profile h(u).
7. Change of viewpoint: Rosen to Brinkmann coordinates exposes h explicitly and reveals exact superposition for waves moving the same way, motivating the collision problem.
8. Idealize further: introduce distributions and build the impulsive plane wave, a null plane of delta curvature separating two flat regions; contrast with sandwich waves.
9. Application: glue two impulsive waves together (Penrose-Khan), map the four regions and the singularities on a space-time diagram.
10. Generalize and caution: Szekeres' characteristic-initial-value framework and sandwich collisions still produce singularities; later matter-coupled solutions raise interpretive controversies.

**Signature moves**

- Using the orbit topology of the Killing vectors, rather than their algebra, to decide between cylindrical and plane waves.
- Constructing the Einstein-Rosen radial coordinate as the square root of the product of the two Killing-orbit metric coefficients, so rho measures the area of the invariant 2-surfaces.
- Promoting a linearized metric to an exact ansatz by writing its coefficients as squares, then testing it against the full field equations.
- Presenting the same plane wave in two coordinate systems and reading different physics off each: Rosen for the metric functions and caustics, Brinkmann for the profile and superposition.
- Treating the impulsive wave as the zero-thickness limit of a sandwich wave and doing the curvature calculation with elementary distribution identities.
- Carrying the colliding-wave story almost entirely by a labelled (t, x) diagram with regions I-IV, marking where curvature is zero, delta-like, finite, or divergent.
- Closing with explicit warnings that problem formulation and physical interpretation are delicate, rather than a tidy conclusion.

## Section by section

### §22.1 Gravitational waves and symmetries — p.451 (pdf 466)

Explains the strategy of hunting for exact radiative vacuum solutions by assuming symmetry. Spherical symmetry fails because Birkhoff's theorem forces staticity. The next option is a pair of independent spacelike Killing vectors whose orbits form 2-surfaces; these are taken to commute. If one Killing vector has closed (circle) orbits and the other open (line) orbits the waves are cylindrical; if both are lines the waves are plane.

**Concepts:** Birkhoff's theorem, Killing vector, commuting Killing vectors, orbit topology of Killing vectors, cylindrical symmetry, plane symmetry, exact solution

**Key moves**
- Rule out spherical symmetry via Birkhoff's theorem.
- Assume two independent spacelike Killing vectors with surface-forming orbits and deduce [V, W] = 0, citing (10.15).
- Classify by topology of integral curves: S^1 and R gives cylindrical waves; R and R gives plane waves.

### §22.2 Einstein-Rosen waves — p.451 (pdf 466)

Adapts coordinates (t, rho, phi, z) to rotational and translational Killing vectors, imposes reflection symmetry in phi and z to remove cross terms, defines a new radial coordinate from the orbit metric coefficients and a potential psi from the z-coefficient, and uses conformal flatness of the (t, rho) 2-metric to reach the Einstein-Rosen metric with two functions psi and gamma. The vacuum equations reduce to a flat-space cylindrical wave equation for psi and first-order equations for gamma whose compatibility follows from the wave equation. Separation of variables gives Bessel modes (regular J0 kept, singular N0 dropped), and the Weber-Wheeler superposition gives a regular, energy-carrying pulse, overturning Einstein and Rosen's initial scepticism.

**Concepts:** cylindrical symmetry, whole-cylinder symmetry, adapted coordinates, conformal flatness of two-dimensional metrics, Einstein-Rosen metric, cylindrical wave equation, integrability condition for gamma, Bessel-mode solutions, Weber-Wheeler pulse, coordinate singularity versus physical singularity

**Key moves**
- Discrete symmetries z -> -z and phi -> -phi eliminate all dphi dx^a and dz dx^a cross terms.
- New radial coordinate rho = (CD)^{1/2} and psi = (1/2) ln D put the Killing part of the metric in the form rho^2 e^{-2psi} dphi^2 + e^{2psi} dz^2.
- Any 2-metric is conformally flat, so the (t, rho) block becomes e^{2gamma - 2psi}(dt^2 - drho^2).
- Vacuum equations split into a linear wave equation for psi (22.5) and quadratures for gamma (22.6)-(22.7).
- Integrability gamma_{,rho t} = gamma_{,t rho} holds automatically because of (22.5).
- Separable Bessel modes; discard N0 because it diverges on the axis.
- Superpose modes with exponential weight into a pulse (22.9) whose closed form is a complex-conjugate pair, then integrate for gamma.

### §22.3 Exact plane wave solutions — p.454 (pdf 469)

Introduces double null coordinates u = t - x, v = t + x and rewrites the linearized h22 wave as the Rosen line element du dv - f^2(u) dy^2 - g^2(u) dz^2. Treated as an exact ansatz, it has four non-zero Christoffel symbols, two independent Riemann components, and a single vacuum equation f''/f + g''/g = 0, so an arbitrary function h(u) generates a linearly polarized exact plane wave. A coordinate change gives the Brinkmann form with h appearing explicitly as the wave amplitude. The section then argues that GR has no general superposition principle, but plane waves travelling in the same direction superpose by adding their h functions; genuine scattering needs counter-propagating waves, and a boost can always make a collision head-on.

**Concepts:** plane symmetry, double null coordinates, h22 wave, Rosen form of a plane wave, linearly polarized exact plane gravitational wave, wave profile function h(u), Brinkmann form of a plane wave, non-linearity and failure of superposition, exact superposition of co-propagating plane waves, head-on frame for colliding waves

**Key moves**
- Square the metric functions of the linearized h22 wave so the signature is guaranteed, then drop the smallness assumption.
- Compute Gamma^1_22 = 2ff', Gamma^1_33 = 2gg', Gamma^2_02 = f'/f, Gamma^3_03 = g'/g, and R_0202 = ff'', R_0303 = gg''.
- The only vacuum equation is f''/f + g''/g = 0; name f''/f = h and solve g''/g = -h, so any h(u) works.
- Transform (22.16) to Brinkmann coordinates where the metric is flat plus h(U)(Z^2 - Y^2) dU^2.
- Linearity in h of the Brinkmann metric shows co-moving waves add; non-linearity must show up only in collisions.
- Reduce oblique collisions to head-on ones by choosing a boosted observer (Exercise 4.10).

### §22.4 Impulsive plane gravitational waves — p.455 (pdf 470)

Digresses to define the Heaviside step and Dirac delta (as a distribution) and records theta' = delta, u delta = 0, u theta' = 0. Choosing f = 1 + u theta(u), g = 1 - u theta(u) in the Rosen form satisfies the vacuum equation with f''/f = delta, so Ricci vanishes while Riemann (equal to Weyl in vacuum) has delta components on u = 0. In Brinkmann form h = delta, and off u = 0 the metric is Minkowski in null coordinates. The result is a single null plane of curvature (shock or impulsive wave) between two flat regions. A sandwich wave is defined as a curved vacuum slab bounded by flat regions; an observer crossing it feels a finite-duration transverse tidal effect, and the impulsive wave is its thin limit.

**Concepts:** Heaviside step function, Dirac delta distribution, impulsive plane gravitational wave, Weyl tensor equals Riemann tensor in vacuum, sandwich wave, transverse tidal acceleration in a plane wave

**Key moves**
- Establish theta' = delta by integrating against a test function and integrating by parts.
- Use u delta(u) = 0 to show f' = theta exactly (the u delta term drops).
- Evaluate f''/f = delta because f = 1 wherever delta is supported.
- Read off R_0202 = -R_0303 = delta(u) and zero Ricci: curvature confined to the wavefront.
- Brinkmann form with h = delta reduces to flat space for U != 0.
- Interpret the impulsive wave as the zero-thickness limit of a sandwich wave.

### §22.5 Colliding impulsive plane gravitational waves — p.457 (pdf 472)

Generalizes the Rosen form so all metric functions depend on both u and v, and quotes the Penrose-Khan vacuum solution built from p = u theta(u), q = v theta(v). Four regions are identified: flat region I before either wave, flat regions II and III each crossed by one wave, and curved interaction region IV. An observer at x = 0 meets both waves at t = 0 and reaches a curvature singularity at u^2 + v^2 = 1 in finite proper time. The lines u = 1 in II and v = 1 in III are fold singularities (caustics where null geodesics cross), not curvature singularities. The authors warn that the diagram makes escape through u = 1 look possible when it is not, note a step discontinuity in curvature on the post-collision wavefronts, and conclude that the waves focus each other into an unavoidable spacelike singularity.

**Concepts:** colliding plane gravitational waves, Penrose-Khan solution, interaction region, mutual focusing and caustics, fold singularity, curvature singularity, spacelike versus timelike singularity, step wave

**Key moves**
- Generalized Rosen ansatz l du dv - f^2 dy^2 - g^2 dz^2 with l, f, g functions of u and v.
- Penrose-Khan functions in terms of p, q, r, w, m; region of validity bounded by u = 1, v = 1 and u^2 + v^2 = 1.
- Classify curvature by region: zero in I-III, delta on the incoming fronts, a finite (step) jump in addition to the delta on the two wavefront segments that bound region IV (u = 0 with 0 < v < 1, v = 0 with 0 < u < 1), divergent at u^2 + v^2 = 1.
- Distinguish fold (caustic) singularities from scalar-invariant blow-up.
- Argue that an observer near the caustic cannot escape and ends on the spacelike singularity.

### §22.6 Colliding gravitational waves — p.458 (pdf 473)

Asks whether the singularity is an artefact of high symmetry, as had once been suspected for Schwarzschild. Szekeres recast colliding waves as a characteristic initial value problem with data on u = 0 and v = 0 and showed the interaction region is uniquely determined. Assuming two commuting translational Killing vectors everywhere, the metric takes a Rosen-type form with four functions M, U, V, W; in regions II and III these depend on only one null coordinate, and for constant parallel polarizations W = 0 and the problem reduces to an integral for V and quadratures for M. Sandwich-wave collisions also end in singularities. The section closes by noting many later exact solutions (with electromagnetic fields, fluids, null dust) and the unresolved question of whether colliding null dust streams interpenetrate or become a stiff fluid. The authors end by stressing that such collision problems mix initial-value and boundary-value features, so both the formulation and the physical reading of the resulting solutions need care.

**Concepts:** characteristic initial value problem, Szekeres colliding-wave metric, collinear (parallel) polarization, sandwich wave, null dust, stiff fluid, curvature singularity

**Key moves**
- Pose the collision as data on two intersecting null hypersurfaces with a flat region I.
- Well-posedness: given data determine region IV uniquely.
- Szekeres metric with functions M, U, V, W; reduction to one-null-coordinate dependence in II and III.
- Parallel constant polarizations allow W = 0 and reduce the problem to an integral plus quadratures.
- Sandwich-wave collisions still singular, so the singularity is not an artefact of impulsiveness.
- Flag interpretive ambiguity in matter-coupled collisions (null dust versus stiff fluid).

### Exercises and further reading — p.459 (pdf 474)

Seven exercises complete the omitted verifications: the gamma integrability condition, the complex pulse solution, the Rosen connection and field equation, the Rosen-to-Brinkmann map and its inverse, a rescaled Rosen convention, distribution identities, and the impulsive-wave curvature. Further reading points to Kramer et al. for exact solutions, Thorne (1965) for Einstein-Rosen waves, and Griffiths (1991) for colliding waves.

**Concepts:** integrability condition for gamma, Rosen form of a plane wave, Brinkmann form of a plane wave, Dirac delta distribution

**Key moves**
- Exercises carry the computational verification the text skipped.
- Exercise 22.5 shows constant conformal rescalings leave connection and Ricci unchanged.

## Concepts

### exact solution

*definition · developed* · also: exact vacuum solution, solution of the full field equations

A metric satisfying the complete non-linear Einstein equations with no perturbative truncation, as opposed to a solution of the linearized equations about flat space.

**How introduced:** Opening contrast with Chapter 21: instead of small ripples, the chapter seeks full vacuum solutions that still describe radiation, using symmetry to make the search tractable.

**Prerequisites:** Einstein field equations, linearized gravitational waves

$$
R_{ab}=0
$$

**Where:** DIV §22.1 p.451 (pdf 466)

### Birkhoff's theorem

*theorem · revisited*

Any spherically symmetric vacuum solution is, in the exterior region, necessarily static (locally Schwarzschild), so spherical symmetry cannot support vacuum gravitational radiation.

**How introduced:** Invoked in one line to rule out the simplest symmetry assumption and motivate two-Killing-vector symmetry.

**Prerequisites:** Schwarzschild solution, Killing vector

**Where:** DIV §22.1 p.451 (pdf 466)

### Killing vector

*mathematical-object · revisited* · also: infinitesimal isometry generator

A vector field X along which the metric is unchanged, L_X g_ab = 0, equivalently nabla_(a X_b) = 0; its flow is a continuous symmetry of space-time.

**How introduced:** Used as the language of symmetry: spherical symmetry has three rotational spacelike Killing vectors, and the wave solutions assume two.

**Prerequisites:** Lie derivative, metric

$$
\mathcal{L}_X g_{ab}=0
$$

**Where:** DIV §22.1 p.451 (pdf 466); DIV §22.2 p.451 (pdf 466)

### commuting Killing vectors

*definition · core* · also: abelian two-parameter isometry group, G2 on spacelike orbits

Two independent spacelike Killing vectors V, W with vanishing Lie bracket, so coordinates can be chosen in which both are coordinate basis vectors and the metric is independent of the two corresponding coordinates.

**How introduced:** Assumed as the next-simplest symmetry after spherical; the authors justify commutation from the surface-forming property of the orbits with a pointer to (10.15).

**Prerequisites:** Killing vector, Lie bracket

$$
[V,W]=0
$$

**Notes:** Frobenius alone only puts [V,W] in the span of V and W; commutation additionally reflects the abelian structure of the symmetry groups used (rotation x translation, or two translations).

**Where:** DIV §22.1 p.451 (pdf 466)

### orbit topology of Killing vectors

*definition · introduced* · also: topology of integral curves

The global shape of the integral curves of a Killing vector: closed circles (S^1) for rotations, open lines (R) for translations; the combination distinguishes cylindrical from plane symmetry.

**How introduced:** Used as the classification criterion right after [V,W]=0 is established.

**Prerequisites:** Killing vector

**Where:** DIV §22.1 p.451 (pdf 466)

### cylindrical symmetry

*definition · core* · also: cylindrically symmetric space-time

A space-time with two commuting spacelike Killing vectors, one generating rotations with closed 2pi-periodic orbits and one generating translations along an axis, so metric components depend only on t and a radial coordinate.

**How introduced:** Adapted coordinates (t, rho, phi, z) with Killing vectors d/dphi and d/dz.

**Prerequisites:** commuting Killing vectors, orbit topology of Killing vectors

$$
g_{ab}=g_{ab}(t,\rho)
$$

**Where:** DIV §22.1 p.451 (pdf 466); DIV §22.2 p.451 (pdf 466)

### whole-cylinder symmetry

*definition · introduced* · also: reflection symmetry in phi and z, diagonal cylindrical metric

The additional discrete invariance of a cylindrically symmetric metric under z -> -z and phi -> -phi, which removes all cross terms between dphi or dz and the other coordinates.

**How introduced:** Stated as an extra assumption right after the adapted coordinates are set up, with the name credited to authors such as Thorne (1965); the book translates it at once into the absence of dphi dx^a and dz dx^a cross terms.

**Prerequisites:** cylindrical symmetry

$$
ds^2=g_{AB}(t,\rho)dx^Adx^B-C(t,\rho)d\phi^2-D(t,\rho)dz^2
$$

**Notes:** Reviewer gloss, not in the book: a diagonal metric of this kind carries a single fixed polarization, which is why the Einstein-Rosen family has one dynamical function psi.

**Where:** DIV §22.2 p.452 (pdf 467)

### adapted coordinates

*technique · developed* · also: coordinates adapted to Killing vectors

Coordinates in which the given Killing vectors are coordinate basis fields, so the metric components do not depend on the corresponding coordinates.

**How introduced:** Constructed first for cylindrical symmetry and then for plane symmetry (d/dy, d/dz), with the star-equals notation marking coordinate-dependent statements.

**Prerequisites:** Killing vector, commuting Killing vectors

$$
V\stackrel{*}{=}\partial_\phi,\; W\stackrel{*}{=}\partial_z
$$

**Where:** DIV §22.2 p.451 (pdf 466); DIV §22.3 p.454 (pdf 469)

### conformal flatness of two-dimensional metrics

*theorem · revisited* · also: 2-metrics are conformally flat

Every two-dimensional metric can locally be written as a positive function times a flat metric; for Lorentzian signature, E(t,rho)(dt^2 - drho^2), obtained by taking null curves as coordinate lines.

**How introduced:** Quoted from Section 6.13 to simplify the (t, rho) block of the cylindrical metric.

**Prerequisites:** metric, conformal transformation

$$
d\sigma^2=E(t',\rho')(dt'^2-d\rho'^2)
$$

**Notes:** The theorem in 6.13 is stated for Riemannian 2-manifolds; the Lorentzian version needed here is Exercise 6.31.

**Where:** DIV §22.2 p.452 (pdf 467)

### Einstein-Rosen metric

*solution-or-model · core* · also: general cylindrically symmetric metric, Einstein-Rosen line element

The whole-cylinder-symmetric line element e^{2gamma-2psi}(dt^2 - drho^2) - rho^2 e^{-2psi} dphi^2 - e^{2psi} dz^2, with psi(t,rho) and gamma(t,rho) as the only unknowns.

**How introduced:** Reached in three coordinate steps: radial coordinate from the orbit coefficients, psi from the z-coefficient, conformal gauge for the remaining 2-metric.

**Prerequisites:** whole-cylinder symmetry, conformal flatness of two-dimensional metrics, adapted coordinates

$$
ds^2=e^{2\gamma-2\psi}(dt^2-d\rho^2)-\rho^2e^{-2\psi}d\phi^2-e^{2\psi}dz^2
$$

**Notes:** The book calls (22.4) the general cylindrically symmetric metric, but it was derived under the whole-cylinder (reflection) assumption and a spacelike gradient of the area radius; general cylindrical waves with two polarizations need cross terms.

**Where:** DIV §22.2 p.452 (pdf 467)

### cylindrical wave equation

*law · core* · also: axisymmetric flat-space wave equation for psi

psi_tt - (1/rho)(rho psi_rho)_rho = 0: the ordinary linear wave equation in flat 3-space for a field independent of phi and z; in Einstein-Rosen waves it is the full content of the dynamics.

**How introduced:** Emerges from the vacuum equations for the Einstein-Rosen metric, highlighted as an unexpected linear core inside non-linear GR.

**Prerequisites:** Einstein-Rosen metric

$$
\psi_{,tt}-\frac{1}{\rho}(\rho\,\psi_{,\rho})_{,\rho}=0
$$

**Where:** DIV §22.2 p.453 (pdf 468)

### integrability condition for gamma

*identity · developed* · also: compatibility of the gamma equations

The requirement that mixed partial derivatives of gamma agree, which holds for (22.6)-(22.7) precisely when psi satisfies the cylindrical wave equation, so gamma follows by line integration.

**How introduced:** Noted as a consistency check after the field equations are stated; verification is Exercise 22.1.

**Prerequisites:** cylindrical wave equation, Einstein-Rosen metric

$$
\gamma_{,\rho t}=\gamma_{,t\rho}
$$

**Where:** DIV §22.2 p.453 (pdf 468)

### Bessel-mode solutions

*solution-or-model · introduced* · also: separable Einstein-Rosen waves, standing cylindrical modes

Separable solutions psi = A J0(omega rho) cos(omega t) + B N0(omega rho) sin(omega t) of the cylindrical wave equation; regularity on the axis requires B = 0 since N0 diverges at rho = 0.

**How introduced:** Obtained by separation of variables; used to illustrate that gamma can then be integrated and to recall Einstein and Rosen's worries about singular behaviour.

**Prerequisites:** cylindrical wave equation

$$
\psi=A\mathcal{J}_0(\omega\rho)\cos\omega t+BN_0(\omega\rho)\sin\omega t
$$

**Where:** DIV §22.2 p.453 (pdf 468)

### Weber-Wheeler pulse

*solution-or-model · developed* · also: Webber-Wheeler solution (book's spelling), cylindrical gravitational pulse

A regular cylindrical wave packet formed by superposing J0 modes with an exponential frequency weight, with closed form C[(a - it)^2 + rho^2]^{-1/2} + c.c.; it implodes onto the axis and re-expands, carries energy, and has a closed-form gamma.

**How introduced:** Presented as the more interesting solution that refuted Einstein and Rosen's belief that such waves were unphysical.

**Prerequisites:** Bessel-mode solutions, cylindrical wave equation

$$
\psi=C[(a-it)^2+\rho^2]^{-1/2}+C[(a+it)^2+\rho^2]^{-1/2}
$$

**Notes:** On the axis psi(t,0) = 2Ca/(a^2+t^2), a Lorentzian pulse of width a (our evaluation). The book misprints the weight as e^{a omega}; convergence needs e^{-a omega}.

**Where:** DIV §22.2 p.453 (pdf 468)

### coordinate singularity versus physical singularity

*definition · revisited* · also: coordinate singularity, intrinsic singularity

A coordinate singularity is a breakdown of a particular chart or metric representation that disappears in better coordinates; a physical (intrinsic) singularity is signalled by divergence of curvature scalar invariants.

**How introduced:** Appears twice: historically in Einstein and Rosen's doubts about their waves, and in the Penrose-Khan analysis separating fold singularities from the curvature singularity.

**Prerequisites:** curvature singularity

**Where:** DIV §22.2 p.453 (pdf 468); DIV §22.5 p.458 (pdf 473)

### plane symmetry

*definition · core* · also: plane-symmetric space-time

Two commuting spacelike Killing vectors whose orbits are both open lines, realized as translations d/dy and d/dz, so the metric depends only on t and x (or u and v).

**How introduced:** Introduced as the second branch of the orbit-topology classification and made concrete with translations in y and z.

**Prerequisites:** commuting Killing vectors, orbit topology of Killing vectors

$$
V\stackrel{*}{=}\partial_y,\;W\stackrel{*}{=}\partial_z
$$

**Where:** DIV §22.1 p.451 (pdf 466); DIV §22.3 p.454 (pdf 469)

### double null coordinates

*convention · core* · also: null coordinates u, v, light-cone coordinates

Coordinates u = t - x and v = t + x whose level sets are null hypersurfaces; the flat 2-metric dt^2 - dx^2 becomes du dv.

**How introduced:** Introduced to rewrite the linearized h22 wave, then used for every plane and colliding wave in the chapter.

**Prerequisites:** null hypersurface

$$
u=t-x,\;v=t+x,\;dt^2-dx^2=du\,dv
$$

**Where:** DIV §22.3 p.454 (pdf 469)

### h22 wave

*solution-or-model · revisited* · also: + polarized linearized plane wave

The linearized plane wave of Chapter 21 with metric perturbation h22 = -h33 depending on t - x, which stretches y and squeezes z alternately.

**How introduced:** Used as the seed for the exact Rosen ansatz.

**Prerequisites:** linearized gravitational waves, polarization of gravitational waves

f^2=1-\varepsilon h_{22}(u),\;g^2=1+\varepsilon h_{22}(u)

**Where:** DIV §22.3 p.454 (pdf 469)

### Rosen form of a plane wave

*solution-or-model · core* · also: Rosen line element, Rosen coordinates

The plane-wave line element du dv - f^2(u) dy^2 - g^2(u) dz^2 in which freely falling particles sit at constant (y, z) and the transverse geometry is encoded in scale factors f and g.

**How introduced:** Obtained by squaring the linearized h22 coefficients and taking the result as an exact ansatz; generalized in 22.5 to l du dv - f^2 dy^2 - g^2 dz^2 with u and v dependence.

**Prerequisites:** double null coordinates, h22 wave, plane symmetry

$$
ds^2=du\,dv-f^2(u)dy^2-g^2(u)dz^2
$$

**Notes:** Rosen coordinates degenerate where f or g vanish even when curvature is finite (the fold singularities of 22.5).

**Where:** DIV §22.3 p.454 (pdf 469); DIV §22.3 p.455 (pdf 470); DIV §22.5 p.457 (pdf 472)

### linearly polarized exact plane gravitational wave

*solution-or-model · core* · also: exact plane wave, plane-fronted wave with fixed polarization

A vacuum solution in Rosen form with f''/f + g''/g = 0, fixed by an arbitrary profile h(u); it represents a plane-fronted wave of constant polarization travelling in +x with no source.

**How introduced:** The field equation collapses to one ODE; naming f''/f = h and solving g''/g = -h produces a family labelled by an arbitrary function.

**Prerequisites:** Rosen form of a plane wave, Riemann tensor, vacuum field equations

$$
f''/f+g''/g=0
$$

**Where:** DIV §22.3 p.454 (pdf 469)

### wave profile function h(u)

*physical-quantity · core* · also: amplitude function, profile of the plane wave

The freely specifiable function of retarded time u that sets the curvature (and hence tidal strength) of an exact plane wave: h = f''/f = -g''/g in Rosen form and the coefficient of (Z^2 - Y^2) dU^2 in Brinkmann form.

**How introduced:** Named as a convenience in solving the field equation and identified as the wave amplitude through the Brinkmann form.

**Prerequisites:** linearly polarized exact plane gravitational wave

$$
h=f''/f=-g''/g
$$

**Where:** DIV §22.3 p.454 (pdf 469); DIV §22.3 p.455 (pdf 470)

### Brinkmann form of a plane wave

*solution-or-model · core* · also: Brinkmann coordinates, harmonic coordinates for plane waves

The plane-wave line element h(U)(Z^2 - Y^2) dU^2 + dU dV - dY^2 - dZ^2: flat space plus a single term quadratic in the transverse coordinates, with the profile appearing explicitly.

**How introduced:** Reached from Rosen form via (22.16); the authors use it to exhibit the free function and the limited superposition principle.

**Prerequisites:** Rosen form of a plane wave, coordinate transformation of a line element

$$
ds^2=h(U)(Z^2-Y^2)dU^2+dU\,dV-dY^2-dZ^2

**Where:** DIV §22.3 p.455 (pdf 470)

### non-linearity and failure of superposition

*principle · developed* · also: no superposition principle in GR

Because the field equations are non-linear, the sum of two vacuum solutions is generally not a solution, so gravitational waves can scatter each other, unlike electromagnetic waves in vacuum.

**How introduced:** Set against the linearity of Maxwell theory to motivate studying wave collisions.

**Prerequisites:** exact solution

**Where:** DIV §22.3 p.455 (pdf 470)

### exact superposition of co-propagating plane waves

*principle · introduced* · also: limited superposition principle

Plane waves moving in the same direction can be combined exactly by adding their Brinkmann profile functions, because the Brinkmann metric is linear in h; hence they do not scatter each other.

**How introduced:** Read off from the structure of (22.17).

**Prerequisites:** Brinkmann form of a plane wave, non-linearity and failure of superposition

$$
h(U)=h_1(U)+h_2(U)

**Where:** DIV §22.3 p.455 (pdf 470)

### head-on frame for colliding waves

*technique · mention* · also: boosting to a head-on collision

For two plane waves meeting at an angle, a suitable boost produces observers who see them approaching along a common line from opposite directions, so it suffices to study head-on collisions.

**How introduced:** Asserted with reference to the photon version, Exercise 4.10.

**Prerequisites:** Lorentz transformation, aberration

### impulsive plane gravitational wave

*solution-or-model · core* · also: shock wave, impulsive wave

A plane-wave vacuum space-time whose curvature is a delta function supported on a single null hypersurface u = 0, with flat space on both sides; in Rosen form f = 1 + u theta(u), g = 1 - u theta(u), and in Brinkmann form h = delta(U).

**How introduced:** Built by choosing Rosen functions with a kink at u = 0 and checking the field equation with distribution identities; pictured spatially and in a space-time diagram.

**Prerequisites:** Rosen form of a plane wave, Dirac delta distribution, Brinkmann form of a plane wave

$$
f=1+u\theta(u),\;g=1-u\theta(u)
$$

$$
R_{0202}=-R_{0303}=\delta(u)
$$

**Notes:** Physically the wave gives freely falling particles an instantaneous transverse kick: afterwards y-separations grow and z-separations shrink linearly in u, so z-neighbours meet at u = 1 (our reading of f and g).

**Where:** DIV §22.4 p.456 (pdf 471)

### sandwich wave

*solution-or-model · introduced*

A curved vacuum region confined between two parallel null hypersurfaces, with flat space before and after; an impulsive wave is its limit as the slab thickness goes to zero.

**How introduced:** Defined with a three-region space-time diagram and used to describe what a geodesic observer experiences; later Szekeres' more realistic collisions use sandwich waves.

**Prerequisites:** linearly polarized exact plane gravitational wave, Weyl tensor equals Riemann tensor in vacuum

**Where:** DIV §22.4 p.456 (pdf 471); DIV §22.6 p.459 (pdf 474)

### Weyl tensor equals Riemann tensor in vacuum

*identity · mention*

When the Ricci tensor vanishes, the Weyl (trace-free) part of curvature is the whole Riemann tensor, so vacuum curvature is purely tidal and free.

**How introduced:** Parenthetical remark when listing the non-zero impulsive-wave curvature components.

**Prerequisites:** Riemann tensor, Ricci tensor

$$
R_{ab}=0\Rightarrow C_{abcd}=R_{abcd}
$$

**Where:** DIV §22.4 p.456 (pdf 471)

### transverse tidal acceleration in a plane wave

*phenomenon · mention* · also: geodesic deviation in a plane wave

Neighbouring freely falling particles in a plane wave accelerate relative to one another only in the directions perpendicular to propagation, with sign opposite in the two polarization axes.

**How introduced:** Mentioned for an observer crossing a sandwich wave; builds on the tidal picture of Chapter 21.

**Prerequisites:** geodesic deviation, h22 wave

$$
\ddot Y=h\,Y,\quad \ddot Z=-h\,Z\ \text{(Brinkmann geodesics, our derivation)}
$$

**Where:** DIV §22.4 p.456 (pdf 471)

### colliding plane gravitational waves

*phenomenon · core* · also: plane wave collisions

The non-linear interaction of two counter-propagating plane gravitational waves, modelled by metrics depending on both null coordinates, in which the waves scatter and focus each other.

**How introduced:** Motivated by the failure of superposition and made tractable by the head-on frame and impulsive idealization.

**Prerequisites:** non-linearity and failure of superposition, impulsive plane gravitational wave, double null coordinates

$$
ds^2=\ell\,du\,dv-f^2dy^2-g^2dz^2
$$

**Where:** DIV §22.5 p.457 (pdf 472); DIV §22.6 p.458 (pdf 473)

### Penrose-Khan solution

*solution-or-model · core* · also: Khan-Penrose solution

The exact vacuum solution for two colliding linearly polarized impulsive plane waves: flat regions I-III, a curved interaction region IV ending in a spacelike curvature singularity at u^2 + v^2 = 1, and fold singularities at u = 1 and v = 1.

**How introduced:** Quoted in closed form with a region-by-region space-time diagram.

**Prerequisites:** colliding plane gravitational waves, impulsive plane gravitational wave

$$
\ell=\frac{m^3}{rw(pq+rw)^2}
$$

$$
f^2=m^2\frac{r+q}{r-q}\frac{w+p}{w-p}
$$

$$
g^2=m^2\frac{r-q}{r+q}\frac{w-p}{w+p}
$$

**Notes:** In region II (q = 0) the functions reduce to l = 1, f = 1 + u, g = 1 - u, recovering the single impulsive wave (22.24); we checked this symbolically.

**Where:** DIV §22.5 p.457 (pdf 472); DIV §22.5 p.458 (pdf 473)

### interaction region

*definition · developed* · also: region IV

The part of a colliding-wave space-time that lies to the future of both incoming wavefronts, where both waves are present and the metric depends on u and v.

**How introduced:** Labelled region IV in both Fig. 22.4 and Fig. 22.5.

**Prerequisites:** colliding plane gravitational waves

$$
u>0,\;v>0
$$

**Where:** DIV §22.5 p.458 (pdf 473); DIV §22.6 p.458 (pdf 473)

### mutual focusing and caustics

*phenomenon · developed* · also: caustic 2-surface, focusing of null geodesics by a wave

Each plane wave acts like a lens on null rays and test particles crossing it; rays converge and cross on caustic surfaces, and in a collision each wave focuses the other's wavefront.

**How introduced:** Used to explain both the fold singularities at u = 1, v = 1 and, ultimately, the curvature singularity.

**Prerequisites:** impulsive plane gravitational wave, null geodesic

**Where:** DIV §22.5 p.458 (pdf 473)

### fold singularity

*definition · developed* · also: topological singularity, caustic singularity

A surface where the Rosen-type coordinates degenerate because the null geodesic generators used to build them cross; curvature invariants stay finite, so it is not an intrinsic singularity.

**How introduced:** Identified at u = 1 in region II and v = 1 in region III of the Penrose-Khan solution, described as a seam in the v = 0 (or u = 0) hypersurface.

**Prerequisites:** mutual focusing and caustics, coordinate singularity versus physical singularity

$$
u=1\ (v<0),\quad v=1\ (u<0)
$$

**Where:** DIV §22.5 p.458 (pdf 473)

### curvature singularity

*definition · developed* · also: intrinsic singularity, scalar curvature singularity

A place where curvature scalar invariants built from the Riemann tensor diverge, so no change of coordinates can remove it.

**How introduced:** The fate of region IV in the Penrose-Khan solution, reached by the observer x = 0 in finite proper time.

**Prerequisites:** Riemann tensor

$$
u^2+v^2=1
$$

**Where:** DIV §22.5 p.458 (pdf 473); DIV §22.6 p.459 (pdf 474)

### spacelike versus timelike singularity

*principle · revisited* · also: avoidable and unavoidable singularities

A spacelike singularity lies to the future of every observer in the region approaching it and cannot be dodged; a timelike singularity is a place in space that observers can steer around.

**How introduced:** Used to summarize the Penrose-Khan result, recalling black-hole singularities from earlier chapters.

**Prerequisites:** curvature singularity, causal structure

**Where:** DIV §22.5 p.458 (pdf 473)

### step wave

*phenomenon · mention*

A wavefront carrying a finite jump in the curvature tensor (a discontinuity rather than a delta function), which appears on the fronts u = 0, 0 < v < 1 and v = 0, 0 < u < 1 after the collision, in addition to the delta part.

**How introduced:** Noted briefly while listing curvature features of the Penrose-Khan space-time.

### characteristic initial value problem

*technique · introduced* · also: characteristic data on null hypersurfaces, Goursat problem

Prescribing field data on a pair of intersecting null hypersurfaces (here u = 0 and v = 0 meeting in a spacelike 2-surface) and solving for the region to their common future; for colliding waves it is well posed.

**How introduced:** Presented as the core of Szekeres' framework with Fig. 22.5; full treatment deferred to Section 23.5.

### Szekeres colliding-wave metric

*solution-or-model · introduced* · also: Szekeres form, Rosen-type metric with four functions

The general metric for plane-symmetric colliding waves with two translational Killing vectors: e^{-M} du dv - e^{-U}(e^V cosh W dy^2 - 2 sinh W dy dz + e^{-V} cosh W dz^2), with M, U, V, W functions of u and v.

## Key equations

### (22.1) Commuting Killing vectors · supporting · DIV §22.1 p.451 (pdf 466)

$$
[V,W]=0
$$

The two spacelike symmetry generators commute, so both can be made coordinate vector fields at once.

**Symbols:** V, W spacelike Killing vectors; [ , ] Lie bracket

### Whole-cylinder-symmetric metric · derivation-step · DIV §22.2 p.452 (pdf 467)

$$
ds^2=g_{AB}(t,\rho)\,dx^A dx^B-C(t,\rho)\,d\phi^2-D(t,\rho)\,dz^2,\quad A,B=0,1
$$

Reflection symmetry in phi and z removes cross terms, leaving a (t, rho) block and two positive orbit coefficients.

**Symbols:** C, D positive functions; x^A = (t, rho)

### Area radius and potential psi · derivation-step · DIV §22.2 p.452 (pdf 467)

$$
\tilde t=t,\quad \tilde\rho=C^{1/2}D^{1/2},\quad \psi=\tfrac12\ln D
$$

Choose the radial coordinate so that the Killing-orbit part of the metric has determinant rho^2, and encode the remaining freedom in psi.

**Symbols:** rho-tilde new radial coordinate; psi scalar potential

### (22.2) · derivation-step · DIV §22.2 p.452 (pdf 467)

$$
ds^2=\tilde g_{AB}\,d\tilde x^A d\tilde x^B-\tilde\rho^2 e^{-2\psi}d\phi^2-e^{2\psi}dz^2
$$

Intermediate cylindrical metric after the change to area radius.

**Symbols:** g-tilde_AB depends on (t-tilde, rho-tilde)

### (22.3) · derivation-step · DIV §22.2 p.452 (pdf 467)

$$
d\sigma^2=\tilde g_{00}\,d\tilde t^2+2\tilde g_{01}\,d\tilde t\,d\tilde\rho+\tilde g_{11}\,d\tilde\rho^2
$$

The 2-metric on the (t, rho) surfaces orthogonal to the Killing orbits.

**Symbols:** sigma 2-dimensional line element

### Conformal gauge · derivation-step · DIV §22.2 p.452 (pdf 467)

$$
d\sigma^2=E(t',\rho')(dt'^2-d\rho'^2),\qquad E=e^{2\gamma-2\psi}
$$

Any Lorentzian 2-metric is conformally flat; the conformal factor is written so the final metric looks symmetric in psi.

**Symbols:** E conformal factor; gamma second metric function

### (22.4) Einstein-Rosen metric · central · DIV §22.2 p.452 (pdf 467)

$$
ds^2=e^{2\gamma-2\psi}(dt^2-d\rho^2)-\rho^2e^{-2\psi}d\phi^2-e^{2\psi}dz^2
$$

General whole-cylinder-symmetric metric in terms of two functions of t and rho.

**Symbols:** psi(t,rho), gamma(t,rho); phi 2pi-periodic

### (22.5) Cylindrical wave equation · central · DIV §22.2 p.453 (pdf 468)

$$
\frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial t^2}-\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}\left(\rho\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\rho}\right)=0
$$

The dynamical vacuum equation: psi obeys the flat-space wave equation with cylindrical symmetry.

**Symbols:** psi Einstein-Rosen potential

### (22.6) · central · DIV §22.2 p.453 (pdf 468)

$$
\frac{\partial\gamma}{\partial\rho}=\rho\left[\left(\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\rho}\right)^2+\left(\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}\right)^2\right]
$$

Radial gradient of gamma is fixed by an energy-density-like quadratic in psi derivatives, so gamma grows outward wherever the wave has energy.

**Symbols:** gamma second metric function

### (22.7) · central · DIV §22.2 p.453 (pdf 468)

$$
\frac{\partial\gamma}{\partial t}=2\rho\,\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\rho}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}
$$

Time derivative of gamma is a flux-like product; together with (22.6) gamma follows by quadrature.

### Integrability condition · supporting · DIV §22.2 p.453 (pdf 468)

$$
\gamma_{,\rho t}=\gamma_{,t\rho}
$$

Mixed derivatives of gamma must agree; this holds automatically once psi solves (22.5).

### (22.8) Bessel modes · supporting · DIV §22.2 p.453 (pdf 468)

$$
\psi=A\,\mathcal{J}_0(\omega\rho)\cos(\omega t)+B\,N_0(\omega\rho)\sin(\omega t)
$$

Separable monochromatic cylindrical waves; axis regularity forces B = 0.

**Symbols:** J0, N0 Bessel functions of first and second kind; omega angular frequency

### (22.9) Weber-Wheeler pulse · central · DIV §22.2 p.453 (pdf 468)

$$
\psi=2C\int_0^\infty e^{-a\omega}\mathcal{J}_0(\omega\rho)\cos(\omega t)\,d\omega=C\left[(a-it)^2+\rho^2\right]^{-1/2}+C\left[(a+it)^2+\rho^2\right]^{-1/2}
$$

A regular pulse built from J0 modes; the two terms are complex conjugates so psi is real.

**Symbols:** C amplitude constant (length); a > 0 pulse width

### gamma for the Weber-Wheeler pulse (corrected) · supporting · DIV §22.2 p.453 (pdf 468)

$$
\gamma=\tfrac12C^2\left\{a^{-2}-\rho^2\left[(a-it)^2+\rho^2\right]^{-2}-\rho^2\left[(a+it)^2+\rho^2\right]^{-2}-a^{-2}(t^2+a^2-\rho^2)\left[t^4+2t^2(a^2-\rho^2)+(a^2+\rho^2)^2\right]^{-1/2}\right\}

### (22.29) Generalized Rosen form · central · DIV §22.5 p.457 (pdf 472)

$$
ds^2=\ell\,du\,dv-f^2dy^2-g^2dz^2,\quad \ell,f,g\ \text{functions of}\ (u,v)
$$

Ansatz allowing waves moving in both directions.

**Symbols:** ell conformal factor of the (u,v) block

### Penrose-Khan solution · central · DIV §22.5 p.457 (pdf 472)

$$
\ell=\frac{m^3}{rw(pq+rw)^2},\quad f^2=m^2\left(\frac{r+q}{r-q}\right)\left(\frac{w+p}{w-p}\right),\quad g^2=m^2\left(\frac{r-q}{r+q}\right)\left(\frac{w-p}{w+p}\right)
$$

Exact vacuum space-time of two colliding impulsive plane waves.

### Penrose-Khan auxiliary functions · supporting · DIV §22.5 p.457 (pdf 472)

$$
p=u\theta(u),\quad q=v\theta(v),\quad r=\sqrt{1-p^2},\quad w=\sqrt{1-q^2},\quad m=\sqrt{1-p^2-q^2}
$$

p and q switch on after each wave; m vanishes on the singularity.

### Penrose-Khan regions · supporting · DIV §22.5 p.457 (pdf 472)

$$
\text{I: }u<0,v<0;\ \text{II: }0<u<1,v<0;\ \text{III: }u<0,0<v<1;\ \text{IV: }u>0,v>0,u^2+v^2<1
$$

Domain of validity divided into three flat regions and one curved interaction region.

### Szekeres colliding-wave metric · central · DIV §22.6 p.459 (pdf 474)

$$
ds^2=e^{-M}du\,dv-e^{-U}\left(e^{V}\cosh W\,dy^2-2\sinh W\,dy\,dz+e^{-V}\cosh W\,dz^2\right)
$$

General plane-symmetric collision metric; W = 0 for parallel constant polarizations.

**Symbols:** M, U, V, W functions of (u,v) (not the Brinkmann coordinates)

### Complex seed solution (Exercise 22.2) · derivation-step · DIV p.459 (pdf 474)

$$
\psi=\left[\rho^2-(t+ia)^2\right]^{-1/2}
$$

Complex solution of (22.5) whose real part gives the Weber-Wheeler pulse.

### Alternative Rosen normalization (Exercise 22.5) · derivation-step · DIV p.460 (pdf 475)

$$
ds^2=2\,d\bar u\,d\bar v-\bar f^2(\bar u)\,d\bar y^2-\bar g^2(\bar u)\,d\bar z^2
$$

Convention with a factor 2 on the null term, related to (22.10) by rescaling; the printed exercise has g-bar depending on v-bar, likely a slip.

## Worked examples

### Example · standard · DIV §22.2 p.452 (pdf 467)

**Problem:** Put the general whole-cylinder-symmetric metric into a canonical form with as few free functions as possible.

**Method:** Use discrete reflections to kill cross terms; choose the square root of the product of the orbit coefficients as radius and half the log of the z coefficient as psi; apply conformal flatness to the (t, rho) block and write the factor as exp(2 gamma - 2 psi).

**Key insight:** Symmetry plus two coordinate freedoms leave only two unknown functions, and the chosen radius makes the orbit area explicit.

**Result:** The Einstein-Rosen metric (22.4).

**Concepts:** whole-cylinder symmetry, conformal flatness of two-dimensional metrics, Einstein-Rosen metric

### Example · standard · DIV §22.2 p.453 (pdf 468)

**Problem:** Solve the Einstein-Rosen vacuum equations by separation of variables.

**Method:** Separate psi into a radial times a harmonic time factor; the radial equation is Bessel's of order zero; reject N0 for axis regularity; integrate (22.6)-(22.7) for gamma.

**Key insight:** The non-linear system has a linear core, so standard mathematical physics applies to psi.

**Result:** Standing-wave modes (22.8) with B = 0.

**Concepts:** Bessel-mode solutions, cylindrical wave equation

### Example · challenging · DIV §22.2 p.453 (pdf 468)

**Problem:** Build a localized cylindrical pulse and its gamma (Weber and Wheeler).

**Method:** Superpose J0 cos modes with an exponential weight; evaluate the integral using the Laplace-type transform of J0 to get a conjugate pair; integrate the gamma equations in closed form.

**Key insight:** A regular, finite-energy exact gravitational wave exists, answering the historical doubt.

**Result:** psi = C[(a-it)^2+rho^2]^{-1/2} + c.c.; gamma as displayed (with the misprints corrected).

**Concepts:** Weber-Wheeler pulse, integrability condition for gamma

### Example · standard · DIV §22.3 p.454 (pdf 469)

**Problem:** Decide whether the linearized h22 metric can be made an exact vacuum solution.

**Method:** Write it in double null coordinates with squared transverse coefficients, compute the four Christoffel symbols and two Riemann components, and contract to Ricci.

**Key insight:** Only one field equation survives, so an arbitrary function generates a full family of exact solutions.

**Result:** f''/f + g''/g = 0; define h = f''/f and solve g''/g = -h.

**Concepts:** Rosen form of a plane wave, linearly polarized exact plane gravitational wave, wave profile function h(u)

### Example · standard · DIV §22.3 p.455 (pdf 470)

**Problem:** Rewrite the Rosen plane wave so the profile appears explicitly in the metric.

**Method:** Apply U = u, V = v + y^2 ff' + z^2 gg', Y = fy, Z = gz; expand, cancel the cross terms, and use f'' = hf, g'' = -hg.

**Key insight:** The metric becomes flat space plus h(U)(Z^2 - Y^2) dU^2, linear in h, which exposes limited superposition.

**Result:** Brinkmann form (22.17). The printed sign h(U)(Z^2 - Y^2) is correct: expanding gives dU dV - dY^2 = du dv - f^2 dy^2 + y^2 f f'' du^2, and f f'' y^2 = h Y^2 (re-derived by hand in review, and consistent with the Brinkmann geodesic equation d^2Y/dU^2 = hY matching Y = f y with fixed y).

**Concepts:** Brinkmann form of a plane wave, exact superposition of co-propagating plane waves

### Example · standard · DIV §22.4 p.456 (pdf 471)

**Problem:** Construct a plane wave whose curvature is a delta function on a null plane.

**Method:** Choose f = 1 + u theta, g = 1 - u theta; differentiate using theta' = delta and u delta = 0; check (22.13); read off Riemann; translate to Brinkmann with h = delta.

**Key insight:** Distribution identities make the computation almost trivial and show the space-time is flat except on the front.

**Result:** R_0202 = -R_0303 = delta(u), Ricci = 0, flat metric (22.28) off u = 0.

**Concepts:** impulsive plane gravitational wave, Dirac delta distribution, Heaviside step function

### Example · challenging · DIV §22.5 p.458 (pdf 473)

**Problem:** Interpret the Penrose-Khan solution region by region.

**Method:** Split the (u, v) plane by the signs of u and v; evaluate where the metric is flat, where curvature has delta or step parts, where Rosen coordinates fold (u = 1, v = 1), and where curvature invariants diverge (m = 0); follow the x = 0 observer.

**Key insight:** Each wave focuses the other; the focusing of the interaction region ends in an unavoidable spacelike singularity reached in finite proper time.

**Result:** Regions I-III flat, IV curved; caustics at u = 1, v = 1; singularity at u^2 + v^2 = 1.

**Concepts:** Penrose-Khan solution, fold singularity, curvature singularity, spacelike versus timelike singularity

### Example · challenging · DIV §22.6 p.459 (pdf 474)

**Problem:** Set up the general colliding plane-wave problem (Szekeres).

**Method:** Pose data on u = 0 and v = 0 with flat region I; assume two translational Killing vectors everywhere; adopt the four-function metric; note one-variable dependence in II and III and W = 0 for collinear polarization.

**Key insight:** The collision becomes a well-posed characteristic problem, and the singularity persists for sandwich waves.

**Result:** Reduction to an integral for V and quadratures for M in the collinear case.

**Concepts:** characteristic initial value problem, Szekeres colliding-wave metric, collinear (parallel) polarization


## Analogies and intuitions

### Electromagnetic waves in vacuum pass through each other untouched, whereas gravitational waves need not. → non-linearity and failure of superposition · strong · DIV §22.3 p.455 (pdf 470)

Maxwell theory is used as the linear foil that makes the question of gravitational wave scattering meaningful.

**Where it breaks down:** Even in GR, co-propagating plane waves superpose exactly, so the contrast only bites for waves moving in different directions.

**App idea:** Split screen: two EM pulses crossing unchanged versus two gravitational pulses that emerge distorted and focused.

### An impulsive wave as the zero-thickness limit of a slab of curvature. → impulsive plane gravitational wave · useful · DIV §22.4 p.456 (pdf 471)

The sandwich wave is defined first as the physically sensible object; the delta-function wave is then read as its thin limit, justifying the idealization.

**Where it breaks down:** The limit keeps the integrated strength fixed; quantities quadratic in curvature (energy density) are ill-defined for a delta profile.

**App idea:** Thickness slider that squeezes a smooth profile into a spike while the particle trajectories converge to a kinked limit.

### A caustic as a seam in the wavefront hypersurface. → fold singularity · useful · DIV §22.5 p.458 (pdf 473)

Explains why the u = 1 line on the diagram is not a door to escape through: it is a lower-dimensional crease in v = 0 that observers essentially never hit.

**Where it breaks down:** The diagram suppresses two dimensions, so the 'seam' is hard to picture without a 3D view.

**App idea:** 3D view of the null hypersurface v = 0 with its generators converging and crossing along a highlighted crease.

### A plane gravitational wave acts like an astigmatic lens (our framing, not in the book). → mutual focusing and caustics · strong · DIV §22.4 p.456 (pdf 471)

Suggested for tutoring: f = 1 + u and g = 1 - u after an impulsive wave mean rays spread in y and converge in z, meeting at a focal distance of one unit, exactly like a cylindrical lens.

**Where it breaks down:** A lens acts on light in space; here the focusing acts on all geodesics and the 'focal length' is a null affine distance.

**App idea:** Ray-optics style panel: parallel null rays cross an impulsive wave and focus to a line; slider sets focal length.

### The Einstein-Rosen potential psi behaves like a scalar ripple on a pond spreading from a long rod. → cylindrical wave equation · useful · DIV §22.2 p.453 (pdf 468)

The book stresses that (22.5) is just the ordinary flat cylindrical wave equation, so all intuition about axisymmetric ripples transfers to psi.

**Where it breaks down:** The geometry feels the wave through gamma, which accumulates non-linearly; psi's linearity does not make the space-time linear.

**App idea:** Animate a Weber-Wheeler pulse imploding and re-expanding, with a second trace of gamma(t, rho) growing behind it.

### Suspecting that singularities come only from excessive symmetry, as once feared for Schwarzschild. → curvature singularity · useful · DIV §22.6 p.458 (pdf 473)

The authors recall the black-hole debate to motivate asking whether the collision singularity survives more general data, which Szekeres showed it does.

**Where it breaks down:** Robustness under relaxing impulsiveness does not settle robustness under breaking plane symmetry, which the chapter does not address.


## Misconceptions addressed

### A spherically pulsating or collapsing star should emit gravitational waves into the surrounding vacuum. · DIV §22.1 p.451 (pdf 466)

**Correction:** Birkhoff's theorem makes any spherically symmetric vacuum exterior static, so no spherical gravitational radiation exists; waves need less symmetry.

**Why tempting:** Spherical sound or light pulses are familiar, and Newtonian intuition does not connect symmetry to time dependence.

### Gravitational waves superpose linearly, just like electromagnetic waves. · DIV §22.3 p.455 (pdf 470)

**Correction:** The field equations are non-linear; only special cases such as co-propagating plane waves add exactly. Counter-propagating waves interact.

**Why tempting:** Chapter 21's linearized treatment and detector-physics experience are built on superposition.

### Any two gravitational waves must scatter each other because GR is non-linear. · DIV §22.3 p.455 (pdf 470)

**Correction:** Plane waves travelling in the same direction do not scatter: their Brinkmann profiles simply add.

**Why tempting:** Once non-linearity is emphasized, learners overgeneralize it to all configurations.

### Oblique collisions of plane waves need a separate, harder analysis. · DIV §22.3 p.455 (pdf 470)

**Correction:** A boost can always make the collision look head-on, so the head-on case is general.

**Why tempting:** In a single frame the angle looks like physical information.

### If the Ricci and Einstein tensors vanish, space-time must be flat. · DIV §22.4 p.456 (pdf 471)

**Correction:** Vacuum only kills Ricci; the Weyl part of Riemann can be non-zero, as the delta-function components of the impulsive wave show.

**Why tempting:** The vacuum field equations are often summarized as 'curvature is zero where there is no matter'.

### An impulsive gravitational wave curves the whole of space-time. · DIV §22.4 p.456 (pdf 471)

**Correction:** Curvature is confined to the null plane u = 0; on either side the metric is exactly Minkowski, although crossing the front changes the relative velocities of free particles, so the two flat regions are not joined trivially.

**Why tempting:** Waves are usually pictured as extended oscillations filling space.

### The Dirac delta is an ordinary function that is infinite at zero. · DIV §22.4 p.455 (pdf 470)

**Correction:** It is a distribution defined by its action under an integral; identities like u delta = 0 are statements about integrals against test functions.

**Why tempting:** Physics shorthand treats delta as a spike-shaped function.

### Einstein-Rosen waves are unphysical because their metric has singularities. · DIV §22.2 p.453 (pdf 468)

**Correction:** The singularities Einstein and Rosen worried about were coordinate artefacts; Weber and Wheeler showed regular, energy-carrying pulses.

**Why tempting:** Singular metric components look like singular physics without an invariant check.

### The caustics at u = 1 and v = 1 in the Penrose-Khan solution are curvature singularities. · DIV §22.5 p.458 (pdf 473)

**Correction:** They are fold singularities where null geodesics cross and the Rosen-type coordinates degenerate; curvature invariants remain finite there.

**Why tempting:** Metric functions vanish there and the diagram draws them with the same wavy line as the true singularity.

### An observer in region II can escape the collision by crossing the caustic at u = 1. · DIV §22.5 p.458 (pdf 473)

**Correction:** The caustic is a thin seam that is practically never hit, and anyone approaching it is caught by the second wave and carried into the interaction region, ending on the spacelike singularity.

**Why tempting:** In the suppressed-dimension diagram u = 1 looks like an open boundary leading away from the collision.

### The singularity from colliding waves is an artefact of impulsive (delta-function) waves or of excessive symmetry. · DIV §22.6 p.458 (pdf 473)

**Correction:** Szekeres' sandwich-wave collisions also become singular; the effect comes from mutual focusing, not from the delta profile.

**Why tempting:** Idealized sources often produce idealized pathologies, and similar suspicions once surrounded Schwarzschild.

### After the collision, the space-time loses all its planar symmetry. · DIV §22.5 p.458 (pdf 473)

**Correction:** The translational Killing vectors d/dy and d/dz persist throughout region IV (the Szekeres setup assumes it); what disappears is the pure plane-wave character of each wave. The book's phrase about losing planar symmetry should be read in that sense.

**Why tempting:** The text states that the waves no longer have planar symmetry after impact.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### Coordinate singularities of Rosen coordinates indicate something physical happens where f or g vanish. · DIV §22.3 p.455 (pdf 470)

**Correction:** Rosen coordinates tie the grid to freely falling particles, which a wave focuses; where they cross the chart fails. Brinkmann coordinates cover the same region smoothly.

**Why tempting:** The metric determinant vanishes, which is usually alarming.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*

### The exact plane wave is only valid for small amplitude because it came from linearized theory. · DIV §22.3 p.454 (pdf 469)

**Correction:** Linearized theory only suggested the ansatz; the Rosen metric solves the full equations for any profile h(u).

**Why tempting:** Equation (22.11) still contains the small parameter epsilon.

*Inferred: the book guards against this implicitly.*


## Thought experiments

### Riding through the collision at x = 0 · DIV §22.5 p.458 (pdf 473)

**Setup:** An observer at rest at x = 0 in the flat region before two counter-propagating impulsive plane waves arrive; both waves reach the observer at t = 0.

**Lesson:** After the simultaneous hit the observer is inside a curved interaction region and meets a curvature singularity after a finite proper time; nothing can be done to avoid it.

**App idea:** Cockpit view with a proper-time clock and a transverse ring of particles that first shears then collapses as the singularity approaches (about 0.54 focal units of proper time, our estimate).

### Crossing a sandwich wave · DIV §22.4 p.456 (pdf 471)

**Setup:** A freely falling observer with nearby test particles passes from flat region I through curved slab II into flat region III.

**Lesson:** Tidal accelerations act only during the finite crossing and only transversely; before and after, relative motion is uniform.

**App idea:** Timeline slider showing the ring of particles deforming only while the band passes the observer.

### Trying to escape through the caustic · DIV §22.5 p.458 (pdf 473)

**Setup:** An observer in region II heads toward the line u = 1 hoping to avoid the collision.

**Lesson:** The caustic is a measure-zero seam; approaching it means meeting the other wave and being carried into region IV's spacelike singularity.

**App idea:** Steerable world line on the Penrose-Khan map; every allowed path from region II ends on the singularity arc.

### Boosting an oblique collision head-on · DIV §22.3 p.455 (pdf 470)

**Setup:** Two plane waves cross at an angle in some frame; find observers moving so that the waves approach along a line.

**Lesson:** Head-on collision is a frame choice, so studying it loses no generality.

**App idea:** Aberration widget: rotate two wave vectors and slide a boost velocity until they are antiparallel.

### Colliding waves each trailed by null dust · DIV §22.6 p.459 (pdf 474)

**Setup:** Two planar impulsive waves, each followed by a stream of null dust, meet head-on.

**Lesson:** Exact solutions admit different interpretations (interpenetrating dust or a stiff fluid with p = rho), so formulation and interpretation must be handled with care.


## Applications and observations

- **Plane waves as far-zone models of real radiation** (astrophysical-system, DIV §22.3 p.455 (pdf 470)): The authors hope infinite plane waves capture properties of radiation from bounded sources far away. Our estimate of why this is reasonable at detectors: wavefront curvature radius equals the source distance, vastly larger than the wavelength. Key numbers: Our estimate for a GW150914-like event: distance about 400 Mpc (about 1e22 km) versus wavelength about 1e3 to 1e4 km, so the front is flat to about one part in 1e18 across a wavelength.
- **Proper time from collision to singularity for the x = 0 observer** (numerical-estimate, DIV §22.5 p.458 (pdf 473)): Our calculation, not in the book: along u = v = t the Penrose-Khan conformal factor is (1 - 2t^2)^{3/2}/(1 - t^2), so proper time is the integral of its square root from t = 0 to 1/sqrt(2). Key numbers: Coordinate time to singularity 1/sqrt(2), about 0.707; proper time about 0.543 (re-computed numerically in review: 0.5432), in units where each wave's focal length is 1.
- **Focal length of an impulsive plane wave** (numerical-estimate, DIV §22.4 p.456 (pdf 471)): Our reading of (22.24): after the wave, g = 1 - u, so particles initially separated in z at rest converge and meet at u = 1 while y-separations double by then. Restoring a strength parameter, f = 1 + u/L gives focal length L. Key numbers: Focusing at u = 1 in the book's normalization; the fold singularity of Fig. 22.4 sits exactly there.
- **Axis profile and asymptotic gamma of the Weber-Wheeler pulse** (numerical-estimate, DIV §22.2 p.453 (pdf 468)): Our evaluation from (22.9) and the corrected gamma: on the axis the pulse is Lorentzian in time; far from the axis gamma tends to a constant, which shows up as a conical deficit of the outer geometry tied to the pulse energy per unit length (Thorne's C-energy). Key numbers: psi(t, 0) = 2Ca/(a^2 + t^2); gamma(rho -> infinity) = C^2/a^2; gamma = 0 on the axis.

## Historical notes

- **Albert Einstein, Nathan Rosen (1937):** Found cylindrically symmetric exact wave solutions but doubted their physical reality because of apparent singularities. — First exact gravitational wave solutions; the episode shows how coordinate artefacts can mislead even the founders. (DIV §22.2 p.453 (pdf 468))
- **Joseph Weber, John A. Wheeler (1957):** Constructed a regular cylindrical pulse and showed that it carries energy (the book spells the first name 'Webber'). — Helped establish that gravitational waves are physical, shortly before Weber's detector programme. (DIV §22.2 p.453 (pdf 468))
- **Nathan Rosen:** Origin of the plane-wave line element with transverse scale factors that bears his name. — Rosen coordinates remain the standard starting point for colliding plane waves. (DIV §22.3 p.455 (pdf 470))
- **H. W. Brinkmann:** Origin of the plane-wave form with the profile explicit in a single metric coefficient. — Brinkmann coordinates are global and expose superposition and tidal structure. (DIV §22.3 p.455 (pdf 470))
- **Kip S. Thorne (1965):** Analysed energy in infinitely long cylindrical systems; source of the term whole-cylinder symmetry. — Recommended reading for Einstein-Rosen waves and their energy. (DIV §22.2 p.452 (pdf 467))
- **Roger Penrose, K. A. Khan:** Exact solution for colliding impulsive plane gravitational waves (published 1971; the book does not date it). — First demonstration that colliding plane waves focus each other into a spacelike singularity. (DIV §22.5 p.457 (pdf 472))
- **Peter Szekeres (early 1970s):** General characteristic-initial-value framework for colliding plane waves, with sandwich-wave solutions that also become singular. — Showed the singularity is not merely due to impulsive idealization; opened a large exact-solution literature. (DIV §22.6 p.458 (pdf 473))

## Notation and conventions

- **Metric signature:** (+, -, -, -): ds^2 = dt^2 - dx^2 - dy^2 - dz^2, so spatial terms carry minus signs. (DIV §22.3 p.454 (pdf 469))
- **Units:** Geometric units with c = 1 (the wavefront moves at 'Speed = 1'). — Impulsive-wave coordinates are further normalized so the focal length is 1. (DIV §22.4 p.456 (pdf 471))
- **Star-equals:** A star over the equals sign marks an equation valid only in the chosen (adapted) coordinates, e.g. V = d/dphi. (DIV §22.2 p.451 (pdf 466))
- **Double null coordinates:** u = t - x, v = t + x with flat metric du dv (no factor 2 and no 1/2). — Exercise 22.5 compares with the convention 2 du dv used by other authors. (DIV §22.3 p.454 (pdf 469))
- **Index labels for the Rosen metric:** x^a = (u, v, y, z) so 0 = u, 1 = v, 2 = y, 3 = z; primes denote d/du. — Inferred from the connection components (22.12), which we verified. (DIV §22.3 p.454 (pdf 469))

## Teaching gems

### A symmetry ladder: start from spherical symmetry, see Birkhoff kill radiation, drop to two commuting Killing vectors, and let circle-versus-line orbit topology choose cylindrical or plane waves. · DIV §22.1 p.451 (pdf 466)

**Why it works:** Learners see why these particular exact solutions exist instead of meeting them as a zoo.

**App idea:** Symmetry picker: toggle rotational and translational Killing fields on a 3D slice; the app reports what is forced (static, cylindrical, plane) and opens the matching wave demo.

### The Einstein-Rosen system hides a flat, linear wave equation inside non-linear GR; the non-linearity is pushed into gamma, which is found afterwards by integration. · DIV §22.2 p.453 (pdf 468)

**Why it works:** It demystifies exact solutions and lets learners reuse ordinary wave intuition.

**App idea:** Cylindrical ripple simulator: evolve psi from user-drawn initial data, then integrate gamma live and show the conical deficit building up at large radius.

### Promote a linearized solution to an exact ansatz: square the coefficients of the h22 metric and find that the full equations reduce to one ODE with an arbitrary function. · DIV §22.3 p.454 (pdf 469)

**Why it works:** Links Chapter 21 to exact theory and shows a reusable research tactic.

**App idea:** Toggle between linear h22 and exact Rosen f, g for a chosen profile; at large amplitude the linear version visibly fails (negative squared lengths) while the exact one stays consistent.

### One wave, two coordinate systems: Rosen coordinates ride with free particles (grid deforms), Brinkmann coordinates are a fixed grid with particles moving; each makes different facts obvious. · DIV §22.3 p.455 (pdf 470)

**Why it works:** Teaches coordinate freedom through a single concrete example with visible payoffs (caustics versus superposition).

**App idea:** Side-by-side transverse grids for the same plane wave, one comoving and one Brinkmann, with a slider through u and highlighted particle pairs.

### An impulsive wave is two flat space-times glued along a null plane with a delta of curvature: free particles get a position-proportional velocity kick, spreading one way and focusing the other. · DIV §22.4 p.456 (pdf 471)

**Why it works:** Curvature becomes a single, local, visualizable event rather than an abstract field.

**App idea:** Kick demo: a grid of dust particles passes through the front; arrows show the kick velocities; a timer shows z-neighbours meeting at the focal distance.

### Consistency check (ours): set q = 0 in the Penrose-Khan functions and region II collapses to l = 1, f = 1 + u, g = 1 - u, the single impulsive wave; the fold at u = 1 is simply where that wave's focusing brings z-neighbours together. · DIV §22.5 p.457 (pdf 472)

**Why it works:** Turns an opaque formula into something built from pieces the learner already understands and explains the caustic physically.

**App idea:** Formula dissector: hover regions of the Penrose-Khan map to see the metric functions simplify symbolically region by region.

### Read a collision off a labelled null diagram: flat, delta, step and divergent curvature each assigned to a region or boundary. · DIV §22.5 p.457 (pdf 472)

**Why it works:** Gives a compact mental map of a complicated exact solution without heavy algebra.

**App idea:** Penrose-Khan explorer with a curvature-type overlay and draggable observers carrying proper-time clocks.

### Colliding waves as a characteristic initial value problem: specify data on two intersecting null surfaces and the interaction region is determined. · DIV §22.6 p.458 (pdf 473)

**Why it works:** Shows a genuinely relativistic way of posing problems, complementary to 3+1 Cauchy data, and previews Chapter 23.

**App idea:** Draw incoming wave profiles on the two null edges and watch region IV fill in on a (u, v) grid.


## Gaps and pitfalls

- **Printed (22.9) has weight e^{a omega}; the integral diverges unless the weight is e^{-a omega} (a > 0). The closed form on the right is correct for e^{-a omega}, which we checked numerically.** — A careful reader cannot reproduce the closed form and may doubt the result. *Suggestion:* Use e^{-a omega} and derive via the Laplace transform of J0: integral of e^{-s omega} J0(omega rho) = (s^2 + rho^2)^{-1/2} with s = a -/+ it.
- **In the printed gamma for the Weber-Wheeler pulse the last bracket contains (a^2 + rho^2) instead of (a^2 + rho^2)^2; only the squared version is dimensionally consistent and satisfies (22.6)-(22.7) (we verified numerically).** — Anyone checking gamma against the field equations gets a mismatch. *Suggestion:* Teach the corrected formula and point out that the bracket is |(a - it)^2 + rho^2|^2.
- **'Webber' should be Weber (Joseph Weber).** — Makes the original paper hard to look up and obscures the link to Weber's detector work. *Suggestion:* Cite Weber and Wheeler, Reviews of Modern Physics (1957).
- **The text says the impulsive wave is like the plane wave of Fig. 22.1 except with a single front, then cites Fig. 22.1 again; the first reference presumably intended the linearized plane-wave figure of Chapter 21.** — Circular figure reference confuses what is being compared. *Suggestion:* Show the oscillatory linear plane wave (DIV Fig. 21.1) beside the impulsive one.
- **The Penrose-Khan discussion says an observer near the caustic in region II will be swept into region III. Along future-directed causal curves u and v cannot decrease, so from region II (u > 0) one can only enter region IV.** — Contradicts the causal structure the diagram is meant to teach. *Suggestion:* Read 'region III' as 'region IV' and demonstrate with the monotonicity of u and v along timelike curves.
- **Exercise 22.5 writes g-bar as a function of v-bar; for a plane wave in Rosen form both transverse functions depend on the same null coordinate u-bar.** — Learners may think the alternative convention allows v dependence and fail to match (22.10). *Suggestion:* Correct the argument to u-bar before assigning the exercise.
- **The step from surface-forming orbits to [V, W] = 0 is terse: Frobenius only puts the bracket in the span of V and W, and commutation relies on the abelian nature of the chosen symmetry groups.** — Readers may believe any two Killing vectors with 2-surface orbits commute. *Suggestion:* Note the counterexample of rotations on a sphere (orbits are surfaces but generators do not commute) and state commutation as part of the assumption.
- **The Einstein-Rosen reduction silently uses two facts it never states. First, the area radius (CD)^{1/2} can serve as a coordinate only if its gradient is spacelike (not automatic in cosmological settings). Second, after passing to conformal coordinates (t', rho') the primes are dropped as though rho' were still the area radius; that is legitimate only because the vacuum equations make (CD)^{1/2} a solution of the flat 2D wave equation in the (t, rho) block, so it can be taken as one conformal coordinate with its harmonic conjugate as time.** — A careful reader sees two coordinate choices (area radius and conformal gauge) that appear to use up more freedom than a 2-metric has, and cannot justify the final form (22.4). *Suggestion:* Before quoting (22.4), show that a combination of the vacuum equations makes sqrt(CD) a solution of the 2D wave equation of the (t, rho) block. In null coordinates it then splits as F(v) + G(u), so F + G and F - G can be used as new rho and t, which keeps the block conformally flat. State the spacelike-gradient assumption explicitly.
- **The Einstein-Rosen field equations (22.5)-(22.7) are quoted without derivation, and axis regularity (gamma = 0 on the axis to avoid a conical singularity) and the physical meaning of gamma (C-energy) are not discussed.** — gamma appears as an auxiliary function with no geometric meaning; the energy claim of Weber and Wheeler is unsupported. *Suggestion:* Add a short computation (or symbolic-algebra notebook) and explain that the large-radius value of gamma measures a deficit angle related to energy per unit length.
- **The statement that the waves lose planar symmetry after impact is loose: d/dy and d/dz remain Killing vectors in region IV.** — Conflicts with the Szekeres assumption two paragraphs later that both Killing vectors exist everywhere. *Suggestion:* Say instead that the scattered waves are no longer pure plane (null, type N) waves; link to the Petrov classification in Chapter 23.
- **No amplitude parameter appears in f = 1 + u theta(u); the Penrose-Khan coordinates are silently normalized so each wave has unit focal length, which also fixes the positions of the caustics and singularity.** — Learners may think u = 1 and u^2 + v^2 = 1 are universal rather than set by wave strength. *Suggestion:* Reintroduce strengths via f = 1 + u theta/a, g = 1 - u theta/a and show the diagram scaling.
- **The physical effect of a plane wave on free particles is only gestured at (transverse acceleration in a sandwich wave); no geodesic or geodesic-deviation equation is written for the exact wave.** — The focusing that drives the whole Penrose-Khan story is never shown quantitatively. *Suggestion:* Derive the Brinkmann transverse equations Y'' = hY, Z'' = -hZ and apply them to h = delta.
- **Rosen coordinates generically break down where f or g vanish even for smooth waves; the text mentions fold singularities only in the collision context.** — Misreading metric-function zeros as physical singularities. *Suggestion:* Emphasize that Brinkmann coordinates are global for plane waves and that Rosen coordinates follow a focusing congruence.
- **Heavy symbol reuse: V, W as Killing vectors; U, V as Brinkmann coordinates; U, V, W, M as Szekeres metric functions; rho as radius and as density; C as function and constant.** — Easy to conflate objects when moving between sections. *Suggestion:* Use distinct symbols in app and tutor materials (e.g. xi, eta for Killing vectors).
- **Section 22.6 presents singularity formation as the generic outcome, reflecting the 1970s view. Later work (for example Chandrasekhar and Xanthopoulos in the mid-1980s) found colliding-wave solutions with Killing-Cauchy horizons instead, subsequently argued to be unstable.** — An oversimplified picture of the literature. *Suggestion:* Mention the horizon-forming solutions and their instability, pointing to Griffiths (1991).
- **The avoidability of timelike and unavoidability of spacelike singularities is asserted without argument.** — Readers without the black-hole chapters take it on faith. *Suggestion:* Show on the Penrose-Khan diagram that the arc u^2 + v^2 = 1 lies in the future of every point in region IV.

## Tutor notes

- Open with: 'Chapter 21 treated waves as tiny ripples that pass through each other. What might change if the ripples are not tiny?' Then ask why a pulsating spherical star cannot radiate (Birkhoff).
- Order of explanation: symmetry ladder, Einstein-Rosen (linear core), Rosen plane waves (one ODE), Brinkmann form (superposition), impulsive wave (distributions), Penrose-Khan diagram, Szekeres framework.
- Before 22.2, check the learner is comfortable with Killing vectors as symmetries and with conformal flatness in 2D; before 22.4, with the delta as a distribution.
- When presenting (22.9) and the gamma formula, use the corrected versions (e^{-a omega}; (a^2 + rho^2)^2) and tell the learner the book has misprints so they do not lose confidence.
- Check understanding: 'Where is the curvature of an impulsive wave, and what is Ricci there?' Expected: only on u = 0, delta-like Riemann, Ricci zero everywhere.
- Build physical intuition with the lens picture: after an impulsive wave, y-separations grow and z-separations shrink linearly, meeting at u = 1. Then reveal that Penrose-Khan region II is exactly this and that u = 1 is the caustic.
- Check understanding: 'Which of u = 1 and u^2 + v^2 = 1 is a real singularity, and how would you tell?' Expected: the latter, because curvature invariants diverge.
- If the learner asks why co-moving waves superpose but colliding ones do not: point to the Brinkmann metric being linear in h and the absence of any cross-coupling when both profiles depend on the same U.
- If the learner asks whether this is relevant to LIGO: plane waves are excellent local models far from sources, but amplitudes are about 1e-21, far too weak for collision singularities to matter astrophysically.
- Fix the causal slip on p. 458: from region II an observer can only move into region IV, not III; use it as an exercise in reading null coordinates.
- Common question: 'Do the waves lose their symmetry after the collision?' Answer: the two translational Killing vectors survive; the waves stop being pure plane waves.
- For advanced learners, connect the collision singularity to focusing (Raychaudhuri-type arguments) and to the characteristic initial value problem of Chapter 23.
- Keep symbols distinct when speaking: say 'Brinkmann U' versus 'Szekeres function U', and 'radius rho' versus 'density rho'.
