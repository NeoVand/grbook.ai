# Matter, fields and the stress-energy tensor

How matter and non-gravitational fields are described in relativity and why they gravitate. Starts from the continuum picture and rest-frame (MCRF) quantities: number density, number flux and particle conservation, energy density and its gamma-squared transformation. Builds the stress-energy tensor as a table of four-momentum fluxes, with its symmetry, the dust and perfect-fluid models, equations of state and the trace. Covers local energy-momentum conservation in flat and curved spacetime and its consequences: the relativistic Euler and energy equations, relativistic thermodynamics, adiabatic flow, geodesic motion of dust and the limits of global conservation. Includes Newtonian fluid mechanics as the limit, covariant electromagnetism with the Maxwell stress-energy tensor, scalar-field and vacuum stress-energy, the eigenframe classification and the energy conditions. Excludes the variational (Hilbert) definition of T, pressure as a source in the field equations, stellar and cosmological equations of state, and uses of energy conditions inside singularity theorems.

| Concept | Tier | Summary | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [[continuum-approximation]] Continuum approximation and fluid elements | foundation | Matter made of enormous numbers of particles is described by smooth fields such as density, velocity and pressure, obtained by averaging over fluid elements that are large compared with the particle spacing but small compared with the scale on which the averages change. | — |
| [[fluid]] Fluid | foundation | A fluid is a continuum in which forces acting along the interface between neighbouring elements (shear) are weak compared with the normal push of pressure, so the material keeps deforming under any shear and flows. | — |
| [[momentarily-comoving-reference-frame]] Momentarily comoving reference frame | foundation | The momentarily comoving reference frame (MCRF) of a particle or fluid element at an event is the inertial frame in which it is instantaneously at rest; every scalar property of a fluid (number density, energy density, pressure, temperature, entropy) is defined by its value measured there. | — |
| [[four-velocity-field]] Four-velocity field of a flow | foundation | A flow is described by a unit timelike vector field u whose value at each event is the four-velocity of the fluid element there; its integral curves (flow lines or streamlines) form a non-crossing congruence filling spacetime. | — |
| [[continuity-equation]] Continuity equation | foundation | A quantity is locally conserved when its density changes only by flow through the boundary: the time derivative of the density plus the divergence of its flux vanishes, which in four-vector form says the four-divergence of the current is zero. | — |
| [[pressure]] Pressure | foundation | Pressure is the isotropic normal stress of a fluid: in the rest frame of an element it is the force per unit area across any surface, the same in every direction, and it equals the momentum flux carried across the surface by forces or by moving particles. | — |
| [[convective-derivative]] Convective derivative | prerequisite | The convective (material) derivative gives the rate of change of a quantity following a fluid element: the local rate of change at a fixed point plus the change due to being carried along the velocity, d/dt = partial_t + v.grad. | — |
| [[euler-equation]] Euler equation (Newtonian) | prerequisite | Euler's equation is Newton's second law per unit volume for an inviscid fluid: mass density times convective acceleration equals minus the pressure gradient plus the gravitational (body) force density. | — |
| [[hydrostatic-equilibrium]] Hydrostatic equilibrium | prerequisite | A fluid at rest in a gravitational field is in hydrostatic equilibrium when its pressure gradient exactly balances gravity, grad p = -rho grad Phi; for uniform density in uniform gravity pressure rises linearly with depth (Pascal's law). | — |
| [[vorticity]] Vorticity and irrotational flow | prerequisite | Vorticity is the curl of a fluid velocity field and measures the local rotation of fluid elements; flow with zero vorticity is irrotational, and on a simply connected region its velocity is the gradient of a velocity potential. | — |
| [[bernoulli-equation]] Bernoulli's equation | prerequisite | Bernoulli's equation expresses energy conservation for inviscid flow: for steady isentropic flow, v^2/2 plus the gravitational potential plus the specific enthalpy is constant along each streamline, and for irrotational flow a version holds throughout the fluid. | — |
| [[number-density]] Number density | foundation | The number density n of a fluid is the number of particles per unit volume measured in the MCRF; an observer who sees the particles moving with speed v measures gamma n, because the box containing them is Lorentz contracted. | — |
| [[number-flux-four-vector]] Number-flux four-vector | foundation | The number-flux four-vector N = n u packages particle density and particle flux: its time component in any frame is the density measured there and its spatial components are the fluxes of particles across surfaces of constant x^i. | — |
| [[conservation-of-particle-number]] Conservation of particle number | foundation | If particles are neither created nor destroyed the number-flux vector is divergence-free, (n u^a)_{,a} = 0 in flat spacetime and (n u^a)_{;a} = 0 in curved spacetime; in practice this is applied to baryon number or rest mass. | — |
| [[rest-mass-density]] Rest-mass density | foundation | The rest-mass density rho_0 = m n is the rest mass of the particles per unit volume in the MCRF; it differs from the total energy density rho, which also counts thermal, chemical and interaction energy, and the two coincide only for dust. | — |
| [[energy-density]] Energy density of matter | foundation | The energy density rho of a fluid is its total energy per unit volume measured in the MCRF, including rest mass plus internal energy (thermal, chemical, interaction), so rho = n(m + Pi) = rho_0(1 + epsilon). | — |
| [[transformation-of-energy-density]] Gamma-squared transformation of energy density | foundation | An observer who sees dust of rest-frame energy density rho moving at speed v measures energy density gamma^2 rho: one gamma from the larger energy of each particle and one from Lorentz contraction of the volume. Because two factors of gamma appear, energy density cannot be a component of a four-vector and needs a rank-2 tensor. | — |
| [[stress-energy-tensor]] Stress-energy tensor | foundation | The stress-energy tensor T is the symmetric rank-2 tensor whose component T^{ab} is the flux of the a-component of four-momentum across a surface of constant x^b: T^00 is energy density, T^0i energy flux, T^i0 momentum density and T^ij stress. It describes all non-gravitational matter and fields and is the source of gravity in general relativity. | — |
| [[stress-tensor]] Stress tensor | foundation | The spatial block T^ij of the stress-energy tensor is the stress tensor: the flux of i-momentum across a surface with normal along j, equivalently the i-component of force per unit area exerted across that surface, with normal stresses (pressures) on the diagonal and shear stresses off it. | — |
| [[symmetry-of-stress-energy-tensor]] Symmetry of the stress-energy tensor | foundation | The stress-energy tensor is symmetric, T^{ab} = T^{ba}: in the MCRF T^{0i} = T^{i0} because energy flux and momentum density are the same thing when energy is inertia, and T^{ij} = T^{ji} because otherwise small elements would suffer unbounded angular acceleration. | — |
| [[dust]] Dust | foundation | Dust is matter whose particles share one velocity at each event and do not interact, so it has no pressure or other stress; its stress-energy tensor is T = rho u (x) u, the outer product of the momentum carried per particle with the number-flux vector. | — |
| [[point-particle-stress-energy-tensor]] Point-particle stress-energy tensor | core | A point particle of mass m on world line z(tau) can be treated with field tools by writing its densities as proper-time integrals of a four-dimensional delta function, giving T^{ab}(x) = m integral of u^a u^b delta^4(x - z(tau)) dtau, which is nonzero only on the world line. | — |
| [[local-conservation-of-energy-momentum]] Local conservation of energy-momentum | foundation | In special relativity the stress-energy tensor of any closed system is divergence-free, T^{ab}_{,b} = 0: in every inertial frame the rate of change of the density of each four-momentum component is balanced by the divergence of its flux. | — |
| [[viscosity-and-heat-conduction]] Viscosity and heat conduction | foundation | Viscosity is the tangential force between neighbouring fluid elements and appears as off-diagonal stresses T^{ij} in the MCRF, while heat conduction is energy transport without bulk motion and appears as nonzero T^{0i} (and T^{i0}) there; their absence defines a perfect fluid. | — |
| [[perfect-fluid]] Perfect fluid | foundation | A perfect fluid has no viscosity and no heat conduction in the rest frame of each element, so it is fully described by its energy density rho, isotropic pressure p and four-velocity u; it is the relativistic generalization of an ideal inviscid fluid. | — |
| [[perfect-fluid-stress-energy-tensor]] Perfect-fluid stress-energy tensor | foundation | The stress-energy tensor of a perfect fluid is T^{ab} = (rho + p) u^a u^b + p g^{ab} (signature -+++), which is diag(rho, p, p, p) in the fluid rest frame; it is found by writing the rest-frame matrix and recognizing a tensor expression that reproduces it. | — |
| [[trace-of-stress-energy-tensor]] Trace of the stress-energy tensor | foundation | The trace T = g_{ab} T^{ab} is a scalar; for a perfect fluid it equals -rho + 3p (signature -+++), so it is -rho for dust and vanishes for radiation and for the electromagnetic field. | — |
| [[projection-tensor]] Projection tensor orthogonal to the four-velocity | foundation | For a unit timelike vector u the tensor h_{ab} = g_{ab} + u_a u_b removes the component of any vector along u, leaves vectors orthogonal to u unchanged, and acts as the spatial metric of the local rest space of that observer; its trace is 3. | — |
| [[equation-of-state]] Equation of state | foundation | An equation of state is the relation fixed by microphysics that gives a fluid pressure in terms of other state variables, p = p(rho, S) or p = p(n, T), reducing to a barotropic p = p(rho) when entropy or temperature is uniform; it closes the fluid equations. | — |
| [[radiation-equation-of-state]] Radiation equation of state | foundation | A gas of photons or of any ultra-relativistic particles has pressure equal to one third of its energy density, p = rho/3, which is exactly the condition that its stress-energy tensor be traceless. | — |
| [[relativistic-first-law-of-thermodynamics]] Relativistic first law of thermodynamics | foundation | For a fluid element with a fixed number of particles, energy conservation in the MCRF reads d rho = ((rho + p)/n) dn + n T ds, where s is entropy per particle; the combination rho + p appears because compressing the element does p dV work. | — |
| [[relativistic-euler-equation]] Relativistic Euler equation | foundation | The part of the conservation law of a perfect fluid orthogonal to u gives (rho + p) nabla_u u^a = -h^{ab} partial_b p: fluid elements accelerate down the pressure gradient measured in their rest space, with rho + p as the inertia. | — |
| [[inertial-mass-density]] Inertial mass density rho + p | foundation | In relativistic fluid dynamics the resistance of a fluid element to acceleration is measured by rho + p, not rho: pressure contributes to inertia, which matters when pressure is comparable to energy density. | — |
| [[perfect-fluid-energy-equation]] Energy equation for a perfect fluid | foundation | Contracting the conservation law of a perfect fluid with u gives the energy equation d rho/d tau = -(rho + p) nabla.u: the energy density following a fluid element falls both by dilution as the element expands and by the p dV work its pressure does. | — |
| [[adiabatic-flow]] Adiabatic flow of a perfect fluid | foundation | Comparing the energy equation of a particle-conserving perfect fluid with the first law shows that each element keeps its entropy per particle, u^a s_{,a} = ds/dtau = 0: perfect-fluid flow is adiabatic (isentropic along flow lines). | — |
| [[speed-of-sound]] Relativistic speed of sound | advanced | Small adiabatic disturbances of a fluid travel at the sound speed c_s given by c_s^2 = (dp/d rho) at fixed entropy; linearizing particle conservation and the relativistic Euler equation about a static background gives a wave equation with this speed. | — |
| [[newtonian-limit-of-fluid-equations]] Newtonian limit of relativistic fluid dynamics | foundation | When bulk speeds are small, pressure is small compared with energy density (p << rho c^2) and gravity is weak, the relativistic conservation laws reduce to the Newtonian continuity and Euler equations, and the stress-energy tensor is dominated by T^00 = rho c^2. | — |
| [[covariant-conservation-of-energy-momentum]] Covariant conservation of energy-momentum | core | In curved spacetime local energy-momentum conservation becomes nabla_b T^{ab} = 0: it holds in every local inertial frame, contains the equations of motion of matter, and is guaranteed by the field equations through the contracted Bianchi identity. | — |
| [[geodesic-motion-of-dust]] Geodesic motion of dust | core | For dust, covariant conservation of T splits into rest-mass conservation along u and u^b nabla_b u^a = 0 orthogonal to it, so dust particles follow timelike geodesics; more generally a small body exchanging no energy-momentum with its surroundings moves on a geodesic. | — |
| [[global-energy-momentum-conservation]] Global conservation laws for matter in curved spacetime | core | Covariant conservation of T gives a conserved total energy or momentum of matter only when the spacetime has a corresponding symmetry (a Killing vector); in a generic curved spacetime matter and gravity exchange energy and there is no global conservation law for matter alone. | — |
| [[four-current]] Four-current | foundation | The four-current J^a = (rho, j) combines the charge density and current density of electromagnetism (or of any conserved scalar quantity) into one four-vector; it sources the electromagnetic field and local charge conservation is d_a J^a = 0. | — |
| [[faraday-tensor]] Faraday tensor | foundation | The Faraday (electromagnetic field) tensor F_{ab} is an antisymmetric rank-2 tensor whose time-space components are the electric field and space-space components the magnetic field; E and B therefore mix under Lorentz transformations as parts of one object. | — |
| [[electromagnetic-four-potential]] Electromagnetic four-potential | foundation | The four-potential A_a combines the scalar and vector potentials into a one-form field whose antisymmetrized derivatives give the Faraday tensor; it is defined only up to adding the gradient of a scalar (a gauge transformation). | — |
| [[maxwell-equations-in-tensor-form]] Maxwell equations in tensor form | foundation | In special relativity Maxwell's equations become d_b F^{ab} = J^a (Gauss and Ampere-Maxwell laws) and d_{[a} F_{bc]} = 0 (no monopoles and Faraday's law), manifestly Lorentz covariant once E, B, rho and j are assumed to transform as parts of F and J. | — |
| [[maxwell-equations-in-curved-spacetime]] Maxwell equations in curved spacetime | core | By minimal coupling, Maxwell's equations in curved spacetime are nabla_b F^{ab} = J^a and partial_{[a} F_{bc]} = 0; the second may keep partial derivatives because the symmetric connection terms cancel under antisymmetrization. | — |
| [[maxwell-stress-energy-tensor]] Maxwell stress-energy tensor | foundation | The electromagnetic field carries stress-energy T^{ab} = F^{ac} F^b_c - (1/4) g^{ab} F_{cd} F^{cd} (Heaviside-Lorentz units, signature -+++): its time-time part is the field energy density, time-space part the Poynting flux, and space-space part minus the Maxwell stress. It is symmetric, traceless, and divergence-free when the source-free Maxwell equations hold. | — |
| [[superposition-of-stress-energy-tensors]] Superposition of stress-energy tensors | foundation | The stress-energy tensor of several non-interacting components is the sum of their separate tensors; for interacting components only the total is conserved, and the exchange between them expresses their mutual forces. | — |
| [[scalar-field-stress-energy-tensor]] Stress-energy tensor of a scalar field | core | A real scalar field with potential V has T_{ab} = partial_a phi partial_b phi - g_{ab}[(1/2) g^{cd} partial_c phi partial_d phi + V(phi)]; in flat space its energy density (1/2) phi-dot^2 + (1/2)/grad phi/^2 + V is positive for V >= 0. | — |
| [[scalar-field-as-perfect-fluid]] Energy density and pressure of a homogeneous scalar field | core | A spatially uniform scalar field behaves as a perfect fluid with rho = phi-dot^2/2 + V and p = phi-dot^2/2 - V: kinetic energy adds to both, while potential energy adds to density but subtracts from pressure, so a potential-dominated field has p close to -rho. | — |
| [[vacuum-stress-energy-tensor]] Vacuum stress-energy tensor | core | Vacuum energy has stress-energy tensor T^vac_{ab} = -rho_vac g_{ab}, the only form that looks the same to every observer; it is a perfect fluid with p = -rho, and moving a cosmological-constant term to the source side gives exactly this with rho_vac = Lambda/(8 pi G). | — |
| [[null-dust]] Null dust | advanced | Null dust is incoherent radiation modelled by a dust-like stress-energy tensor T^{ab} = rho k^a k^b with a null vector k in place of the timelike four-velocity; it describes streams of massless particles all moving in one direction at light speed. | — |
| [[hawking-ellis-classification]] Eigenframe classification of the stress-energy tensor | advanced | Solving the eigenvalue problem T^a_b v^b = lambda v^a for ordinary matter gives one timelike and three spacelike eigenvectors; in that orthonormal frame T is diagonal with the energy density as one eigenvalue and the principal stresses p_1, p_2, p_3 as the others. | — |
| [[energy-conditions]] Energy conditions | core | Energy conditions are inequalities on the stress-energy tensor, such as non-negative energy density for every observer or causal energy flow, that express what physically reasonable matter should satisfy; they are the matter inputs to focusing and singularity theorems. | — |
| [[weak-energy-condition]] Weak energy condition | core | The weak energy condition requires every observer to measure non-negative energy density, T_{ab} v^a v^b >= 0 for all timelike v; for a perfect fluid it means rho >= 0 and rho + p >= 0. | — |
| [[dominant-energy-condition]] Dominant energy condition | core | The dominant energy condition requires every observer to measure non-negative energy density and a causal (timelike or null) energy flux, so energy never flows faster than light; for a perfect fluid it means rho >= /p/. | — |
| [[strong-energy-condition]] Strong energy condition | core | The strong energy condition requires (T_{ab} - (1/2) T g_{ab}) v^a v^b >= 0 for all timelike v, which through the Einstein equation is R_{ab} v^a v^b >= 0; for a perfect fluid it means rho + 3p >= 0 and rho + p >= 0, i.e. gravity is attractive for timelike geodesics. | — |
