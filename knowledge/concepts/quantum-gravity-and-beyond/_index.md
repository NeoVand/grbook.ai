# Quantum effects, quantum gravity and beyond Einstein

Where general relativity meets quantum physics and where it may be modified. Semiclassical effects (vacuum fluctuations, quantum fields on curved backgrounds, the Unruh effect, Hawking radiation and temperature, Bekenstein-Hawking entropy, the laws of black-hole mechanics, evaporation, the generalized second law and the information problem); gravity as a quantum field (Planck scales, force carriers, graviton exchange, spin-2 polarizations and trace subtraction, non-renormalizability and effective field theory); geometric unification and extra dimensions (Kaluza-Klein theory, compactification, braneworlds); candidate quantum theories (string theory and its world-sheet mechanics, supersymmetry, holography and AdS/CFT, canonical, Euclidean and loop quantum gravity, twistors); quantum and philosophical cosmology at the frontier (cosmological constant problem, quantum cosmology, anthropic arguments, Weyl curvature hypothesis); and classical alternatives to general relativity (Brans-Dicke, torsion theories). Classical black-hole theorems, classical field theory, the PPN testing machinery, inflation and elementary quantum mechanics belong to their own domains.

| Concept | Tier | Summary | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [[quantum-gravity]] Quantum gravity | advanced | The still-missing theory that consistently combines general relativity with quantum theory; it is needed wherever gravity is strong and quantum effects matter at once, such as the Big Bang, black-hole singularities and the end of black-hole evaporation. | — |
| [[planck-units]] Planck units | advanced | The unique length, time and energy that can be formed from G, c and hbar: l_P = sqrt(hbar G/c^3) ~ 1.6e-35 m, t_P = sqrt(hbar G/c^5) ~ 5.4e-44 s and E_P = m_P c^2 ~ 1.2e19 GeV. They mark the scale at which quantum effects and gravity are expected to be simultaneously important, far beyond any accelerator. | — |
| [[planck-mass]] Planck mass | advanced | The mass sqrt(hbar c/G) ~ 2.2e-8 kg (about 1.2e19 GeV); in hbar = c = 1 units its square is the coefficient that makes the Einstein-Hilbert action dimensionless, so it sets how weakly gravity couples. It also marks where a black hole is so small that a single Hawking quantum carries a sizable fraction of its mass and semiclassical physics fails. | — |
| [[planck-scale-localization-limit]] Planck-scale localization limit | frontier | Confining a particle to a region of size L needs energy of order hbar c/L, whose gravitational radius grows as L shrinks; the two meet at the Planck length, so no experiment can resolve smaller distances without forming a black hole. This is a common heuristic for why spacetime geometry itself should be quantized. | — |
| [[vacuum-fluctuations]] Vacuum fluctuations | advanced | In quantum field theory the vacuum is not static: it continually creates short-lived virtual particle pairs, allowed by the energy-time uncertainty relation to borrow an energy Delta E for a time of order hbar/Delta E before annihilating. | — |
| [[particles-as-field-excitations]] Particles as quantized field excitations | advanced | In quantum field theory each particle species is a quantized excitation of a field: a classical field whose wave solutions carry energy is promoted to an operator that creates and annihilates quanta. Gravitational waves, as excitations of the metric perturbation, would correspondingly be made of gravitons. | — |
| [[quantum-field-theory-in-curved-spacetime]] Quantum field theory in curved spacetime | advanced | The semiclassical framework in which quantum fields propagate on a fixed classical curved metric that is not itself quantized; it underlies the Unruh effect and Hawking radiation and fails when the back-reaction of the quanta on the geometry becomes large, near the Planck scale. | — |
| [[path-integral]] Path integral (sum over histories) | advanced | Feynman's formulation of quantum mechanics: the amplitude to go from one configuration to another is a sum of exp(iS/hbar) over every possible history, with S the classical action; classical motion emerges where neighbouring phases add constructively. | — |
| [[spacetime-foam]] Spacetime foam | frontier | The expectation that the metric, like any quantum field, has zero-point fluctuations that grow to order unity at the Planck length, so that at that scale no single smooth geometry describes space. | — |
| [[unruh-effect]] Unruh effect | advanced | A uniformly accelerated observer in the Minkowski vacuum detects a thermal bath at temperature k_B T = hbar a/(2 pi c), whereas an inertial observer sees no particles; particle content therefore depends on the observer. For a = g the temperature is only about 4e-20 K. | — |
| [[horizon-pair-creation-picture]] Horizon pair-creation picture | advanced | A heuristic for Hawking radiation: a virtual pair forms just outside the horizon, one member crosses to where negative Killing energy is allowed and need not recombine, and the other escapes as a real quantum. Requiring the infalling partner to reach the horizon within the fluctuation lifetime gives an escaping energy of order hbar/M. | — |
| [[hawking-radiation]] Hawking radiation | advanced | Black holes emit thermal, black-body radiation because of quantum field effects near the horizon, and the energy carried away comes out of the hole's mass. Hawking's 1974 calculation showed that the spectrum is exactly thermal at the Hawking temperature. | — |
| [[hawking-temperature]] Hawking temperature | advanced | The temperature of a black hole's thermal emission, k_B T = hbar kappa/(2 pi); for Schwarzschild it is hbar c^3/(8 pi G M k_B), about 6e-8 K for one solar mass. It is inversely proportional to mass, so smaller holes are hotter. | — |
| [[wick-rotation]] Wick rotation and Euclidean periodicity | advanced | Continuing time to imaginary values turns the near-horizon Schwarzschild metric into a flat plane in polar coordinates, which is smooth at the origin only if Euclidean time is periodic with period 8 pi M; since a thermal state has imaginary-time period hbar/k_B T, this reproduces the Hawking temperature. | — |
| [[black-hole-thermodynamics]] Black-hole thermodynamics | advanced | The correspondence in which a black hole behaves as a thermodynamic body: mass plays the role of internal energy, surface gravity (times hbar/2 pi k_B) of temperature and horizon area (times k_B/4 hbar) of entropy, so that dM = T dS and the area theorem acts as a second law. | — |
| [[first-law-of-black-hole-mechanics]] First law of black-hole mechanics | advanced | Neighbouring stationary black holes obey dM = (kappa/8 pi) dA + Omega_H dJ + Phi_H dQ in G = c = 1 units, relating changes in mass to changes in horizon area, angular momentum and charge. Read with T = hbar kappa/2 pi, it is dM = T dS plus work terms. | — |
| [[bekenstein-hawking-entropy]] Bekenstein-Hawking entropy | advanced | The entropy of a black hole, S = k_B c^3 A/(4 G hbar) = k_B A/(4 l_P^2), one quarter of the horizon area in Planck units. It follows from the first law once the Hawking temperature is known, and its enormous size measures how much information about the collapsed matter the horizon hides. | — |
| [[laws-of-black-hole-mechanics]] Laws of black-hole mechanics | advanced | Four statements about stationary black holes that mirror thermodynamics: surface gravity is constant over the horizon (zeroth), dM = kappa dA/8 pi + Omega dJ + Phi dQ (first), horizon area never decreases classically (second), and kappa cannot be reduced to zero in finitely many steps (third). | — |
| [[negative-heat-capacity-of-black-holes]] Negative heat capacity of black holes | advanced | Because a Schwarzschild hole's temperature scales as 1/M, adding energy cools it and removing energy heats it: C = dM/dT = -hbar/(8 pi k_B T^2) in G = c = 1 units. A black hole therefore cannot sit in stable equilibrium with an infinite heat bath, and its evaporation runs away. | — |
| [[black-hole-evaporation]] Black-hole evaporation | advanced | An isolated black hole loses mass to Hawking radiation at a rate proportional to 1/M^2 (horizon area times T^4), so it heats up as it shrinks and its lifetime scales as M^3: about 1e10 yr for 1e12 kg but roughly 1e67 yr for a solar mass. Real astrophysical holes are colder than the cosmic microwave background and are still growing. | — |
| [[penrose-diagram-of-evaporating-black-hole]] Penrose diagram of an evaporating black hole | advanced | The conformal diagram of a star that collapses and whose black hole later evaporates completely: the event horizon and spacelike singularity exist only for a finite stretch, after which a regular centre continues to future timelike infinity. It is drawn on the provisional assumption that nothing is left behind. | — |
| [[generalized-second-law]] Generalized second law | advanced | The total of black-hole entropy (k_B A/4 hbar) plus ordinary entropy outside black holes never decreases. It rescues the second law when Hawking evaporation shrinks the horizon area, since the emitted radiation carries at least the entropy lost. | — |
| [[unitarity-of-quantum-evolution]] Unitarity of quantum evolution | prerequisite | Closed quantum systems evolve by unitary operators, so the state at one time fixes the state at any other and information is never destroyed; this is why complete black-hole evaporation into thermal radiation would be troubling. | — |
| [[black-hole-information-problem]] Black-hole information problem | frontier | If a black hole evaporates completely into exactly thermal radiation, the quantum information in the matter that formed it seems to be destroyed, contradicting unitary evolution. Whether subtle correlations in the radiation, remnants, or new physics carry the information out is unresolved. | — |
| [[field-description-of-gravity]] Field description of gravity | foundation | Describing gravity by a field defined at every event, which each body both sources and responds to locally, instead of by direct action at a distance between bodies; in general relativity that field is the metric itself. | — |
| [[gravity-as-a-spin-2-field]] Gravity as a spin-2 field on flat spacetime | advanced | The programme, associated with Feynman's gravitation lectures, of treating weak gravity as a massless symmetric tensor field h_mu_nu on fixed Minkowski space, coupled to energy-momentum and quantized like other gauge fields. It reproduces linearized general relativity and gravitons, but the flat background is a useful fiction that fails beyond first order. | — |
| [[feynman-diagram]] Feynman diagram | prerequisite | A pictorial shorthand for one term in the perturbative expansion of a scattering amplitude: external lines for incoming and outgoing particles, vertices for couplings, and internal lines for propagators of virtual particles. | — |
| [[scattering-amplitude]] Scattering amplitude | prerequisite | The complex amplitude whose squared modulus gives the probability for incoming states to scatter into outgoing ones; at lowest order for exchange between two sources it has the form source times propagator times source. | — |
| [[force-carrying-particle]] Force-carrying particle | advanced | In quantum field theory an interaction between two sources arises from the virtual exchange of a mediating particle (Yukawa's idea); the mediator's mass sets the range of the force and its spin sets the tensor character and sign of the interaction. | — |
| [[yukawa-potential]] Yukawa potential | advanced | The static potential from exchanging a particle of mass m, proportional to e^{-r/lambda}/r with range lambda = hbar/(mc); it is the point-source solution of the static Klein-Gordon equation, and only a massless mediator gives a long-range 1/r law. | — |
| [[exchange-force-sign-rule]] Spin of the mediator and sign of the force | advanced | Exchange of an odd-spin boson (such as the spin-1 photon) makes like charges repel and unlike charges attract, while exchange of an even-spin boson (spin 0 or 2) gives a force of one sign between all sources; universal attraction is why the graviton must have even spin. | — |
| [[field-rank-and-particle-spin]] Field rank and particle spin | advanced | Massless quanta of spin 0, 1 and 2 are described by a scalar, a vector (one-form) and a symmetric rank-2 tensor, and they couple to sources of matching rank: a density, a current and the stress-energy tensor. Hence photons carry spin 1 and gravitons spin 2. | — |
| [[helicity]] Helicity | advanced | The component of a particle's spin along its direction of motion: a polarization state of helicity h picks up a phase e^{i h theta} under a rotation by theta about the momentum. A massless particle of spin S has only the two helicities +S and -S, so photons have h = +-1 and gravitons h = +-2. | — |
| [[photon-exchange-amplitude]] Photon exchange amplitude | advanced | The lowest-order amplitude for two currents to interact by exchanging a virtual photon, J_a . J_b/k^2 with propagator -i eta/(k^2 + i epsilon). Using current conservation k_mu J^mu = 0 splits it into an instantaneous charge-density term, which is Coulomb's law, and a retarded term containing only the two transverse polarizations. | — |
| [[graviton]] Graviton | advanced | The hypothetical quantum of the gravitational field: a massless particle of spin 2 with two helicity states. Its masslessness follows from the exact 1/r Newtonian potential and its even spin from the universally attractive character of gravity; it appears automatically in string theory. | — |
| [[graviton-exchange-amplitude]] Graviton exchange amplitude | advanced | The lowest-order amplitude for two energy-momentum distributions to interact by exchanging one virtual graviton, built from the linear coupling h_mu_nu T^mu_nu: after trace correction it is (-T_a . T_b + (1/2) T_a T_b)/k^2. Energy-momentum conservation k_mu T^mu_nu = 0 separates it into instantaneous and propagating parts. | — |
| [[newtonian-limit-of-graviton-exchange]] Newtonian limit of graviton exchange | advanced | The instantaneous T^00 T^00 part of the graviton exchange amplitude Fourier transforms to the Newtonian potential energy between mass densities; its sign is opposite to the corresponding Coulomb term, which is why like gravitational sources attract. | — |
| [[graviton-trace-subtraction]] Trace subtraction in graviton exchange | advanced | A symmetric rank-2 source contains a scalar trace, so the naive graviton amplitude also exchanges a spurious spin-0 mode and shows three transverse structures; adding (1/2) T_a T_b/k^2 cancels it and leaves only helicity +-2. The required factor 1/2 is exactly the trace reversal of the Einstein equation, obtained without geometry. | — |
| [[graviton-polarization]] Graviton polarizations | advanced | For a graviton moving along z only the transverse traceless combinations T^11 - T^22 and T^12 couple to the propagating part of the exchange amplitude, giving plus and cross polarization tensors that match the two linear polarizations of classical gravitational waves; their circular combinations have helicity +-2. | — |
| [[graviton-propagator]] Graviton propagator | advanced | The tensor D that contracts two stress-energy tensors into the graviton exchange amplitude, T_a D T_b; in harmonic (de Donder) gauge it is proportional to (eta eta + eta eta - eta eta)/k^2, where the last product implements the trace subtraction. | — |
| [[renormalization]] Ultraviolet divergences and renormalization | advanced | In perturbative quantum field theory, point-like interactions make loop integrals diverge at short distances; renormalization absorbs these divergences into a finite number of redefined parameters, which works for the Standard Model but not for gravity. | — |
| [[non-renormalizability-of-quantum-gravity]] Non-renormalizability of perturbative quantum gravity | advanced | Because Newton's constant has dimensions of inverse energy squared in natural units, graviton scattering grows order by order like (G E^2)^n and its divergences cannot be absorbed into finitely many parameters. Perturbative quantum gravity is predictive only for energies far below the Planck energy. | — |
| [[effective-field-theory]] Effective field theory | advanced | A description valid below some energy scale that keeps the terms in the action with the fewest derivatives and represents unknown short-distance physics by higher-derivative terms suppressed by powers of a small length; renormalization-group reasoning explains how its parameters depend on scale. | — |
| [[higher-curvature-corrections]] Higher-curvature corrections to the Einstein-Hilbert action | frontier | Scalar terms quadratic in curvature (R^2, R_mu_nu R^mu_nu, R_abcd R^abcd) added to the Einstein-Hilbert Lagrangian with dimensionless coefficients times a length squared; they matter only where curvature radii approach that length, so they are invisible in ordinary tests. | — |
| [[effective-field-theory-of-gravity]] Effective field theory of gravity | frontier | The view that general relativity, though non-renormalizable, can be quantized and used reliably for processes far below a cutoff near the Planck energy, with quantum corrections organized as higher-derivative terms; above the cutoff a more fundamental theory must take over. | — |
| [[unification-of-fundamental-interactions]] Unification of fundamental interactions | frontier | The programme of describing distinct interactions, originally gravity and electromagnetism and today gravity plus the Standard Model, as manifestations of one underlying theory; its ultimate form is sometimes called a theory of everything. | — |
| [[extra-dimension]] Extra spatial dimension | frontier | An additional spacelike coordinate appended to the four of spacetime, labelled x^5 or z, so that events in the enlarged spacetime carry five labels; the linearized metric perturbation and its gauge law carry over to five dimensions unchanged in form. | — |
| [[gauge-transformation-as-fifth-coordinate-shift]] Gauge transformation as a shift of the fifth coordinate | frontier | If the mixed metric components are h_mu5 = l A_mu, the five-dimensional coordinate change x^5 -> x^5 + l chi(x^mu) reproduces exactly the electromagnetic gauge transformation A -> A - d chi, so electromagnetic gauge freedom becomes part of five-dimensional general covariance. With the fifth dimension compact, this is an independent rotation of the internal circle at each event. | — |
| [[compactification]] Compactification | frontier | Making an extra spatial dimension finite and periodic, for example identifying z with z + 2 pi a so that every point of four-dimensional spacetime carries a tiny circle; physics at scales much larger than a is unchanged, which explains why the dimension is unobserved. | — |
| [[kaluza-klein-metric]] Kaluza-Klein metric | frontier | The five-dimensional line element ds^2 = g_mu_nu dx^mu dx^nu + (dz + l A_mu dx^mu)^2, which packages the four-dimensional metric and the electromagnetic potential into one geometry; the bracket is invariant under the combined shift z -> z + l chi and A -> A - d chi. | — |
| [[kaluza-klein-reduction]] Kaluza-Klein reduction | frontier | Integrating the five-dimensional Einstein-Hilbert action over the compact circle: for the Kaluza-Klein metric the 5D Ricci scalar equals the 4D Ricci scalar minus F_mu_nu F^mu_nu/4, so pure 5D gravity yields 4D gravity plus Maxwell's Lagrangian, and the 4D Planck mass is fixed by m_P^2 = 2 pi a m_K^3. | — |
| [[electric-charge-as-extra-dimensional-momentum]] Electric charge as momentum in the fifth dimension | frontier | A neutral particle moving freely in the Kaluza-Klein geometry obeys, in the four ordinary dimensions, the Lorentz force law, with its conserved momentum around the compact circle (times l) playing the role of electric charge. Charge becomes motion in the hidden direction. | — |
| [[kaluza-klein-charge-quantization]] Charge quantization in Kaluza-Klein theory | frontier | A single-valued quantum wavefunction around a circle of radius a allows only momenta p = n hbar/a; since charge is l times that momentum, every electric charge is an integer multiple of an elementary unit l/a. | — |
| [[kaluza-klein-theory]] Kaluza-Klein theory | frontier | The theory in which four-dimensional spacetime is extended by a fifth spatial dimension compactified on a small circle, so that pure five-dimensional gravity reproduces four-dimensional gravity plus electromagnetism, with gauge transformations as circle shifts and charge as circle momentum. It is the prototype of geometric unification and of extra dimensions in string theory. | — |
| [[kaluza-klein-tower]] Kaluza-Klein tower | frontier | A quantum particle that can move around a compact dimension of radius a has its momentum there quantized in units of hbar/a, adding a ladder of heavy states above the ordinary ones; since probing the extra dimension needs momentum of order hbar/a, a microscopic a hides it from all low-energy experiments. | — |
| [[higher-dimensional-gravity]] Gravity in higher dimensions | frontier | With d spatial dimensions Newtonian gravity spreads its flux over a (d-1)-sphere, so the force falls as 1/r^(d-1); if some dimensions are compact with volume V_c, distant observers see ordinary inverse-square gravity with G = G^(D)/V_c. The weakness of gravity could thus reflect large extra dimensions, and the true higher-dimensional Planck length could be much larger than l_P. | — |
| [[relativistic-string]] Relativistic string | frontier | A one-dimensional extended object with no internal parts, taken as fundamental in string theory; it carries a tension T0 (energy per unit length) that plays the role rest mass plays for a point particle, and its vibration modes appear as different particle species. | — |
| [[world-sheet]] World sheet | frontier | The two-dimensional surface X^mu(tau, sigma) swept out by a moving string, the analogue of a particle's world line; the spacetime metric induces on it a 2x2 metric gamma_ab = eta_mu_nu dX^mu/dxi^a dX^nu/dxi^b. | — |
| [[nambu-goto-action]] Nambu-Goto action | frontier | The action of a relativistic string, minus its tension times the proper area of its world sheet, S = -T0 integral sqrt(-gamma) dtau dsigma; it is invariant under relabelling of the world sheet, and its Euler-Lagrange equations state that the world-sheet momentum currents are conserved. | — |
| [[world-sheet-gauge-choice]] World-sheet reparametrization gauge | frontier | Because the Nambu-Goto action does not depend on how the world sheet is labelled, the parameters tau and sigma may be chosen to simplify the equations, like choosing an affine parameter for a world line. The static gauge tau = t makes each constant-tau curve the string at one coordinate time. | — |
| [[string-wave-equation]] String wave equation | frontier | In static gauge, with sigma chosen so the velocity is normal to the string, the Nambu-Goto equations reduce to the wave equation d^2X/dt^2 = d^2X/dsigma^2 plus constraints; only the transverse velocity is physical, and solutions are left- and right-moving waves that would become particles after quantization. | — |
| [[open-and-closed-strings]] Open and closed strings | frontier | An open string has two endpoints, which may be free or attached to branes, and sweeps out a ribbon-like world sheet; a closed string is a loop sweeping out a tube. The massless spin-2 graviton appears among closed-string states. | — |
| [[d-brane]] D-brane | frontier | An extended object of p spatial dimensions on which the endpoints of open strings are fixed; the D stands for the Dirichlet boundary conditions that pin the endpoint coordinates transverse to the brane. | — |
| [[string-theory]] String theory | frontier | A candidate unified quantum theory in which elementary particles are vibration modes of tiny strings in higher-dimensional spacetime; because it contains a massless spin-2 graviton and softens the short-distance divergences of point-particle graviton exchange, it is a quantum theory of gravity, but it lacks direct experimental confirmation. | — |
| [[supersymmetry]] Supersymmetry | frontier | A proposed symmetry that turns bosons into fermions and vice versa, giving every particle a superpartner of opposite statistics; since no equal-mass partners are seen it must be broken if it exists, and its lightest partners are dark-matter candidates. | — |
| [[superstring-theory]] Superstring theory | frontier | String theory with supersymmetry, whose Planck-length strings have both bosonic and fermionic states; its various versions are thought to be limits of a single M-theory that also contains higher-dimensional membranes, though none has yet produced falsifiable predictions. | — |
| [[braneworld]] Braneworld | frontier | A scenario, arising from string theory, in which our universe is a four-dimensional brane in a higher-dimensional bulk: non-gravitational forces are confined to the brane while gravity extends into the bulk, modifying the inverse-square law at short distances (tested down to tens of micrometres). Matter on a nearby parallel brane would be felt only through gravity. | — |
| [[holographic-principle]] Holographic principle | frontier | The proposal, associated with 't Hooft and suggested by black-hole entropy scaling with area, that the physics of a gravitating region can be fully encoded by degrees of freedom on its lower-dimensional boundary, as a hologram stores a 3D image on a 2D plate. | — |
| [[ads-cft-correspondence]] AdS/CFT correspondence | frontier | Maldacena's conjecture that certain quantum gravity (string) theories in (d+1)-dimensional anti-de Sitter spacetime are exactly equivalent to conformal field theories without gravity on its d-dimensional timelike boundary, with the radial bulk direction corresponding to energy scale in the boundary theory. | — |
| [[canonical-quantization-of-gravity]] Canonical quantization of gravity | frontier | Quantizing general relativity by first putting it in Hamiltonian form, where constraints generate the gauge freedom, and then promoting the canonical variables (for example Ashtekar variables) to operators that must annihilate physical states. | — |
| [[superspace]] Superspace (space of 3-geometries) | frontier | Wheeler's configuration space for quantum geometrodynamics: the set of all spatial 3-geometries, to each of which a quantum state assigns an amplitude; a classical spacetime is a single path through it, while a quantum state spreads over many. | — |
| [[einstein-hamilton-jacobi-equation]] Einstein-Hamilton-Jacobi equation | frontier | General relativity in Hamilton-Jacobi form: a functional differential equation for an action S on superspace, quadratic in dS/dgamma_ij through the DeWitt supermetric plus a 3-curvature term, which with the momentum constraint encodes the Einstein equations and is the semiclassical limit of the Wheeler-DeWitt equation. | — |
| [[euclidean-quantum-gravity]] Euclidean quantum gravity | frontier | Hawking's approach that defines quantum-gravity amplitudes as path integrals over Riemannian (positive-definite) metrics obtained by rotating time to imaginary values; its simplest success is recovering black-hole temperature and entropy from smooth Euclidean geometries. | — |
| [[angular-momentum-algebra]] Angular momentum algebra | prerequisite | The commutation relations [L_i, L_j] = i hbar epsilon_ijk L_k imply that L^2 takes the values hbar^2 j(j+1) with integer or half-integer j and that only L^2 and one component can be sharp at once; it is the model for how commutators generate quantization. | — |
| [[loop-quantum-gravity]] Loop quantum gravity | frontier | A non-perturbative approach that quantizes spatial geometry directly and finds discrete spectra for area and volume at the Planck scale, so the smooth manifold is replaced by a network of quantum cells; in cosmological applications the Big Bang can become a bounce. | — |
| [[quantum-of-area]] Quantum of area | frontier | If the area vectors of a tetrahedron's faces (which classically sum to zero, the closure condition) obey angular-momentum-like commutators with a Planck-scale constant, each face area takes only discrete values proportional to sqrt(j(j+1)) and the directions of face normals cannot all be sharp, so geometry is fuzzy at the Planck scale. | — |
| [[quantum-of-volume]] Quantum of volume | frontier | In the quantized tetrahedron the volume operator commutes with the four face areas and the closure constraint, so states can be labelled by four spins and a volume eigenvalue, and volume changes only in discrete steps. | — |
| [[spin-network]] Spin network | frontier | The graph that labels a quantum state of geometry in loop quantum gravity: nodes are chunks of volume, links are shared faces carrying area quanta, and closed paths through the graph are the loops that give the theory its name. | — |
| [[quantum-gravity-bounce]] Quantum gravity bounce | frontier | The proposal, found in loop quantum cosmology and other approaches, that quantum-gravity effects halt a collapse or a backward-extrapolated expansion before a singularity forms, producing a rebound; a Big Bang would then be a bounce from an earlier contracting phase. | — |
| [[spinor]] Spinor | frontier | An object transforming under the spin group, the double cover of the Lorentz group; two-component complex spinors live in a 2D space whose metric is antisymmetric, and vectors and tensors can be built from them, giving an efficient language for relativity and quantum physics. | — |
| [[twistor-theory]] Twistor theory | frontier | Penrose's programme that recasts spacetime geometry in a complex projective space of twistors, in which light rays rather than events are fundamental, as a possible route to quantum gravity. | — |
| [[cosmological-constant-problem]] Cosmological constant problem | frontier | If the cosmological constant is the zero-point energy of quantum fields, naive estimates of the vacuum energy density exceed the observed dark-energy density by tens to over a hundred orders of magnitude, and why Lambda is so small remains unexplained. | — |
| [[quantum-cosmology]] Quantum cosmology | frontier | The application of quantum theory to the universe as a whole, needed for the earliest epochs near the Planck time and entangled with the interpretation problems of quantum mechanics, since there is no outside observer. | — |
| [[weyl-curvature-hypothesis]] Weyl curvature hypothesis | frontier | Penrose's conjecture that the initial cosmological singularity had very small or zero Weyl curvature, unlike black-hole singularities, so the universe began in an extremely smooth, low gravitational-entropy state that accounts for the thermodynamic arrow of time. | — |
| [[conformal-cyclic-cosmology]] Conformal cyclic cosmology | frontier | Penrose's proposal of an endless sequence of aeons in which the conformally rescaled remote future of one universe becomes the Big Bang of the next; it is offered as an alternative to inflation. | — |
| [[anthropic-principle]] Anthropic principle | frontier | The explanatory stance, named by Carter, that observed features of the universe are constrained by the requirement that observers like us can exist in it, so some apparent coincidences are selection effects. | — |
| [[weak-anthropic-principle]] Weak anthropic principle | frontier | Only some regions or epochs of the universe allow life, so we necessarily observe one of them; for example we find the universe billions of years old because galaxies, heavy elements, planets and evolution take that long. | — |
| [[strong-anthropic-principle]] Strong anthropic principle | frontier | In d'Inverno and Vickers's version: among many universes (quantum branches, or disjoint regions with their own conditions or laws) only a few permit life, so the apparent fine-tuning of fundamental constants is a selection effect rather than evidence of design. | — |
| [[arrow-of-time]] Arrows of time | frontier | The distinct directions singled out in time: the thermodynamic arrow of increasing entropy, the cosmological arrow of expansion and the psychological arrow of experience; why they agree is an open question sometimes tied to low-entropy initial conditions or anthropic reasoning. | — |
| [[alternative-theories-of-gravity]] Alternative theories of gravity | advanced | Relativistic, generally covariant theories of gravity other than general relativity, usually with extra fields besides the metric; the physical criteria behind Einstein's equations do not single them out uniquely, but precision tests since the 1960s have excluded many rivals and none is simpler. | — |
| [[brans-dicke-theory]] Brans-Dicke theory | advanced | The 1961 scalar-tensor alternative to general relativity, motivated by Mach's principle, in which a dynamical scalar field sets the effective gravitational constant so that G can vary with cosmic epoch; its coupling omega gives PPN gamma = (1 + omega)/(2 + omega), recovering general relativity as omega -> infinity. | — |
| [[torsion-based-gravity]] Torsion-based theories of gravity | frontier | Formulations such as Einstein-Cartan and teleparallel gravity in which the connection has torsion, so that torsion carries gravitational effects alongside or instead of curvature; Einstein-Cartan theory couples torsion to the spin density of matter. | — |
