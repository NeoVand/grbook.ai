# Connection, covariant derivative and parallel transport

How to differentiate tensor fields when the basis changes from point to point, and how that defines parallelism. Starts from curvilinear coordinates and position-dependent (coordinate and noncoordinate) bases in flat space, shows why partial derivatives of components are not tensorial, introduces Christoffel symbols and their inhomogeneous transformation law, and builds the covariant derivative of vectors, one-forms, general tensors and tensor densities with the Leibniz rule. Then the affine connection and its axioms, the covariant (absolute) derivative along a curve, parallel transport and its equation, torsion and torsion-free connections, metric compatibility, the Levi-Civita connection and its uniqueness, the Christoffel formula from the metric, the contracted Christoffel identity and divergence formula, second covariant derivatives, geodesic coordinates, covariant versus coordinate acceleration, parallel-propagated frames and Fermi-Walker transport. Geodesics, path dependence and curvature, Riemann normal coordinates, connection forms and gauge connections are owned elsewhere.

| [[comparing-vectors-at-different-points]] Comparing vectors at different points | foundation | Vectors at two different points of a manifold live in different tangent spaces, so a bare manifold offers no invariant way to subtract them or call them equal. Differentiating a vector field therefore needs extra structure: a flow (giving the Lie derivative) or a connection (giving the covariant derivative). | — |
| [[curvilinear-coordinates]] Curvilinear coordinates | foundation | Curvilinear coordinates label flat space with coordinate lines that are not straight, uniformly spaced and mutually perpendicular, polar coordinates being the standard example. Because the increments relate to Cartesian ones only through a position-dependent matrix of partial derivatives, Pythagoras no longer holds in its naive form. | — |
| [[position-dependent-basis]] Position-dependent basis vectors | foundation | In curvilinear coordinates the coordinate basis vectors and basis one-forms change in direction and length from point to point: in plane polars e_theta has length r and d-theta has length 1/r. A constant vector field can therefore have non-constant components, which is why differentiating components alone does not differentiate the vector. | — |
| [[noncoordinate-basis]] Noncoordinate basis | foundation | A noncoordinate basis is a field of basis vectors that are not the partial-derivative directions of any coordinate system, such as the unit polar vectors r-hat and theta-hat = (1/r) d/d theta. Its vectors generally fail to commute, so connection coefficients in such a basis need not be symmetric in their lower indices even for a torsion-free connection. | — |
| [[physical-versus-coordinate-components]] Physical versus coordinate components | foundation | The same vector has different components in a coordinate basis and in the normalized (orthonormal) basis built from it; in polar coordinates a vector with coordinate components (a, b) has orthonormal components (a, r b). Formulas from vector-calculus books usually use the orthonormal ones, so they look different from tensor formulas while describing the same quantity. | — |
| [[non-tensoriality-of-partial-derivatives]] Partial derivatives of tensors are not tensors | foundation | Under a general coordinate change the partial derivatives of vector components pick up, besides the tensor term, an extra term with second derivatives of the coordinate transformation, so they do not form a tensor. The extra term vanishes only for linear transformations such as Lorentz transformations, which is why flat-space inertial calculus never needed a correction. | — |
| [[christoffel-symbols]] Christoffel symbols (connection coefficients) | foundation | Christoffel symbols Gamma^mu_{alpha beta} are the coefficients that express how basis vectors change from point to point: the derivative of a basis vector along a coordinate direction, expanded back on the basis. In flat plane polar coordinates the only non-zero ones are Gamma^r_{theta theta} = -r and Gamma^theta_{r theta} = Gamma^theta_{theta r} = 1/r. | — |
| [[covariant-derivative]] Covariant derivative | foundation | The covariant derivative nabla differentiates a tensor field while accounting for the changing basis, so its result is again a tensor; for a vector V^alpha_{;beta} = V^alpha_{,beta} + Gamma^alpha_{mu beta} V^mu. On a scalar it is just the partial derivative, and in a local inertial frame or Cartesian coordinates it reduces to ordinary partial differentiation. | — |
| [[leibniz-rule-for-covariant-derivatives]] Leibniz rule for covariant derivatives | foundation | Covariant differentiation obeys the ordinary product rule for tensor products and commutes with contraction: differentiate each factor in turn and add. This rule, together with the requirement that a scalar differentiates to its gradient, fixes how the covariant derivative acts on one-forms and on higher-rank tensors. | — |
| [[covariant-derivative-of-a-one-form]] Covariant derivative of a one-form | foundation | For a one-form the connection term enters with the opposite sign: p_{alpha;beta} = p_{alpha,beta} - Gamma^mu_{alpha beta} p_mu. The sign follows from applying the Leibniz rule to the scalar p_alpha V^alpha, or equivalently from the fact that basis one-forms must change oppositely to basis vectors to keep their pairing constant. | — |
| [[covariant-derivative-of-a-tensor]] Covariant derivative of a general tensor | foundation | The covariant derivative of a tensor of any rank is the partial derivative of its components plus one Christoffel term for each upper index and minus one for each lower index, each contracted into that index. The result has one more lower index than the original tensor. | — |
| [[transformation-law-of-the-connection]] Transformation law of the connection | foundation | Under a coordinate change the Christoffel symbols transform like a (1,2) tensor plus an inhomogeneous term built from second derivatives of the coordinate transformation, so they are not tensor components and can vanish in one chart but not another. That extra term exactly cancels the non-tensorial part of the partial derivative, and the difference of two connections is a genuine tensor. | — |
| [[nonzero-christoffel-symbols-in-flat-space]] Non-zero Christoffel symbols do not imply curvature | foundation | Christoffel symbols can be non-zero in perfectly flat space simply because curvilinear coordinates are used, as in plane polar coordinates, so their presence says nothing about curvature. Only a tensor built from them and their derivatives, the Riemann tensor, can decide whether a space is curved. | — |
| [[affine-connection]] Affine connection | foundation | An affine connection is the extra structure on a manifold that defines parallelism between neighbouring points and hence a covariant derivative. Abstractly it is an operator nabla that is linear over functions in its direction slot, additive, and obeys the Leibniz rule; in components it is a set of coefficients Gamma^a_{bc} with the inhomogeneous transformation law. | — |
| [[covariant-derivative-along-a-curve]] Covariant derivative along a curve | foundation | Along a curve with tangent u the covariant (absolute) derivative is DV/dlambda = nabla_u V, with components dV^mu/dlambda + Gamma^mu_{alpha beta} u^alpha V^beta. It needs the field only on the curve and is the tensorial replacement for an ordinary rate of change along a path. | — |
| [[parallel-transport]] Parallel transport | foundation | Parallel transport carries a vector (or tensor) along a curve so that it undergoes no intrinsic change: its covariant derivative along the tangent vanishes, nabla_u V = 0. In a local inertial frame its components are momentarily constant, and all apparent change of components comes from the turning basis. | — |
| [[parallel-transport-equation]] Parallel transport equation | foundation | In components parallel transport reads dV^mu/dlambda + Gamma^mu_{alpha beta} u^alpha V^beta = 0, a first-order linear ordinary differential equation along the curve. It therefore has a unique solution for any starting vector, and the change in components exactly compensates the change of basis. | — |
| [[covariant-derivative-via-parallel-transport]] Covariant derivative via parallel transport | foundation | The covariant derivative can be defined as a limit: carry the vector at the neighbouring point back by parallel transport, subtract the vector already there, and divide by the separation. Transport puts both vectors in the same tangent space, so the difference is meaningful. | — |
| [[torsion-tensor]] Torsion tensor | advanced | The torsion of a connection is the (1,2) tensor T(u,v) = nabla_u v - nabla_v u - [u,v], with coordinate components Gamma^a_{bc} - Gamma^a_{cb}. It is a tensor because the inhomogeneous term in the connection transformation law is symmetric in the lower indices; general relativity sets it to zero. | — |
| [[torsion-free-connection]] Torsion-free (symmetric) connection | foundation | A torsion-free connection has vanishing torsion, which in any coordinate basis means Gamma^mu_{alpha beta} = Gamma^mu_{beta alpha}, and index-free means nabla_u v - nabla_v u = [u,v]. It is the connection assumed in general relativity; its symmetry can be proved by requiring second covariant derivatives of a scalar to commute. | — |
| [[metric-compatibility]] Metric compatibility | foundation | A connection is metric compatible when the covariant derivative of the metric vanishes, nabla g = 0 (and so does that of the inverse metric). Equivalently parallel transport preserves all inner products, lengths and angles, and indices can be raised and lowered freely through covariant derivatives. | — |
| [[levi-civita-connection]] Levi-Civita connection | foundation | On a (pseudo-)Riemannian manifold there is exactly one connection that is both torsion-free and metric compatible, the Levi-Civita (metric) connection. Its coefficients are the Christoffel symbols built from first derivatives of the metric, and general relativity uses only this connection. | — |
| [[christoffel-symbols-from-the-metric]] Christoffel symbols from the metric | foundation | For the Levi-Civita connection in a coordinate basis, Gamma^mu_{alpha beta} = (1/2) g^{mu nu} (g_{nu alpha,beta} + g_{nu beta,alpha} - g_{alpha beta,nu}). It follows from writing metric compatibility for three cyclic permutations of the indices, adding two, subtracting the third and using the symmetry of Gamma. | — |
| [[christoffel-symbols-of-the-first-kind]] Christoffel symbols of the first kind | foundation | The Christoffel symbols of the first kind are the all-lower-index combinations Gamma_{lambda mu nu} = (1/2)(g_{lambda mu,nu} + g_{lambda nu,mu} - g_{mu nu,lambda}), symmetric in the last two indices. Raising the free index with the inverse metric gives the usual symbols of the second kind. | — |
| [[contracted-christoffel-symbol-identity]] Contracted Christoffel symbol identity | foundation | Contracting the upper index of the Levi-Civita Christoffel symbols with a lower one gives Gamma^alpha_{mu alpha} = d_mu ln sqrt/g/, with g the metric determinant. It follows from the metric formula for Gamma and Jacobi formula for the derivative of a determinant. | — |
| [[covariant-divergence-formula]] Covariant divergence formula | foundation | The covariant divergence of a vector field can be computed without Christoffel symbols: V^alpha_{;alpha} = (1/sqrt/g/) d_alpha (sqrt/g/ V^alpha). The sqrt/g/ factors account for coordinate cells changing their physical volume, and in plane polars it reproduces (1/r) d_r(r V^r) + d_theta V^theta. | — |
| [[second-covariant-derivative]] Second covariant derivative | foundation | Applying the covariant derivative twice gives a tensor such as the covariant Hessian phi_{;mu nu}, whose trace with the inverse metric is the curved-spacetime wave operator. For a scalar and a torsion-free connection the two derivatives commute, but on vectors and higher tensors they generally do not, and by convention indices after the semicolon are applied left to right. | — |
| [[covariant-derivative-of-a-tensor-density]] Covariant derivative of a tensor density | advanced | For a tensor density of weight W the covariant derivative takes all the connection terms of a tensor with the same indices and subtracts W Gamma^d_{dc} times the density, giving a density of the same weight. With this rule the metric determinant, sqrt(-g) and the Levi-Civita symbol are covariantly constant. | — |
| [[geodesic-coordinates]] Geodesic coordinates | foundation | For a symmetric connection one can always choose coordinates about a point P in which all connection coefficients vanish at P, by adding to any chart a quadratic term whose coefficients are Gamma(P). Covariant derivatives then equal partial derivatives at P, which makes tensor identities easy to prove there, though derivatives of Gamma cannot be removed in general. | — |
| [[covariant-acceleration]] Covariant acceleration | core | The physical acceleration of a particle is the covariant derivative of its four-velocity along itself, a = nabla_u u, which an attached accelerometer measures and which vanishes in free fall. It differs from the coordinate acceleration d^2x/dt^2 by Christoffel terms, and in non-inertial coordinates on flat spacetime those terms are exactly the familiar inertial forces. | — |
| [[parallel-propagated-frame]] Parallel-propagated frame | core | A parallel-propagated frame is an orthonormal tetrad carried along a geodesic by parallel transport, with its time leg equal to the four-velocity. Because transport preserves inner products the frame stays orthonormal, and physically its spatial axes are the directions of freely falling, non-rotating gyroscopes. | — |
| [[fermi-walker-transport]] Fermi-Walker transport | advanced | Fermi-Walker transport carries vectors along an accelerated timelike world line so that they do not rotate: it keeps the four-velocity mapped to itself, preserves all inner products and keeps spatial vectors orthogonal to u. It reduces to parallel transport when the acceleration vanishes, and it is the law obeyed by the axes of a gyroscope on an accelerated path. | — |
