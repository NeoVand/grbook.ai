# Cosmology II: Friedmann dynamics and model universes

How Einstein's equations drive the scale factor of a homogeneous, isotropic universe: Newtonian cosmology as a warm-up and its boundary problem; the FRW model with a perfect-fluid source; the Friedmann, second Friedmann and acceleration equations; the fluid equation and the dilution of matter, radiation and vacuum energy; vacuum energy as a p = -rho fluid, negative pressure and the condition for acceleration; critical density, density parameters, the cosmic sum rule and the dimensionless Friedmann equation; qualitative analysis through the effective potential, the classification of Friedmann models and the Omega_m-Omega_Lambda plane; the catalogue of model universes (Milne, Einstein-de Sitter, radiation-dominated, de Sitter, matter-plus-Lambda, closed and open dust, negative Lambda, Einstein static and its instability, Lemaitre, Eddington-Lemaitre, bouncing); de Sitter and anti-de Sitter as maximally symmetric spacetimes; the explicit big bang and big crunch of Friedmann models, the Hubble time and age of the universe, Hubble friction for a scalar field, anisotropic Bianchi and Kasner models, and the steady-state alternative. Robertson-Walker geometry, redshift, distances and horizons belong to cosmological-kinematics; observations, Lambda-CDM parameters, thermal history and inflation to physical-cosmology.

| Concept | Tier | Summary | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [[newtonian-cosmology]] Newtonian cosmology | core | A uniform, expanding ball of self-gravitating dust treated with Newtonian gravity: energy conservation for a test mass on a comoving shell gives an equation for the scale factor with exactly the form of the Friedmann equation, with the curvature term reinterpreted as the shell's total energy. | — |
| [[boundary-problem-of-newtonian-cosmology]] Boundary problem of Newtonian cosmology | advanced | For an infinite uniform medium Poisson's equation has no unique solution: the Newtonian field inside depends on the shape of a distant boundary, so an edgeless Newtonian cosmology is not well defined, whereas in general relativity dynamics are fixed by what lies inside past light cones. | — |
| [[friedmann-robertson-walker-model]] Friedmann-Robertson-Walker model | core | The standard relativistic cosmological model: a Robertson-Walker metric, justified by the cosmological principle and Weyl's postulate, whose scale factor is determined by Einstein's equations (with cosmological constant) sourced by a homogeneous perfect fluid. Solving for different curvature, Lambda and matter content gives the family of Friedmann models. | — |
| [[cosmological-perfect-fluid]] Perfect-fluid model of the cosmic contents | core | Galaxies and radiation are smoothed into a homogeneous perfect fluid at rest in comoving coordinates, with density and pressure depending only on cosmic time; any bulk flow or anisotropic stress would pick out a direction and break isotropy. | — |
| [[friedmann-equation]] Friedmann equation | core | The time-time Einstein equation for a Robertson-Walker universe: H^2 = (adot/a)^2 = 8 pi G rho/3 + Lambda/3 - k/a^2. It fixes the expansion rate from the total energy density and spatial curvature, and, having no second derivative, acts as a constraint on the initial data rather than an evolution equation. | — |
| [[second-friedmann-equation]] Second Friedmann equation | core | The space-space Einstein equation for a Robertson-Walker universe, 2 addot/a + (adot/a)^2 + k/a^2 = -8 pi G p + Lambda, which contains the acceleration of the scale factor and is sourced by pressure. For an expanding universe it is not independent: it follows from differentiating the first Friedmann equation and using the fluid equation. | — |
| [[fluid-equation]] Fluid equation | core | Energy conservation for the cosmic fluid, rhodot + 3(adot/a)(rho + p) = 0, equivalently d(rho a^3) = -p d(a^3): the energy in a comoving volume falls both by dilution and by the p dV work done as the volume grows. It is the time component of the covariant conservation of the stress-energy tensor in an FRW spacetime. | — |
| [[acceleration-equation]] Acceleration equation | core | Combining the two Friedmann equations eliminates the curvature and velocity terms: addot/a = -(4 pi G/3)(rho + 3p) + Lambda/3. Ordinary matter and positive pressure decelerate the expansion, with pressure counting three times, while positive Lambda accelerates it. | — |
| [[density-scaling-with-scale-factor]] Scaling of energy density with the scale factor | core | Integrating the fluid equation for a fixed equation of state gives how each component dilutes: pressureless matter rho proportional to a^-3 (fixed particle number in a growing volume), radiation a^-4 (dilution plus redshift of each quantum), and vacuum energy constant. Hence radiation dominates early, matter later and Lambda last. | — |
| [[vacuum-energy]] Vacuum energy as a perfect fluid | core | Moving the Lambda g term to the source side turns the cosmological constant into a perfect fluid of constant energy density rho_Lambda = Lambda/(8 pi G) and pressure p = -rho_Lambda. Conversely, any vacuum energy must have a Lorentz-invariant stress-energy tensor proportional to the metric, so it behaves exactly like a cosmological constant and does not dilute as space expands. | — |
| [[negative-pressure]] Negative pressure and cosmic repulsion | core | A component whose pressure is negative (a tension) contributes rho + 3p to the gravitating density; for vacuum energy with p = -rho this is -2 rho, so its gravity is repulsive and it accelerates the expansion even though its energy density is positive. | — |
| [[condition-for-accelerated-expansion]] Condition for accelerated expansion | core | From the acceleration equation, the expansion speeds up (addot > 0) only if the dominant content satisfies rho + 3p < 0, i.e. p < -rho/3, which violates the strong energy condition; for a homogeneous scalar field this means its kinetic energy is less than its potential energy. | — |
| [[critical-density]] Critical density | core | The total energy density rho_c = 3H^2/(8 pi G) that makes a Friedmann universe spatially flat at the current expansion rate; today it is of order 10^-26 kg m^-3, a few hydrogen atoms per cubic metre. | — |
| [[density-parameter]] Density parameter | core | The ratio Omega = rho/rho_c of an energy density to the critical density, defined for each component (Omega_m for matter, Omega_r for radiation, Omega_Lambda = Lambda/3H_0^2 for vacuum energy) and for their total; the present values together with H_0 specify an FRW model and are the targets of observation. | — |
| [[curvature-density-parameter]] Curvature density parameter | core | The curvature term of the Friedmann equation written as if it were a density fraction, Omega_k = -k/(a_0^2 H_0^2) (equivalently a curvature energy density -3k/(8 pi G a^2)); it is positive for open, zero for flat and negative for closed universes. | — |
| [[cosmic-sum-rule]] Cosmic sum rule | core | Evaluated today and divided by H_0^2, the Friedmann equation states that the density parameters of all components plus the curvature parameter add to exactly one, Omega_r + Omega_m + Omega_Lambda + Omega_k = 1, so the spatial curvature follows from the measured contents. | — |
| [[density-parameter-and-geometry]] Density parameter and spatial geometry | core | Rewriting the Friedmann equation as k/adot^2 = Omega - 1 shows that total density relative to critical fixes the sign of the curvature: Omega > 1 gives a closed (spherical) universe, Omega = 1 flat and Omega < 1 open (hyperbolic); since k is constant, the sign of Omega - 1 can never change. | — |
| [[multi-component-friedmann-equation]] Multi-component Friedmann equation | core | The Friedmann equation with the total density split into components that each scale with their own power of the scale factor: H^2 = (8 pi G/3)[rho_r0 (a_0/a)^4 + rho_m0 (a_0/a)^3 + rho_Lambda] - k/a^2, so knowing the densities and expansion rate at one time determines a(t) for all times. | — |
| [[dimensionless-friedmann-equation]] Dimensionless Friedmann equation | core | Measuring the scale factor against its present value and time in Hubble times turns the Friedmann equation into (dx/dt~)^2 = Omega_r/x^2 + Omega_m/x + Omega_Lambda x^2 + Omega_k, so the density parameters alone fix the shape of the expansion history while H_0 only sets the time scale. | — |
| [[effective-potential-for-scale-factor]] Effective potential for the scale factor | core | Writing the Friedmann equation as (1/2) adot^2 + V(a) = E turns cosmic expansion into one-dimensional motion of a particle in a potential: the universe can only be where V is below E, points where V = E are turning points, and the dominant term at small or large a gives power-law or exponential behaviour. | — |
| [[classification-of-friedmann-models]] Classification of Friedmann models | core | Dust Friedmann models fall into a finite set of qualitatively distinct histories labelled by the curvature index k and by the sign of Lambda and its size relative to the critical value: big-bang models that expand forever, recollapse, or loiter, and models that bounce or approach a static state. | — |
| [[omega-matter-omega-lambda-plane]] Omega_m-Omega_Lambda plane | core | With radiation neglected, each point (Omega_m, Omega_Lambda) is an FRW model, and curves divide the plane: Omega_m + Omega_Lambda = 1 separates closed from open, Omega_Lambda = Omega_m/2 separates accelerating from decelerating, and curves from the effective potential separate recollapse from eternal expansion and big-bang models from bouncing ones. | — |
| [[fate-of-the-universe]] Fate of the universe | core | Whether a Friedmann universe expands forever or recollapses follows from its curvature and contents: with Lambda = 0, closed models recollapse to a big crunch while flat and open ones expand forever; a positive Lambda eventually dominates any ever-expanding model and can make even a closed universe expand forever. | — |
| [[milne-universe]] Milne universe | core | The empty Robertson-Walker model: with no matter and Lambda = 0, the Friedmann equation allows k = 0 with constant a (Minkowski space in ordinary coordinates) or k = -1 with a = t, which is again flat Minkowski spacetime sliced by hyperboloids of constant proper time from an origin. | — |
| [[friedmann-equation-for-dust]] Friedmann equation for dust | core | For pressureless matter plus a cosmological constant, conservation makes rho a^3 constant and the Friedmann equation becomes adot^2 = C/a + Lambda a^2/3 - k with C = (8 pi G/3) rho a^3; this single nonlinear equation generates the whole catalogue of classical model universes. | — |
| [[einstein-de-sitter-universe]] Einstein-de Sitter universe | core | The spatially flat, Lambda = 0 universe containing only dust: a grows as t^(2/3), H = 2/(3t), the deceleration parameter is 1/2, and its age is 2/(3H_0). It sits exactly at critical density and describes the early behaviour of every dust big-bang model, since the matter term dominates at small a. | — |
| [[radiation-dominated-universe]] Radiation-dominated universe | core | A flat universe whose gravity is dominated by radiation (rho proportional to a^-4, p = rho/3) expands as a proportional to t^(1/2) with H = 1/(2t), faster at early times than matter-dominated growth because pressure adds to the gravitating density; it begins from a = 0 with infinite density. | — |
| [[matter-dominated-era]] Matter-dominated era | core | The epoch after radiation domination and before vacuum domination when pressureless matter controls the expansion, so rho a^3 is constant and, with curvature and Lambda negligible, the universe follows the Einstein-de Sitter law a proportional to t^(2/3). | — |
| [[de-sitter-universe]] De Sitter universe | core | The flat Robertson-Walker model containing only a positive cosmological constant: the Friedmann equation gives constant H = sqrt(Lambda/3) and exponential expansion a proportional to exp(Ht), with no big bang and no preferred epoch, since a time shift is undone by rescaling space. | — |
| [[vacuum-dominated-era]] Vacuum domination and late-time exponential expansion | core | In any ever-expanding model with positive Lambda, the constant vacuum term eventually outgrows matter and radiation, so the expansion approaches de Sitter behaviour with a proportional to exp(sqrt(Lambda/3) t); de Sitter is the late-time attractor of these models. | — |
| [[flat-matter-lambda-universe]] Flat matter-plus-Lambda universe | core | The k = 0 model with dust and a positive cosmological constant has the exact solution a proportional to [cosh(sqrt(3 Lambda) t) - 1]^(1/3) (equivalently sinh^(2/3)): it decelerates like Einstein-de Sitter at early times, passes through an inflection where attraction and repulsion balance, and then accelerates into de Sitter expansion. | — |
| [[negative-cosmological-constant-universe]] Negative cosmological constant universe | advanced | A flat dust model with Lambda < 0: the right-hand side of adot^2 = C/a + Lambda a^2/3 vanishes at a finite size, so the expansion halts and the universe recollapses, with a proportional to [1 - cos(sqrt(-3 Lambda) t)]^(1/3); a negative vacuum energy attracts like ordinary matter. | — |
| [[closed-friedmann-universe]] Closed Friedmann universe | core | The k = +1, Lambda = 0 dust model whose spatial slices are 3-spheres: it expands from a big bang to a maximum radius set by its matter content and recollapses to a big crunch, with a cycloid solution a = (C/2)(1 - cos eta), t = (C/2)(eta - sin eta) in conformal time. | — |
| [[open-friedmann-universe]] Open Friedmann universe | core | The k = -1, Lambda = 0 dust model with hyperbolic spatial slices: it expands forever, starting like Einstein-de Sitter as t^(2/3) and approaching the coasting law a proportional to t at late times, when the curvature term dominates. | — |
| [[einstein-static-universe]] Einstein static universe | core | Einstein's 1917 static, spatially closed (R x S^3) universe in which a positive cosmological constant exactly balances the attraction of dust: staticity requires k = +1, Lambda = 1/a^2 and 4 pi G rho = Lambda, i.e. rho + 3p = 0 for the total content. Its metric, drawn as a cylinder, is also the standard backdrop for conformal diagrams. | — |
| [[instability-of-einstein-static-universe]] Instability of the Einstein static universe | core | Perturbing the scale factor about the Einstein static radius gives d^2(delta a)/dt^2 = Lambda delta a, so small disturbances grow exponentially: the static universe sits at a maximum of the effective potential and any slight excess or deficit of matter makes it collapse or expand. | — |
| [[critical-cosmological-constant]] Critical cosmological constant | advanced | For closed dust models with Friedmann constant C, the value Lambda_c = 4/(9C^2) (the Einstein static value Lambda_E) at which the static solution exists at radius 3C/2; it divides closed models with positive Lambda into those that recollapse or bounce (below) and those that expand forever (above). | — |
| [[lemaitre-universe]] Lemaitre universe | advanced | A closed dust model with Lambda slightly above the critical value: it emerges from a big bang, loiters for a long time near the Einstein static radius where the expansion rate nearly vanishes, and then accelerates into de Sitter-like expansion; the closer Lambda is to critical, the longer the pause (growing like the logarithm of 1/epsilon). | — |
| [[eddington-lemaitre-universe]] Eddington-Lemaitre universe | advanced | The closed dust model with Lambda exactly critical: in one branch it starts arbitrarily close to the Einstein static state in the infinite past and then expands into exponential growth, so it has no big bang; the other branch starts from a big bang and approaches the static state asymptotically. | — |
| [[bouncing-universe]] Bouncing universe | advanced | A closed dust model with 0 < Lambda < Lambda_c that contracts from very large size, reaches a nonzero minimum radius and re-expands exponentially without ever reaching a = 0; for the same parameters a separate branch expands from a big bang and recollapses, the two branches being separated by a forbidden region of the effective potential. | — |
| [[de-sitter-spacetime]] De Sitter spacetime | advanced | The maximally symmetric vacuum solution with positive cosmological constant, realized globally as a one-sheeted hyperboloid in five-dimensional Minkowski space; in global coordinates its 3-sphere slices contract to a minimum radius sqrt(3/Lambda) and re-expand, while flat and static coordinates each cover only part of it. | — |
| [[penrose-diagram-of-de-sitter-space]] Penrose diagram of de Sitter space | advanced | Conformally compactified, global de Sitter space maps onto a square strip of the Einstein static cylinder, with spacelike past and future null infinity as top and bottom edges; the flat (steady-state) slicing occupies half of this square. | — |
| [[anti-de-sitter-spacetime]] Anti-de Sitter spacetime | advanced | The maximally symmetric vacuum solution with negative cosmological constant: a hyperboloid in a flat five-dimensional space with two time directions, of constant negative curvature. Its periodic embedding time is unrolled to remove closed timelike curves, and its conformal boundary is timelike, so light reaches infinity and returns in finite time. | — |
| [[big-bang-singularity]] Big bang singularity | core | In Friedmann models with rho + 3p > 0 (more generally rho + 3p > Lambda/(4 pi G)) that are expanding today, the acceleration equation makes a(t) concave, so the scale factor reached zero a finite time ago; there density and curvature diverge and the spacetime cannot be continued, which is conventionally chosen as t = 0. | — |
| [[big-crunch]] Big crunch | core | The final singularity of a recollapsing Friedmann universe, where the scale factor returns to zero after reaching a maximum: the time reverse of a big bang, drawn as a spacelike future boundary on the Penrose diagram. | — |
| [[hubble-time]] Hubble time | core | The reciprocal of the Hubble constant, t_H = 1/H_0 (about 14 billion years), the natural time scale of the expansion; if the expansion has always decelerated, the tangent to a(t) today reaches a = 0 one Hubble time ago, so the true age is shorter than 1/H_0. | — |
| [[age-of-the-universe]] Age of the universe | core | The cosmic time elapsed since a = 0, found by separating variables in the dimensionless Friedmann equation: H_0 t_0 is an integral fixed by the density parameters, equal to 2/3 for Einstein-de Sitter and about 0.96 for a flat model with Omega_m = 0.3 and Omega_Lambda = 0.7. The same integral stopped at redshift z gives the cosmic time at which light was emitted. | — |
| [[cosmic-age-problem]] Time-scale problem | advanced | Before the 1950s the Hubble-time upper bound on the age of a decelerating universe (under 2 billion years with the then-accepted H_0) was shorter than the radiometric age of the Earth; proposed remedies included loitering Lemaitre or Eddington-Lemaitre models, and it was resolved by revised measurements of the Hubble constant. | — |
| [[steady-state-theory]] Steady-state theory | advanced | The 1948 Bondi-Gold-Hoyle cosmology based on the perfect cosmological principle: the universe looks the same at all epochs, expanding with the exponential de Sitter metric while matter is continuously created to keep the density constant. It made sharp predictions, such as an exact linear Hubble law, and was ruled out chiefly by the cosmic microwave background. | — |
| [[hubble-friction]] Hubble friction | advanced | In an expanding universe the equation of a homogeneous scalar field, phiddot + 3H phidot + dU/dphi = 0, contains a term 3H phidot that acts like velocity-proportional friction on a ball rolling in the potential, reflecting the dilution of the field's kinetic energy by expansion; when H is large it keeps the field rolling slowly. | — |
| [[bianchi-cosmology]] Bianchi cosmologies | advanced | Cosmological models that are spatially homogeneous but not isotropic: their slices are homogeneous under a three-dimensional symmetry group, classified into Bianchi types I to IX, and the field equations reduce to ordinary differential equations in time. | — |
| [[kasner-solution]] Kasner metric | advanced | The vacuum Bianchi type I solution ds^2 = dt^2 - t^(2p1) dx^2 - t^(2p2) dy^2 - t^(2p3) dz^2 with p1 + p2 + p3 = p1^2 + p2^2 + p3^2 = 1, which expands in some directions while contracting in another and has cigar or pancake singularities. | — |
