# Differential forms, orthonormal frames and Cartan methods

Index-free exterior calculus and frame methods on a manifold: wedge products, p-forms and multivectors with their sheet-tube-cell pictures and pairings, the exterior derivative and dd = 0, closed and exact forms and the Poincare lemma, integration of forms over oriented chains, the boundary operator and the generalized Stokes theorem, volume forms, hypersurface forms and the Hodge star, Maxwell's equations and automatic conservation in form language; orthonormal frames, tetrads and vielbeins (including null tetrads and the static-observer frame) with frame components as observer measurements; and Cartan's calculus of vector-valued forms, connection 1-forms, the two structure equations, curvature 2-forms, the Cartan method for computing curvature and the Bianchi identity in forms. Coordinate volume elements and densities, noncoordinate bases, parallel-propagated frames and Lie derivatives are owned by neighbouring domains.

| Concept | Tier | Summary | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [[orientation]] Orientation (handedness) | foundation | An orientation is the sign attached to an ordered set of directions spanning an area or volume: swapping any two directions reverses it. Antisymmetric objects such as wedge products and volume forms measure oriented, signed sizes rather than absolute ones. | — |
| [[wedge-product]] Wedge product | foundation | The wedge (exterior) product is the antisymmetrized tensor product of forms or of vectors; for 1-forms sigma wedge tau = sigma tensor tau - tau tensor sigma, so any 1-form wedged with itself vanishes. A p-form wedged with a q-form gives a (p+q)-form, and swapping the factors multiplies by (-1)^{pq}. | — |
| [[bivector]] Bivector | foundation | A bivector is an antisymmetric (2,0) tensor such as u wedge v; it represents the oriented plane element spanned by two vectors, and its components are the signed areas of that parallelogram projected onto the coordinate planes. | — |
| [[two-form]] Two-form | foundation | A 2-form is an antisymmetric (0,2) tensor, written (1/2) B_{mu nu} omega^mu wedge omega^nu, with n(n-1)/2 independent components in n dimensions. It can be pictured as a lattice of oriented tubes where two stacks of 1-form sheets intersect; fed two vectors it returns the signed number of tubes threading their parallelogram. | — |
| [[differential-form]] Differential form (p-form) | foundation | A differential p-form is a totally antisymmetric (0,p) tensor field: it takes p vectors, returns a number, and changes sign when any two inputs are swapped. Functions are 0-forms and covector fields are 1-forms; in n dimensions forms of degree above n vanish and there are n choose p independent basis p-forms. | — |
| [[ordered-index-summation]] Ordered index summation (vertical-bar indices) | foundation | Indices enclosed in vertical bars, as in B_{/mu nu/}, are summed only over increasing orderings (mu < nu), so each independent basis form appears once and the 1/p! factor of a full sum is dropped. Mixing the two conventions is a common source of factor-of-two errors. | — |
| [[multivector]] Multivector (p-vector) | foundation | A p-vector is a totally antisymmetric (p,0) tensor built from wedge products of vectors; a simple p-vector u_1 wedge ... wedge u_p represents the oriented p-dimensional parallelepiped spanned by its factors. Bivectors, trivectors and 4-vectors are the cases p = 2, 3, 4. | — |
| [[interior-product]] Interior product | foundation | The interior product inserts a vector into the first slot of a p-form, leaving a (p-1)-form; for example, inserting u into sigma wedge tau gives sigma(u) tau - tau(u) sigma. | — |
| [[pairing-of-forms-and-multivectors]] Pairing of forms with multivectors | foundation | A p-form and a p-vector combine into a number, normalized so a basis p-form paired with the matching basis p-vector gives 1; for a simple p-vector it equals the form evaluated on the constituent vectors, and geometrically it counts the form's sheets, tubes or cells inside the parallelepiped. | — |
| [[exterior-derivative]] Exterior derivative | foundation | The exterior derivative d is the unique linear map from p-forms to (p+1)-forms that gives the gradient 1-form df on functions, obeys the graded Leibniz rule d(alpha wedge beta) = d alpha wedge beta + (-1)^p alpha wedge d beta, and squares to zero. In components it differentiates each coefficient and wedges the new differential on the left, needing no connection or metric. | — |
| [[nilpotency-of-exterior-derivative]] Nilpotency of the exterior derivative (dd = 0) | foundation | Applying the exterior derivative twice to any form gives zero, because mixed partial derivatives are symmetric while wedge products are antisymmetric. It packages curl grad = 0 and div curl = 0 and is the form-side counterpart of the boundary of a boundary vanishing. | — |
| [[closed-and-exact-forms]] Closed and exact forms | foundation | A form is closed if its exterior derivative vanishes and exact if it is the exterior derivative of another form; every exact form is closed because dd = 0. Integrals of an exact 1-form depend only on endpoints, like the work of a conservative force. | — |
| [[poincare-lemma]] Poincare lemma | advanced | On a contractible region (for instance a star-shaped one) every closed p-form with p >= 1 is exact. Where the region has holes, closed forms can fail to be exact, so exactness detects topology. | — |
| [[integration-of-differential-forms]] Integration of differential forms | foundation | A p-form is the natural integrand over an oriented p-dimensional region: parametrize the region, pair the form with the wedge of the tangent vectors to get a function of the parameters, and integrate that ordinarily. Line integrals are integrals of 1-forms and flux integrals are integrals of 2-forms, and the Jacobian appears automatically from wedge antisymmetry. | — |
| [[chains-and-boundaries]] Chains, cycles and boundaries | advanced | A p-chain is an oriented p-dimensional integration domain (formally an integer combination of oriented pieces), and the boundary operator sends it to its oriented (p-1)-dimensional edge. Chains with no boundary are cycles, and integration pairs p-forms with p-chains the way 1-forms pair with vectors. | — |
| [[boundary-of-a-boundary-vanishes]] The boundary of a boundary is zero | foundation | Applying the boundary operator twice to any chain gives nothing: a closed surface bounding a volume has no edge, because every edge of its faces is traversed twice in opposite directions. Through Stokes theorem this is dual to dd = 0. | — |
| [[induced-boundary-orientation]] Induced boundary orientation | foundation | If a region is oriented by an ordered set of tangent vectors whose first member points outward at the boundary, the boundary is oriented by the remaining vectors in order. In one dimension this gives plus at the end point and minus at the start; on a spacetime box the outward normal on the earlier time face points to the past. | — |
| [[generalized-stokes-theorem]] Generalized Stokes theorem | foundation | For a (p-1)-form omega and an oriented p-dimensional region V, the integral of d omega over V equals the integral of omega over the boundary of V with its induced orientation. It unifies the fundamental theorem of calculus, Green, Kelvin-Stokes and Gauss divergence theorems in one statement. | — |
| [[volume-form]] Volume form | foundation | The volume form is the top-degree form equal to the wedge of an oriented orthonormal basis of 1-forms; in coordinates it is sqrt(/g/) dx^0 wedge ... wedge dx^3. Fed n vectors it returns the signed n-volume of the parallelepiped they span. | — |
| [[hodge-star-operator]] Hodge star operator | advanced | The Hodge star is the metric-dependent linear map, built from the volume form, that sends a p-form to an (n-p)-form (or to an (n-p)-vector) with the same number of independent components and the same geometric content. Applying it twice returns the original up to the sign (-1)^{s+p(n-p)}, where s counts negative signs in the signature. | — |
| [[hypersurface-3-form]] Hypersurface 3-form | advanced | In four dimensions, inserting a basis vector into the volume form gives the 3-form dSigma_mu, and inserting three edge vectors gives a 1-form; these represent oriented 3-volume elements of hypersurfaces. The spatial volume an observer with four-velocity u measures for a box with edges A, B, C is omega(u, A, B, C). | — |
| [[faraday-2-form]] Faraday 2-form | advanced | The Faraday 2-form is the electromagnetic field tensor written as F = (1/2) F_{alpha beta} dx^alpha wedge dx^beta = E_i dx^i wedge dt + B_x dy wedge dz + B_y dz wedge dx + B_z dx wedge dy. It is the exterior derivative of the potential 1-form A, so it is automatically closed. | — |
| [[maxwell-2-form]] Maxwell 2-form (dual Faraday form) | advanced | The Maxwell 2-form is the Hodge dual of the Faraday 2-form; its components are those of F with E replaced by -B and B by E. The sources couple to it, since the exterior derivative of F itself vanishes identically. | — |
| [[current-3-form]] Current 3-form | advanced | The current 3-form is the Hodge dual of the four-current, J^mu dSigma_mu; its purely spatial component is the charge density and its time-containing components carry the current density. Integrating it over a 3-surface gives the charge on or crossing that surface. | — |
| [[maxwell-equations-in-differential-forms]] Maxwell's equations in differential forms | advanced | In form language electromagnetism is F = dA, hence dF = 0 (no monopoles and Faraday's law), together with d*F = *J (Gauss and Ampere-Maxwell laws), up to unit-dependent factors. The statement holds unchanged in any coordinates and in curved spacetime. | — |
| [[boundary-of-a-boundary-principle]] Boundary-of-a-boundary principle (automatic conservation) | advanced | When a field equation sets a source equal to the exterior derivative of a field form, conservation of the source is automatic: its flux through any closed 3-surface becomes an integral over the boundary of that surface, which is empty. For electromagnetism d*J = dd*F = 0 gives charge conservation; for gravity the same logic, via the contracted Bianchi identity, makes stress-energy conservation a consequence of the Einstein equation. | — |
| [[orthonormal-frame]] Orthonormal frame (tetrad) | core | An orthonormal frame is a set of basis vectors at each point, one timelike and three spacelike in spacetime, that are mutually orthogonal and unit-normalized so their inner products are eta. Components referred to it are what an observer carrying the frame measures, and frame indices are raised and lowered with eta rather than g. | — |
| [[vielbein]] Vielbein | core | The vielbein is the matrix (e_mu)^{a-hat} of components relating coordinate basis vectors to an orthonormal frame, together with its inverse; it satisfies g_{mu nu} = eta_{ab} (e_mu)^a (e_nu)^b. Finding a frame means finding a matrix that turns g into eta. | — |
| [[orthonormal-coframe]] Orthonormal coframe (basis 1-forms) | core | The orthonormal coframe is the set of basis 1-forms omega^{a-hat} = (e_mu)^{a-hat} dx^mu dual to an orthonormal frame, in which the line element becomes a sum of signed squares, ds^2 = -(omega^0)^2 + (omega^1)^2 + (omega^2)^2 + (omega^3)^2. It is the starting point of Cartan's method. | — |
| [[frame-components]] Frame components | core | Frame components of a tensor are obtained by contracting each coordinate index with one vielbein (or inverse vielbein) factor, e.g. T_{a-hat b-hat} = T_{mu nu} (e_a-hat)^mu (e_b-hat)^nu. In an orthonormal frame they are the numbers an observer carrying the frame actually measures. | — |
| [[orthonormal-frame-for-diagonal-metric]] Orthonormal frame of a diagonal metric | core | When the metric is diagonal, the orthonormal frame at rest in the coordinates is obtained by dividing each coordinate basis vector by sqrt/g_{mu mu}/; equivalently omega^{t-hat} = sqrt(-g_tt) dt, omega^{r-hat} = sqrt(g_rr) dr, and so on. | — |
| [[static-observer-orthonormal-frame]] Orthonormal frame of a static observer | core | A static observer holding fixed spatial coordinates carries the orthonormal frame obtained by normalizing the coordinate basis, with time leg along the observer's four-velocity. In Schwarzschild the vielbein components are (1 - 2M/r)^{1/2}, (1 - 2M/r)^{-1/2}, r and r sin theta, and the frame exists only outside r = 2M. | — |
| [[frame-formalism]] Frame formalism and the frame metric | advanced | In the frame (tetrad) formalism tensors are described by their components along any four linearly independent vector fields rather than coordinate basis vectors; the inner products of the frame vectors form the frame metric g_ij, which raises and lowers frame indices and rebuilds g_ab = g_ij e^i_a e^j_b. | — |
| [[null-tetrad]] Null tetrad | advanced | A null tetrad consists of two real future-pointing null vectors l and n with l.n normalized to one, and a complex conjugate pair m, m-bar orthogonal to both with m.m-bar normalized; all four legs are null. It is built from an orthonormal frame as (T +/- X)/sqrt2 and (Y +/- iZ)/sqrt2. | — |
| [[vector-valued-form]] Vector-valued differential form | advanced | A vector-valued 1-form eats a vector and returns a vector instead of a number; nabla v and de_mu are examples, and componentwise it is a sum of basis vectors tensored with 1-forms. Higher-degree versions, such as the curvature operator, are vector- or bivector-valued p-forms. | — |
| [[exterior-covariant-derivative]] Exterior covariant derivative | advanced | The exterior covariant derivative extends d to vector- and tensor-valued forms using the connection: for a vector field, dv is the vector-valued 1-form whose value on u is nabla_u v, computed by the product rule dv = e_mu tensor (dv^mu + omega^mu_nu v^nu). | — |
| [[connection-one-forms]] Connection 1-forms | advanced | The connection 1-forms omega^nu_mu record how basis vectors turn as one moves: de_mu = omega^nu_mu tensor e_nu, so omega^nu_mu(e_alpha) = Gamma^nu_{alpha mu}. In an orthonormal frame they act like infinitesimal rotation and boost generators of the frame. | — |
| [[metric-compatibility-of-connection-forms]] Metric compatibility of connection 1-forms | advanced | Metric compatibility in form language reads dg_{mu nu} = omega_{mu nu} + omega_{nu mu}; in an orthonormal frame the metric is constant, so omega_{a b} = -omega_{b a}. With a Lorentzian eta this means spatial pairs are antisymmetric (omega^i_j = -omega^j_i) while time-space pairs are symmetric (omega^t_i = +omega^i_t). | — |
| [[cartan-first-structure-equation]] Cartan's first structure equation | advanced | For a torsion-free connection the coframe satisfies d omega^mu = -omega^mu_nu wedge omega^nu. Together with the antisymmetry of the orthonormal connection forms it determines the connection 1-forms, often by inspection. | — |
| [[second-exterior-covariant-derivative]] Curvature as the second exterior covariant derivative | advanced | Applying the exterior covariant derivative twice to a vector field does not give zero: all derivatives of the field cancel and what remains is a curvature operator acting on the vector, d^2 v = e_mu tensor R^mu_nu v^nu. A connection is flat exactly when d^2 v = 0 for every v. | — |
| [[curvature-2-form]] Curvature 2-form | advanced | The curvature 2-forms R^mu_nu package the Riemann tensor by its antisymmetric last index pair: R^mu_nu = (1/2) R^mu_{nu alpha beta} omega^alpha wedge omega^beta, one 2-form per pair of frame indices. They are the gravitational analogue of the Faraday 2-form. | — |
| [[cartan-second-structure-equation]] Cartan's second structure equation | advanced | Cartan's second structure equation computes curvature from the connection forms: R^mu_nu = d omega^mu_nu + omega^mu_alpha wedge omega^alpha_nu. It is the form-language version of the Riemann formula built from derivatives and products of Christoffel symbols. | — |
| [[cartan-method-for-curvature]] Cartan's method for computing curvature | advanced | Cartan's method finds the Riemann tensor without Christoffel symbols: choose an orthonormal coframe, compute its exterior derivatives, read off the connection 1-forms from the first structure equation and antisymmetry, build curvature 2-forms from d omega + omega wedge omega, and read Riemann components from their expansion, converting to coordinates with the vielbein if needed. | — |
| [[bianchi-identity-in-differential-forms]] Bianchi identity in Cartan form | advanced | Differentiating the second structure equation and substituting it back gives dR^mu_nu = R^mu_alpha wedge omega^alpha_nu - omega^mu_alpha wedge R^alpha_nu; equivalently the exterior covariant derivative of the curvature 2-form vanishes. It is the forms version of the differential Bianchi identity. | — |
