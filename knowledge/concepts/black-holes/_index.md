# Black holes

Classical black holes, from the horizon to astrophysics: why r = 2M in Schwarzschild is a coordinate rather than a curvature singularity; radial infall, finite proper time and infinite redshift; tides and spaghettification; the interior where r is timelike and the r = 0 singularity; tortoise, Eddington-Finkelstein, Painleve-Gullstrand and Kruskal-Szekeres coordinates and the null-coordinate technique behind them; event horizons and their global nature; gravitational collapse; the Kruskal extension with its white hole, Einstein-Rosen bridge and Penrose diagrams; Reissner-Nordstrom and Kerr(-Newman) black holes with inner horizons, extremality and maximal extensions; frame dragging, the stationary limit, ergosphere, negative-energy orbits, the Penrose process and Kerr orbits; horizon area, the area theorem, Killing horizons and surface gravity as classical geometry; uniqueness (no-hair) and stability; and astrophysical black holes with their observation. Hawking radiation and black-hole thermodynamics, general causal-structure tools and gravitational-wave signals of black holes are handled in their own domains.

| Concept | Tier | Summary | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [[newtonian-dark-star]] Newtonian dark star | foundation | An eighteenth-century idea that a body compact enough for its Newtonian escape speed to exceed c would trap corpuscular light. Its critical radius 2GM/c^2 happens to equal the Schwarzschild radius, but in the Newtonian picture light still rises and falls back, whereas in general relativity nothing inside the horizon moves outward at all. | — |
| [[black-hole-mean-density]] Mean density of a black hole | core | The mass of a hole divided by the Euclidean volume 4 pi r_s^3/3 scales as 1/M^2, so a billion-solar-mass hole forms at roughly the density of water. Black-hole formation therefore needs no exotic high-density physics for large masses, and the tidal field at the horizon weakens in the same proportion. | — |
| [[coordinate-singularity]] Coordinate singularity | core | A place where metric components in a particular chart blow up, vanish or become degenerate only because the coordinates fail there, while curvature invariants and physical measurements stay finite. It disappears under a suitable change of coordinates; r = 2M in Schwarzschild coordinates and the poles of spherical coordinates are the standard examples. | — |
| [[radial-null-geodesics-in-schwarzschild]] Radial null geodesics in Schwarzschild spacetime | core | Radial light rays in the Schwarzschild geometry obey dt/dr = +/-(1 - 2M/r)^{-1}, so t = +/-(r + 2M ln\|r/2M - 1\|) + const. Drawn in Schwarzschild coordinates their light cones narrow to vertical slits as r approaches 2M, a distortion of the chart that makes any approach to the horizon take infinite coordinate time. | — |
| [[radial-infall-into-a-black-hole]] Radial infall into a black hole | core | A radial timelike geodesic in Schwarzschild with conserved energy per unit mass E; for release from rest at infinity (E = 1) dr/dtau = -(2M/r)^{1/2}, the same form as the Newtonian result. The fall is smooth through r = 2M: the coordinate speed dr/dt goes to zero there while the speed measured by a static observer approaches c. | — |
| [[horizon-crossing-in-finite-proper-time]] Finite proper time to cross the horizon | core | An infalling body reaches r = 2M and then r = 0 after a finite proper time (4M/3 from horizon to centre for fall from rest at infinity), yet the Schwarzschild time t along its path diverges logarithmically, with r - 2M shrinking like exp(-t/2M). The infinity belongs to the t coordinate, not to the traveller, which is the first clue that r = 2M is a coordinate singularity. | — |
| [[schwarzschild-black-hole-interior]] Interior of a Schwarzschild black hole | core | For r < 2M the metric coefficients of dt^2 and dr^2 swap sign, so r is a time coordinate and t labels a spatial direction; the interior is not static. Every future-directed timelike or null curve must move to smaller r and reaches r = 0 in finite proper time, however hard a rocket fires. | — |
| [[schwarzschild-singularity]] Schwarzschild singularity at r = 0 | core | The genuine singularity of the Schwarzschild geometry, where the Kretschmann scalar 48M^2/r^6 and all tidal accelerations diverge. Because r is timelike inside the horizon, r = 0 is a spacelike moment in the future of everything in the black-hole region rather than a point in space; in the Kruskal extension there is also a past copy bounding the white hole. | — |
| [[schwarzschild-tidal-field]] Tidal field of a Schwarzschild black hole | core | In the orthonormal frame of a static or radially falling observer the Schwarzschild curvature gives radial relative acceleration +2M/r^3 per unit separation and transverse relative acceleration -M/r^3, matching the Newtonian tidal tensor. These components are finite at r = 2M and diverge only as r goes to 0. | — |
| [[spaghettification]] Spaghettification | core | The radial stretching and sideways squeezing of a body falling toward a black hole, which grows without bound near r = 0 and tears any object apart. A star is disrupted where the hole's tidal acceleration exceeds its self-gravity, at R_T ~ (M_H/rho)^{1/3}; since the horizon grows faster with mass, stellar-mass holes shred infallers outside the horizon while supermassive ones swallow stars whole. | — |
| [[tortoise-coordinate]] Tortoise coordinate | core | The radial coordinate r* = r + 2M ln\|r/2M - 1\|, defined by dr*/dr = (1 - 2M/r)^{-1}, which pushes the horizon to r* = -infinity and turns radial light rays into straight lines t = +/-r* + const. In (t, r*) the radial part of the Schwarzschild metric is (1 - 2M/r) times flat 1+1 Minkowski space. | — |
| [[eddington-finkelstein-coordinates]] Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates | core | Coordinates that replace Schwarzschild t by a null coordinate, v = t + r* (ingoing) or u = t - r* (outgoing), keeping r and the angles. With v the metric -(1 - 2M/r) dv^2 + 2 dv dr + r^2 dOmega^2 is regular across r = 2M, so infalling matter and ingoing light cross the future horizon smoothly; the outgoing version does the same for the past horizon. | — |
| [[painleve-gullstrand-coordinates]] Painleve-Gullstrand coordinates | core | Schwarzschild coordinates whose time is the proper time of observers falling radially from rest at infinity, giving -(1 - 2M/r) dT^2 + 2(2M/r)^{1/2} dT dr + dr^2 + r^2 dOmega^2. The metric is regular at r = 2M and its constant-T slices are flat Euclidean space, which supports the picture of space flowing inward at the Newtonian escape speed. | — |
| [[light-cone-tipping-at-the-horizon]] Light-cone tipping at the horizon | core | In horizon-penetrating coordinates the future light cones lean further toward decreasing r as r decreases. Outside r = 2M one edge still points outward; on the horizon the outgoing edge stays at fixed r; inside, both edges point inward, so no future-directed path can increase r. | — |
| [[event-horizon]] Event horizon | core | The boundary of the region of an asymptotically flat spacetime from which no future-directed causal curve reaches distant observers (future null infinity). It is a null hypersurface generated by light rays that neither escape nor fall in; for Schwarzschild it is r = 2M, where curvature is finite but crossing is one-way. | — |
| [[black-hole]] Black hole | core | A region of spacetime from which no signal, not even light, can escape to distant observers, bounded by an event horizon. The simplest, non-rotating and uncharged case is the Schwarzschild geometry continued inside r = 2M, whose interior ends at the curvature singularity r = 0. | — |
| [[infinite-redshift-at-the-horizon]] Infinite redshift at the horizon | core | Light sent outward by a source falling toward r = 2M reaches distant observers ever later and more redshifted, the frequency dying away as exp(-t/4M), so the source appears to slow, redden and fade without ever being seen to cross. The crossing itself happens in finite proper time; only finitely many signals are emitted before it. | — |
| [[teleological-nature-of-event-horizon]] Teleological nature of the event horizon | advanced | Whether an event lies inside the event horizon depends on the entire future of the spacetime, so the horizon cannot be found by local measurements. A horizon starts growing before matter destined to fall in arrives, and in a collapsing star it forms at the centre before the surface reaches 2M. | — |
| [[gravitational-collapse]] Gravitational collapse | core | The unhalted contraction of a body whose self-gravity no pressure can resist; in general relativity pressure itself gravitates, so beyond a critical compactness support fails. In spherical symmetry the exterior stays Schwarzschild, the surface crosses r = 2M in finite proper time forming a horizon, and all the matter reaches r = 0. | — |
| [[oppenheimer-snyder-collapse]] Oppenheimer-Snyder collapse | advanced | The exact model of a homogeneous, pressure-free ball of dust collapsing to a black hole: the interior is a closed Friedmann-type dust universe joined to an exterior Schwarzschild region. It shows explicitly that the surface crosses r = 2M in finite proper time and that the event horizon forms at the centre before the surface gets there. | — |
| [[maximal-analytic-extension]] Maximal analytic extension | core | Enlarging a spacetime known on one chart to the largest spacetime with an analytic metric that contains it, so that every geodesic either continues to infinite affine parameter or ends on a genuine singularity. Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates give a partial extension of Schwarzschild; Kruskal's is the unique maximal one. | — |
| [[null-coordinate-reparametrization]] Null coordinate reparametrization | core | A method for removing coordinate singularities in a two-dimensional (t, r) geometry: build null coordinates from the two families of radial light rays so the metric reads f du dv, then replace u and v by monotonic functions of themselves chosen to make the factor regular. Exponentials give Kruskal coordinates; arctangents give finite ranges for a Penrose diagram. | — |
| [[kruskal-szekeres-coordinates]] Kruskal-Szekeres coordinates | core | Coordinates (T, X) built from exponentiated Eddington-Finkelstein null coordinates, in which the Schwarzschild metric is (32M^3/r) e^{-r/2M}(-dT^2 + dX^2) + r^2 dOmega^2 with r fixed implicitly by (r/2M - 1) e^{r/2M} = X^2 - T^2. Radial light rays run at 45 degrees everywhere and the metric is regular at r = 2M, failing only at r = 0. | — |
| [[kruskal-diagram]] Kruskal diagram | core | The (T, X) plane of the Schwarzschild geometry with each point a 2-sphere, drawn with 45-degree light cones. The horizons are the diagonals, r = 0 is a pair of spacelike hyperbolae, and the diagonals cut the plane into our exterior, the black-hole interior, a second exterior and a white-hole interior. | — |
| [[rindler-kruskal-analogy]] Rindler-Kruskal analogy | core | Schwarzschild coordinates relate to Kruskal coordinates as Rindler (uniformly accelerated) coordinates relate to inertial Minkowski ones: each covers a wedge, is singular on its null boundary, and uses a hyperbolic angle as time. Close to r = 2M the (t, r) geometry is literally Rindler space with acceleration parameter 1/4M, so a hovering observer is like a rocket. | — |
| [[kruskal-extension]] Kruskal extension | core | The unique maximal analytic extension of the vacuum Schwarzschild geometry: two asymptotically flat exteriors, a black-hole region bounded by a future singularity and a white-hole region emerging from a past singularity, joined across horizons at r = 2M. A black hole formed by stellar collapse uses only part of our exterior and the black-hole region; the rest is replaced by the star. | — |
| [[white-hole]] White hole | core | The time reverse of a black hole: a region emerging from a past singularity out of which matter and light can only leave and which nothing can enter. It appears as region II' of the Kruskal extension and as the interior of the outgoing Eddington-Finkelstein extension, and is not expected to form astrophysically. | — |
| [[einstein-rosen-bridge]] Einstein-Rosen bridge | core | The spatial geometry of a slice through both exteriors of the Kruskal extension: two asymptotically flat sheets joined by a throat whose smallest 2-sphere has areal radius 2M on the time-symmetric slice. Embedded in flat space it looks like a funnel connecting two Flamm paraboloids. | — |
| [[non-traversability-of-schwarzschild-wormhole]] Non-traversability of the Schwarzschild wormhole | core | No timelike or null curve connects the two exteriors of the Kruskal extension. Viewed as a sequence of slices, the throat opens from a pinch at the past singularity to radius 2M and closes again at the future singularity faster than any signal could cross. | — |
| [[penrose-diagram-of-kruskal-spacetime]] Penrose diagram of the Kruskal spacetime | core | The conformal diagram of the maximally extended Schwarzschild geometry: two exterior diamonds, each with its own i+, i0, i-, scri+ and scri-, a black-hole region above bounded by a horizontal future singularity and a white-hole region below bounded by the past singularity. It shows at a glance which events can signal which. | — |
| [[penrose-diagram-of-gravitational-collapse]] Penrose diagram of gravitational collapse | core | The conformal diagram of a spherical star collapsing to a black hole: the star's interior borders the regular centre r = 0, the vacuum exterior runs out to scri, an event horizon forms inside the star and rises at 45 degrees to i+, and a spacelike singularity caps the interior. No second exterior or white hole appears. | — |
| [[reissner-nordstrom-metric]] Reissner-Nordstrom metric | core | The static, spherically symmetric, asymptotically flat solution of the Einstein-Maxwell equations for mass M and charge Q: ds^2 = -f dt^2 + f^{-1} dr^2 + r^2 dOmega^2 with f = 1 - 2M/r + Q^2/r^2 in geometrized Gaussian units, with a radial Coulomb field. The field energy produces the extra repulsive Q^2/r^2 term, and Q = 0 gives Schwarzschild. | — |
| [[inner-and-outer-horizons]] Inner and outer horizons | core | Charged and rotating black holes have two horizons where the radial metric function vanishes: r_+/- = M +/- (M^2 - Q^2)^{1/2} for Reissner-Nordstrom and M +/- (M^2 - a^2)^{1/2} for Kerr. The outer one is the event horizon; between them r is timelike and every sphere is trapped, and inside the inner (Cauchy) horizon r becomes spacelike again and the singularity is timelike. | — |
| [[extremal-black-hole]] Extremal black hole | advanced | A black hole with the largest charge or spin compatible with a horizon, Q = M or a = M, where the inner and outer horizons merge into one degenerate horizon and the region between them disappears. Sub-extremal holes (Q^2 + a^2 < M^2) have two horizons; super-extremal parameters give no horizon at all and a naked singularity. | — |
| [[reissner-nordstrom-maximal-extension]] Maximal extension of Reissner-Nordstrom | advanced | The analytically extended charged black hole is an infinite vertical chain on its Penrose diagram: pairs of exteriors join through a trapped region to regions bounded by timelike singularities, which open through further horizons into new exteriors. A traveller can avoid the singularity and emerge into a different asymptotically flat universe, but never return to the original one. | — |
| [[blueshift-instability-of-inner-horizon]] Blueshift instability of the inner horizon | advanced | An observer crossing the inner horizon of a charged or rotating black hole receives the entire future history of an exterior region in finite proper time, so incoming radiation is blueshifted without bound. Small perturbations are therefore expected to destroy the smooth inner horizon, making the maximal extensions beyond it unphysical. | — |
| [[kerr-metric]] Kerr metric | core | The two-parameter (mass M, spin a = J/M) stationary, axisymmetric, asymptotically flat vacuum solution describing a rotating black hole; in Boyer-Lindquist coordinates it depends on Delta = r^2 - 2Mr + a^2 and rho^2 = r^2 + a^2 cos^2 theta and has one off-diagonal term g_t phi. It is independent of t and phi, reduces to Schwarzschild at a = 0, and is unchanged only when t and phi are reversed together. | — |
| [[kerr-spin-parameter]] Kerr spin parameter | core | The parameter a = J/M in the Kerr metric, the hole's angular momentum per unit mass, which has dimensions of length in geometrized units. A horizon exists only for \|a\| <= M, so a/M between 0 and 1 is the dimensionless spin, and its sign gives the sense of rotation. | — |
| [[boyer-lindquist-coordinates]] Boyer-Lindquist coordinates | core | The standard (t, r, theta, phi) coordinates for Kerr, adapted to its two Killing vectors so that g_t phi is the only off-diagonal component. Their r is not an areal or Euclidean radius: surfaces of constant r are confocal oblate spheroids and r = 0 is a disc of radius a, and the chart is singular where Delta = 0. | — |
| [[frame-dragging]] Frame dragging | core | A rotating mass forces nearby free particles, light and local inertial frames to acquire angular velocity in its sense of rotation. In Kerr a particle with zero angular momentum still moves with dphi/dt = omega = -g_t phi/g_phi phi = 2Mar/[(r^2 + a^2)^2 - a^2 Delta sin^2 theta], which falls off as r^{-3} far away. | — |
| [[zero-angular-momentum-observer]] Zero-angular-momentum observer | advanced | An observer at fixed r and theta with zero angular momentum, who therefore circulates at the frame-dragging rate omega. ZAMOs serve as local reference clocks near a rotating hole: the energy they measure for a particle with conserved E and L is proportional to E - omega L, which must be positive. | — |
| [[stationary-limit-surface]] Stationary limit surface | core | The surface g_tt = 0 of a rotating black hole, at r_0 = M + (M^2 - a^2 cos^2 theta)^{1/2} for Kerr, inside which no observer or light ray can stay at rest relative to infinity because even counter-rotating light is swept along. It touches the outer horizon at the poles, reaches 2M at the equator, and is timelike elsewhere, so it can be crossed in both directions. | — |
| [[ergosphere]] Ergosphere | core | The region between the outer horizon and the stationary limit surface of a rotating black hole, outside the hole itself, where the time-translation Killing vector is spacelike. Everything there must co-rotate, but escape to infinity is still possible, and conserved Killing energy can be negative. | — |
| [[ring-singularity]] Ring singularity | core | The curvature singularity of Kerr at rho^2 = r^2 + a^2 cos^2 theta = 0, which is r = 0 on the equator and, in Kerr-Schild Cartesian terms, a ring of radius a. It is timelike and can be avoided, and curves passing through the disc it bounds enter a region with negative r. | — |
| [[maximal-extension-of-kerr]] Maximal extension of Kerr | advanced | The analytic extension of Kerr across its horizons and through the disc bounded by the ring singularity. Along the axis its Penrose diagram is an infinite chain of exteriors, trapped regions and inner regions like Reissner-Nordstrom, and passing through the disc leads to an asymptotically flat region with r < 0 where the field resembles that of a negative mass and closed timelike curves occur. | — |
| [[kerr-equatorial-orbits]] Equatorial orbits in Kerr | advanced | Geodesics in the equatorial plane of Kerr, solved with the conserved Killing energy and angular momentum and an effective potential that reduces to the Schwarzschild one at a = 0. Because of frame dragging, prograde and retrograde particles and photons have different potentials, circular-orbit radii and ISCOs. | — |
| [[carter-constant]] Carter constant | advanced | A conserved quantity for Kerr geodesics associated with the theta motion that does not follow from any evident spacetime symmetry. Together with energy, angular momentum and rest mass it makes Kerr orbits fully integrable, and related separability lets wave equations on Kerr be solved. | — |
| [[principal-null-congruences]] Principal null congruences | advanced | The two families of null geodesics in Kerr at fixed theta that are the most outgoing and most ingoing light rays at each point, playing the role of radial light rays in Schwarzschild. Coordinates adapted to them, such as the ingoing Kerr coordinates, are regular across the horizon. | — |
| [[kerr-schild-form]] Kerr-Schild form | advanced | Writing a metric as the Minkowski metric plus a multiple of the square of a covector that is null with respect to Minkowski. Kerr found his solution in this form, and Schwarzschild is the case with coefficient 2M/r and l = (1, x/r, y/r, z/r). | — |
| [[newman-janis-algorithm]] Newman-Janis algorithm | advanced | A solution-generating shortcut: write a static seed metric as a null tetrad in advanced null coordinates, complexify r, shift v and r by i a cos theta, and demand real results. Applied to Schwarzschild it yields Kerr and applied to Reissner-Nordstrom it yields Kerr-Newman, although why it works is not fully understood. | — |
| [[kerr-newman-metric]] Kerr-Newman metric | advanced | The stationary, axisymmetric electrovacuum black hole with mass M, spin a and charge Q, containing Kerr and Reissner-Nordstrom as special cases. It has a horizon when a^2 + Q^2 <= M^2, and by the uniqueness theorems it is the most general stationary black hole of Einstein-Maxwell theory. | — |
| [[black-hole-uniqueness-theorem]] Black hole uniqueness theorem | core | Every stationary, asymptotically flat black hole of vacuum general relativity is a Kerr black hole, fixed by its mass and angular momentum alone; with electromagnetism it is Kerr-Newman, adding charge. All other details of the collapsed matter are radiated away or hidden behind the horizon. | — |
| [[black-hole-stability]] Black hole stability | frontier | The expectation, proven for linear perturbations of Schwarzschild and slowly rotating Kerr, that a disturbed black hole sheds all asymmetries as gravitational waves and settles to a Kerr state. It justifies using exact stationary solutions for real holes and underlies the ringdown seen after mergers. | — |
| [[negative-energy-orbits]] Negative-energy orbits | core | Where the time-translation Killing vector is spacelike, inside the ergosphere or the horizon, the conserved energy at infinity E = -p.xi of a physical particle can be negative, because it is no longer an energy any local observer measures. What must stay positive is the energy -p.u measured by a local future-directed observer, which for a ZAMO requires E > omega L. | — |
| [[penrose-process]] Penrose process | core | A body entering the ergosphere splits so that one fragment falls into the hole on a negative-energy orbit while the other escapes with more energy than the original body. Because the fallen fragment must also carry negative angular momentum, the hole loses both mass and spin. | — |
| [[superradiance]] Superradiance | advanced | The amplification of waves scattered off a rotating body when the wave's angular phase velocity is less than the body's rotation rate, which draws rotational energy out. For a Kerr black hole it is the wave counterpart of the Penrose process. | — |
| [[accretion-disk]] Accretion disk | core | A rotating disk of gas spiralling onto a compact object: friction, turbulent or magnetic, carries angular momentum outward so gas moves inward, turning gravitational energy into heat that radiates strongly, in X-rays near stellar-mass black holes. Its inner edge sits near the innermost stable circular orbit. | — |
| [[blandford-znajek-mechanism]] Blandford-Znajek mechanism | frontier | Extraction of a spinning black hole's rotational energy by magnetic fields, supplied by an accretion disk, that thread the ergoregion and horizon. It is the leading explanation for the power of relativistic jets from quasars and is often described as a practical realization of the Penrose idea. | — |
| [[ergoregion-instability]] Ergoregion instability | advanced | A runaway in a rotating star that has an ergoregion but no horizon: a negative-energy wave trapped there leaks positive energy to infinity, which deepens its negative energy and makes it grow until the star spins down. Kerr black holes avoid it because the horizon absorbs the trapped wave. | — |
| [[black-hole-horizon-area]] Horizon area of a black hole | core | The proper area of a stationary black hole's horizon cross-section, found by integrating the induced 2-metric: A = 16 pi M^2 for Schwarzschild and A = 4 pi (r_+^2 + a^2) = 8 pi M r_+ for Kerr, which halves between a = 0 and a = M at fixed mass. It is the key quantity of the area theorem and, later, black-hole entropy. | — |
| [[hawking-area-theorem]] Hawking area theorem | core | In classical general relativity with matter obeying the null energy condition, the total area of event horizons never decreases. Black holes can therefore merge but never split, and the area of a merger remnant bounds how much energy can be radiated. | — |
| [[killing-horizon]] Killing horizon | advanced | A null hypersurface on which some Killing vector field is null and normal to the surface. For stationary black holes in general relativity the event horizon is a Killing horizon (of partial_t for Schwarzschild, of partial_t + Omega_H partial_phi for Kerr), which gives the horizon a local geometric characterization. | — |
| [[surface-gravity]] Surface gravity | advanced | For a Killing horizon generated by a Killing vector normalized at infinity, the constant kappa defined by xi^b nabla_b xi^a = kappa xi^a on the horizon; physically, the force per unit mass needed at infinity to hold a body at the horizon. It is 1/4M for Schwarzschild, (r_+ - r_-)/2r_+^2 for Reissner-Nordstrom, vanishes for extremal holes, and sets the exponential redshift of signals leaving the horizon. | — |
| [[x-ray-binary]] X-ray binary | core | A close binary in which a neutron star or black hole pulls gas from a companion star into a hot, X-ray-bright accretion disk. When the compact object's mass, measured from the companion's orbit, exceeds the maximum neutron-star mass, it is taken to be a black hole; Cygnus X-1 is the classic case. | — |
| [[stellar-mass-black-hole]] Stellar-mass black hole | core | A black hole of roughly 5 to 60 solar masses left by the collapse of a massive star, found first in X-ray binaries and now in large numbers through gravitational waves. | — |
| [[supermassive-black-hole]] Supermassive black hole | core | A black hole of about a million to ten billion solar masses at the centre of nearly every large galaxy. Such holes power quasars and active galaxies, have low mean density and weak horizon tides, and grow by accretion and mergers. | — |
| [[intermediate-mass-black-hole]] Intermediate-mass black hole | frontier | A black hole between stellar-mass and supermassive, roughly a hundred to a hundred thousand solar masses, with candidates in bright X-ray sources and globular-cluster centres. The roughly 150-solar-mass remnant of the merger GW190521 gave the first clear example. | — |
| [[sagittarius-a-star]] Sagittarius A* | core | The supermassive black hole at the centre of the Milky Way, about four million solar masses, weighed from the Keplerian orbits of nearby S-stars that share a dark focus. Star S2 shows relativistic orbital precession, and the Event Horizon Telescope has imaged its shadow. | — |
| [[quasar]] Quasar | core | An extremely luminous galactic nucleus powered by gas accreting onto a supermassive black hole, first mistaken for a star because it looks point-like. | — |
| [[black-hole-shadow]] Black hole shadow | core | The dark patch on an observer's sky from which no light arrives, because rays traced backwards in those directions fall into the hole; for Schwarzschild it is bounded by the critical impact parameter sqrt(27) M. Around it lensing piles emission into a bright ring; a static observer near 3M sees half the sky dark, and almost all of it near 2M. | — |
| [[event-horizon-telescope]] Event Horizon Telescope | core | An Earth-sized very-long-baseline radio interferometer that resolves emission near the horizons of M87* (image released 2019) and Sagittarius A* (2022), showing a bright ring around a dark shadow whose size agrees with general relativity. | — |
| [[primordial-black-hole]] Primordial black hole | frontier | A hypothetical black hole formed in the very early universe rather than from a star, possibly light enough (around 10^12 kg) to be finishing its Hawking evaporation now in a final gamma-ray burst. Searches have found none so far; they are also discussed as dark-matter candidates. | — |
