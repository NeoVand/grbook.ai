# Linearized gravity and gravitational-wave theory

Gravity as a small perturbation of flat spacetime and the waves it supports, treated as theory: the metric perturbation and its Lorentz covariance, linearized curvature, linearized gauge transformations, trace reversal, the linearized Einstein equations and the Lorenz gauge, residual gauge freedom, the slow-motion ordering, the far field of stationary sources, gravitoelectromagnetism and the Lense-Thirring effect; plane waves, the transverse-traceless gauge, polarizations, the effect of waves on free test masses (strain and geodesic deviation); wave energy (flux, short-wave averaging, Isaacson tensor, angular momentum); generation by the retarded solution, multipoles, the quadrupole formula and luminosity, the absence of monopole and dipole radiation, circular binaries, radiation-driven inspiral and chirp mass; exact plane, impulsive, colliding and cylindrical waves; quasinormal modes of black holes as perturbation theory; Bondi-Sachs radiating spacetimes, news, Bondi mass loss and BMS symmetry; and the algebraic classification of curvature (principal null directions, Petrov types, optical scalars, peeling). Detectors, data analysis, sources and detections belong to gravitational-wave-astronomy; gravitons to quantum-gravity-and-beyond.

| Concept | Tier | Summary | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [[metric-perturbation]] Metric perturbation | core | In a weak field one can pick coordinates where the metric is the Minkowski metric plus a small symmetric correction, g = eta + h with every /h/ much less than 1; h carries all the gravitational information to first order, its indices are moved with eta, and its trace is h = eta^{mu nu} h_{mu nu}. | — |
| [[linearized-gravity]] Linearized gravity | core | Linearized gravity keeps Einstein's equations only to first order in the metric perturbation h, turning general relativity into a linear tensor field theory on flat spacetime; it is valid when fields are weak everywhere in the region studied and is the setting for the Newtonian limit, gravitomagnetism and gravitational waves. | — |
| [[lorentz-covariance-of-metric-perturbation]] Lorentz covariance of the metric perturbation | core | Under a constant Lorentz transformation of the background coordinates eta is unchanged, so h transforms exactly like a rank-2 tensor of special relativity; this justifies treating weak gravity as a symmetric tensor field on flat spacetime, a bookkeeping picture that holds only for such transformations and to first order. | — |
| [[linearized-riemann-tensor]] Linearized Riemann tensor | core | To first order in h the connection is Gamma^mu_{ab} = (1/2) eta^{mu nu}(h_{a nu,b} + h_{b nu,a} - h_{ab,nu}), products of connection coefficients drop out, and the Riemann tensor becomes a combination of four second derivatives of h: R_{a mu b nu} = (1/2)(h_{mu b,nu a} + h_{nu a,mu b} - h_{mu nu,ab} - h_{ab,mu nu}). | — |
| [[gauge-transformation-of-metric-perturbation]] Gauge transformation of the metric perturbation | core | A small coordinate shift x -> x + xi, with xi and its derivatives small, changes the perturbation by h_{mu nu} -> h_{mu nu} - xi_{mu,nu} - xi_{nu,mu} while describing the same spacetime; this redundancy is the gravitational analogue of A -> A - d chi in electromagnetism and can be spent to simplify the field equations. | — |
| [[gauge-invariance-of-linearized-curvature]] Gauge invariance of linearized curvature | core | The first-order Riemann tensor, and hence its contractions, does not change under a linearized gauge transformation, because the added terms cancel pairwise as commuting partial derivatives of xi; curvature rather than h is therefore the physical content of a weak field. | — |
| [[trace-reversed-metric-perturbation]] Trace-reversed metric perturbation | core | The combination h-bar_{mu nu} = h_{mu nu} - (1/2) eta_{mu nu} h has trace minus that of h, and applying the operation twice returns h; writing the linearized Einstein tensor in terms of h-bar removes the term in second derivatives of the trace and makes the field equations look like wave equations. | — |
| [[linearized-einstein-equations]] Linearized Einstein equations | core | Contracting the linearized Riemann tensor gives first-order Ricci and Einstein tensors; in an arbitrary gauge the resulting equations read -box h-bar_{mu nu} - eta_{mu nu} h-bar^{ab}_{,ab} + h-bar_{mu a,nu}^{,a} + h-bar_{nu a,mu}^{,a} = 16 pi T_{mu nu} (G = c = 1), a linear system whose divergence terms can be removed by a gauge choice. | — |
| [[lorenz-gauge]] Lorenz gauge | core | The Lorenz gauge imposes four conditions h-bar^{mu nu}_{,nu} = 0 on the trace-reversed perturbation (the gravitational twin of d_mu A^mu = 0); it can always be reached by solving box xi^mu = h-bar^{mu nu}_{,nu} for the gauge vector, and it collapses the linearized Einstein tensor to -(1/2) box h-bar_{mu nu}. | — |
| [[wave-equation-for-metric-perturbation]] Linearized field equations in Lorenz gauge | core | In Lorenz gauge the linearized Einstein equations become ten decoupled flat-space wave equations, box h-bar^{mu nu} = -16 pi T^{mu nu} (G = c = 1); their static limit gives Poisson's equation and their source-free solutions are gravitational waves travelling at the speed of light. | — |
| [[residual-gauge-freedom]] Residual gauge freedom | core | Imposing the Lorenz condition does not fix the gauge completely: any further shift generated by a vector obeying box xi = 0 preserves it, and for a plane wave choosing xi with the same wave vector supplies four free constants that can remove further components of the amplitude. | — |
| [[slow-motion-approximation]] Slow-motion approximation | core | For sources moving at speeds v much less than c, time derivatives are smaller than space derivatives by a factor of order v, so the d'Alembertian reduces to the Laplacian at leading order, and the stress-energy components are ordered T^00 >> T^0i >> T^ij (energy density, then momentum density ~ rho v, then stresses ~ rho v^2). | — |
| [[far-field-of-stationary-source]] Far field of a stationary source | core | Far from any isolated stationary source, the vacuum linearized equations in Lorenz gauge force the 1/r part of the field to be h-bar^00 = 4M/r only, so the metric approaches -(1 - 2M/r)dt^2 + (1 + 2M/r)(dx^2 + dy^2 + dz^2); this defines the mass M even for bodies with strong internal gravity. | — |
| [[gravitoelectromagnetism]] Gravitoelectromagnetism | core | For slowly moving sources, linearized gravity can be written like electromagnetism: the Newtonian potential acts as a scalar potential and h-bar_0i, sourced by mass currents, as a vector potential, giving gravitoelectric and gravitomagnetic fields that obey Maxwell-like equations with signs flipped because like masses attract. | — |
| [[lense-thirring-effect]] Lense-Thirring effect | core | The gravitomagnetic field of a rotating mass drags nearby inertial frames: gyroscopes precess with an angular velocity of dipole form proportional to G/(c^2 r^3)[3(J.r-hat)r-hat - J], and satellite orbital planes precess; the effect has been measured around Earth by laser-ranged satellites and Gravity Probe B. | — |
| [[gravitational-wave]] Gravitational wave | core | A gravitational wave is a propagating ripple of spacetime curvature that travels at the speed of light, is produced by accelerating non-spherical mass-energy, and carries energy and angular momentum; in linearized theory it is a source-free solution of box h-bar = 0, and gauge-invariantly the linearized Riemann tensor itself obeys a wave equation. | — |
| [[plane-gravitational-wave]] Plane gravitational wave | core | The plane-wave solution h-bar^{mu nu} = Re[A^{mu nu} exp(i k.x)] of the vacuum linearized equations requires a null wave vector (omega = /k/, no dispersion) and, from the Lorenz condition, an amplitude transverse to it, k_nu A^{mu nu} = 0; every vacuum solution is a superposition of such waves. | — |
| [[transverse-traceless-gauge]] Transverse-traceless gauge | core | Spending the residual gauge freedom, a vacuum wave can be made traceless and orthogonal to a chosen observer's four-velocity as well as transverse; for propagation along z only h_xx = -h_yy and h_xy survive, h-bar equals h, and no gauge freedom remains, so these two components are physical. | — |
| [[gravitational-wave-polarization]] Gravitational-wave polarization | core | A gravitational wave has two independent linear polarizations: plus, which stretches a ring of free particles along one transverse axis while squeezing it along the perpendicular one, and cross, the same pattern rotated by 45 degrees; any TT wave is h_+ e_+ + h_x e_x with basis tensors e_+ = e_x e_x - e_y e_y and e_x = e_x e_y + e_y e_x. | — |
| [[circular-polarization-of-gravitational-waves]] Circular and elliptical polarization of gravitational waves | core | Complex combinations of the two linear states give circular polarization, h_xy = +/- i h_xx, in which the deformation ellipse rotates without changing shape, and elliptical polarization in general; a circular binary emits circularly polarized waves along its orbital axis and linearly polarized waves in its orbital plane, so the ellipticity encodes the inclination. | — |
| [[free-particles-in-tt-gauge]] Free test masses in TT coordinates | core | In TT gauge Gamma^i_00 vanishes, so a free particle initially at rest keeps constant spatial coordinates as a wave passes and TT time is its proper time; the coordinates are simply attached to free masses, so the wave's effect must be read from proper distances, light travel times or curvature, never from coordinate positions. | — |
| [[gravitational-wave-strain]] Gravitational-wave strain | core | The proper distance between free masses a coordinate distance L apart along x oscillates as L(1 + h_+/2) to first order, and in general a mass at transverse offset x^j is displaced by (1/2) h^TT_ij x^j; the fractional change, of order h and at most about 1e-21 at Earth, grows with separation, which is why detectors are kilometres long. | — |
| [[geodesic-deviation-in-gravitational-wave]] Geodesic deviation in a gravitational wave | core | In the local inertial frame of one free particle, the separation to a neighbour obeys d^2 xi^j/dt^2 = -R^j_{0k0} xi^k with R_{j0k0} = -(1/2) d_t^2 h^TT_jk, a transverse quadrupolar tidal field that reverses each half period; read as an effective force it adds to non-gravitational forces, which is why a rigid ruler barely stretches while free masses do. | — |
| [[geometrical-optics-for-gravitational-waves]] Geometrical optics for gravitational waves | advanced | When a wave's wavelength and period are short compared with the curvature and change scales of the background, it travels along null geodesics and parallel-transports its polarization tensor, just like light, but it is almost unaffected by intervening matter because its coupling to matter is so weak. | — |
| [[feynman-sticky-bead-argument]] Feynman sticky-bead argument | core | Feynman's 1957 thought experiment: beads that slide with friction on a rod are moved by a passing wave's tidal field and heat the rod, so the wave must carry energy; it helped settle the debate over whether gravitational waves are physical. | — |
| [[gravitational-wave-energy-flux]] Gravitational-wave energy flux | core | A plane gravitational wave carries a period-averaged energy flux F = (1/32 pi) <(d_t h^TT_ij)(d_t h^TT_ij)>, which is omega^2 (A_+^2 + A_x^2)/32 pi for a monochromatic wave in G = c = 1 (c^3/16 pi G times <h_+dot^2 + h_x dot^2> in SI); it is meaningful only as an average and only in TT form. | — |
| [[short-wave-approximation]] Short-wave approximation and averaging | advanced | To give waves an energy, split the metric into a slowly varying background plus a small rapidly oscillating ripple, expand Einstein's equations to second order in the ripple, and average over regions several wavelengths across but smaller than the background curvature scale; under the average total derivatives vanish, removing gauge-dependent oscillating terms. | — |
| [[isaacson-stress-energy-tensor]] Isaacson stress-energy tensor | advanced | Moving the averaged second-order part of the Einstein tensor to the source side defines an effective stress-energy for gravitational waves, T^GW_{mu nu} = (1/32 pi) <d_mu h^TT_ij d_nu h^TT ij> (G = c = 1); the average makes it gauge invariant, it sources the background curvature, and its T^0z component reproduces the plane-wave flux. | — |
| [[linearized-gravity-lagrangian]] Lagrangian for linearized gravity | advanced | Linearized general relativity follows from a Lagrangian density quadratic in first derivatives of h whose Euler-Lagrange equations reproduce the linearized Einstein tensor, showing that weak-field gravity is an ordinary field theory on flat space and suggesting that wave energy is quadratic in derivatives of h. | — |
| [[retarded-solution-for-metric-perturbation]] Retarded solution of the linearized field equations | core | The causal solution of box h-bar_{mu nu} = -16 pi T_{mu nu} is h-bar_{mu nu}(t, x) = 4 integral T_{mu nu}(t - /x - y/, y)//x - y/ d^3y, an integral over the past light cone that keeps only outgoing waves; for a sinusoidal source outside it the leading solution is an outgoing spherical wave falling as 1/r. | — |
| [[far-zone-approximation]] Far-zone approximation | core | For an observer much farther from a compact source than the source's size, 1//x - y/ in the retarded integral is replaced by 1/r to the source centre, leaving a field that falls as 1/r; the region where this radiative 1/r part dominates is the wave zone, where the field looks locally like a plane wave. | — |
| [[long-wavelength-approximation]] Long-wavelength (slow-motion) approximation for radiation | core | If the emitted wavelength is much larger than the source, equivalently if internal speeds are much less than c, retardation differences across the source can be ignored and every source element is evaluated at the same retarded time t - r; the leading radiation is then quadrupolar. | — |
| [[mass-and-current-multipole-moments]] Mass and current multipole moments | core | The far field of a bounded source can be expanded in multipoles weighted by moments of its mass density (I_l ~ M L^l) and of its mass current (S_l ~ M v L^l); current moments are suppressed by a factor v relative to mass moments of the same order, so for slow sources the time-varying field is dominated by the mass quadrupole. | — |
| [[absence-of-monopole-and-dipole-radiation]] Absence of monopole and dipole gravitational radiation | core | Conservation of an isolated source's mass-energy, momentum and angular momentum fixes its monopole, mass dipole (centre of mass) and current dipole, so none can oscillate; gravitational radiation therefore starts at the quadrupole, unlike electromagnetism where the electric dipole radiates. | — |
| [[quadrupole-moment-tensor]] Mass quadrupole moment tensor | core | The second moment of the mass-energy distribution, I^{jk}(t) = integral T^00 x^j x^k d^3x, is the source quantity whose second time derivative sets the leading gravitational wave field of a slow source. | — |
| [[tensor-virial-identity]] Tensor virial identity | core | For a compact source with conserved stress-energy (d_nu T^{mu nu} = 0 in linearized theory), the volume integral of the stresses equals half the second time derivative of the mass quadrupole: integral T^{jk} d^3x = (1/2) d^2/dt^2 integral T^00 x^j x^k d^3x. | — |
| [[reduced-quadrupole-moment]] Reduced (trace-free) quadrupole moment | core | The trace-free part of the mass quadrupole, I-bar_jk = I_jk - (1/3) delta_jk I^l_l; the TT wave field and the radiated power depend only on it, so a spherically symmetric mass distribution, whose quadrupole is proportional to delta_jk, contributes nothing. | — |
| [[quadrupole-formula]] Quadrupole formula | core | Far from a slowly moving compact source the wave field is h-bar_jk = (2/r) d^2 I_jk/dt^2 evaluated at retarded time t - r (2G/(c^4 r) in SI), obtained from the far-zone retarded integral of T_jk by using the tensor virial identity. | — |
| [[transverse-traceless-projection]] Transverse-traceless projection | core | To extract the radiative part of a field propagating along unit vector n, project a symmetric tensor onto the transverse plane with P_ij = delta_ij - n_i n_j and subtract half its transverse trace: X^TT_ij = (P_ik P_jl - (1/2) P_ij P_kl) X_kl. | — |
| [[quadrupole-luminosity-formula]] Quadrupole luminosity formula | core | The total power radiated by a slow-motion source is L = (G/5c^5) <d^3 I-bar_jk/dt^3 d^3 I-bar^jk/dt^3>, found by integrating the TT energy flux over a large sphere; the tiny prefactor G/5c^5 means only compact, rapidly moving astrophysical masses radiate appreciably. | — |
| [[angular-momentum-carried-by-gravitational-waves]] Angular momentum carried by gravitational waves | advanced | Gravitational waves remove angular momentum at the rate dJ_i/dt = -(2G/5c^5) epsilon_ijk <d^2 I-bar_jl/dt^2 d^3 I-bar_kl/dt^3>; an axisymmetric source loses none, and for a circular binary the loss matches the orbital energy-angular momentum relation. | — |
| [[spherically-symmetric-sources-do-not-radiate]] No gravitational radiation from spherical motion | core | A source that pulsates, collapses or explodes while remaining spherically symmetric emits no gravitational waves at any order, because Birkhoff's theorem fixes its exterior as static Schwarzschild; in the quadrupole picture its trace-free quadrupole vanishes. | — |
| [[gravitational-waves-from-circular-binary]] Gravitational waves from a circular binary | core | Applying the quadrupole formula to two masses in a Newtonian circular orbit gives waves at twice the orbital frequency with amplitude falling as 1/r, radiation pattern proportional to (1 + cos^2 theta) for h_+ and 2 cos theta for h_x, and total power 32 G^4 m1^2 m2^2 (m1 + m2)/(5 c^5 a^5). | — |
| [[binary-inspiral]] Radiation-driven binary inspiral | core | A bound binary losing energy to gravitational waves becomes more tightly bound, so its separation and period shrink and its orbital speed, wave frequency and amplitude all rise until merger; balancing the Newtonian orbital energy E = -G m1 m2/(2a) against the quadrupole luminosity gives the rate, with df/dt proportional to f^(11/3). | — |
| [[chirp-mass]] Chirp mass | core | The chirp mass (m1 m2)^(3/5)/(m1 + m2)^(1/5) is the only combination of a binary's masses that enters the leading-order waveform amplitude, luminosity and frequency evolution, so it can be read directly from f and df/dt and is the best-measured parameter of an inspiral. | — |
| [[exact-plane-gravitational-wave]] Exact plane gravitational wave | advanced | Einstein's equations admit exact vacuum plane waves, for example ds^2 = -du dv + f(u)^2 dx^2 + g(u)^2 dy^2 with u = t - z, whose only field equation is f''/f + g''/g = 0; one profile function can be chosen freely, small amplitudes reproduce the linear plus-polarized wave, and all scalar curvature invariants vanish although the Riemann tensor does not. | — |
| [[brinkmann-coordinates]] Brinkmann form of a plane wave | advanced | In Brinkmann coordinates a plane wave reads h(U)(Z^2 - Y^2) dU^2 + dU dV - dY^2 - dZ^2: flat space plus one term quadratic in the transverse coordinates with the profile explicit, so co-propagating waves superpose exactly by adding profiles and do not scatter each other. | — |
| [[sandwich-wave]] Sandwich wave | advanced | A sandwich wave is an exact plane wave whose curvature is confined to a slab between two parallel null hypersurfaces, with flat spacetime before and after; a geodesic observer crossing it feels a transient transverse tidal acceleration. | — |
| [[impulsive-gravitational-wave]] Impulsive plane gravitational wave | advanced | An impulsive plane wave is the thin-slab limit of a sandwich wave: flat spacetime on both sides of a single null hypersurface carrying delta-function curvature, given in Rosen form by f = 1 + u theta(u), g = 1 - u theta(u) and in Brinkmann form by h = delta(U). | — |
| [[colliding-plane-gravitational-waves]] Colliding plane gravitational waves | advanced | Because Einstein's equations are nonlinear, two counter-propagating plane waves do not simply pass through each other: in the region where both are present the metric depends on both null coordinates and the waves scatter and focus one another, typically producing a singularity. | — |
| [[penrose-khan-solution]] Khan-Penrose solution | advanced | The Khan-Penrose solution describes two colliding linearly polarized impulsive plane waves exactly: flat regions before the collision, and a curved interaction region that ends in a spacelike curvature singularity, with fold singularities along its edges. | — |
| [[einstein-rosen-waves]] Einstein-Rosen cylindrical waves | advanced | For whole-cylinder symmetry the metric reduces to e^{2 gamma - 2 psi}(dt^2 - d rho^2) - rho^2 e^{-2 psi} d phi^2 - e^{2 psi} dz^2, and the vacuum equations become the ordinary linear flat-space cylindrical wave equation for psi, with gamma then fixed by line integration; regular pulses such as the Weber-Wheeler solution implode and re-expand and carry energy. | — |
| [[quasinormal-modes]] Quasinormal modes | advanced | A perturbed black hole settles to a stationary Kerr state by emitting gravitational waves in damped characteristic oscillations whose frequencies and damping times depend only on the final mass and spin; they die away within a few cycles, much faster than stellar oscillation modes. | — |
| [[bondi-sachs-coordinates]] Bondi-Sachs radiation coordinates | advanced | Radiation coordinates are adapted to outgoing null cones: retarded time u labels the null hypersurfaces, r runs along each null ray and is chosen so that the u, r = const two-surfaces have area 4 pi r^2, and the angles label rays by where they reach future null infinity. | — |
| [[bondi-sachs-metric]] Bondi-Sachs radiating metric | advanced | The general axisymmetric, non-rotating metric in radiation coordinates has four free functions V, U, beta, gamma of (u, r, theta); demanding asymptotic flatness and no incoming radiation and expanding in 1/r (for example gamma = n/r + q/r^3 + ..., V = r - 2M + ...) organizes the solution by a few functions of retarded time and angle. | — |
| [[bondi-hierarchy]] Bondi hierarchy of the field equations | advanced | Bondi sorted the vacuum equations for his metric into hypersurface equations that fix beta, U and V along each null cone once gamma is known, one evolution equation giving gamma's retarded-time derivative, one trivial equation and supplementary conditions that need hold only at one radius; this yields a marching scheme from null cone to null cone. | — |
| [[news-function]] Bondi news function | advanced | The news function is the retarded-time derivative of the 1/r coefficient of gamma; it is the free data at null infinity that drives the evolution, vanishes exactly when no radiation is emitted, and without axial symmetry comes as two functions, one per polarization. | — |
| [[bondi-mass]] Bondi mass | advanced | The Bondi mass is the total mass-energy of an isolated system measured on a cut of future null infinity, the angular average of the mass aspect M(u, theta) in V = r - 2M + ...; it agrees with the ADM and Komar masses when the system is stationary, and it is non-negative when the dominant energy condition holds. | — |
| [[bondi-mass-loss-formula]] Bondi mass-loss formula | advanced | For an asymptotically flat isolated system the Bondi mass obeys dm/du = -(1/2) integral (n_,u)^2 sin theta d theta <= 0, so the mass is constant exactly when there is no news and strictly decreases otherwise; this proves without any weak-field approximation that gravitational radiation carries away mass-energy. | — |
| [[bms-group]] Bondi-Metzner-Sachs group | frontier | The BMS group consists of the coordinate transformations that preserve the Bondi form of an asymptotically flat metric and its fall-off; it is the asymptotic symmetry group of radiating isolated systems, larger than the Poincare group because it contains angle-dependent translations called supertranslations. | — |
| [[principal-null-directions]] Principal null directions | advanced | Where the Weyl tensor is nonzero, the null vectors l satisfying l_[a C_b]ef[c l_d] l^e l^f = 0 define between one and four principal null directions at each point, and the pattern in which they coincide characterizes the algebraic type of the gravitational field. | — |
| [[petrov-classification]] Petrov classification | advanced | The Petrov classification sorts the Weyl tensor at a point by coincidences of its principal null directions: type I (four distinct), II (one pair coincides), D (two pairs), III (three coincide), N (all four) and O (Weyl tensor zero); spacetimes other than type I are algebraically special, and types can only become more special along the arrows of Penrose's diagram. | — |
| [[szekeres-test-particle-interpretation]] Tidal signatures of Petrov types | advanced | Szekeres read each Petrov type through the relative accelerations of a sphere of free particles: type N distorts only transverse to the propagation direction like a linear wave, type III adds a distortion tilted in a plane containing that direction, and type D stretches along one axis and squeezes the two perpendicular ones like the tides of a static mass. | — |
| [[optical-scalars]] Optical scalars of a null congruence | advanced | For an affinely parametrized null geodesic congruence, the expansion, twist and shear built from the covariant derivative of its tangent describe how a small cross-section of the beam grows, rotates and distorts along the rays; Sachs showed they are the enlargement, rotation and shearing of a shadow cast on a nearby screen, the same for every screen velocity. | — |
| [[newman-penrose-formalism]] Newman-Penrose formalism | advanced | The Newman-Penrose formalism writes all geometric quantities as components along a null tetrad of two real and two complex null vectors, packaging the Weyl tensor into five complex scalars; it is the standard language for radiation, peeling and exact-solution work. | — |
| [[peeling-theorem]] Peeling theorem | advanced | Along an outgoing null ray in an asymptotically flat radiating spacetime the curvature falls off as N/r + III/r^2 + II/r^3 + I/r^4, each term having the ray as a principal null direction of decreasing multiplicity; far away the field is type N, a local plane wave, and the principal null directions separate as one moves inward. | — |
| [[goldberg-sachs-theorem]] Goldberg-Sachs theorem | advanced | In vacuum, the Weyl tensor has a repeated principal null direction exactly when the spacetime admits a shear-free congruence of null geodesics running along that direction. It links the algebraic Petrov type of the curvature to the geometry of light rays, and underlies the Kerr principal null congruences. | — |
