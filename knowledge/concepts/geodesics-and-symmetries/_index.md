# Geodesics, symmetries and conserved quantities

Curves of free fall and the symmetries that make them solvable. Geodesics as autoparallel and as extremal curves; affine and non-affine parametrization; timelike, null and spacelike geodesics and the normalization of their tangents; variational methods (length functional, quadratic geodesic Lagrangian, free-particle action); maximal proper time, normal geodesics and conjugate points; the geodesic postulate, including forced motion. Lie brackets, Lie dragging and Lie derivatives; isometries, Killing vectors and Killing's equation, adapted coordinates and isometry groups; stationary, static, axisymmetric, cylindrical and plane-symmetric spacetimes; and the conserved energy and angular momentum that metric symmetries give along geodesics. Geodesic deviation, congruence focusing, motion in specific spacetimes, field-theoretic Noether currents and global charges are handled by other domains.

| [[test-particle]] Test particle | foundation | A test particle is an idealized body that responds to the gravitational field but is too small to change it, so the spacetime it moves through can be treated as fixed in advance. | — |
| [[geodesic]] Geodesic | foundation | A geodesic is the straightest possible curve in a space with a connection: its tangent vector is parallel transported along itself, nabla_u u = 0 with an affine parameter. Through each point and in each direction there is locally exactly one geodesic. | — |
| [[affine-parameter]] Affine parameter | foundation | An affine parameter is a parameter along a geodesic in which the geodesic equation has no term proportional to the tangent; any two are related by lambda -> a lambda + b. Proper time (or proper length) is affine for non-null geodesics, while null geodesics need some other affine parameter because proper time does not advance along them. | — |
| [[geodesic-equation]] Geodesic equation | foundation | In coordinates a geodesic obeys d^2x^mu/dlambda^2 + Gamma^mu_ab (dx^a/dlambda)(dx^b/dlambda) = 0 with lambda affine; it is a second-order ODE fixed by a starting point and initial tangent. In GR it is the equation of motion of a freely falling particle. | — |
| [[non-affine-parametrization]] Non-affinely parametrized geodesic | foundation | With a general parameter a geodesic satisfies nabla_u u = f u: the tangent keeps its direction but changes length, and the extra term reflects the choice of parameter, not a force. Being a geodesic is a property of the image curve, and a reparametrization that removes f makes the parameter affine. | — |
| [[timelike-spacelike-and-null-geodesics]] Timelike, spacelike and null geodesics | foundation | Geodesics in a Lorentzian spacetime are classified by their tangent as timelike, spacelike or null. Because a metric connection preserves the tangent's norm along an affinely parametrized geodesic, the character never changes along the curve; in Minkowski coordinates all three are straight lines. | — |
| [[geodesic-tangent-normalization]] Normalization of the geodesic tangent | foundation | Along an affinely parametrized geodesic g_ab u^a u^b is constant, so it can be fixed once: -1 for a massive particle parametrized by proper time, 0 for light, and +1 for a spacelike geodesic parametrized by proper length (signs flip in the +--- signature). This first integral is usually the easiest extra equation when solving geodesic problems. | — |
| [[null-geodesic]] Null geodesic | core | A null geodesic is a geodesic whose tangent has zero norm everywhere, so it runs along the local light cone and has no proper time; it must be labelled with an affine parameter. Freely propagating light follows null geodesics. | — |
| [[extremal-property-of-geodesics]] Geodesics as extremal curves | foundation | A curve joining two points is a metric geodesic if its length (or proper time) is stationary under small variations with fixed ends; applying the Euler-Lagrange equations to L = /g_ab xdot^a xdot^b/^(1/2) reproduces the geodesic equation of the Levi-Civita connection. Because the length integral does not depend on how the curve is parametrized, one varies first and only then chooses the parameter to be arc length or proper time, making L = 1. | — |
| [[affine-versus-metric-geodesics]] Affine versus metric geodesics | advanced | A manifold with an independent connection and metric has two families of privileged curves: autoparallels, which never change direction, and metric geodesics, which extremize length. They coincide exactly when the connection is the torsion-free metric-compatible Levi-Civita connection, which is why GR can use either definition. | — |
| [[relativistic-free-particle-action]] Relativistic free-particle action | core | The action of a free massive particle is minus its rest mass times the proper time along its world line, S = -m integral dtau. Its stationary paths are timelike geodesics, the minus sign turns maximal proper time into an extremum of S, and the integral takes the same value for any monotonic parametrization. | — |
| [[geodesic-lagrangian]] Geodesic Lagrangian | foundation | With an affine parameter, geodesics of every causal type are the Euler-Lagrange solutions of K = (1/2) g_ab xdot^a xdot^b, which gives the same equations as the square-root length Lagrangian but has no square roots and also works for null curves. Coordinates missing from the metric give conserved momenta at once, and K itself is a first integral. | — |
| [[lagrangian-method-for-christoffel-symbols]] Lagrangian method for Christoffel symbols | foundation | Write the Euler-Lagrange equations of K = (1/2) g_ab xdot^a xdot^b as xddot^a + (terms quadratic in xdot) = 0; the coefficient of xdot^b xdot^c is Gamma^a_bc when b = c and 2 Gamma^a_bc when b and c differ. For diagonal metrics this is usually much quicker than the Christoffel formula. | — |
| [[great-circle-geodesics]] Great circles as geodesics of the sphere | foundation | On a unit sphere with metric dtheta^2 + sin^2 theta dphi^2 the geodesic equations have great circles as solutions, such as meridians and the equator, while other circles of latitude are not geodesics. It is the standard first example of geodesics in a curved space and of geodesics from one point meeting again. | — |
| [[maximal-proper-time-of-geodesics]] Maximal proper time of timelike geodesics | core | Among nearby timelike curves joining two events, a timelike geodesic has the greatest proper time (provided no conjugate point lies between them); there is no minimum, because curves close to null zigzags have almost none. The Lorentzian sign reverses the Euclidean statement that geodesics are locally shortest. | — |
| [[geodesic-postulate]] Geodesic postulate | core | In general relativity a massive particle subject to no non-gravitational force moves along a timelike geodesic of spacetime, and light moves along null geodesics. It generalizes Newton's first law: gravity is no longer a force but the geometry that decides which world lines are straight. | — |
| [[geodesic-equation-for-four-momentum]] Geodesic equation for four-momentum | core | Using tau/m as affine parameter turns the tangent into the four-momentum, so free fall reads nabla_p p = 0. The same equation then covers photons, which have a four-momentum but no four-velocity; it is the curved-spacetime version of conservation of four-momentum. | — |
| [[geodesic-equation-with-external-force]] Geodesic equation with an external force | core | When a non-gravitational four-force f acts, m(d^2x^mu/dtau^2 + Gamma^mu_ab u^a u^b) = f^mu: the left side is mass times the covariant acceleration an accelerometer reads, and f = 0 gives back free fall. Keeping u.u = -1 requires f.u = 0. | — |
| [[kinetic-energy-metric]] Kinetic-energy metric on configuration space | advanced | Writing a mechanical kinetic energy as T = (1/2) g_ab qdot^a qdot^b makes g_ab a Riemannian metric on configuration space; the Euler-Lagrange equations become a geodesic equation with its Christoffel symbols plus a generalized force. Force-free Newtonian motion is therefore geodesic motion, which shows the geodesic idea is not unique to GR. | — |
| [[conformal-invariance-of-null-geodesics]] Conformal invariance of null geodesics | advanced | Two metrics related by a conformal factor, g' = Omega^2 g, have the same null geodesics as unparametrized curves, and so the same light cones and causal relations. This is why conformally compactified diagrams display causal structure faithfully. | — |
| [[normal-geodesic]] Normal geodesics | advanced | Normal geodesics leave a hypersurface along its unit normal. A curve of extremal length (or maximal proper time) from a point to a surface must be such a geodesic meeting the surface at a right angle, since otherwise sliding its endpoint along the surface would improve it. | — |
| [[conjugate-point]] Conjugate point | advanced | A point Q on a geodesic from P is conjugate to P if neighbouring geodesics leaving P in slightly different directions meet again at Q (a focal point is the analogue for geodesics normal to a surface). Past a conjugate point a geodesic is no longer locally shortest, or for timelike curves locally longest in proper time. | — |
| [[lie-bracket]] Lie bracket | foundation | The Lie bracket of two vector fields is the vector field [X,Y] = XY - YX acting on functions, with components X^b partial_b Y^a - Y^b partial_b X^a; the second derivatives cancel. Geometrically it is the gap by which a small loop of flows along X, Y, -X, -Y fails to close. | — |
| [[jacobi-identity]] Jacobi identity | foundation | For any three vector fields [X,[Y,Z]] + [Y,[Z,X]] + [Z,[X,Y]] = 0. Together with antisymmetry it makes vector fields under the bracket a Lie algebra, and it recurs for commutators of operators generally. | — |
| [[commuting-vector-fields]] Commuting vector fields | foundation | Two vector fields with vanishing Lie bracket can be made coordinate basis vectors of one chart, and their integral curves mesh into 2-surfaces; conversely, coordinate basis fields always commute. A basis field set is therefore a coordinate basis exactly when every pair commutes. | — |
| [[lie-dragging]] Lie dragging | foundation | Lie dragging carries a tensor along the flow of a vector field X using only the map x -> x + epsilon X that moves each point along its integral curve, with no metric or connection. A vector field that is Lie dragged keeps joining the same two neighbouring flow lines as it is carried along. | — |
| [[lie-derivative]] Lie derivative | foundation | The Lie derivative L_X T measures how a tensor field changes along the flow of X: compare the field actually present at a nearby point with the value dragged there from the starting point, and take the limit. It needs no metric or connection, maps (k,l) tensors to (k,l) tensors, obeys the Leibniz rule, commutes with contraction and reduces to X^a partial_a f on scalars. | — |
| [[lie-derivative-of-a-vector-field]] Lie derivative of a vector field | foundation | The Lie derivative of a vector field Y along X equals their Lie bracket, L_X Y = [X,Y]. The first term of the components is the change of Y along the flow; the second corrects for the way the flow itself stretches and rotates a dragged vector. | — |
| [[lie-derivative-of-a-tensor]] Lie derivative of a general tensor | foundation | In components L_X T is X^c partial_c of the components, minus one term T^..c.. partial_c X^a for each upper index, plus one term T_..c.. partial_b X^c for each lower index. The one-form case follows from the vector case and the Leibniz rule, and the rest by repetition. | — |
| [[lie-derivative-with-covariant-derivatives]] Lie derivative with covariant derivatives | foundation | For a torsion-free connection every partial derivative in a Lie derivative formula can be replaced by a covariant derivative, because the Christoffel terms cancel in pairs; for vectors L_X Y = nabla_X Y - nabla_Y X. This makes Lie-derivative statements manifestly tensorial. | — |
| [[lie-derivative-versus-covariant-derivative]] Lie derivative versus covariant derivative | foundation | L_X T at a point depends on the derivatives of X there, not only on its value, so it cannot define a rate of change of T in a single direction. A covariant derivative, which does depend only on the direction, is still needed for parallel transport and curvature; the Lie derivative instead expresses symmetry and dragging. | — |
| [[isometry]] Isometry | core | An isometry is a diffeomorphism that carries the metric into itself, so all lengths, angles and intervals are preserved; more generally a map is a symmetry of any tensor field it leaves unchanged. Lorentz and Poincare transformations are the isometries of Minkowski spacetime, and homogeneity and isotropy are statements about isometries. | — |
| [[lie-derivative-of-the-metric]] Lie derivative of the metric | core | (L_X g)_ab = X^c partial_c g_ab + g_cb partial_a X^c + g_ac partial_b X^c, which for the Levi-Civita connection equals nabla_a X_b + nabla_b X_a. It measures how the flow of X distorts distances. | — |
| [[killing-vector]] Killing vector field | core | A Killing vector field is a vector field whose flow is an isometry, L_xi g = 0: dragging the metric along it leaves the metric unchanged. Each Killing vector encodes a continuous symmetry of spacetime and yields a conserved quantity along geodesics. | — |
| [[killing-equation]] Killing's equation | core | A vector field is Killing exactly when nabla_a xi_b + nabla_b xi_a = 0, i.e. the covariant derivative of the lowered field is antisymmetric. As a tensor equation it holds in every coordinate system once it holds in one. | — |
| [[adapted-coordinates]] Coordinates adapted to a Killing vector | core | If no metric component depends on a coordinate x^k, then partial/partial x^k is a Killing vector, because the Lie derivative along a coordinate basis vector is just partial_k of the components. Conversely, any non-vanishing vector field can be made a coordinate basis vector, and several Killing vectors can be adapted at once exactly when they commute. | — |
| [[killing-vectors-of-flat-spacetime]] Killing vectors of flat spacetime | core | In Cartesian or Minkowski coordinates every Killing vector has the form X_a = omega_ab x^b + t_a with omega antisymmetric, so an n-dimensional flat space has n(n+1)/2 of them. Euclidean 3-space has six (three translations and three rotations); Minkowski spacetime has ten (four translations and six Lorentz generators), matching the Poincare group. | — |
| [[isometry-group]] Isometry group | advanced | The isometries of a metric form a group under composition. Its infinitesimal generators, the Killing vectors, form a Lie algebra: linear combinations and brackets of Killing vectors are again Killing, and maximally symmetric spaces have the largest possible algebra. | — |
| [[killing-vector-ricci-identity]] Second-derivative identity for Killing vectors | advanced | A Killing vector obeys nabla_c nabla_b X_a = R_abcd X^d (sign depending on convention), so on a connected manifold it is fixed everywhere by its value and first derivatives at one point. Contracting relates the wave operator of X to the Ricci tensor, nabla^a nabla_a X_b proportional to R_bc X^c, the identity used to build Komar integrals. | — |
| [[time-translation-killing-vector]] Time-translation Killing vector | core | A Killing vector that is timelike (at least far from the source) generates time translations: in adapted coordinates it is xi = partial_t and no metric component depends on t. Far away it coincides with the four-velocity of an observer at rest, which is what makes -p.xi an energy. | — |
| [[rotational-killing-vector]] Rotational Killing vector | core | A spacelike Killing vector with closed circular orbits generates rotations; in adapted coordinates it is eta = partial_phi, expressing that the metric does not depend on the azimuthal angle. A spherically symmetric spacetime has three of them obeying the so(3) bracket relations. | — |
| [[stationary-spacetime]] Stationary spacetime | core | A spacetime is stationary if it admits a Killing vector that is timelike (at least in an asymptotic region), equivalently a chart in which no metric component depends on the time coordinate. Time-space cross terms g_0i are allowed, so steady rotation is permitted: a uniformly rotating star is stationary but not static. | — |
| [[hypersurface-orthogonal-vector-field]] Hypersurface-orthogonal vector field | advanced | A vector field is hypersurface-orthogonal if it is everywhere proportional to the normal of a family of hypersurfaces, X_a = lambda partial_a f. This holds exactly when X_[a nabla_b X_c] = 0; for a Killing vector this condition makes the orthogonal slices a natural time foliation. | — |
| [[static-spacetime]] Static spacetime | core | A spacetime is static if it has a hypersurface-orthogonal timelike Killing vector; equivalently there are coordinates in which the metric is independent of t and has no dt dx^i cross terms, so it is unchanged under both t -> t + c and t -> -t. Stationary observers then share preferred simultaneity slices. | — |
| [[axisymmetric-spacetime]] Axisymmetric spacetime | core | A spacetime is axisymmetric if it has a spacelike Killing vector whose orbits are closed circles about an axis, so in adapted coordinates the metric does not depend on phi. It is the symmetry that survives when rotation breaks spherical symmetry, as in the Kerr metric. | — |
| [[commuting-killing-vectors]] Commuting Killing vectors | advanced | Two independent Killing vectors with vanishing bracket can be made coordinate basis vectors together, so the metric depends on only the remaining two coordinates. Whether their orbits are closed circles or open lines distinguishes cylindrical from plane symmetry. | — |
| [[cylindrical-symmetry]] Cylindrical symmetry | advanced | A cylindrically symmetric spacetime has two commuting spacelike Killing vectors, one with closed rotational orbits and one generating translations along the axis, so the metric depends only on t and a radial coordinate. It is the setting for exact cylindrical gravitational waves. | — |
| [[plane-symmetry]] Plane symmetry | advanced | A plane-symmetric spacetime has two commuting spacelike Killing vectors whose orbits are both open lines, such as translations in y and z, so the metric depends only on t and x (or null coordinates u and v). It underlies exact plane gravitational wave solutions. | — |
| [[conserved-momentum-from-metric-symmetry]] Conserved momentum from metric symmetry | core | If no metric component depends on a coordinate x^beta, the covariant component p_beta of a freely falling particle's four-momentum (massive or massless) is constant along its geodesic. It follows from the lowered geodesic equation m dp_beta/dtau = (1/2) g_na,beta p^n p^a. | — |
| [[conserved-quantity-from-killing-vector]] Conserved quantity from a Killing vector | core | If xi is a Killing vector and u is the tangent of an affinely parametrized geodesic, then u.xi is constant along the geodesic: nabla_u(u.xi) = u^a u^b nabla_a xi_b, which vanishes because Killing's equation makes nabla_a xi_b antisymmetric. In adapted coordinates this is the rule that u_alpha is conserved when the metric does not depend on x^alpha. | — |
| [[energy-at-infinity]] Energy at infinity | core | In a stationary spacetime E = -p.xi = -p_t is conserved along every geodesic, and it equals the energy that a static observer far away would measure. Locally measured energies differ from it by redshift factors, and where xi becomes spacelike (inside a horizon or ergosphere) E need not be positive. | — |
| [[conserved-angular-momentum-along-geodesics]] Conserved angular momentum along geodesics | core | In an axisymmetric spacetime the component p_phi = p.eta of a geodesic's momentum along the rotational Killing vector is conserved. For a nearly flat metric and slow motion it reduces to the Newtonian angular momentum m r^2 dphi/dt. | — |
