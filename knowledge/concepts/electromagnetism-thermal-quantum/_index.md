# Electromagnetism, thermal physics and quantum background

Non-gravitational background physics the books assume and briefly re-teach, in three-vector and elementary form: electric charge and its local conservation, the Lorentz force, Maxwell equations and Gauss law, scalar and vector potentials with gauge freedom and the Coulomb gauge, electromagnetic waves, plane waves and polarization, retarded potentials, and field energy density, Poynting flux and Maxwell stress; the laws of thermodynamics and free energy, black-body radiation, the Stefan-Boltzmann law and photon-gas statistics; and elementary quantum and particle physics (Planck and de Broglie relations, photons and the photoelectric effect, uncertainty relations, the Pauli principle, Fermi momentum and degenerate Fermi gases, antiparticles, virtual particles, isospin and the Standard Model as vocabulary). Covariant tensor and forms versions of Maxwell theory, relativistic fluid thermodynamics, stellar degeneracy pressure and black-hole thermodynamics are owned by later domains.

| Concept | Tier | Summary | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [[electric-charge]] Electric charge | prerequisite | Electric charge is the conserved scalar property of a particle that fixes how strongly it couples to the electromagnetic field; in a relativistic action it is the constant multiplying the line integral of the four-potential along the world line. | — |
| [[charge-conservation]] Charge conservation | prerequisite | Charge is conserved locally: the charge density can change in a region only by current flowing through its boundary, expressed as the continuity equation d(rho)/dt + div j = 0 (a vanishing four-divergence of the current). Integrating the density over a volume gives the total charge, whose change equals the net outward flux. | — |
| [[lorentz-force-law]] Lorentz force law | prerequisite | A charge q moving with velocity v in fields E and B feels the force q(E + v x B); covariantly, the rate of change of its four-momentum with proper time is q times the Faraday tensor contracted with its four-velocity, so in curved spacetime it measures the departure from geodesic motion. | — |
| [[cyclotron-motion]] Cyclotron motion | prerequisite | A charged particle in a uniform magnetic field circles the field direction at a fixed angular rate while moving uniformly along it, so its path is a helix. | — |
| [[maxwell-equations]] Maxwell equations | prerequisite | The four field equations of electromagnetism: div E = rho and curl B - dE/dt = j contain the sources, while div B = 0 and curl E + dB/dt = 0 do not and become identities once the fields are written in terms of potentials. | — |
| [[gauss-law]] Gauss law | prerequisite | Integrating div E = rho over a region and applying the divergence theorem shows that the enclosed charge equals the electric flux through the bounding surface; total charge of an isolated system is the flux through a sphere at infinity. | — |
| [[electromagnetic-potentials]] Electromagnetic potentials | prerequisite | The fields can be derived from a scalar potential V and a vector potential A as E = -grad V - dA/dt and B = curl A; together (V, A) form the components of the four-potential, and this form makes the source-free Maxwell equations automatic. | — |
| [[electromagnetic-gauge-transformation]] Electromagnetic gauge transformation | prerequisite | Adding the gradient of an arbitrary scalar function to the four-potential, A_mu -> A_mu - d_mu chi, leaves E, B and the Maxwell equations unchanged; this redundancy lets one impose a convenient gauge condition, and it is the template for gauge freedom in linearized gravity. | — |
| [[coulomb-gauge]] Coulomb gauge | prerequisite | The gauge condition div A = 0; in a source-free region it can be combined with A_0 = 0 (the radiation gauge), which leaves only transverse components of the potential. | — |
| [[electromagnetic-wave-equation]] Electromagnetic wave equation | prerequisite | In Lorenz gauge and away from sources each component of the four-potential, and hence of E and B, obeys the wave equation, so electromagnetic disturbances travel at the speed of light. | — |
| [[electromagnetic-plane-wave]] Electromagnetic plane wave | prerequisite | A vacuum solution A = epsilon exp(i k.x) with null wave vector; the gauge conditions force the polarization vector to be perpendicular to the propagation direction, leaving exactly two independent transverse polarizations. | — |
| [[retarded-potential]] Retarded potential | prerequisite | The causal solution of the sourced wave equation: the potential at (t, x) is an integral of the source divided by distance, with each source element evaluated at the retarded time t - /x - x'//c when a light-speed signal must have left it. | — |
| [[electromagnetic-energy-density]] Electromagnetic energy density | prerequisite | Electric and magnetic fields store energy per unit volume proportional to E^2 + B^2 ((E^2 + B^2)/2 in Heaviside-Lorentz units, (E^2 + B^2)/8 pi in Gaussian units); it is the time-time component of the Maxwell stress-energy tensor. | — |
| [[poynting-vector]] Poynting vector | prerequisite | The vector E x B (times a unit-dependent constant) gives the rate at which field energy flows through unit area; with c = 1 it also equals the momentum density of the field, the T^0i components of the stress-energy tensor. | — |
| [[maxwell-stress-tensor]] Maxwell stress tensor | prerequisite | The purely spatial block of the electromagnetic stress-energy tensor, giving the flux of field momentum: the pressures and shears that fields exert across surfaces. | — |
| [[fermat-principle]] Fermat principle | prerequisite | Light travels between two points along a path whose travel time is stationary (usually a minimum) with respect to small variations of the path. | — |
| [[first-law-of-thermodynamics]] First law of thermodynamics | prerequisite | Energy conservation for a thermodynamic system: the change in internal energy equals heat absorbed minus work done, dU = T dS - p dV for reversible changes. | — |
| [[second-law-of-thermodynamics]] Second law of thermodynamics | prerequisite | The entropy of an isolated system never decreases; for a relativistic fluid element this means the entropy per baryon cannot decrease along its world line. | — |
| [[helmholtz-free-energy]] Helmholtz free energy | prerequisite | At fixed temperature a system in equilibrium minimizes F = U - TS, which balances the tendency toward low energy against the tendency toward high entropy. | — |
| [[planck-relation]] Planck relation | prerequisite | A quantum of electromagnetic radiation with frequency nu carries energy E = h nu (equivalently hbar omega). | — |
| [[photoelectric-effect]] Photoelectric effect | prerequisite | Light, especially ultraviolet, ejects electrons from a metal; Einstein explained it by the absorption of individual quanta of energy h nu, making it key early evidence for light quanta. | — |
| [[wave-particle-duality]] Wave-particle duality | prerequisite | Light behaves as a wave in interference and diffraction but as a stream of particles when it exchanges energy with matter, as in the photoelectric effect. | — |
| [[photon]] Photon | prerequisite | The quantum of the electromagnetic field: a particle moving at c with zero rest mass, energy h nu and momentum h nu/c along its direction of travel. | — |
| [[de-broglie-relation]] de Broglie relation | prerequisite | Every particle has an associated wave whose wave vector is proportional to momentum, p = hbar k with /k/ = 2 pi/lambda; relativistically the four-momentum is hbar times the wave four-vector. | — |
| [[heisenberg-uncertainty-principle]] Heisenberg uncertainty principle | prerequisite | Position and momentum cannot both be sharp: their spreads obey Delta x Delta p of order hbar or more, so a particle confined to a volume has a minimum momentum spread. | — |
| [[energy-time-uncertainty-relation]] Energy-time uncertainty relation | prerequisite | Heuristically Delta E Delta t ~ hbar: a state that lasts only a time Delta t has an energy uncertain by about hbar/Delta t, which bounds how long an energy-violating fluctuation can persist. | — |
| [[black-body-spectrum]] Black-body spectrum | prerequisite | Radiation in thermal equilibrium at temperature T has spectral energy density proportional to nu^3/(exp(h nu/k_B T) - 1); the peak lies near h nu = 2.82 k_B T and the mean photon energy is about 2.7 k_B T. | — |
| [[stefan-boltzmann-law]] Stefan-Boltzmann law | prerequisite | Integrating the black-body spectrum gives a radiation energy density a T^4 and an emitted power per unit area sigma T^4, so a black body of area A radiates power proportional to A T^4. | — |
| [[photon-gas-partition-function]] Photon gas partition function | prerequisite | Statistical mechanics of an ultra-relativistic gas: a single-particle partition function proportional to V T^3 gives mean energy 3 k_B T per particle and pressure equal to one third of the energy density. | — |
| [[pauli-exclusion-principle]] Pauli exclusion principle | prerequisite | No two identical fermions can occupy the same quantum state; since each momentum-space cell of volume h^3/V is one state, it holds at most two spin-1/2 fermions. | — |
| [[fermi-momentum]] Fermi momentum | prerequisite | When N fermions fill the lowest momentum states in volume V, the largest occupied momentum p_F satisfies n = 8 pi p_F^3/(3 h^3) for spin-1/2 particles, so it depends only on number density and not on particle mass. | — |
| [[degenerate-fermi-gas]] Degenerate Fermi gas | prerequisite | A fermion gas so dense or cold that every momentum state up to the Fermi momentum is filled; its energy density and pressure are set by density alone and stay large even at zero temperature. | — |
| [[antiparticle]] Antiparticle | prerequisite | Every observed particle and antiparticle has positive energy; the negative-energy root of the relativistic dispersion relation is reinterpreted as an antiparticle with positive energy and reversed momentum, equivalently a negative-energy state running backward in time. | — |
| [[virtual-particle]] Virtual particle | prerequisite | An intermediate quantum whose four-momentum does not satisfy the free mass-shell condition p.p = -m^2; the energy-time uncertainty relation lets it exist only briefly, so it appears only inside processes and sets the range of the force it carries. | — |
| [[isospin]] Isospin | prerequisite | An internal quantum number that treats related particles such as the proton and neutron as two states of one object, with a symmetry rotating one identity into the other at each point. | — |
| [[standard-model]] Standard Model of particle physics | prerequisite | The quantum gauge field theory with symmetry group SU(3) x SU(2) x U(1) that describes quarks, leptons, the Higgs field and the strong, weak and electromagnetic interactions; gravity is not included. | — |
| [[many-worlds-interpretation]] Many-worlds interpretation | prerequisite | An interpretation of quantum mechanics in which every measurement-like interaction splits the universe into non-interacting branches, one for each possible outcome. | — |
